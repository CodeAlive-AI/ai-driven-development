## FPF.Reference:13 - Architecture As Structure Of Holons

FPF treats architecture as structure of a holon in a context.

This makes architecture broad. There can be architecture of a physical system, software system, organization, work system, body of knowledge, publication system, research program, AI-agent arrangement, or FPF itself. Wherever holons have structure, architecture can be discussed.

An architecture description or structural view makes claims about the architecture. A viewpoint provides concerns and conformance rules for a view; diagrams and models can represent selected structures; a publication form presents selected content. Use each through its own relation to the architecture and its source.

Use the pattern that governs the current architecture question: `C.30` for architecture as an EntityOfConcern, `A.22` for selected structure, `C.30.ASV` for architecture structural-view assertions, `C.30.AD` for architecture-description assertions, and `A.6.M` for module-interface relation repair. `C.31` and related architecture pattern descriptions locate exact rule content for modularity, reusable structure, scale, interlevel tension, and architecture-changing assertions. Architecture, selected structure, each description episteme, each view, each publication, and each change remain separate subjects.

Architecture work asks which structure matters, what characteristic changes, what trade-off is visible, what description is needed, what interface claim is being made, what evidence supports the decision, and which move changes the architecture.

Assess how hard a holon is to understand, change, control, reuse or improve under the declared architectural characteristics and concerns. Keep the structural dependencies that contribute to this difficulty visible in descriptions, including simplified diagrams.

A Method can be part of another Method and have constituents of its own. Updating a running total can contribute both to a calculation and to the preparation of a report. Hold the report and the measured job fixed. A final sum suffices to answer a question about that sum. Two histories with the same sum may first cross a limit at different times. To answer when the first crossing occurred, the representation must preserve enough about order, time and the values reached to distinguish those answers. A record that establishes the first crossing within the required interval can settle it directly. C.39.SA helps choose what to retain, and B.5.MR helps compare what two models of that subject admit for the same inquiry. A different notation can express the same model. Recover the constitutive links among the actions through B.1.5.EW and assess a replacement across their uses through B.1.5.RS.

The Method's constituent–whole relations also help identify what performers must be able to do or obtain, and which shared resources must fit their combined work. C.30.ASV:4.5a separates composition, result use and other relevant structures; A.22:4.3a explains how a drawing can represent them vertically or horizontally under a stated convention. A practitioner may know a basic operation and understand the whole procedure while still lacking an intermediate operation needed to connect them. To develop personal skill, practise that operation. A team may obtain it from a specialist, configure a tool or secure access to an existing service. Return to the whole task with the contribution the selected arrangement actually supplies. `A.22.CGUS` keeps these capability and resource conditions visible when judging an available continuation. Earlier completed work can supply a needed result without being enacted again now.

Extractable structural information is a reader-relative characteristic of a publication, assessed for the intended reader's preparation and available budget. It applies, for example, to a pattern's text, an explanation in a guide or an architecture description. Epiplexity formalizes structural information extractable from data by computationally bounded observers ([Finzi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2), §3); `C.29:4.2c` governs the use of that mathematical lens. `A.6.3.NAR` supplies the source-to-narrative relation for narrative publications. What counts as an improvement depends on the named object and intended use.
