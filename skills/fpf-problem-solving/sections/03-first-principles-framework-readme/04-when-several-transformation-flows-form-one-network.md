## When Several Transformation Flows Form One Network

Inspect `E.18.NET` when the current question joins independently identified transformation-flow structures through exact direct relation occurrences. Several stages, several paths, or several valuations of one flow do not make a network.

- **Build the builder.** A compiler-source-change flow, a bootstrap-compiler build flow, an application build flow, and a deployment-and-operation flow keep their own changes, work, positions, and local state. Recover the project's exact relation that says which executable compiler is used for the application build and its exact delivery relation to deployment. A selected tool-build network can itself be a member of a selected release network; follow a finite member path rather than inventing “level 2” and “level 3” kinds. Inspect `E.18.NET`; if the case is only one flow with several stages or valuations, stay in `E.18`.
- **Change a product and its production system.** Product-development Work changes and verifies the product definition; production-system-change Work redesigns, retools, and qualifies equipment; production Work makes units; operation supplies observations that may inform later product-development Work. Treat their transformation-flow structures as separately identified selected structures: the structures organize positions and exact relations but do not perform the Work. Keep the product and production system distinct, and recover the exact release, readiness, production, and observation relations needed by the current case. A selected product-development network and a selected production-system-change network can be members of a larger selected network without becoming one giant flow. Inspect `E.18.NET`; if only one architecture correspondence is current, use its direct architecture pattern instead.

Decide which conclusion about the entity is needed. To date its first existence, apply the identity criterion to its history. To date a specified state, use the corresponding state criterion and observations. A bound on the date can suffice. A claim attributing production to someone’s work requires the additional grounds below (`A.15.PROD:5.7`).

1. Use A.13 for each System credited at the scale of the production claim. Establish its identity, applicable local agential kind and criterion, classification, scope, working situation, window and the obtaining assignment on which the attribution relies. The workshop’s production may be known while the maker of one component remains unknown. Identify that contribution when it is part of the claim. Add a characteristic profile when the use requires it.
2. `A.15.1` independently admits one `U.Work` occurrence from the exact performance history, at least one Method actually followed, temporal extent, and at least one obtaining locally declared containing-System relation. That admission neither assumes nor requires F.6.
3. Only when the receiving use needs precise assignment-bound performer attribution, F.6 tests `performedUnderAssignment` through the same obtaining A.13 assignment, direct case fact, holder equality, declared species and participants, and coverage. An unresolved attribution alone leaves independently admitted Work intact. Recheck any consumed A.13/A.15.1 premise invalidated by the same finding and its dependent conclusions. A compact attribution sentence may omit an assignment identifier that its receiving use does not need; the omission changes only the sentence, not those facts.
4. For production through that Work, `A.15.PROD` tests the link from the admitted Work to the entity. Establish the entity’s inception or the completion of production separately if the receiving claim includes it.

Missing agency basis, Work granularity, production-participation predicate, identity-specification or inception basis, completion criterion or applicability, boundary or composition facts, or required substrate is a branch-local blocker. Actual change, output or result, identifier allocation, evidence, delivery and acceptance remain separate claims.
