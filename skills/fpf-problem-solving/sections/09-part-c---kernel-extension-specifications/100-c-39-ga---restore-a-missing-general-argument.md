## C.39.GA - Restore a Missing General Argument

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable
> **Normativity:** Normative

### C.39.GA:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** the grounds in a pattern, guide, recommendation or line of reasoning cover only some of the conditions its conclusion claims. Also use it when an author promises to explain how to reach a decision or construct an explanation but makes a necessary choice for the recipient and omits how to make it. Even for a single case, showing that a solution works can leave the promised explanation of how to construct it missing. Start with what the recipient must be able to explain, construct or decide. Two accounts can recommend the same action while giving different explanations of how the whole works. The text might explain it completely in a single example. If the commission asks only for a decision about one occurrence, its supported answer can finish the work.

### C.39.GA:2 - Problem

An author knows why a choice worked here. In explaining the method, the author carries that choice into the instructions as if someone else had already made it. Every sentence about the case can be true while the reader has no way to choose under different conditions. The same loss occurs without a story when an argument for one permitted arrangement stands in for all of them. The given argument can even be valid throughout its range. An author who promises to teach how to build an explanation may demonstrate why a finished design works and leave the reader to invent how to choose its parts or develop the explanation.

### C.39.GA:3 - Forces

A named part, action or event makes reasoning easy to follow. The decisive premise may be introduced there. Replacing its name with “an entity” can hide that premise just as effectively as copying the example’s answer. A repaired explanation keeps the working vocabulary and shows which changes its reasoning allows.

### C.39.GA:4 - Solution

**A mantra for restoring a general argument.** *What must the reader explain, construct or decide, and which choices are theirs? Which kinds of holon, over which composition scales for each kind? What connects these kinds? What holds at each scale, follows from part to whole or from whole to part, or depends on the parts working together? Which levels of abstraction of the relevant models does that work need, and which alternatives remain open at each? Which conclusion carries between models, in which direction, or needs several models together? Which promised choice or operation can the reader still make only because it was settled in the material? Where does the argument rely on a condition fixed only for a narrower use? Restore the way to make that choice or the grounds for the inference. Which later conclusions follow, under which conditions, and what is still owed?* Use the questions about composition and models when the work calls for those relations.

1. **Recover the promise.** From the commission, state the result the recipient must obtain or explain, which choices they must make and what can vary. For a claim across scales, identify the whole for each relevant kind of holon. Does that result need a rule at each scale, a link between scales or a result of several working together? For a range of model abstraction, ask what the model may leave open.
2. **Locate the lost step.** Work back from that conclusion to the premises it needs. Read forward from what the passage, its accessible sources and the audience’s preparation supply. Where does an inference first depend on a choice already made for this case or subclass? Could two rules give the same answer here and different answers under another permitted condition? Retain an argument made inside the example when it supplies grounds the recipient needs. There is a gap if the recipient needs an inference and has no adequate grounds for it in the account, its accessible sources or the agreed preparation. Also start from each operation promised to the recipient. Which inputs and ordinary help would be available to them before that choice was settled? A sound argument for that answer can leave the promised way of constructing an explanation wholly absent. Two different ways can both be adequate when the recipient is free to choose either.
3. **Repair the inference where it is used.** For a transfer, name the property to be established and the direction of the inference. Trace a permitted situation through the relation to the receiving situation. Show why the property is preserved, or identify a permitted exception. When several premises are needed, establish the relation between their subjects. A record may concern the very job being assessed, an earlier test may cover the new operating condition, or two actions may compete for the same resource. When a shared constraint restricts combinations, establish which combinations it permits. One feasible combination can establish possibility. A promise that must hold throughout a range needs grounds covering every admissible combination in that range. For an explanation of how to choose or build something, identify which needed choice the material already settles. Starting from the information the recipient actually has, develop a way to find alternatives and distinguish the ones that matter to the promised use. If the promise is to explain an organization, trace how its constituent contributions fit together and what their arrangement makes possible. Two arrangements that justify the same immediate action may require different explanations. A known working construction can yield a reusable operation under C.39.RO. Develop a missing construction in its subject practice. Keep a worked case when it supplies a needed inference or helps the recipient recognize when to use it. Show which fact or choice in the case does that work. If the recipient could complete it by using a distinction the author supplied but was meant to teach them to choose, restore the choice at the point of use. Replace or remove an episode that contributes nothing to the commissioned work. A sufficient direct argument can lead straight to application. If the grounds run out, give the supported answer and identify what is still owed.
4. **Recheck the reach.** Follow the repaired step through the conclusions that depend on it. A derivation for an arbitrary member, a sufficient bound or an exhaustive division into cases can cover a class. An empirical claim needs observations and grounds for extending them to the population and conditions claimed. When the missing work was a choice or construction, follow the repaired procedure from what the recipient has before that choice. Check whether the recipient can now make the entrusted choice, obtain the result and give the promised grounds with the help available there. Another admissible construction can be equally adequate. A changed case can expose an error. One sound argument can already cover the range, even when it gives different answers for different inputs.

### C.39.GA:5 - Archetypal Grounding

A guide promises to teach when an earlier inspection can support a later acceptance decision. It recounts how a team corrected the drawing title and accepted a component on yesterday’s inspection. “Reuse the inspection after a document edit” gives the right answer in that episode. The same wording would also cover an edit that changed the allowable load. At the first step from “the title changed” to “the previous inspection suffices,” the guide relied on what this particular edit left unchanged.

A repair the recipient can use is to identify the property that acceptance depends on, the item inspected, the conditions the inspection covered and the evidence the acceptance agreement calls for. Compare these with the changed order. If the earlier evidence establishes the required property under the conditions now in force and satisfies the agreement, use it. If the load changed, an inspection that already covered the new load can still suffice. If the earlier result leaves performance there open, obtain the missing grounds before accepting. An order for a fresh inspection creates a further obligation. The title correction illustrates one way the earlier grounds can remain sufficient. The reason for reuse also covers changes that leave those grounds sufficient in other ways. If the reader must learn to choose which property matters, let them examine the changed order and give their grounds before identifying the load for them.

### C.39.GA:6 - Bias-Annotation

A technical audience may supply a familiar unit conversion unaided. A reviewer who invents a new criterion for choosing the unit has contributed something different. Credit the passage, the intended preparation and the reader’s own construction for what each supplies.

### C.39.GA:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The result supported by the actual reasoning answers the commissioned question about its subject, including any explanation or construction entrusted to the recipient.
- The recipient can make the promised inference or perform the promised construction at the stated reach, or identify which consequential part remains open.
- Where the conclusion crosses composition scales or model abstraction levels, the explanation supplies the link it uses. A change of values within one model can test an assumption while keeping the model level fixed.

### C.39.GA:8 - Common Anti-Patterns

Calling the subject “a holon” leaves a rule for a system component unchanged if the rule still presupposes a physical interface. To use it for a constituent Method, establish how the whole Method admits that constituent and what its contribution makes possible. A common word alone gives no grounds for the transfer.

### C.39.GA:9 - Consequences

An individual or a team can use this repair in writing or reviewing a paragraph or a whole guide. For a whole publication, follow each promised conclusion to the place where its grounds become available, including consequential uses in diagrams, assignments and keys.

### C.39.GA:10 - Rationale

Agreement on one instance leaves open which premise produced that agreement. Restoring the premise lets the recipient work out where the inference holds, where it fails and where the evidence is insufficient. Changing the example alone can preserve the same omission.

### C.39.GA:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Mason and Pimm explain how a particular example can stand for an arbitrary member in a mathematical argument. Keep that argument when the reader can recover why it applies throughout the class. In experiments with negotiation strategies, Gentner and colleagues found that comparing cases helped participants recognize a relation they could use in another negotiation. Such a comparison can reveal the relation worth explaining. Its validity in the intended practice still depends on the subject grounds. [Mason and Pimm, *Generic examples*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00312078) · [Gentner, Loewenstein and Thompson, *Learning and Transfer*](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/gentner/papers/GentnerLoewensteinThompson03.pdf)

### C.39.GA:12 - Relations

When the composition scales or levels of model abstraction still need to be chosen or revised, C.39.SA supplies that selection. With the reach established, the four steps in C.39.GA:4 complete the ordinary repair. C.39.WA develops an account of a whole or field. C.39.RO derives a reusable operation from a known working construction. C.37.MM works out connections among kinds of holons and models, and B.5.MR tests a claimed refinement. EXD.Reference:3 develops the subject account for explanation design. Use those methods when the particular missing step calls for their work.
