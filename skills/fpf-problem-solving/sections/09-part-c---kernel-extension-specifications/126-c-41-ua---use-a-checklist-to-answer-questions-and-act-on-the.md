## C.41.UA - Use a Checklist to Answer Questions and Act on the Results

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable


### C.41.UA:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** a checklist is being used on an occasion, or an earlier answer is being considered for reuse. The list may be familiar, but the cards, description, room or software state can have changed.

This Method obtains answers with sufficient grounds for their intended use and informs a separate next action. Start by identifying the subject and the question being answered. The useful result can be Yes, No, an unresolved answer, an applicable existing result, or a missing contribution. A positive completion record is only one possible outcome.

Use C.41 or C.41.QC when the questions or checking means lack a sufficient basis. Use the work's ordinary decision or authorization arrangement when a consequential next action calls for it.

### C.41.UA:2 - Problem

People can confuse doing the work, checking its result, recording an answer and authorizing a continuation. An agent can report that it implemented error handling while the catalogue was never inspected. An organizer can reuse yesterday's green summary against today's changed description.

The problem is to answer the present question on sufficient grounds, preserve what remains useful and avoid inventing certainty to finish the form.

### C.41.UA:3 - Forces

| Tension | Practical implication |
| --- | --- |
| Sufficient confidence and repeated checking cost | Reuse competent results when their grounds still apply. |
| Historical truth and present relevance | Preserve old answers while deciding what supports the current question. |
| Prompt action and uncertainty | Name the unresolved issue and choose a proportionate way forward. |
| Local answer and wider decision | Keep correspondence with a description separate from usefulness and action. |
| Recording and observation | Capture enough for the receiving use without substituting paperwork for the check. |

### C.41.UA:4 - Solution

A.15.10 supplies changed-premise recovery. The operation here uses that boundary to answer the selected checklist questions from adequate present or retained grounds, then distinguishes the answer from the decision and any corrective work. Reuse a supplied current answer; reopen only the reliance whose relevant premise changed.

#### C.41.UA:4.1 - Bind the question to this subject and occasion

Identify what is being checked, which description or criterion applies, and the relevant time or condition. If every admissible reading warrants the same action on sufficient grounds, give that answer. Resolve any difference that still affects the work, including an order to identify the exact subject. A file name can refer to a different edition; “the cards” can refer to another box.

Recover prior answers or competent results that might already settle the question. Administrative claim-checking Methods supply useful distinctions; apply the competent examination for the present domain. Record-maintenance Methods supply record-use distinctions when the answer is retained for later reliance. Decide whether the subject, criterion, observation and conditions supporting those results still apply. When those grounds still apply, the earlier result can answer the subject question now. If the terms also require a fresh check, a particular participant's own observation or an independent assessment, establish that work under the stated conditions.

After an interruption, recover what the checks were to support and whether that work is still due. Resume the first unfinished operation that can change the remaining work, with the content already established. A marker at card seven helps only if the next person knows which cards were compared, with which description, and what remains unresolved. Reconsider any changed subject or basis before resuming. Retain only the context needed for that continuation; a position marker is neither a completed answer nor proof that earlier grounds still apply.

Use a position cue alone when the performer can already recover the question, basis and unfinished work unambiguously. Otherwise retain the missing content alongside it. Compare the effort of recovering adequate prior grounds with obtaining a fresh observation. Observe again when the earlier basis no longer applies, or when recovering it would cost more than obtaining adequate current grounds. A new observation can also be the commissioned work even when the earlier answer is sufficient. When only the answer is due and that answer is established, return it.

A changed description can settle a new comparison immediately. It does not automatically make all prior facts unknown. A changed subject can instead remove the basis for a current answer while leaving the historical answer intact.

Keep evidence of effort and evidence of the intended result separate. A packer's report of two hours spent preparing cards does not settle their count or wording; inspect or reuse grounds for those conditions. The result described in a report is also distinct from the report's own completion.

#### C.41.UA:4.2 - Obtain and express the answer

Perform or obtain the check needed. Use a direct observation, executable comparison or competent judgement according to the question. Where the mode is read-do, carry out the prompted work and then establish the result; where it is do-confirm, check the selected conditions of work already performed.

If conditions change during use, reconsider what can usefully be checked now while sustaining essential work. A limited time window may call for a competent choice of the most consequential feasible contributions, help from another participant or a pause. Keep omitted or interrupted checks distinguishable from positive answers and communicate their consequences for continued reliance. Completing the visible list is not sufficient reason to sacrifice the result it was meant to support.

Express what the grounds support. A supported negative answer is **No**. **Unknown** means the available grounds do not settle the question. **Not checked** describes the absence of a check for this occasion, not the subject's condition. If a competent earlier result settles it, record the answer as reused, not as newly observed. Give a reason for **not applicable** that concerns scope.

Keep the answer associated with the subject, criterion and grounds needed by its recipient. A tool failure can be evidence about the checking attempt while leaving the subject question unresolved. Another agent's agreement may contribute judgement; it does not supply an unperformed observation.

When the grounds are in a document or model, examine the relevant content using an appropriate way of interpreting it. A polished report can omit the decision being asked about; unfinished meeting notes can already identify that decision and its grounds. The document's existence, formatting or completion does not settle the condition of what it describes. Obtain enough detail for the recipient's reliance, and retain a usable route to it when the answer will be reused.

#### C.41.UA:4.3 - Decide what the answer changes

Ask what follows for the intended work. A mismatch can support changing the subject, revising a mistaken description, changing the intended use, obtaining more information, or choosing a bounded continuation. Recover the decision grounds and any separately applicable authority. Under a standing rule, recording a particular answer can put an agreed consequence into effect. Establish that the rule applies and its conditions are met. Where the decision remains with a participant, establish their act before reporting the decision. To report that a subsequent operation ran, use evidence of that operation.

If a change is selected, recover how it could produce the intended result: the suitable Method or alternative Methods, a capable performer, needed means and a useful occasion. Return missing Method knowledge to the domain practice; writing another question does not supply it. For a noisy workshop, closing a door, changing rooms and moving the discussion online are different possible interventions, with different conditions of use. An expected time for quiet is a planning judgement, not an observation that quiet has been achieved.

Distinguish the selected work from checking and recording it. A decision to replace a card does not establish that the card was replaced. After the change, check the affected question on adequate grounds. An operating-case continuation Method separates continuation, performance, records and resulting state when an operating case needs another action; an applicable operating-control Method addresses the quality and reliability question in continuing operation.

Keep historical answers and unaffected grounds. Reopen other questions only where the change can defeat their basis. Stop when the selected questions are sufficiently answered for the intended use, or report which observation, interpretation, capability or decision is missing. Use C.41.SR when a decision depends on how several answers fit together or a changed premise can affect a conclusion drawn from them. One person can need that reconciliation. Several recipients can also use a single sufficient answer directly.

### C.41.UA:5 - Archetypal Grounding

Set S has twenty readable cards matching wording v2 and is assembled for delivery. Count/readability was observed at 10:00, wording at 10:05 and assembly at 10:10. The answers were entered at 10:12.

At 10:15, the facilitator changes the comparison description to v3, replacing one phrase.

| Current information | Answer to “Does S match v3?” | Useful continuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S is known to be unchanged since its comparison with v2; the changed phrase differs. | **No.** The retained content observation and the v3 description establish a mismatch. | Decide whether to change the cards, description or intended exercise. A new inspection is unnecessary to establish this negative answer. |
| S may have been replaced; current wording is not known. | **Unknown.** The old observation no longer settles current content. | Inspect the affected content or obtain another adequate current result. |
| The affected card has been replaced and compared with v3; sufficient grounds remain for the other cards. | **Yes**, for the wording question on those combined grounds. | Examine whether handling also affected count, readability or assembly. |

In this constructed case, the facilitator decides to keep v3 and replace the affected card. Replacement is the selected work. A subsequent comparison establishes the new answer. If opening the set and changing a card could leave it incomplete, recounting is useful. If the remaining cards were protected and their wording unchanged, repeating every wording comparison adds no corresponding gain.

The v2 answer remains a historical answer to its question. Recording the v3 answer later does not alter when the earlier observation occurred, and neither observation alone establishes when a condition first became true.

A spill can make already checked cards unreadable while their wording and the completed printing work remain unchanged. Observed damage supports a current negative answer to readability; it does not erase the earlier work. In another branch, the workshop is cancelled while every physical-set answer remains true. The decision about further preparation then uses the changed occasion and value of the work, rather than trying to turn the physical answers negative.

For an importer, the agent's message “error handling implemented” reports work. To decide what happened in a particular failed import, use an adequate comparison of the catalogue before and after that run. If the only observation tool failed and the later state is unknown, the question stays open. A confirmed change to the state that should have been preserved settles it in the negative. One such failure within the promised range refutes the claim that the importer preserves the catalogue on every failed import. If preservation is required, this run violated that requirement. Apply the terms in force to the breach and to any later import. They may already suspend further imports or reserve a decision for the catalogue owner. An unchanged catalogue settles the question for that run. Extending that success to other inputs calls for grounds that cover them. A further run is due when separately commissioned or needed for an outstanding question.

### C.41.UA:6 - Bias-Annotation

Completion pressure can turn uncertain or negative answers into apparent passes. An expert may also rely on tacit judgement that others cannot recover. Record the grounds proportionately to how the result will be used, and preserve an unresolved answer.

Familiarity can encourage both stale reuse and unnecessary repetition. Examine relevance of the premises rather than preferring either “always recheck” or “already green.”

### C.41.UA:7 - Conformance Checklist

For the answers on this occasion, ask:

- Is each answer bound to the subject, criterion and relevant conditions?
- Do its grounds settle that question, including when an earlier result is reused?
- Are No, unknown, not checked and inapplicability interpreted without changing the subject claim?
- Which observations and decisions took place, what did the entry put into effect under the governing terms, and which actions were actually performed?
- Did a changed premise reopen exactly the affected reliance while preserving historical and unaffected information?

### C.41.UA:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Failure | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| A revised description turns a known mismatch into “unknown.” | Use the retained subject facts and new description to answer the comparison. |
| An old pass is copied to a subject that may have changed. | Recover current grounds or state the unresolved question. |
| A completion entry is taken as evidence that work happened. | Obtain the observation or competent result on which the answer depends. |
| A negative answer automatically orders corrective work. | Separate the comparison from the decision about subject, description and use. |

### C.41.UA:9 - Consequences

The Method produces usable information even when the answer is negative or incomplete. It supports economical reuse and targeted recovery after change. Its cost is attention to the premises of reliance. It cannot supply a missing domain judgement or make an unauthorized action acceptable merely by recording a pass.

### C.41.UA:10 - Architectural Rationale

Question selection and checker qualification happen at different scopes from an answer. Repeating them in every use wastes effort; omitting them when their premises fail produces unsupported certainty. This Method uses adequate supplied criteria and checking means; when either proves inadequate, return the affected question to C.41 or C.41.QC.

Keeping decision and corrective work separate preserves the user's choice to revise a mistaken description.

### C.41.UA:11 - SoTA-Echoing

How much recovery or fresh checking is needed after an interruption or changed premise? **Adopt** sufficient existing grounds for the question, and **adapt** the resumption cue to the missing context. The alternatives are a position marker, a marker with question-specific content, and obtaining a fresh observation. Section 4.1 chooses among them by whether the question and prior grounds are recoverable and still apply, including the cost of that recovery.

A bare “card seven” is enough only when the performer already knows what was compared and against which description. When that context is missing, retaining it avoids either guessing or repeating all comparisons. The trade-off is a little more preparation and reading for a more dependable continuation. If recovering a trustworthy record costs more than an adequate inspection, inspect again. In §5, however, known unchanged S and the changed v3 phrase support No: another observation adds no needed information. If S may have changed, current inspection supplies a missing basis. These branches **reject** both automatic repetition and automatic reuse.

[Chen and colleagues' laboratory experiments](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ergon.2023.103453) supply a contextual-cue candidate and comparisons with other task support, with effects that depend on the experimental condition and measure. They motivate testing which content matters; they do not rank every workplace recovery arrangement or establish a universal interface. The supplied *Methodology* and *Systems Management* manuscripts distinguish useful work from reporting. [Anthropic's 2025 account](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents) supplies a concrete testing-before-pass arrangement; its [2026 account](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps) motivates revisiting the means as capabilities change. Those engineering cases do not establish that every resumed occasion needs a new test.

The selected line spends recovery effort only on information needed for a sound current answer, while preserving historical and unaffected answers in §§4.2–4.3. Reopen the choice when a cue fails to recover the next operation, a subject or basis changes, or a cheaper adequate observation becomes available. If cue alternatives remain indistinguishable on that use question, keep the simpler one.

### C.41.UA:12 - Relations

C.41 supplies selected questions, C.41.TF their usable expression and C.41.OF an occasion with access to answers. C.41.QC supplies a qualification when criterion or checker uncertainty is material. C.41.SR connects answers across shared work.

Administrative practice supplies claim checking and fit-for-use records. Operating practice supplies case continuation, exception handling, and quality and reliability control. [FPF E.10.D2](../11-part-e---the-fpf-constitution-and-authoring-guides/35-e-10-d2---entityofconcern-description-episteme-and-specifica.md) preserves subject and description, and [A.15.10](../07-cluster-a-v---constitutional-principles-of-the-kernel/25-a-15-10---resume-work.md) governs changed-premise use across work.
