## A.3.1.WT - Design and Interpret a Method Work Trial

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### A.3.1.WT:0 - Use This When

Use this pattern to design a trial, to carry it out and interpret its observations, or to answer a question using suitable observations from existing Work. Which of those results did the recipient commission? What do they need to distinguish, about which Method or candidate, in what conditions? Those answers determine what the trial design must make observable and whether a new performance is due.

For a design, deliver an executable arrangement of tasks, choices, conditions, observations and interpretation. Explain which possible findings would change the recipient’s decision and which would leave it open. An order to conduct a trial also requires the specified actual Work and its observations. For an evidence question, a suitable record of earlier Work can supply the answer if it covers the relevant conditions.

Here *trial* is Plain practice wording for intentionally selected Work and observation. It is not a new subtype of `U.Work`, a Method admission, an evaluation result, or a claim that the situation statistically represents a population.

The requested test, reading or trial account can be the complete delivery. If its observations are used to judge description coherence, fit, transfer, practical worth or a causal effect, the further conclusion needs the grounds for that question. A.3.1.CC governs coherence, A.3.1.FT governs fit or transfer, and C.11.MV governs practical worth.

#### A.3.1.WT:0.1 - Working Distinctions

| Item | Working meaning here | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| trial question | What the recipient needs to learn or receive from the trial under the specified conditions. | A request for the reading under load can be complete in its own right. |
| representative situation | A situation selected because stated characteristics match the named current-use question. | The word does not imply random sampling, population coverage, or transfer. |
| discriminating situation | A situation selected because a condition or combination of conditions can expose a claimed limit, burden, or failure. | State which claim the situation can test and identify what would distinguish the possible answers. |
| WorkPlan | Intended performers, tasks, Methods, situations, observations, and stops. | A plan, script, simulation description, or scheduled demonstration is not performed Work. |
| admitted Work | One dated Work occurrence admitted under `A.15.1` from its performers, action history, enacted Method, temporal extent, and required containing-System relation. | A report, trace, attendance record, or output can support the claim but does not create the occurrence. |
| candidate-account trial branch | Actual Work is admitted separately while claims in a candidate Method account are compared with observations. | The candidate whole is not said to have been enacted. Name each independently admitted Method followed and its grounded `enactsMethod` relation. |
| capability input | A claim about a holder System, Work family or result class, operating envelope, measures, qualification or currentness window, and evidence used for reliance. | Training, availability, assignment, permission, authority, or one success does not substitute for it. |
| trial evidence | Observations, reports and records about the occurrence, including results, burdens, adaptations and gaps qualified for named later uses. | What each source establishes depends on the claim for which it is used. |

### A.3.1.WT:1 - Problem Frame

Method trials are often easiest to describe after the fact: a team used some material, produced a result, and reported value. That compression hides what Method was admitted, which Work occurred, who performed it, which support conditions were present, what changed, and which conclusions the observations can support.

Selecting only convenient success cases can miss conditions that change the outcome. A Method may look usable in a familiar task and fail when feedback is closed-loop, information is incomplete, capability is stale, or a required relation is absent. Representative and discriminating situations therefore serve different purposes and must be selected for an explicit later question.

### A.3.1.WT:2 - Problem

A planned trial can be reported as performed Work. A candidate whole can be reported as enacted because some constituent activities occurred. Training completion can be reported as capability, tool logs as Work, immediate self-reports as lasting results, and one favourable occurrence as evidence of transfer or effectiveness.

These moves produce a positive story but weak evidence. Later reviewers cannot recover the actual occurrence, compare situations, separate Method claims from support conditions, or find the missing evidence that should stop a broader judgment.

### A.3.1.WT:3 - Forces

| Force | Tension |
| --- | --- |
| Real Work | Natural Work exposes practical burdens, while uncontrolled conditions complicate attribution. |
| Deliberate discrimination | Changed conditions can reveal a limit, while an artificial stress case may not answer the current-use question. |
| Status preservation | Candidate claims need testing, while useful Work must not be reported as enactment of an unidentified Method whole. |
| Observability | Later decisions need traceable observations, while instrumentation and reporting can burden or distort Work. |
| Capability | Performer ability can change the result, while training or one success invites premature capability claims. |
| Failure value | Failure can localize a correction, while a preference for a favourable narrative can hide it. |
| Evidence reach | Several occurrences can establish who used a technique and what resulted on those occasions. Transfer, practical worth or attribution of a change to the technique may require further grounds. |

### A.3.1.WT:4 - Solution

A.15.1 identifies performed Work and A.10 governs a claimed evidence use. Construct the trial from the distinction the recipient needs to make. Ask which plausible alternatives for this Method or its use would lead them to different decisions. Choose an attainable condition or observation whose outcomes can tell those alternatives apart, what the performer needs to do, and what would warrant stopping. A design can be complete before any trial takes place. Reuse an existing observation when its conditions and reach answer the evidence question. A separately commissioned new trial remains a performance to deliver.

For a design, return the arrangement and its limits after checking feasibility. For a performed trial, return what the commissioned test or observation establishes. Its result may also supply evidence when a later decision about coherence, fit, transfer or worth depends on it.

#### A.3.1.WT:4.1 - Pattern-Use Unfolding

1. **Name what the trial must establish.** A commission may ask for a test, a reading under specified conditions or an account of what happened. Identify the observation and how the recipient will use it. When a later decision concerns coherence (A.3.1.CC), fit or transfer (A.3.1.FT), or practical worth (C.11.MV), derive the trial question from that decision. Distinguish each requested conclusion.
2. **Preserve the subject branch.** Identify the admitted Method under `A.3.1`, or keep the proposed whole as a candidate account. In the candidate branch, state which candidate claims are being compared and which Methods, if any, are separately admitted and may be enacted.
3. **Select a representative situation when the trial question needs one.** Name the intended-use characteristics that matter to the question: task purpose, development phase, technical or domain conditions, performer population, holder capabilities, participating Systems, direct relations, constraints, and qualification window. State why this situation covers the current question without claiming population representativeness.
4. **Select a discriminating situation when the decision needs one.** Identify an admissible change, or a combination of changes, that could defeat the claim. A method might work with unfamiliar material and also with reduced support, yet fail when both occur together. Such a case can test the guarantee under those conditions. To attribute the difference to the material, the support or their interaction, construct a comparison or use other grounds that distinguish those explanations. Use an existing sufficient observation where it answers the question.
5. **Keep intention and performance distinct.** When designing a new trial, provide the WorkPlan with intended tasks, performers, admitted Methods, support conditions, observation points, protected conditions, burdens to record, stop rules, and the later use of each observation. When the trial assesses a choice the performer must make, compare the instructions, materials they will see and normal assistance with that choice. Leave the judgement to the performer and keep grounds for distinguishing their contribution from a supplied answer. A commissioned design is complete when the assigned participants can carry out the work and the resulting observations can reach whoever will interpret them, in a usable form and with the necessary permissions. Show which conclusions that arrangement can support for the named question. For existing Work, recover any available plan and leave unsupported claims about prior intentions unresolved. Distinguish planned or described activity from what was performed. A performed simulation or demonstration may itself be admitted as Work under §4.1(7); distinguish what was actually performed from what was only represented or planned.
6. **Check capability and support inputs separately.** When reliance on capability changes interpretation, identify the holder System, Work family or result class, operating envelope, measures, qualification or currentness condition, and evidence. Recover only A.15.8.MS material, access, tool, provider, feedback, and decision conditions supported for the relevant task rows; preserve retained candidates, split boundaries, untested conditions, and gaps rather than treating them as configured inputs.
7. **Admit only Work that occurred.** Apply `A.15.1` to the dated occurrence. Establish its action history, an admitted Method followed, temporal extent and required containing-System relation. Establish the A.13 basis for each System whose performance the account claims. A trial performed by a reviewing service can be established while its internal division of work remains open. A claim about one participant's judgement or the joint performance of named contributors needs grounds for those contributions. For precise assignment-bound attribution, use F.6 for the contribution and the obtaining A.13 assignment that covered its performer. Add System use, operation binding, affected referent, resource use, or result relations only when the claim needs them and their own predicates obtain.
8. **Record conditions and departures.** For each occurrence, record the conditions and findings the trial question needs. These can include relied-on Systems and relations, capabilities, departures from a WorkPlan, adaptations, burdens, stops or domain results. Identify required observations that are missing and which decisions they leave open. Keep the domain result, report, log, and evidence-use relation separate from Work.
9. **Qualify the evidence for the next decision.** In a design, explain which possible observations would distinguish the answers that matter and what each would warrant. For performed Work, establish what the available observations and records show. An observer who saw an expert select the test and a trainee run it can report both operations. To entrust selection to the trainee alone, establish what the trainee can select with the support available next time. Keep a participant's report and the investigator's interpretation attributable to their sources. If the next decision relies on a causal explanation, use C.28 to identify what the comparison or other causal grounds support. State any missing grounds that affect that decision.
10. **Return what the recipient commissioned.** Deliver the executable design with its prospective interpretation, or the requested performance, reading or trial account with its conditions and evidential limits. Where a further question was commissioned, take description or relation evidence to A.3.1.CC, situation evidence to A.3.1.FT, and results, burdens, alternatives or side effects to C.11.MV as appropriate. Identify the premise missing for the particular result, for example `missing-performed-work`, `missing-method-admission`, `missing-capability-basis` or `missing-direct-relation`.

#### A.3.1.WT:4.2 - Record the Result

| Result position | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| trial use | The commissioned trial question, the result its recipient needs, applicable qualification window and stop. |
| subject status | Admitted Method, or candidate account plus each separately admitted Method whose enactment is claimed. |
| situations | Representative or discriminating situations selected for the question, selection criteria, any relevant matched or changed conditions, and the question or claim each addresses. |
| plan baseline | Available plan for existing Work, or intended Work for a new trial, with performers, Methods, support conditions, observations, burdens, protected conditions, stop rules and any missing plan basis relevant to the question. |
| performed Work | For a claim about a trial that occurred, identify each admitted Work occurrence, its performers at the scale claimed, enacted admitted Methods, temporal extent, containing System and relevant departures from any available plan. For a design, state what is to be performed and under which conditions. |
| relied-on conditions | Holder capabilities, Systems, Agent-performed Work, direct relations, access, assignments, permissions, authority, tools, providers, and support conditions that obtain or remain missing. |
| observations | Domain results, burdens, adaptations, failures, participant reports, direct observations, and their source or evidence-use relations. |
| reach and gaps | Claim the trial supports, source and conditions that support it, missing grounds for the intended receiving use, and conditions that would reopen the slice. |
| return | The design, test result, reading or account commissioned, with the conditions needed to interpret it, or the missing premise. Where a coherence, fit, transfer or worth decision also depends on that result, identify the contribution it makes. |

Keep these distinctions in the account and its referenced records. A table is useful when several occurrences need comparison; a single account need not use one row per position.

#### A.3.1.WT:4.3 - What Changes in Practice

The trial designer can deliver a feasible way to distinguish the alternatives before a trial date. The person reporting a performance can point to the actual Work, the admitted Methods it enacted, the conditions under which it took place, and the later question each observation can answer.

A failed or incomplete occurrence remains useful. It can expose a description defect, a representation difficulty, a missing support relation, a capability condition, or a situation boundary without being inflated into a universal verdict on the Method.

#### A.3.1.WT:4.4 - Constructed Trial Case

This constructed case illustrates how later checks can reuse the same Work while reaching their own conclusions.

`M-Unit-Review-1` is admitted for reviewing temperature-sensor calibration reports before release. Its current description tells the reviewer to identify every source unit, normalize values, compare them with the declared tolerance, and stop on an unresolved mismatch. `WP-ME11-1` plans two review tasks for the same week. `S-Rep-1` is a current same-unit report of the ordinary kind. `S-Disc-1` keeps the product, decision, reviewer, description, and support fixed but introduces a Celsius/Kelvin mismatch; it is selected because the live claim is that the Method lets this reviewer expose a unit mismatch before release.

The interpretation relies on `CAP-R17-1`: holder `Reviewer-17`; calibration-report-review Work family; temperature-sensor-report envelope; measure “four of four seeded unit cases detected with description edition 4”; current through day D30; qualification record `QR-R17-1`. The qualification, assignment, permission to inspect the report, and release authority remain different facts. The trial also records description edition 4 and the manually retrieved conversion table as A.15.8.MS support inputs.

Two occurrences then happen and are admitted separately. `W-Rep-1` is performed by `Reviewer-17` inside `Calibration-Team-A` from 10:03 to 10:10, enacts `M-Unit-Review-1`, accepts the same-unit report, and records seven minutes of review burden. `W-Disc-1` is performed by the same System in the same containing System from 11:14 to 11:26, enacts the same admitted Method, normalizes the Celsius/Kelvin values before comparison, detects an unresolved mismatch, stops the release decision, and returns the report for correction; it records twelve minutes and one conversion-table lookup. The WorkPlan, two Work occurrences, domain decisions, timing record, and observation account remain separate.

| Later use | Evidence returned | Stop retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A.3.1.CC coherence | The two occurrences identify the description edition and the operations followed; neither exposed an internal contradiction in this slice. | A.3.1.WT does not conclude that the whole MethodDescription or Method construction is coherent. |
| A.3.1.FT fit or transfer | The matched pair records one changed situation condition and the resulting task observations for one current holder. | One discriminating occurrence establishes no transfer to another holder, domain, tool, or qualification window. |
| C.11.MV practical worth | The domain decisions, seven- and twelve-minute burdens, lookup, and stop are available for comparison with named alternatives. | A task pass establishes neither net worth nor causal contribution. |

If only the design was ordered, a report pair with the stated controls, available reviewer, observation points and interpretation can complete it before `W-Disc-1` takes place. If that trial was ordered and the reviewer has yet to perform it, return `missing-performed-work` for the outstanding performance. If only the evidence is requested, an adequate earlier report of comparable Work may already settle the relevant question. State which observation it supplies and which conclusion it supports.

### A.3.1.WT:5 - Archetypal Grounding - SSFD Workplace Projects

The automotive SSFD programme supplies reports and review evidence about actual workplace projects without supplying a universal effectiveness claim. More than 300 engineers participated in a three-year transfer programme; training was followed by supported four-to-six-month workplace projects. Seventy-two of the first 100 reviewed project reports recorded SSFD use, and 41 contained enough detail for deeper analysis. Across those 41, the study reports 95 examples of evidenced individual benefits while distinguishing direct and indirect contribution.

The published paper supplies report evidence. Apply §4.1(7) to establish an occurrence at the scale claimed. The project report, direct observation or other evidence can provide those grounds. Give the findings each source supports and identify any grounds still missing for admission or for crediting particular contributors. The slices below distinguish possible answers to the later questions without admitting Work by citation.

#### A.3.1.WT:5.1 - Preserve the Status Branch

Before using the replay, establish whether SSFD is an admitted Method for the current FPF use. If it is, each supported project occurrence may be tested for its own obtaining `enactsMethod` relation. If it is not, admit the workplace Work independently, retain the SSFD account as a candidate, and name the independently admitted Methods whose enactment relations obtain. The report statement “used SSFD” does not perform either admission automatically.

#### A.3.1.WT:5.2 - Representative and Discriminating Slices

| Slice | Reported project facts used | Evidence returned | Stop retained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Project 11 | The report concerns functional analysis of a new by-wire steering system and records 48 failure modes for four system functions, 44 new requirements and design rules, 44 test cases for individual systems, and 33 requirements and test cases for the overall system. | One bounded application account for checking whether description, representation, and support claims were usable in that project situation, plus the reported results and burdens available from the source. | Do not infer that every programme participant had the same capability or that the reported benefits came from SSFD alone. |
| Project 32 | The report exposes difficulty representing the relation between a closed-loop sensor and controller. | A discriminating representation difficulty for A.3.1.CC and an unlike-situation comparison for A.3.1.FT. A.3.1.CC uses the direct representation governor for the affected action and C.37 when a selection question remains; when a Method-specific cross-use profile is needed, it checks the affected action and cross-use dependency under C.37.MP:4.2. The report establishes neither selection result by itself. | Do not turn the difficulty into general Method failure or claim that Project 11 supplies the counterfactual. |
| 41-report evidence set | The investigators identified 95 instances of benefits in 41 project reports and examined how SSFD figured in those accounts, including uses of its outputs in subsequent work. | The documented outputs and the investigators' interpretation of the pathways can inform later comparison of domain results, burdens and evidence strength in C.11.MV. | An estimate of what would have happened without SSFD requires additional grounds. A claim about a participant's current capability requires evidence about that participant's work. |

The useful A.3.1.WT result is therefore not “SSFD works”. It is a set of reported workplace-project accounts, situation and support conditions, observed representation difficulties, reported results and burdens, evidence-source qualifications, and explicit gaps for three separate later judgments.

#### A.3.1.WT:5.3 - EC-417 Early Stop

In `EC-417`, the three-release statement remains a WorkPlan. The three named-user retrieval, tailoring, and selection-support occurrences from A.15.8.MS establish only those support tasks; they do not perform a release or enact the candidate whole `C-EC-Release-v2`.

For a release trial, apply §4.1(6–9) to the release's own occurrence, support, evidence and authority facts. The observed support tasks do not supply that release Work; until its basis is recovered, return `missing-performed-work`. Even after a release is admitted, keep `C-EC-Release-v2` as a candidate and send the occurrence evidence to the selected A.3.1.CC, A.3.1.FT or C.11.MV question.

### A.3.1.WT:6 - Bias-Annotation

| Bias | Likely drift | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| plan-completion bias | Planned or represented activity is reported as performed trial Work. | Admit each actual Work occurrence separately; otherwise return `missing-performed-work`. |
| candidate-laundering bias | Constituent activity is reported as enactment of the candidate whole. | Preserve the candidate account and name only separately admitted enacted Methods. |
| success-selection bias | Convenient positive occurrences stand in for the intended situation family. | State the situation-selection criteria and select a discriminating situation when the later question needs one. |
| instrument bias | Logs, reports, or telemetry are treated as the Work or result. | Keep the occurrence, result, record, and evidence-use relation separate. |
| capability inflation | Training or one success becomes a holder capability claim. | Require the A.2.2 holder, Work family, envelope, measures, window, and evidence. |
| causal inflation | Reported benefit or association becomes Method-caused effect. | State the observation level and missing comparison or causal basis. |
| trial-judgment collapse | One trial record decides coherence, fit, transfer, and worth at once. | Route evidence to A.3.1.CC, A.3.1.FT, and C.11.MV according to the selected receiving questions. |

### A.3.1.WT:7 - Conformance Checklist

- [ ] The commission identifies the design, new performance or assessment of existing observations that is owed, and the question the recipient needs answered.
- [ ] The Method is admitted, or the proposed whole remains a candidate account throughout.
- [ ] Every claimed enacted Method is independently admitted; no candidate whole is said to be enacted.
- [ ] Situation-selection criteria are named, and every discriminating situation states the claim it can distinguish. If the performer must supply a selection, the instructions, material and ordinary assistance leave that selection open.
- [ ] For a design commission, the assigned participants can carry out their tasks and obtain the planned observations under the proposed conditions. For an order to conduct the trial, establish the specified performance and observations. For a simulation or demonstration, identify what was actually performed.
- [ ] Each claimed Work occurrence independently satisfies the admission basis in §4.1(7).
- [ ] Capability, System use, assignment, permission, authority, support, operation binding, and result relations are asserted only from their own bases.
- [ ] For an occurrence being assessed, the record identifies the conditions, deviations, adaptations, burdens, observations and domain results relevant to the trial question, with any material gaps.
- [ ] For a design, whoever will interpret the trial can use the observations they are entitled to receive to distinguish the answers the design can support. For an assessment of performed Work, the reader can establish what happened to the extent the sources allow. Each use that depends on a causal explanation has grounds for that inference.
- [ ] The result separates any selected A.3.1.CC, A.3.1.FT and C.11.MV uses and supplies evidence or gaps without claiming their judgments.

### A.3.1.WT:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Anti-pattern | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| “The rehearsal ran, so the Method was trialled.” | Recover actual Work and its enacted admitted Method; otherwise keep the rehearsal at its supported status. |
| “The team enacted the candidate Method.” | Admit the Work, preserve the candidate account, and name only independently admitted Methods whose enactment relations obtain. |
| “Everyone was trained, so capability was controlled.” | Record training separately and recover the holder capability input used in interpretation. |
| “The log proves the task occurred and succeeded.” | Use the log as evidence for independently admitted Work and a separately established task result. |
| “Most reports were favourable, so the Method transfers.” | Send the occurrence evidence and situation differences to A.3.1.FT; do not make the transfer judgment here. |
| “Reported benefit proves the Method caused the outcome.” | Preserve what participants reported and how the study classified those reports. Establish the additional basis needed before using them to attribute the outcome to the Method. |

### A.3.1.WT:9 - Consequences

Trial Work produces inspectable evidence. Failures and missing premises survive as useful results, and later assurance decisions can compare the same occurrences without changing their identity or truth status.

The cost is more explicit occurrence recovery and narrower conclusions. Projects must record the performer, enacted Method and situation, together with the capability, support, direct relations, burdens and source limits that affect the receiving question, and they may finish a costly trial with a gap instead of a favourable verdict.

### A.3.1.WT:10 - Rationale

Actual Work gives these inquiries a common empirical base. A record can establish which operations took place and whose work they were. Its bearing on coherence, fit, transfer, worth, capability or the cause of a result depends on what each inquiry asks. The same occurrence can inform several decisions within those limits.

Representative situations answer typical-use questions; discriminating situations test a stated difference or boundary. Select either or both according to the receiving question. Their selection does not create statistical representativeness or a universal situation taxonomy.

### A.3.1.WT:12 - Relations

- B.1.5.CM supplies an admitted Method or a status-preserved candidate account and may supply a realization or trial plan; it does not supply performed Work.
- A.3.2.DE supplies MethodDescription content or candidate-account content for the trial question. A.15.8.MS supplies supported material and task conditions or a retained configuration set, split boundary, or gap; A.3.1.WT consumes only the obtaining support inputs and preserves every unresolved status. Neither pattern establishes trial Work or its result.
- RMP.10 may supply a reconstructed candidate account and a trial question while preserving the candidate branch.
- `A.3.1` governs Method admission; `A.15.2` keeps the WorkPlan separate; `A.15.1` governs each Work occurrence; `A.2.2` governs capability inputs; `A.10` governs relied-on evidence paths.
- A.3.1.CC consumes evidence for construction and description coherence, A.3.1.FT consumes situation evidence and any comparisons needed for the selected fit or transfer question, and C.11.MV consumes results, burdens, alternatives, and evidence strength for practical worth. None of those conclusions is returned by A.3.1.WT.
