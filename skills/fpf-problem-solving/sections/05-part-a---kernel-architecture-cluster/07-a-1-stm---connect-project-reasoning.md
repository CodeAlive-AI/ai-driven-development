## A.1.STM - Connect Project Reasoning (System-Thinking Long Mantra)

> **Type:** Part A practitioner application pattern
> **Status:** Stable
> **Normativity:** Normative unless marked informative

**Plain name.** Use the system-thinking long mantra.

*Long mantra* and *attention map* are Plain names for a repeatable reminder and its readable display of connected questions. The developed account connects knowledge about use, functioning, construction, creation and cooperation. Its short wording helps keep that account in attention; it does not supply the knowledge that the account needs.

### A.1.STM:0 - Practitioner entry

**Use this when.** Use this pattern when participants have knowledge about parts of a project but need to connect it into one systemic account: what should change in use, which system can make that difference, how it could function and be constructed, how it can be obtained, and how its creators can work together. Their answers must fit together, not merely be plausible separately. Establishing the common project target is one necessary part of that reasoning. Use the pattern also when participants say "our system" but mean a component, a provider organization, a tool or the common target without noticing the difference. A narrower use is to locate the first unsupported dependency when the connected account is already sufficient.

**First useful move.** Take the project question now at issue and recover its connection to the intended use: what should become different or remain possible for whom, what system is expected to make that difference, and where its boundary lies. Ask the participants whose decisions govern that result to compare their answers. Use section 4.1 when the common basis is unresolved. When it is already shared, use section 4.3.1 to connect the current answer with the other choices it constrains and the choices on which it relies. Section 4.4 supplies the narrower first-gap inquiry when that is the missing result; there is no requirement to restart every investigation at the beginning of the reminder.

**Memorable reminder.**

> Agree what system must succeed for whom and in what use. Connect the needed outside effect, functioning, construction, ways of obtaining it, capable creators and their cooperation. Repeat for the creators that must themselves be obtained. Make the answers fit together while keeping the common target in view. Follow results into use; when a premise changes, revise the answers that depend on it and renew the affected agreement.

This is the Plain long mantra. Section 4 explains why the questions connect and how to use the reminder without treating it as a fixed work sequence.

**Not this pattern when.** A known local system-recognition, service/access, architecture, Method, Work, transformation, evidence or closure question can be answered through its subject pattern. Such a result need not reopen the whole project account. Use this pattern when the common basis or the connection to it is itself at issue.

**What this buys.** Participants can form and criticize a connected project account, justify why its choices belong together, find a consequential incompatibility or missing answer, and revise the affected part without losing the whole. They distinguish the shared target from their local subjects and preserve valid local results. The account can remain ordinary prose and existing project descriptions; no new universal project record is required.

### A.1.STM:1 - Problem

Division of labour lets different participants work on components, methods, descriptions, tools and provider organizations. Their local choices become coherent only if they can explain what common outcome those choices are intended to obtain. A component may meet its specification while the whole remains unusable; a provider may become efficient at producing something its intended users do not need. Local completion alone cannot settle the shared project purpose.

Systemic project reasoning therefore needs a common **project target system**: the system whose intended functioning, change or cessation in its environment organizes the participants' joint undertaking. This is a role of a system in that undertaking, not an intrinsic system type or a new U-kind. Its role must be identified even while its future holder is only described. "Which system interests me in this investigation?" is a broader question: the answer may be a test rig or builder whose improvement is only a contribution to the common target.

A project can also get every nearby statement locally right and lose the long dependency. A functioning argument can assume one construction while the creation plan can supply only another; capable contributors can require incompatible shared resources. Agreement on the target does not by itself make these answers jointly usable. One team improves a component, another produces a builder, and another measures operation, yet nobody can show how those results are expected to reach the target's use or which actual relations now connect them. A diagram arrow, the word *creates*, or a calendar sequence can hide that gap.

The opposite failure is to turn the reminder into a universal route. Then every result becomes a System, a plan becomes an existing holder, project Work becomes a network, and every arrow appears to be the same relation. A.1.STM develops the shared systemic reasoning while leaving each local truth with its subject pattern.

### A.1.STM:2 - Forces

| Force | Tension |
| --- | --- |
| Whole-project coherence | Participants need a common target and use hypothesis while their interests, local subjects and results differ. |
| Outside before inside | Architecture is justified by a revisable external-use hypothesis, yet discovery and feedback may reopen that hypothesis. |
| Backward justification | Reading from value toward missing support is useful, but it is not temporal order or transformation direction. |
| Forward actuality | Work and changes must be traced through facts that obtain, not through planned arrows or shared names. |
| Local contribution | A team needs to know how its result matters without inventing a universal contribution relation. |
| Recursive builders | Build-the-builder reasoning must recur without a creator kind, fixed levels, or a generic `creates` edge. |
| Evidence and return | A supported answer may later become stale or fail; reopening should be local rather than restarting the whole map. |

### A.1.STM:3 - The attention map

Keep these regions visible. They are questions and result locations, not stages or fields of a record.

| Region | Plain question | Subject pattern or honest stop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outside change and use | What should become different for a beneficiary or relying use? | Use the relevant problem, plan, decision, promise, or description pattern. Return a missing or contested rationale when the expected use is not supported. |
| Common project target | Which system is the joint undertaking meant to make successful in that use, and which boundary are participants agreeing? | Use section 4.1 for the shared target basis, A.15.6 for plan or decision designation, and A.1/A.1.SCR for an actual holder. Keep a future holder in description content until identity inception. Test any technical system-role classification and assignment under A.2/A.2.1. A local system-of-interest need not be the project target. |
| Runtime transformation and system participation | Which exact environment or input referent actually changes in use, and how does the already existing project target system participate? | Use A.3.4 for an actual bounded change of one continuing referent and the exact dynamics, interaction, causality, participation, or Work pattern for the system-side claim. Required behaviour, a use scenario, or an observed output proves no transformation. Causal or interaction participation supplies no work-facing assignment, Method, or Work. |
| Inside and architecture | Which internal organization could support the outside use? | Use C.32.P2S and the C.30 family. Keep architecture choice, selected structures, actual structures, descriptions, and views distinct. |

Otherwise keep a Plain provisional map and name the missing member, governor, predicate result, occurrence, or binding. |
| Local contribution | What is the team's exact subject and which supported relations connect its result to release or use? | Use the subject and relation patterns; use C.28 only for an actual causal-use claim. No generic `contributesTo` edge is implied. |
| Evidence, assurance, and return | What supports each load-bearing answer, what reliance is claimed, and what changed fact reopens it? | Use A.10 for claim-bound evidence and B.3 only for a named assurance use. Reopen the smallest answer whose basis changed. |

Use the connected reasoning in section 4 to establish the common basis. With that basis available, read **backward** across these regions to justify a needed result and locate the first unsupported answer. This is logical attention, not didactic order, a WorkPlan, dated Work order, `U.Transfer`, or transformation direction.

Trace **forward** through independently grounded facts: performer systems and assignments, dated Work, changes of continuing referents, production participation, identity inception, completion, later use, and environment-side change. In the runtime region, identify the exact environment or input referent and its A.3.4 change separately from the direct causal, interaction, functioning, participation, or Work claim for the already existing project target system; add work-facing assignment, Method, or Work only when those claims separately obtain. These facts may occupy several TFS or network members. Shared identity or temporal adjacency connects none of them without a directly governed relation occurrence and its endpoint bindings.

### A.1.STM:4 - Solution

#### A.1.STM:4.1 - Establish the common target, its use and the participants' agreement

Start with the intended use because it tells the participants what their internal choices must accomplish. Ask what would be lost if the proposed system were absent, unusable or no longer maintained. The answer can concern a desired change, a condition that must continue, or a harmful effect that should cease. Identify the affected participants and the relevant conditions of use. "Produce the deliverable" is insufficient while nobody can say what obtaining that deliverable is supposed to enable.

Then propose the system whose functioning or other intended trajectory could meet that need. Describe it first at its boundary: what it encounters, what it does or permits, which conditions it must tolerate, and what its users or other affected participants regard as success. A functional name such as pump, learning aid or diagnostic instrument is a useful hypothesis; it does not settle the physical boundary or the A.1 system criterion.

Compare the participants' accounts. Ask each relevant team and external participant to explain the proposed target in the same use situation and to locate its own contribution. Differences become actionable: one team may describe the provider organization, another the delivered system, and another the report about it; two teams may intend incompatible configurations under the same name. Resolve the consequential differences through inquiry, conversation, option comparison and, when needed, experiments or prototypes. C.11 helps choose among formed alternatives; it supplies neither the creative formation of those alternatives nor proof that several participants understand and accept the same choice.

Seek a working agreement adequate for the decisions being made: the common target, intended use, boundary, success conditions, and the local contributions that rely on them. Participants may retain different interests. State a consequential unresolved disagreement and who can settle the affected decision; do not present a single chooser's designation or a shared document title as evidence that agreement already exists. There is no algorithm guaranteed to discover the correct target. The useful result is a supported, revisable hypothesis that the participants can use together.

Keep three meanings apart:

| Expression | What it selects | Practical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System-of-interest | A system selected for one investigation or decision. | Attention can move to a component, builder or test rig without changing the project's common target. |
| Project target system | The system in the role of the common target of the scoped undertaking. | Its intended use and success conditions justify how local contributions belong to the shared purpose. |
| The target's functional role | What the target is expected to do in its environment, such as circulating coolant. | This constrains functioning and construction; it is different from being the project's target. |

The target-system role is required for a **systemic account of the project**. A participant's current subject need not be a System, and a useful local result can be obtained before that account is complete. If no common target and use hypothesis can yet be supported, keep that basis unresolved rather than silently replacing it with the nearest convenient object.

"Our system" names the system a particular team is making or changing. Locate it in relation to the common target: perhaps it is a constituent, a system in the target's environment, or a builder or tool used in obtaining the target. Those are different relations. A supplier's test rig may be its own team's target in an explicitly bounded project and a builder-side contribution to the wider project. State which project scope is current and keep the wider connection visible. A change of attention alone makes neither project scope nor target designation change.

#### A.1.STM:4.2 - Keep role, holder and future description distinct

The project target-system role states which place a System has in the common undertaking. A holder is the actual System occupying that place; a description states what the participants intend or believe about it. These can be discussed together without treating them as the same object. An existing pump remains the project's target when it fails a performance test: success is what the participants seek, not a condition that makes a failed target disappear from the undertaking.

Use ordinary role wording under A.2:4.9 while it is enough. If classification matters, define the local system-role kind under A.2 and C.3 through its System candidate domain and the work-facing distinction the project needs. For a target-role claim, the operative distinction is the System's position as the common target of the scoped undertaking, grounded in the relevant intention and designation facts. A rig investigated only to support another target does not meet that distinction in the wider project. A generic system-of-interest criterion cannot supply it by name. If the direct criterion or its governing relations are not yet available, retain the Plain target account and leave that technical classification unresolved. If assignment identity matters, A.2.1 requires an obtaining occurrence with an actual System holder under a declared species. System recognition, project designation, classification and assignment remain different claims; none is created by writing the role's name.

A new system can be intended before any holder exists. Agree the target role, intended use, required characteristics and boundary hypothesis in a plan or description. Describe the intended holder, its proposed identity rule and how later instances would be recognized. Before identity inception, this is prospective content, not an actual System or assignment occurrence. A.15.PROD separates production participation, the first satisfaction of the entity's identity rule, and completion. After inception, relate the actual holder to the earlier description and evaluate the claims needed now.

Design reasoning also works with types, variants and repeated provision. A project may intend a series of systems satisfying a common specification rather than one already named individual. State the common intended use, the distinguishing requirements and what counts as one resulting instance; track actual holders as they become identifiable. The specification, the kind it describes and a collection of instances do not thereby become one System. Where materially different target families have different users or use conditions, preserve their separate target accounts before comparing shared builders. A vague umbrella such as "equipment" does not supply that comparison.

#### A.1.STM:4.3 - Derive the long mantra from the intended use

The order below teaches the reasons for connecting the questions. An answer supplies requirements, possibilities or limits for other answers; it need not determine them uniquely. Later construction or capability knowledge can reopen the proposed functioning or use. The project account is assembled by resolving these mutual constraints, not by completing each line once.

1. **From outside need to target functioning.** Describe the change or maintained condition required in the target's environment, then ask what the target must do under the intended conditions for that result to be possible. Work through a use scenario. A required effect becomes a functioning hypothesis to investigate; it does not establish an actual effect.
2. **From functioning to functional role and construction.** Name the function-bearing role only after its contribution is understood. Compare constructions and materials that could realize it. This is why starting with a favourite component or organization can mislead: its properties matter through the functioning they permit. C.32.P2S and the C.30 family develop the outside-use-to-architecture choice; A.1.SCR tests an actual proposed whole when systemhood matters.
3. **From construction to ways of obtaining it.** Determine what must be produced, assembled, changed, learned, verified or maintained for the proposed construction to support the intended use. Compare the necessary Methods and their subject matter. For example, choosing a welded assembly, a learned controller or a different service arrangement changes the ways and capabilities needed to obtain it. A.3.1 and the A.15 family distinguish the reusable way, intended work, performed work and its results.
4. **From Methods to creator roles and capable holders.** Ask which contributions the chosen Methods require and which systems can supply them under the actual conditions. Then choose or develop people, teams, equipment and tools accordingly. A familiar occupation label or an available team is not a substitute for that capability question. Use A.2 and A.2.2 when the role or capability claim matters. Tools can participate in production without being independent agential performers; A.13 and A.15.1 govern any actual performer and Work claims.
5. **From capable creators to their cooperation.** Determine how their contributions can be made usable together. What must each receive from another, what shared conditions must remain true, and which decisions or changes must be agreed? Separate capability from availability and authority: each contributor may know how to perform its part while their combined work cannot obtain the result. Develop candidate arrangements of contributions, information, resources and decisions through C.32; use B.1.5.EW when the constituent and encompassing work is unclear. A common aim explains why to cooperate but does not settle how. If organization or supporting equipment must change, that becomes another creation question rather than a promise supplied by a team name.
6. **Repeat for the creators that must themselves be obtained.** A missing instrument may need manufacture; a team may need training or organization; a production installation may need redesign. Apply the same questions to that local system while retaining its contribution to the original target. A builder's period of use can be a period in which another system is produced or changed. This connection is not composition: a factory is not a constituent of its product.
7. **Follow each local result into its receiving use.** State what receives it next and what that use is expected to enable on the way to the common target. Inspect the resulting facts as work proceeds. Failed tests, new interests or an unworkable method may reopen the construction, functioning, target boundary or even the original purpose. Reopen the premise that failed rather than protect the earlier choice because work has already been spent. When the target, intended use, boundary or expected contribution materially changes, compare the affected participants' accounts again and trace which decisions rely on the changed premise. Restore the agreement needed by those decisions and make its revised basis available to the participants who must use it. Agreement between two teams does not update other dependent teams' decisions; resolve or expose that remaining difference before relying on their combined work. Unaffected decisions need not be reopened, and no particular meeting format is required.

This order is not a waterfall. Discovery can begin with an available person, tool or scientific result and ask where it could be useful; that inverse problem still has to find a supported use and explain the same dependencies. Negotiation, exploration, fabrication and testing may overlap or repeat. Use a WorkPlan for intended execution order and actual histories for what happened.

A local result keeps its own kind and closure. A drawing's content or a research argument can be an episteme; a measurement result can concern a characteristic; a completed workpiece is a separately identified entity. Show the connection through the relation needed by the next receiver, such as use of a description in design, installation of a component, or evidence supporting a decision. A.15.6 recovers the result and closure question; A.15.PROD distinguishes entity inception and production completion from later acceptance or use. The local result need not be a System to matter to the target.

Also separate two questions: **is this Work part of the project Work, and does its result help obtain the target?** Actual Work parthood uses A.15.1. A purchased instrument or a published theory may be useful even though the work that produced it was outside the project; an admitted project Work part may fail to make the expected contribution. Prospective dependencies justify plans under their stated assumptions. Actual relations and outcomes need their own evidence as they occur.

##### A.1.STM:4.3.1 - Assemble and revise a jointly workable account

Begin at the question that can change the current decision. Recover the answers already available and the conditions under which each is supported. A required use, a proposed construction, a demonstrated capability, an agreed resource allocation and an observed result have different grounds. Keep an important unknown or disagreement visible. A drawing that leaves it blank is not evidence that the condition is satisfied.

Explain how the subject matter of each answer constrains the others. The expected outside effect constrains the needed functioning; a construction makes some functioning possible and rules out other functioning; a creation Method requires particular subjects, capabilities and conditions; a cooperation arrangement determines whether those contributions can be available together. These relations need the relevant engineering, research, economic and organizational knowledge. The mantra recalls where that knowledge matters; it cannot deduce an unknown material law or create a missing production technique. Obtain the missing subject answer and bring back its conditions as well as its conclusion.

Compare coherent combinations. Do not combine the performance of one design with the cost of another, the capability of a fully prepared team with the schedule of an unprepared one, or a successful test with a later configuration the test did not examine. For the candidate under consideration, ask why its functioning, construction, obtaining Methods and creator arrangement can jointly serve the intended use. A supported local answer becomes part of this account only with the conditions needed for its use in that combination. The whole may remain a justified proposal; compatibility of the proposal does not establish that it has been realized.

Challenge the connection where a different allowed answer could matter. If a component, operating condition or creator changes, which other answers still hold, which must change and which become uncertain? Follow the material dependencies rather than changing every document or defending every earlier choice. A single defect can have several repairs: revise a construction, obtain another capability, change a way of producing it, or reconsider the promised use with those entitled to make that decision. Each repair has its own conditions and cost. Merely moving to the next phrase of the mantra chooses none of them.

Choose the next inquiry or construction for the consequence it can have. A missing fact may require measurement; incompatible answers may require a new alternative; disagreement may require clarification or negotiation; unavailable capability may require provision or development. Use the relevant subject Method and compare consequential alternatives when necessary. An unresolved release condition can leave release unavailable while inquiry, prototyping or obtaining support remains possible. Several independent questions may be pursued at once. Their results must return to the same candidate assumptions before being relied on together.

This is the useful sense of the analogy with solving simultaneous equations: partial answers restrict one another, and changing one can require changing others. It does not say that all questions have numerical variables, that normative agreement is an equation, or that a unique solution exists. Some hypotheses may remain unresolved; some sets of requirements may be incompatible. A project can responsibly narrow or reject a proposal rather than fill every line with an unsupported answer.

The compact mantra is a logical teaching unfolding of this reasoning. It presents important connections one after another so they can be understood and recalled. Actual reasoning may start with any consequential answer, explore alternatives, return, or develop several answers together. Use A.22.CGUS when the available continuations and their conditions need explicit treatment. Its ordinary branch makes those alternatives and unknowns clear; a formal CGUS claim additionally needs the structure and qualification that pattern defines. A teaching traversal of that structure still does not prescribe the order of Work.

Keep other project inquiries connected through their shared subjects and assumptions. A choice of creation Method affects capability and resource needs; an operational proposal can expose a capacity limit; a strategic change can reopen which result is worth obtaining. These inquiries do not all have the same purpose or one mandatory ordering. Carry a changed answer to the other inquiry only when it changes a premise used there. The result is one mutually intelligible account, not identical documents or one person deciding every question.

For the decision now being made, stop when the necessary answers and their relevant joint conditions are supported, or when the unresolved condition and its consequence are explicit. Preserve accepted local results whose grounds still hold. Continued project reasoning, later operation and further development can open new questions without making every completed local result unfinished.

#### A.1.STM:4.4 - Locate the first unsupported dependency

Use this narrower mode when the shared target and intended-use basis is already sufficient for the current question. It also helps name a precise next inquiry when one part of that basis remains disputed.


1. **Choose by the result needed now.** Name the release, runtime use, or outside change and its connection to the common project target. If a local result is current, say what it is about and ask which supported fact or justified prospective dependency connects it next to production or change, release, runtime use, or that outside change. If another long mantra better answers the local question, use it while retaining the shared target basis.
2. **Place supported answers and the current scope.** Put only answers supported by named facts, observations, decisions, or results in the matching regions. A systemic project account can use ordinary explanations and prospective hypotheses without an E.18.NET selection. When the current question needs that stronger network claim, name its admitted selection and use question; before admission, keep that network proposal provisional and name the missing member, relation occurrence, or endpoint binding. The network is not project Work. For one current case, state only four things in ordinary language: the exact subject or claim; the bounded references and direct claims needed to answer this closure question; the separately governed closure basis; and one named downstream receiving use that remains outside the closed case. Use A.15.6 to choose any technical reference form the case actually needs; do not copy those forms here. Keep plans, descriptions, Methods, Work, systems, changes, relations, evidence, and assurance distinct; do not turn this placement into a dossier or record schema.
3. **Find the first unsupported dependency.** Read backward from the final result and choose the earliest missing, stale, disputed, or unsupported answer that blocks the next dependency. *First* means logical firstness, not calendar firstness.
4. **Use one subject pattern.** Perform the working move described by the pattern whose `Use this when` accepts the missing question, and retain the resulting assertion or named stop.
5. **Return only what the next use needs.** Put the answer back in the map. Select an E.18.NET network only when its members, relations, constraints, use frame, and endpoint bindings are grounded. Return the individual answers when they suffice. Select another A.22 structure only when one named use needs their organization and all four identity discriminators pass. Otherwise keep the direct plurality or Plain provisional map.
6. **Trace forward and test.** Follow actual Work, production and identity facts, later changes, participation in use, and environment-side changes through their subject patterns. Repeat for each relevant builder branch. Bind evidence to the claim it supports; stop at the first unsupported direct link and reopen only the smallest affected earlier answer.

Four orders remain separate throughout: the order used to teach the map; the logical dependency read backward; planned or actual Work order; and the subject relations that obtain. One ordering establishes none of the others.

### A.1.STM:5 - Worked uses

#### A.1.STM:5.1 - Common pump target, separate organizational contributions

A pump-modernization project needs `PumpUnit-3` to restore reliable water delivery in its operating environment. The operator, controller supplier and maintenance team compare their accounts. They agree that the common target is the pump in the stated delivery conditions. The supplier's controller is its own local system; the maintenance team's test rig is a builder-side instrument. Faster testing and a completed controller are useful through their contribution to the pump's intended use. The plan designates the already existing pump as the **project target system**. A.1 recognition, that designation, any local system-role classification and any assignment remain separate questions.

The team already supports the outside-use hypothesis and a controller-architecture choice. Reading backward exposes the first unsupported answer: can the planned controller result become an actual system ready for installation? The team leaves A.1.STM for the subject patterns. It identifies `ControllerSubassembly-7` and other pre-existing materials as the continuing subjects of any A.3.4 changes; recovers each actual fabricator's A.13 core; independently admits fabrication Work under A.15.1; and uses A.15.PROD separately for production participation, controller identity inception, and production completion. This minimal case consumes no exact assignment-bound attribution, so it does not open F.6. It does not describe transformation of the controller before the controller exists.

For the controller-production case, the subject and closure basis are explicit. The case closes only when the independently governed identity-inception, completion or readiness, evidence, and decision claims needed here pass. The named downstream receiving use—installation and later operation in `PumpUnit-3`—is visible but remains outside the closed case.

At project level, the team may select TFS members concerning controller production, pump modification, qualification, and pump operation together under E.18.NET only after each member is independently identified and the required production, installation, participation, use, or feedback occurrences and endpoint bindings obtain. Until then the network remains a Plain provisional explanation with the missing link named. Actual facts are then traced forward from fabrication Work and changes, through controller inception and pump modification, to later pump operation. When runtime use is claimed, the already existing `PumpUnit-3` and its direct participation claim remain separate. If dated runtime Work is claimed, recover every performer's A.13 core and independently admit the occurrence under A.15.1; add F.6 only when precise assignment-bound attribution is also current. Keep either actor-side claim separate from any A.3.4 change of one exact continuing delivery-side environment or input referent; the expected reliable-delivery hypothesis alone establishes neither actuality. A failed relation or missing binding stops that claim without erasing the valid local results.

If later operation shows that reliable water delivery depends on an upstream reservoir-control assembly outside the proposed `PumpUnit-3` boundary, the team reopens the outside-use, project designation, and boundary hypotheses before revising the architecture and network selection. It does not preserve the old inside merely because Work has already begun.

##### A.1.STM:5.1.1 - Locally plausible answers that do not fit together

Suppose a proposed pump assembly must meet a stated delivery condition after installation. A functioning argument assumes a calibrated sensor in the completed assembly. The construction encloses that sensor, the production Method closes the housing before the proposed calibration, and the available calibration instrument needs physical access that the closed housing does not provide. Each team's answer may have worked in another configuration. Together, these particular answers leave the proposed qualification impossible as described. A common target and three signed local documents would not repair that incompatibility.

The team can now compare different whole proposals. It could change the housing to preserve calibration access, provided the changed construction still meets its operating requirements. It could propose calibration before closure, but then must establish why closure and installation would preserve the calibrated condition or how a later check can detect a consequential change. It could obtain another measurement arrangement, provided that arrangement can establish the required claim in the completed assembly and can actually be supplied. None is established merely by being imaginable.

The teaching explanation may run from required delivery through functioning, construction, production and calibration capability. The actual discovery began at a calibration question and returned to construction. A drawing, experiment or qualified measurement may supply the missing answer. If the team changes the housing, it reopens the affected operating and production assumptions; if it changes the instrument, it reopens the measurement and provision assumptions. It retains unaffected knowledge and restores agreement among the contributors whose decisions changed.

This case shows why the mantra must connect whole bodies of subject knowledge. Repeating "function, construction, method, creator" cannot supply the missing measurement argument. Conversely, a complete measurement argument for an inaccessible configuration cannot settle the project question. The connected account identifies what must be explained, which alternatives remain open, and where the answer will matter.

#### A.1.STM:5.2 - One-off service: a usable hairstyle

A client and a stylist agree the intended hairstyle and the conditions in which it must remain usable, including appearance after ordinary movement or rain. That gives a reason to choose the arrangement of hair and fasteners, then the cutting and styling Methods, then a capable stylist and suitable tools. Merely completing the hair operations would leave the purpose unmet if the result cannot survive the agreed use.

The embodied hairstyle is the proposed target; the salon is a provider, the sketch is a description, and the appointment's Work has its own boundary. Before the hairstyle exists, its designator belongs to the agreed description. For an actual system claim, A.1.SCR evaluates that exact proposed hair-and-fastener whole, its identity and physical organization; it does not substitute the client merely because the client is already a recognized System. If the whole is established not to satisfy the System criterion, reject that System interpretation. If necessary facts are missing, leave recognition unresolved and name the missing basis. In either case, preserve the supported service and result claims; do not treat those claims as settling the project's common target. The ordinary hairstyle name alone settles no systemhood question.

The sketch can be complete before styling; styling Work can end before the later use has been observed. Each local closure remains available at its own scope. A later failure under the agreed use conditions can reopen the design or service decision without making the earlier sketch an unfinished episteme or erasing the Work that happened.

#### A.1.STM:5.3 - Knowledge development with an epistemic result

A research team aims to improve a laboratory's ability to distinguish two material failure mechanisms. Its immediate result is an explanatory model and an account of experiments. Those are epistemes, not systems. In the shared systemic account, the team identifies the experimental installation in its intended diagnostic use as the target, then connects model construction, test design and instrument changes to that use. The argument and publication can reach their own completion or acceptance before the installation is changed or the improved diagnosis is demonstrated.

This choice depends on the stated research purpose. A project seeking a theorem, historical explanation or openly reusable knowledge must not invent an arbitrary laboratory, reader or computer as its target merely to fill a position. Its epistemic result can be legitimate and locally complete. If participants need a systemic account, they must still establish which system's intended use or change provides their common target and how knowledge development contributes to it. An unknown or contested receiving use leaves that systemic basis open; it does not turn the episteme into a System or invalidate its separate epistemic value.

### A.1.STM:7 - Recognition before relying on a system

Before relying on an acting system or changed-system boundary in the map, use an adequate available A.1 recognition result for that entity. Use A.1.SCR when a system-dependent question remains unresolved.

A.1.STM consumes only the returned recognition result. It does not repeat the six-component test, replace the exact entity with a convenient neighboring bearer, infer a system-role assignment from causal participation, or infer Method, Work, transformation, promise, permission, project designation, or a system-role kind from systemhood.

### A.1.STM:10 - Consequences

**Benefits.** Participants can justify local choices by a common target and use hypothesis, expose incompatible understandings, and preserve the connection while developing builders and other enabling systems. The narrower mode locates a missing dependency without requiring the whole account to be rebuilt.

**Costs.** Establishing a shared target can require domain inquiry, negotiation and experiments. Keeping local subjects, prospective dependencies and actual outcomes distinct adds work, especially across organizations and future product variants.

**Limits.** This pattern supplies the common systemic reasoning and its return paths. It does not guarantee a correct target choice or settled agreement, and does not independently identify a System, admit Work or transformation, close a case, identify a TFS network, or establish evidence or assurance. A local result can be complete while the common system basis or a downstream use remains open.

### A.1.STM:12 - Rationale

A shared target makes division of labour intelligible: it lets a participant explain why a local choice matters to people outside that local task. Intended use constrains functioning; functioning constrains plausible construction; construction constrains the Methods and capabilities needed to obtain it. Those dependencies explain the mantra's order while criticism and experience explain the returns.

The same reasoning applies recursively to builders without making creation a composition relation or every builder a Work performer. Separating the project role from System identity preserves this organizing principle across different projects and before a holder exists. Separating local completion from receiving use lets a team finish bounded work without mistaking that completion for success of the whole undertaking.
