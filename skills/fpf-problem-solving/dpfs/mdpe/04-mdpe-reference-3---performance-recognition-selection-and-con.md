## MDPE.Reference:3 - Performance, recognition, selection, and continuation are different results

A detector can distinguish a signal without a listener recognizing a style. A listener can recognize a style
without selecting it. A curator or platform can select a variant without receivers being able to enact it. A
receiver can enact it once without retaining it. A score, recording, video, model, or archive can remain while
the capability and support needed for present reproduction disappear.

Use `MDPE.12` to determine what the observations support and `MDPE.23` to assess or develop recognition.
`MDPE.13` examines or designs recognition and selection arrangements. `MDPE.17` addresses receiving performance
and the source’s contribution. `MDPE.18` helps establish what can be recovered from memory or lineage evidence.
`MDPE.19` assesses continuation and helps choose a further test or change when needed and worthwhile.

The method [MDPE.25 - Renew a Shared Music or Dance Practice](42-mdpe-25---renew-a-shared-music-or-dance-practice.md) shows how these contributions can be constructed together. In its worked case, the organizers preserve time for social participation, a valued challenge and recognition while changing the timetable, teaching and selection rule. Use MDPE.14 to assess the conflict or choose a response. The people carrying out a selected change can use MDPE.15. MDPE.25 connects those contributions to the local arrangement, including learning, provision and any revision of the criterion. Use [Recover the subject](52-mdpe-application-5---participation-learning-recognition-and.md#mdpeapplication52---recover-the-subject-of-a-music-or-movement-claim) when a familiar label hides the changing object, and [Connect style, Method structure and acquisition](52-mdpe-application-5---participation-learning-recognition-and.md#mdpeapplication53---connect-style-method-structure-and-acquisition) when a recoverable description, stylistic distinction and learned performance are being confused.
