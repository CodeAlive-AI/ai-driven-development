Begin with the question you have about a performance, rehearsal, teaching encounter, style-development project or the environment that sustains a practice.

Search the Table of Contents by a familiar term or working question. Open the relevant pattern and compare its situation with yours. Use the method for the work someone needs to do. Playing a scored entrance, devising the ensemble’s cue and explaining yesterday’s entrance can concern the same music. Establish which choice the score settles, which choice the player or bandleader must make, and what would change their answer. Use another pattern when you need the result it supplies.

The Readme follows several performance, teaching and practice-development cases. The Architectural Reference explains their shared distinctions. For other questions, use the Table of Contents to find a method, its limits and its worked cases. Creation, rehearsal, performance, observation and development can overlap.

# Music and Dance Practice Engineering Principles Framework Readme

## Practical entries

Bring your question about performance, teaching or practice development. These selected examples show how one pattern's result changes what another method can do, where a useful branch can stop, and what changes when the conditions do. A case can feature the same music and serve another job. Compare the result its method gives you with the decision, explanation or design you owe. For work still open, use the [Table of Contents](_index.md#table-of-contents) to find the method by its question. Use a direct specialist method when it already supplies the needed result.

The [Architectural Reference](01-music-and-dance-practice-engineering-principles-framework-ar.md) explains the shared language and its Music and Dance profiles. You can ask an assisting agent: “Explain this and give me your comments in the language of my work, without framework jargon.”

### MDPE-CARD-STYLE-DEVELOPMENT — Develop a new style without mistaking one showcase for a maintained practice

- **Situation:** A collective has an exciting performance and wants to invest in a recurring practice, but other participants have not yet reproduced the relation that made the showcase work.
- **Question:** Which change and next commitment are justified for the practice the collective wants to develop?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A bounded practice-development question and a supported assessment or choice of arrangement for the intended recurrence. Identify any missing participant, capability, decision authority, permission or support. Specify a representative test when its attainable contribution warrants its cost.
- **Start with:** [MDPE.8](19-mdpe-8---characterize-the-music-or-dance-work-methods-and-ch.md) to distinguish the subjects being changed, [MDPE.1](20-mdpe-1---frame-a-music-or-dance-work-practice-style-or-mediu.md) to frame the project, and [MDPE.21](21-mdpe-21---design-how-a-music-or-dance-style-is-produced-and.md) when different ways to produce and reproduce the intended practice need comparison.
- **Stop or return:** Finish when the evidence supports the requested assessment, recommendation or local decision, including retention of the current arrangement. Return when an observed performance, changed participant, or support condition defeats the selected arrangement.

In the [constructed social-dance and live-music case](48-mdpe-application-1---a-constructed-social-dance-and-live-mus.md), a rehearsed pair and a live coder have performed a successful showcase. The organizers want unfamiliar pairs to sustain partner-responsive timing in a monthly social event. MDPE.8 separates that recurring practice from the showcase and from a possible later style line. MDPE.1 bounds the present project to one twenty-minute block. MDPE.21 compares the showcase arrangement with a social-practice arrangement: the latter needs varied partners, readable timing, a host, usable sound and floor conditions, and another opportunity to practise. The organizers choose to try the second arrangement because it supports the intended social use.

Using [MDPE.22](40-mdpe-22---test-a-support-environment-change-for-a-music-or-d.md), the organizers set aside the event block for practice. They secure participants, a host, floor space and permission for the trial. [MDPE.5](32-mdpe-5---integrate-production-and-presentation-for-performed.md) brings the music, dance, sound, and event contributions together for a pair's performance. [MDPE.12](33-mdpe-12---observe-and-compare-music-or-dance-performance-and.md) guides observation of eight pairs. In three pairs the entrance follows a release whose timing the partner chose, and the dancers identify that release as their cue. Five return to a memorized phrase after a dense accent change, and four on the far side report difficulty locating the pulse.

The organizers use [MDPE.14](41-mdpe-14---choose-a-response-to-conflicting-music-or-dance-re.md) to examine whether the musical changes and partner cue allow dancers to maintain their timing in the same performance. A longer verbal explanation would leave the sound and timing difficulty unresolved. The team compares constraining the musical changes, keeping the showcase arrangement, and maintaining separate showcase and social configurations. It selects the split. The team implements its choice with [MDPE.15](43-mdpe-15---make-and-test-a-local-music-or-dance-practice-chan.md). The musician moves large accent changes away from the partner-cue interval, and the sound operator re-aims the monitor. In the later round, the same observations establish partner-responsive timing for six pairs. The other two use the teacher’s cue. What they could do unaided is still open. Both the sound and musical configuration changed, and the pairs had already practised, so these observations do not isolate either change's effect.

With [MDPE.7](46-mdpe-7---choose-the-next-music-or-dance-development-work.md), the organizers use the changed configuration and the observations to compare the larger festival slot, another transfer trial, capability development, and stopping the social branch. In this case the team chooses an unfamiliar-partner trial and keeps the current event block for a month. The showcase branch remains useful for its own audience. [MDPE.16](44-mdpe-16---keep-music-or-dance-alternatives-available-for-lat.md) can maintain that branch when its performers, material, equipment, and permissions remain available. Another receiving group or a season-long continuation promise would require evidence for that group or interval.

### Renew a shared practice across participation, teaching and recognition

A school may teach more demanding material while its participants lose time, access or willing partners for the occasion they came to enjoy. [MDPE.25 - Renew a Shared Music or Dance Practice](42-mdpe-25---renew-a-shared-music-or-dance-practice.md) starts from that receiving participation and constructs an attainable arrangement across teaching, recognition and provision.

In its [complete Mara case](42-mdpe-25---renew-a-shared-music-or-dance-practice.md#mdpe2551---a-complete-constructed-case-the-evening-that-became-a-qualification-course), the organizers make 150 minutes of the four-hour event available for social participation, retain a bounded challenge and change an entry criterion that excluded a useful small variation. Losing the second room later reopens the timetable while the useful teaching and award distinctions remain. Finish with the supported arrangement, or explain what a proposal would still require. Use MDPE.14 alone when a bounded conflict decision is all that is needed.

### MDPE-PERFORM — Create alternatives and prepare a performing whole

- **Situation:** An attractive arrangement or movement candidate works in a rendering or isolated demonstration, but its live performer and coordination demands remain unsettled.
- **Question:** Which candidate can serve the intended performance, and does any relation need repair before we rely on it?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A comparison adequate for the intended use. It can support keeping a candidate, revising it, preserving different versions for different uses or ruling it out, or show what the available evidence cannot settle. For an open question, identify the performer, condition or observation needed to answer it.
- **Start with:** [MDPE.9](23-mdpe-9---generate-and-compare-music-or-dance-performance-can.md) with the intended use from [MDPE.1](20-mdpe-1---frame-a-music-or-dance-work-practice-style-or-mediu.md). If the candidate is already selected, use its existing capability and interaction assessment where sufficient. For a consequential capability question use [MDPE.10](26-mdpe-10---develop-and-test-music-or-dance-performing-capabil.md). For a question about the configuration use [MDPE.3](27-mdpe-3---configure-and-coordinate-a-music-or-dance-performin.md).
- **Stop or return:** Stop when the creation or performance question is answered. A changed venue, instrument, performer, or receiving use reopens the contribution that depends on that condition.

MDPE.9's constructed music example compares three arrangements for singer, clarinet, cello, and live electronics. The antiphonal exchange requires the musicians to hear and answer one another, leave time to breathe and continue if the electronics fail. Dense spectral doubling, sparse instrumental answers, and generated responses pose different demands. A rendering can reveal limits of range and balance. A short rehearsal with these musicians can show how they breathe, follow cues, cope with latency and recover from an interruption.

Suppose that rehearsal shows that the dense version masks the answers, the sparse version preserves the desired space, and the generated responses arrive too late for the intended exchange. Those observations give grounds to reject the first candidate for this use, keep an acoustic version and return the generated responses for revision. For a further electronic trial, the musical requirement and failed timing relation guide [MDPE.3:4.7](27-mdpe-3---configure-and-coordinate-a-music-or-dance-performin.md#mdpe347---antiphonal-ensemble-cue-late-response-and-acoustic-recovery). Its constructed continuation realizes a visible readiness cue, a cutoff for the current electronic answer and an acoustic fallback, then follows their joint trial to a bounded rehearsal decision. If a performer cannot make the required response even with an adequate cue and setup, MDPE.10 addresses that capability instead.

[MDPE.11](29-mdpe-11---interpret-or-shape-music-or-dance-material-while-p.md) concerns how musicians realize the prepared material and steer its permitted live variation. In the joint trial in MDPE.3:4.7 the musicians sustain the exchange with an acoustic answer when generation is late. Retain that fallback with the conditions under which they used it. MDPE.5 asks whether production and presentation let the intended recipients encounter the same musical relation. A successful monitor mix for the performers can still leave the audience unable to hear the answers.

If the next venue changes audibility or latency, use [MDPE.6](36-mdpe-6---choose-whether-and-how-to-use-a-music-or-dance-resu.md) to decide whether the existing result can be used, revised, or branched for that venue. If the new conditions defeat the earlier assessment for this venue, identify the affected cue, sound relation or receiving use. Retain the original result at its supported scope and obtain a repair when the intended use requires it. A working acoustic branch can remain available while the electronic branch is revised. A later performance also requires available musicians and equipment.

### MDPE-TRANSFER — Move a practice to new conditions and keep it reproducible

- **Situation:** Learners can repeat a taught coordination with the teacher but cannot use it with unfamiliar partners, and a school wants to rely on the practice beyond the present class.
- **Question:** What transferred, what still depends on the teaching situation, and which result is needed for the school's next commitment?
- **First useful result or blocker:** The relations the receiving version must preserve, what the evidence establishes about that use and any missing capability, source content, partner or support that limits the answer.
- **Start with:** [MDPE.6](36-mdpe-6---choose-whether-and-how-to-use-a-music-or-dance-resu.md) for the changed conditions and [MDPE.17](37-mdpe-17---design-and-test-transmission-of-a-music-or-dance-m.md) for receiving performance. Use [MDPE.23](34-mdpe-23---develop-and-test-music-or-dance-recognition-capabi.md) when the uncertainty concerns recognition rather than the movement that follows.
- **Stop or return:** Keep the claim within the receiving conditions the available evidence supports. Claims about tested performance require evidence from the performance. Return a recognition problem to MDPE.23, a performance limitation to MDPE.10, or a missing support relation to MDPE.22. A broader continuation promise requires its own basis.

Suppose the teaching team wants learners to use two already taught partner-coordination variants with unfamiliar partners. MDPE.6 compares the class with that receiving use. The partners must be able to read each other’s timing and contact. Their use of teacher labels and a fixed exercise order can vary. The team can choose a receiving version of the teaching arrangement that varies those conditions while retaining the original class for learners who still need its support.

With MDPE.17, examine what the receiving dancers do, which taught method or variant they enact and what evidence shows about their use of the demonstration or lesson material. When the performance fails, MDPE.23 can separate “did not recognize the current coordination” from “recognized it but could not perform the response”. Its [constructed recognition case](34-mdpe-23---develop-and-test-music-or-dance-recognition-capabi.md#mdpe2342---a-multidance-recognition-transfer-trial) asks for a correct spoken A/B response within one agreed pulse after the cue and before the movement. The learner gives ten such responses in twelve held-out trials with unfamiliar partners and music. A second matched block adds a rapid transition from movement task A to B while retaining the same recording and partner. The next recognition cue is selected independently of that preceding sequence. Only seven of twelve responses qualify. The local ten-of-twelve entry criterion permits a subsequent supported response exercise in the first tested condition. Work on the transition limit before adding rapid switches to that exercise. These counts do not establish movement fluency. A correct recognition followed by a failed response returns to MDPE.10. If competent recognizers cannot maintain the distinction across the intended contexts, revise the distinction or receiving task before drilling it.

These results affect the receiving trial and the next teaching move. The team can use representative dance performances to identify the variant the dancers enacted. Evidence that they used the demonstration or lesson material can establish its use while leaving its causal contribution undecided. A variation introduced by the dancers can be retained when it preserves the relations needed in the new setting.

When the school asks whether another teacher can continue the practice, [MDPE.18](38-mdpe-18---build-usable-music-or-dance-cultural-memory-and-li.md) identifies what the lesson descriptions, recordings, and testimony can help that teacher recover and which bodily relations they omit. [MDPE.19](39-mdpe-19---decide-whether-a-music-or-dance-variant-can-contin.md) uses this evidence together with available performance and support results to assess the stated continuation promise. Existing evidence may support continuation with the current teacher while replacement remains unknown. If evidence about a replacement is needed, use any suitable existing result. Select a further test only when it can supply what is still needed and warrants the full teaching, performer, support and observation burden. A claim that replacement performance was tested must rest on that performance. The school can also propose how to prepare another teacher.
