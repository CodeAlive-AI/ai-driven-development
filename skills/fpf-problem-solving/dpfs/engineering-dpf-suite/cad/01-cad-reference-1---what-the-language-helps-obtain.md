## CAD.Reference:1 - What the language helps obtain

A controller acts on information that can be partial, old or costly to obtain. Determine when the plant must feel the effect of the command to meet its task. A later decision may come due while earlier commands are still in an actuator queue. Include those commands, the available actuation and the continuation the task requires in the model. When planning and feedback are separated, their different models and time scales add another coupling: an attractive nominal command may be impossible for the lower loop to realize.

Start with the decision the control design must support. For one installation the actual configuration can settle which dynamics matter. For a family of installations, establish where each design will be used, whether a single control law must serve the family and what the controller can know when it chooses an action. To qualify one architecture, connect its observations, command meanings, error allowances and execution conditions to the required behavior. To rule out an information arrangement, establish that every permitted policy can encounter a situation in which its available information leaves no way to meet the requirement. A coarse bound can leave that question open.

CAD.5 constructs the information available to a specified controller. CAD.1–CAD.4 develop a planning-and-feedback arrangement when there is reason to separate the decisions. The two branches can be used independently. Their quantitative results connect where an estimate, command or timing claim from one enters the other.

A precise delayed sensor leaves recent disturbances unknown. A larger model can reduce modeling error while missing the deadline. A gain that reduces tracking error can consume more input authority. Conservative sets can protect a guarantee while excluding a useful design. All of these must be judged against the same task; a smaller isolated error measure is not automatically a better control result.

FPF C.43 supplies the common Method for connecting observation, choice and action in ongoing control. Apply it when the available assessment, alternatives and execution conditions need to become one coherent continuation; an adequate architecture can remain unchanged. CAD specializes the engineering construction of the information, command and timing relations. Its state sets, error bounds and feasible commands give quantitative answers under declared models; they do not by themselves settle the legitimacy of a goal, the economics of competing resource uses or a developing subject's next worthwhile problem. A changed information delay returns to CAD.5 and its dependent bounds; an unavailable correction can reopen CAD.3 or the architecture choice; a changed goal first changes the task against which those results are used.

FPF C.43:4.5.1 also supplies the common construction of required temporal relations without imposing a periodic schedule. CAD.2 makes that construction executable through event ordering, behavior between updates and reset rules; CAD.5 preserves sample phase, information age and observation gaps, and CAD.4 qualifies the shared implementation schedule. An action can vary its timing while another required contribution continues, provided their joint resource and dynamic conditions still hold. Changing one of these relations reopens the dependent calculation; equal update frequencies alone do not preserve it.
