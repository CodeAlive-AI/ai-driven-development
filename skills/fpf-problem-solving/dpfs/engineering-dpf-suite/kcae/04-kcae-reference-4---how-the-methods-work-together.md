## KCAE.Reference:4 - How the methods work together

Design begins with a receiving use and its constraints. KCAE.USE produces the workload, source roles, allowed data flow and quality/cost questions that select an arrangement. KCAE.SOURCE produces readable, addressable material with an explicit extraction boundary. KCAE.INDEX uses that material to create reusable finding routes. KCAE.CHANGE preserves the relation between those routes and sources as content changes. These are construction and maintenance operations; they need not run anew for each question.

During an ordinary inquiry, KCAE.SEARCH chooses available routes and follows what remains unknown. It returns source-addressed candidates and a coverage limit. KCAE.ASSESS opens the necessary context and relates the candidate to the receiving result. If several contributions are needed, KCAE.COMPOSE establishes the missing intermediate results, conversions and joint conditions. That can send a new query back to search, or expose a missing capability that no further corpus query can supply. KCAE.DELIVER places the needed instructions and grounds within the recipient's reach. If an available, authorized result already answers the request, deliver it with what the recipient needs to use it. If it supplies one premise, start with the question that remains.

The apparent sequence hides important feedback. Delivery limits can change which otherwise suitable method is affordable. Assessment can reveal that the request presupposes a false diagnosis. A source change can invalidate an assessment while leaving an embedding technically reusable. A capability change in the executor can change the delivery design without changing the subject method. Each return follows the failed dependency; the arrangement need not start over.

KCAE.ENCOUNTER adds a separate entry when an authorized work event creates a useful occasion for inquiry. KCAE.MEMORY preserves open questions and conditional connections so that an altered source or a later episode can reopen them. Neither is necessary for a one-time explicit query. KCAE.EVAL assesses what recipients can obtain under the proposed arrangement. Compare arrangements when the choice turns on their differences, or trace an observed shortfall to the work that needs repair.

Consider the fictional **CedarBench** service, a large documentation corpus for data-export software. A customer says, “Nightly reports finish on time, but our senior analyst removes duplicates every morning. Can we buy faster workers?” The access design supports English and Spanish queries, current operational advice and separate historical explanation. It may read public manuals and an authorized local configuration record; private row data cannot leave the organization.

Two questions follow from the customer’s words. The current offer and its eligibility terms can establish whether the customer can buy faster workers. To advise whether to buy them, establish what the customer expects them to change. Suppose the customer would upgrade only to eliminate the routine duplicate corrections. Find out when a second submission is made, what causes it and which part of the process the paid tier changes. Timely completion of the nightly report can coexist with a retry during preparation or with another submission after acceptance. Those mechanisms give different reasons to buy.

In the worked case the tier shortens preparation. A local rule submits an already acknowledged batch again under a new batch identity, independently of preparation time. The authorized trace connects that second submission to duplicate rows the analyst corrected. The tier is available. Buying it leaves this source of corrections in place. That result answers the customer’s buying criterion under the stated rule. If the second submission instead depends on a timeout, recover when its clock starts and whether faster preparation could avoid it before advising on the purchase.

The operator has a separate question about an identified batch. To decide what may safely be sent again, use the applicable manual and destination configuration, including named receipts if revision 7 requires them. For a question about which exports are missing, a complete count of the distinct intended export IDs accepted for that batch by the relevant time can suffice. Zero accepted means all intended exports are missing. A count equal to the size of that set means none are missing. An intermediate count by itself leaves their identities open.

Scheduling pending exports is a further commission. Each of three jobs reserves 2 GB in a shared 5 GB cache. Any two fit at once. A schedule for all three also depends on when the completed jobs release their reservations and when the last job must finish. KCAE.Application:1 works out that choice. The operator can act on these answers. They bear on the purchase recommendation only if the customer’s buying criterion depends on them.

Revision 8 then changes the resend condition. The source reader can obtain it, but the vector build has failed. A coherent revised exact/lexical route can still serve the new edition, while the old vector view remains limited to an identified unchanged subset or an explicitly historical query. A pending recommendation derived from the changed clause is reassessed before reuse. KCAE.Application:1 provides the concrete source slices, construction and variation behind this account.
