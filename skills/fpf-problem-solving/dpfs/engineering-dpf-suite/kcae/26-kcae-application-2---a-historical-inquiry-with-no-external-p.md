## KCAE.Application:2 - A historical inquiry with no external processing

A compliance analyst asks which instruction was available to a support team during a past incident. Only three named documents matter: the English manual then published, the Spanish localization then distributed and a dated handover note. The processing permission is local-only. The receiving result is an explanation of the contemporaneous information basis, not today's operational advice.

A dated, sufficiently complete register of the editions available to that team may settle the inquiry if it also establishes the permissions, equipment and routes the team could use in time. The analyst’s access today establishes little about those conditions. If the question also depends on what the localization omitted, compare the exact editions locally or use an authorized, adequate finding about their wording. If the analyst was commissioned to make that comparison personally, provide local access or identify the outstanding inspection. A persistent semantic index offers little for this small, named dossier.

To ask what the team actually received, inspect the delivery evidence. A transmission record can establish sending. An acknowledgement tied to an edition can establish receipt by the named service or person. If evidence of receipt is missing, retain the supported finding about availability. For a claim about what an operator read, understood or relied on, look for evidence of that operator’s work. An authorized record or interview can supply it. The rule governing today’s work may have changed since the incident.

The exact reader preserves edition and table context, and the analyst distinguishes source claims from case facts. The search, remote-assessor and automatic-encounter machinery are not needed. If a new incident document appears, reconsider the source cut and explanation; it need not trigger construction of every KCAE component.
