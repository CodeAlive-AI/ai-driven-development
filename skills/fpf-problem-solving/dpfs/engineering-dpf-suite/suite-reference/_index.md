Use this Reference while working on a problem. Section 0 explains the Suite's composition, specialized disciplines and their connections; the worked cases explain how that architecture affects use. The question index points to a pattern and the result it can help you obtain. The cases explain how to combine contributions when one field cannot answer the whole question. The Engineering Suite edition includes five direct DPFs: Systems Engineering, Maintenance Engineering and Management, Knowledge-Corpus Access Engineering, Semantic Integration Engineering and Control Architecture Design. Management describes the organizational specialization, including Organization Change Engineering; Human Capability Engineering has its own specialized Suite. Some other suppliers are independent DPFs or belong to Foundational Thinking. Following a useful result does not by itself decide a field's disciplinary membership.

The engineering repertoire can form part of the Library’s [canon for a shared human–AI culture of thinking and acting](../../../library/reference/02-library-reference-1---one-connected-language-several-kinds-o.md#libraryreference13---a-canon-for-shared-humanai-culture). People and AI agents use its methods to connect reasoning with functioning systems and to develop their joint practice. Allocation to a human, agent or mixed team depends on the capability, authority and support required for the actual contribution. Engineering results and their use can in turn make further development possible. The [professional-contribution account](02-engineering-reference-0---engineering-specialization-and-mai.md#engineeringreference08---connect-professional-contributions-to-the-required-result) explains how to connect broad role names with the required work and its provision.

If you are new to the Suite, the [README (Engineering DPF Suite)](../_index.md) explains what a DPF is, what is available, and how to begin with colleagues or an AI assistant. Here, open the nearest question, then read the relevant pattern's Problem frame, Solution and example. The pattern's full conditions apply to your case.

Section addresses use `Engineering.Reference:n` and nested numbers. They identify sections of this Reference, not patterns. The contents links follow the heading hierarchy; a complete section includes its child sections up to the next heading of the same or a higher level.
