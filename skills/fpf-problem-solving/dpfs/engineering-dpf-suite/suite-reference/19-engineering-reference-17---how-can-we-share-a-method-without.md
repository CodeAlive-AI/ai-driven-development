## Engineering.Reference:17 - How can we share a method without losing the means to develop it?

Six engineering teams use an analysis assistant. Existing review makes its routine outputs usable. A new assignment requires changing a model assumption and explaining the resulting construction; only two shared specialists can currently supply that contribution. Should the practice developer promote the assistant to more teams? The following facts are stipulated for this example, not measured effects of an intervention.

**Recover what the next work needs.** [FPF C.36.RP](../../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/129-c-36-rp---sustain-and-renew-shared-ways-of-working.md) separates obtaining a checked answer from being able to alter its method. The specialists are a valid current supplier; the teams need not all become specialists. Determine who must recognize an unsuitable answer, who must reconstruct the changed model and which contributions can be obtained elsewhere. There is no evidence here that the teams previously had and then lost this ability. If a retention or tool-dependence claim would change the decision, use [HCD.13](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/30-hcd-13-test-human-capability-retention-and-dependence-on-too.md) with its required comparison. A new demand alone does not establish decline.

**Follow uptake into the next contribution.** A demonstration, an attempted procedure, a checked result and an ability to help another team are different events. [DTM.2](../../dtm/10-dtm-2---build-a-transmission-law-from-exposure-and-uptake.md) recovers what the recipient obtains, including a changed construction; [DTM.3](../../dtm/11-dtm-3---couple-change-within-carriers-to-spread-between-them.md) connects that change to subsequent transmission. Use a quantitative model only if its result can change the choice. Counting demonstrations would miss both the receiving work and the demand on specialists.

**Protect the work that supplies the next method.** The two specialists can jointly provide nine hours of specialist work per week for this service, after their other commitments. Current work consumes seven of those hours. Expansion is estimated to need two additional hours of receiving support and two of method development: eleven hours would not fit. The support and development estimates may still be wrong, but accepting them cannot make this plan feasible.

[ECO.7](../../eco/17-eco-7---coordinate-use-and-renewal-of-a-shared-resource.md) distinguishes freely copying a description from consuming a constrained service. [OPS.19](../../management-dpf-suite/ops/53-ops-19---reconcile-simultaneous-operating-work-across-cases.md) helps compare the work competing for those hours while preserving commitments. The service owner can retain the current arrangement, obtain qualified help, change support or defer expansion.

Suppose no additional provider has been secured. One specialist can provide the remaining two hours for a reconstruction probe, including preparation and review; a receiving participant also has time to take part. The question is whether the existing explanation supports a consequential change of assumption or leaves a missing operation. Its answer will inform the choice between improving that explanation, developing a different learning arrangement and continuing to obtain specialist help. The owner judges that this bounded answer warrants the time of both participants and defers expansion. If the answer would not change that choice enough to justify the work, retaining the supported service without the probe would be sufficient.

**Keep an introduction attempt separate from its result.** [FPF A.3.1.MI](../../../sections/05-part-a---kernel-architecture-cluster/28-a-3-1-mi---introduce-a-method-into-practice-and-decide-what.md) keeps the target method, explanation, support and way of introducing the change distinct. Record what the participant can reconstruct and use, with the assistance actually provided. Use [HCD.16](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/33-hcd-16-keep-human-capability-development-method-claims-curre.md) for any conclusion about the human-development method. Two allocated hours demonstrate neither mastery nor that another team can later do the same work.

If the probe yields a useful construction, retain the assumption, operations, explanation and conditions needed to vary it again. C.36.RP returns the next consequential variation to the appropriate development method.

Reading the model, interpreting its quantities and judging its use can be constituent methods enacted together in one review. Teaching another reviewer, maintaining the assistant and operating the shared service have their own work and support relations. A method vertical, a provider arrangement and a population of users need not have the same structure.


### Engineering.Reference:17.1 - Obtain an explanation and another teacher's contribution

A specialist can make today's answer usable by supplying a relation absent from the shared material. If later teams need that help, the practice developer must obtain it for their use: through continued specialist provision, a revised explanation with teaching support, or another workable arrangement. Developing teaching is worthwhile only when that contribution matters to the next work.

**Pass the grounds with the answer.** Keep the task, the participant's response and stated reasoning, and the help actually supplied. Preserve the response before corrective help so that an intervention does not conceal what was missing. Ask the subject specialist to recover the decisive operation or relation, why it works and when it applies. The specialist may expose an omitted preparation step or a needed contribution from another practice. Retain that dependency in the explanation or arrange its supply. If the subject criterion itself is unresolved, return it to the relevant professional or research work.

**Repair the connected material.** The author uses that account to revise the explanation and the examples, tasks and teacher guidance that depend on it. The diagnostic operations of [HCD.19](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/36-hcd-19---evaluate-instructional-material-through-representat.md) help separate inadequate material from missing preparation, unavailable assistance and disputed subject grounds. Agree what the recipient must do, the criterion and allowed help; a judgement of the material uses a compatible [evaluation specification](03-engineering-reference-1---find-a-starting-pattern.md#engineeringreference113---evaluate-instructional-material-for-its-intended-use). Correct answers alone can conceal a missing relation supplied by prior knowledge or a shortcut that happens to work on familiar cases.

**Make the contrast distinguish the reasoning.** Consider the module-release and laboratory-window case in [HCD.8:5.1](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/25-hcd-8---obtain-or-build-missing-human-capability-development.md). The subject specialist establishes whether a test report covers the intended configuration; the workload must also fit the available interval. Suppose familiar examples show a complete ten-change report that permits continuation and an incomplete twelve-change report that does not. Both “up to ten changes is acceptable” and “an applicable report is sufficient” reproduce those verdicts. The author therefore includes these stipulated contrasts:

| Case | Grounds for the next recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Ten changes need 23 hours within 24, but the report omits the relevant firmware. | The work fits; obtain the missing applicability basis before relying on the report. |
| A complete compatible twelve-change report accompanies 27 hours of work and a 24-hour interval. | The report applies, but the work does not fit; revise the work or obtain a feasible interval. |
| The same complete report and 27-hour workload accompany a 28-hour interval. | The capacity objection is removed; proceed to the authorized acceptance decision under the remaining conditions. |

The second and third cases keep the report and work unchanged while changing the available interval. The first case prevents a small workload from standing in for report applicability. A teacher asks for the recommendation and its grounds, then uses the contrast that can distinguish the learner's reasoning. If the response still permits several explanations, return it with the task and assistance used to the author and specialist. They refine the question and examine the resulting response before attributing one misconception. Unexpected grounds, including those behind a correct verdict, can require a new contrast. Feedback can correct the demonstrated error; a claim about a recurring conceptual difficulty needs supporting reasoning across cases.

**Try the teaching contribution in use.** Through [HCD.8](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/25-hcd-8---obtain-or-build-missing-human-capability-development.md), compare an available qualified provider with preparing another willing person. Give the proposed teacher the revised material, permitted subject help and representative learner responses, keeping the assessor's proposed diagnosis out of the assignment. In this release-planning case, require the teacher to justify different recommendations for the complete report at 24 and 28 hours and connect the learner's grounds to useful feedback.

For example, a learner accepts both proposals because the report applies. Useful feedback retains that valid applicability judgement, explains why 27 hours of work cannot fit into 24, and asks for a feasible revision. Have a qualified assessor examine that connection and observe the feedback delivered and used in a representative recipient's next attempt within the promised service conditions. A correct mark without the explanation and usable correction leaves the teaching contribution unestablished. Where the grounds remain ambiguous, a justified clarification or better contrast is an appropriate return.

For the six teams, obtain the grounds and consequential contrast for their own model-assumption change, then try the proposed help in that receiving work. Specialist assistance can remain part of the arrangement; independent performance needs its own task and evidence only when the receiving work requires it.

**Provide for the added work and its continuation.** In the six-team case, the two remaining specialist hours were allocated to the reconstruction probe, including its preparation and review. Those hours do not also establish provision for author revision, teacher preparation, feedback and later maintenance. Obtain a feasible allocation or another qualified contributor, reduce the offer, or retain the supported service while deferring expansion. One willing and capable person can perform several contributions; using the shared practice does not commit every participant to authorship or teaching.

When this work changes a shared repertoire, [CB.14](../../management-dpf-suite/cb/24-cb-14---develop-and-maintain-a-shared-repertoire-of-practice.md) connects the returned explanation to its receiving use and agreement about correction and maintenance. Retain the required expert help until the replacement can supply its contribution. A later case that defeats the subject explanation returns to method or research work; loss of an otherwise adequate teacher or source reopens provision.


### Engineering.Reference:17.2 - When the conditions change

| Changed condition | Consequence for the next move |
| --- | --- |
| Another accessible provider already supplies the required construction. | Repair access or allocation before prescribing learning to every team. Keep any independently required human judgement explicit. |
| Teams can reconstruct the method but the proposed tool's adoption cost exceeds its useful contribution. | Keeping their current practice is a valid result. Rejection alone is not proof of resistance or incapability. |
| Recognition rewards polished outputs while errors in unfamiliar cases remain hidden. | Use [DTM.7](../../dtm/15-dtm-7---locate-diversion-of-a-shared-control-or-cooperation.md) to trace how the visible output earns further use and whether that support sustains the mismatch. A.3.1.MI introduces a justified change. More instruction alone may leave the reward rule unchanged. |
| The new procedure succeeds only in a course or demonstration. | Compare the receiving task and support with the course conditions. In music and dance, [MDPE.21](../../mdpe/21-mdpe-21---design-how-a-music-or-dance-style-is-produced-and.md) and [MDPE.22](../../mdpe/40-mdpe-22---test-a-support-environment-change-for-a-music-or-d.md) address the performance and environment relations; their musical and bodily conditions remain specific to that practice. |

The same distinction matters for a shared technical reference: its descriptions can be copied without exhausting them, while question answering and revision work can exhaust the maintainers' time. Arrange the question-answering and revision work needed for continued use. A support agreement is one result; continuing production of useful methods is another.

Finish with the supported continuation or change, the contributions it needs and the uncertainty it leaves. A local successful attempt establishes neither wider uptake nor a lasting cultural improvement. If that stronger conclusion matters, obtain the corresponding observations; otherwise use the present result.
