## Engineering.Reference:17 - How can we share a method without losing the means to develop it?

Six engineering teams use an analysis assistant. Existing review makes its routine outputs usable. A new assignment requires changing a model assumption and explaining the resulting construction; only two shared specialists can currently supply that contribution. Should the practice developer promote the assistant to more teams? The example stipulates the facts below.

**Recover what the next work needs.** [FPF C.36.RP](../../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/132-c-36-rp---sustain-and-renew-shared-ways-of-working.md) separates obtaining a checked answer from being able to alter its method. The specialists are a valid current supplier; the teams need not all become specialists. Determine who must recognize an unsuitable answer, who must reconstruct the changed model and which contributions can be obtained elsewhere. There is no evidence here that the teams previously had and then lost this ability. If a retention or tool-dependence claim would change the decision, use [HCD.13](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/30-hcd-13-test-human-capability-retention-and-dependence-on-too.md) with its required comparison. A new demand alone does not establish decline.

**Follow uptake into the next contribution.** A demonstration, an attempted procedure, a checked result and an ability to help another team are different events. [DTM.2](../../dtm/10-dtm-2---build-a-transmission-law-from-exposure-and-uptake.md) recovers what the recipient obtains, including a changed construction; [DTM.3](../../dtm/11-dtm-3---couple-change-within-carriers-to-spread-between-them.md) connects that change to subsequent transmission. Use a quantitative model only if its result can change the choice. Counting demonstrations would miss both the receiving work and the demand on specialists.

**Protect the work that supplies the next method.** The two specialists can jointly provide nine hours of specialist work per week for this service, after their other commitments. Current work consumes seven of those hours. Expansion is estimated to need two additional hours of receiving support and two of method development: that would bring weekly demand to eleven hours, two more than are available. Revisit the comparison if either estimate changes.

[ECO.7](../../eco/17-eco-7---coordinate-use-and-renewal-of-a-shared-resource.md) distinguishes freely copying a description from consuming a constrained service. [OPS.19](../../management-dpf-suite/ops/53-ops-19---reconcile-simultaneous-operating-work-across-cases.md) helps compare the work competing for those hours while preserving commitments. The service owner can retain the current arrangement, obtain qualified help, change support or defer expansion.

Suppose no additional provider has been secured. One specialist can provide the remaining two hours for a reconstruction probe, including preparation and review; a receiving participant also has time to take part. The question is whether the existing explanation supports a consequential change of assumption or leaves a missing operation. Its answer will inform the choice between improving that explanation, developing a different learning arrangement and continuing to obtain specialist help. The owner judges that this bounded answer warrants the time of both participants and defers expansion. If the answer would not change that choice enough to justify the work, retaining the supported service without the probe would be sufficient.

**Keep an introduction attempt separate from its result.** [FPF A.3.1.MI](../../../sections/05-part-a---kernel-architecture-cluster/28-a-3-1-mi---introduce-a-method-into-practice-and-decide-what.md) keeps the target method, explanation, support and way of introducing the change distinct. Record what the participant can reconstruct and use, with the assistance provided. Use [HCD.16](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/33-hcd-16-keep-human-capability-development-method-claims-curre.md) for any conclusion about the human-development method. The two hours are an allocation for the probe. Mastery and another team’s later ability to do the work each require evidence of performance under the conditions claimed.

If the probe yields a useful construction, retain the assumption, operations, explanation and conditions needed to vary it again. C.36.RP returns the next consequential variation to the appropriate development method.

Reading the model, interpreting its quantities and judging its use can be constituent methods enacted together in one review. Teaching another reviewer, maintaining the assistant and operating the shared service have their own work and support relations. A method vertical, a provider arrangement and a population of users need not have the same structure.


### Engineering.Reference:17.1 - Obtain an explanation and another teacher's contribution

A specialist can make today's answer usable by supplying a relation absent from the shared material. If later teams need that help, the practice developer must obtain it for their use: through continued specialist provision, a revised explanation with teaching support, or another workable arrangement. Developing teaching is worthwhile only when that contribution matters to the next work.

**Pass the grounds with the answer.** Keep the task, the participant’s response and reasoning, and the help supplied. When corrective help will follow, preserve any first response before giving it. Use an available subject explanation that establishes the decisive operation or relation, why it works and when it applies. If a consequential connection is missing, obtain it from the specialist or another qualified source where possible. Identify any preparation or contribution from another practice on which the answer depends. When the receiving work still needs that contribution, retain it in the explanation or arrange its supply. If a subject criterion remains open, state which conclusions depend on it. When the task needs that criterion settled, seek a determination from the relevant professional or research practice.

**Choose how to provide the explanation.** [HCD.19](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/36-hcd-19---evaluate-instructional-material-through-representat.md) helps distinguish a defect in the material from missing preparation, unavailable help or disputed subject grounds. For an assessment of the material, use a compatible [evaluation specification](03-engineering-reference-1---find-a-starting-pattern.md#engineeringreference113---evaluate-instructional-material-for-its-intended-use) that specifies the task, criterion and allowed help. A correct answer can reflect prior knowledge or a shortcut that happens to work on familiar cases. If revision is warranted and chosen, use the subject account to repair the explanation and the examples, tasks or teacher guidance that depend on it. Access to an adequate explanation or continuing expert help can also meet the need.

**Make the contrast distinguish the reasoning.** Consider the module-release and laboratory-window case in [HCD.8:5.1](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/25-hcd-8---obtain-or-build-missing-human-capability-development.md). The subject specialist establishes whether a test report covers the intended configuration; the workload must also fit the available interval. Suppose familiar examples show a complete ten-change report that permits continuation and an incomplete twelve-change report that does not. Both “up to ten changes is acceptable” and “an applicable report is sufficient” reproduce those verdicts. The author therefore includes these stipulated contrasts:

| Case | Grounds for the next recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Ten changes need 23 hours within 24, but the report omits the relevant firmware. | The work fits; obtain the missing applicability basis before relying on the report. |
| A complete compatible twelve-change report accompanies 27 hours of work and a 24-hour interval. | The report applies, but the work does not fit; revise the work or obtain a feasible interval. |
| The same complete report and 27-hour workload accompany a 28-hour interval. | The capacity objection is removed; proceed to the authorized acceptance decision under the remaining conditions. |

The second and third cases hold the report and work fixed and change the available interval. The first case shows why a small workload alone cannot establish report applicability. In a teaching task, ask for the recommendation and its grounds. A previous response may already establish what the learner did with the stated help. If several explanations of that response remain possible, report the ambiguity. For a stronger diagnosis, use an existing distinguishing case or, when justified, refine the question and examine a further response. Even a correct verdict can rest on unexpected reasoning. Target feedback to a demonstrated error. A claim of a recurring difficulty needs evidence across cases.

**Try the teaching contribution in use.** Through [HCD.8](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/25-hcd-8---obtain-or-build-missing-human-capability-development.md), compare available provision with building the missing teaching service. The result must reach each team in time and give the learner feedback they can use on the next attempt. A service can deliver that feedback where its inputs and criteria distinguish the learner’s reasoning. Arrange qualified help for cases it cannot resolve. If preparing another teacher is selected, give that person the suitable material, permitted subject help and representative learner responses. Let the teacher diagnose those responses before revealing the assessor’s proposed diagnosis. In this release-planning case, require the teacher to justify different recommendations for the complete report at 24 and 28 hours and connect the learner's grounds to useful feedback.

For example, a learner accepts both proposals because the report applies. Useful feedback retains that valid applicability judgement, explains why 27 hours of work cannot fit into 24, and asks for a feasible revision. Have a qualified assessor examine that connection and observe the feedback delivered and used in a representative recipient's next attempt within the promised service conditions. A correct mark without the explanation and usable correction leaves the teaching contribution unestablished. Where the grounds remain ambiguous, a justified clarification or better contrast is an appropriate return.

For the six teams, use the grounds and the consequential contrast for their own change of model assumption. If the chosen arrangement includes the proposed teaching contribution, test its use in that work when the available evidence leaves its suitability open. Specialist assistance can remain part of the arrangement. To establish independent performance when it is required, use evidence of the task under the corresponding conditions.

**Provide for the added work and its continuation.** In the six-team case, the two remaining specialist hours were allocated to the reconstruction probe, including its preparation and review. Those hours do not also establish provision for author revision, teacher preparation, feedback and later maintenance. Obtain a feasible allocation or another qualified contributor, reduce the offer, or retain the supported service while deferring expansion. One willing and capable person can perform several contributions; using the shared practice does not commit every participant to authorship or teaching.

When this work changes a shared repertoire, [CB.14](../../management-dpf-suite/cb/24-cb-14---develop-and-maintain-a-shared-repertoire-of-practice.md) connects the returned explanation to its receiving use and agreement about correction and maintenance. Retain the required expert help until the replacement can supply its contribution. A later case that defeats the subject explanation returns to method or research work; loss of an otherwise adequate teacher or source reopens provision.


### Engineering.Reference:17.2 - When the conditions change

| Changed condition | Consequence for the next move |
| --- | --- |
| Another accessible provider supplies the required construction. | Use the supplied construction if it fits the team's work. If a provider can supply it but access or allocation is missing, arrange that condition. Keep any judgement or learning entrusted to the team. |
| Teams can reconstruct the method but the proposed tool's adoption cost exceeds its useful contribution. | Keeping their current practice is a valid result. Rejection alone is not proof of resistance or incapability. |
| Recognition rewards polished outputs while errors in unfamiliar cases remain hidden. | Use [DTM.7](../../dtm/15-dtm-7---locate-diversion-of-a-shared-control-or-cooperation.md) to trace how the visible output earns further use and whether that support sustains the mismatch. A.3.1.MI introduces a justified change. More instruction alone may leave the reward rule unchanged. |
| The new procedure succeeds only in a course or demonstration. | Compare the receiving task and support with the course conditions. In music and dance, [MDPE.21](../../mdpe/21-mdpe-21---design-how-a-music-or-dance-style-is-produced-and.md) and [MDPE.22](../../mdpe/40-mdpe-22---test-a-support-environment-change-for-a-music-or-d.md) address the performance and environment relations; their musical and bodily conditions remain specific to that practice. |

The same distinction matters for a shared technical reference: its descriptions can be copied without exhausting them, while question answering and revision work can exhaust the maintainers' time. Arrange who will answer questions and supply the revisions users need, and reserve time for that work. A support agreement is one result; continuing production of useful methods is another.

Finish with the supported continuation or change, the contributions it needs and the uncertainty it leaves. A local successful attempt establishes neither wider uptake nor a lasting cultural improvement. Obtain observations of the wider uptake or lasting change when the receiving decision depends on it. Otherwise, use the supported local result.
