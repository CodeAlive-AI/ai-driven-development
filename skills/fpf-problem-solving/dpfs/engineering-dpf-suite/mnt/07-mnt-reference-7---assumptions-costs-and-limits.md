## MNT.Reference:7 - Assumptions, costs and limits

The principal examples concern industrial equipment and draw on aviation for specific protection and human-performance questions. They do not establish a universal transfer to software services, nuclear facilities or every other maintenance setting. An application needs the equipment's failure and operating basis and the protection rules that apply to it now.

The language favours explicit supported claims. That can reveal an uncertainty that an informal plan concealed. It can also save investigation or paperwork when a smaller answer is sufficient. The intended gains are better maintenance reasoning and fewer unsupported conclusions about what can follow. Claims about effects on failures, costs or exposure need evidence from practice.

All application names, observations, quantities and outcomes in this edition are constructed. They illustrate how to reason from the stated conditions. A measured-effect claim requires its own population, comparator, observation and causal basis.
