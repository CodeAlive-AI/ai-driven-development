## SYSE.Reference:1 - Choose a useful result and combine its needed contributions

Use SYSE when local engineering answers leave an important connection unresolved: a component works but the
intended use is unsupported, an architecture is attractive but its realization is missing, or a test result
does not identify the configuration and decision it can support. Seek the result that changes the next
engineering move. Depending on the question, obtain a selected alternative, a bounded usable increment,
a justified reliance decision, a discriminating trial, or a missing contribution.

The patterns describe a repertoire of Methods. A project can use one Method directly or combine several.
Start with the System, receiving use and decision that matter now. Reuse adequate concepts, configurations,
specialist conclusions and evidence. Follow a further pattern when its result is needed, and return the result
to the engineering decision that consumes it. For example, an obtaining comparison in `SYSE.24` can expose a
missing provider commitment for `SYSE.18`; an actual integration result from `SYSE.11` can support, but does
not make, the release decision in `SYSE.14`. The following sections explain these connections.

A provisional project focus can support individual concept work. Before joint work relies on a common focus,
`SYSE.1` makes participants' referents, interests and contribution connections visible through a
concrete use. The result may be sufficient common understanding for the current decision, an unresolved
coordination condition, or distinct projects with a defined receiving contribution.

The product SoI and the immediate engineering subject can differ. A factory, planning organization or shared laboratory can be designed in full as a creator while its products and their uses ground the work. Use `SYSE.1` when this relation is unclear; carry the local subject, required capability and bounded acceptance into the relevant engineering Methods. Several products can share the creator without becoming one product or one project. Local acceptance can finish before their later use, with the remaining assumptions and commitments identified.

The choice of Methods must accommodate useful effects, cost and time, available evidence, and consequences for
other Systems. Preserving an option can be worth more than completing a design early; a faster local operation
can transfer delay or risk to integration, maintenance or a user. Compare the complete arrangements that would
produce the required result, including the work and support each retains. Use `SYSE.20` when shared resources,
conflicting conditions or actual dependencies determine what work can overlap.

If a current specialist Method or available result already answers the whole question, use it directly.
Common Systems Engineering becomes useful when the question crosses those local answers or changes their
receiving use. Stop when the bounded answer is sufficient; a missing premise holds only the conclusion or
action that depends on it.

### SYSE.Reference:1.1 - The system-thinking long mantra in engineering

The **system-thinking long mantra** first looks toward intended receiving use at the operating time. Recover the product systems-of-interest and their intended uses. From each proposed system boundary, examine the larger whole and how its parts function together. Derive the contribution required from the product system before looking inward to construction and backward to obtaining Methods, capable creators and their cooperation. An unknown whole or composition calls for inquiry; leave the dependent function or construction judgement open until it has sufficient grounds. When engineering an organization as a creator, keep those product grounds and examine the creator's own use, functions, construction and obtaining needs. A separate organizational project has its own contribution and acceptance; its name or contract does not create another SoI. Make the answers fit and follow results into use. A proposed future use gives a reason for inquiry; its mechanism and conditions need engineering grounds. The general construction and its reasons are developed in FPF A.1.STM:4.1–4.3.1. Use that pattern when the shared project account is itself unresolved. Here the connections explain how the engineering Methods form a language rather than a sequence of independent tasks.

The needed effect gives a reason to investigate particular functioning, but it does not determine a unique construction. A proposed construction permits some behaviour and requires particular ways of realization. SYSE.2 and SYSE.5 therefore develop related alternatives; SYSE.6 compares whole candidates under the conditions that matter. A proposal cannot borrow performance from one configuration and realization cost from another. SYSE.7 keeps the related engineering descriptions usable together. SYSE.8 develops the offering and provider concept alongside the technical concept, so the promise to a user and the ways of fulfilling it constrain the same proposed result.

Obtaining the chosen system also depends on its creators. SYSE.3 connects recursive realization; SYSE.12 develops the needed engineering environment; SYSE.18 obtains commitments across independently governed contributors. Individual capability does not establish compatible contributions, access to a shared facility or permission to use it. SYSE.20 examines the actual conditions for overlapping work. If a needed capability or commitment is missing, the design can change, another provider can be obtained, or the promised result can be reconsidered.

Configuration and evidence keep this reasoning about the same proposed or actual system. SYSE.9 and SYSE.10 connect inquiry and qualification to the claim at issue; SYSE.13 preserves the configuration distinctions on which the claims depend. SYSE.11 and SYSE.14 distinguish obtaining an increment from authorizing its use, while SYSE.19 traces a changed source or description into the engineering decisions that relied on it. SYSE.15 revises the repertoire of engineering Methods when that is the contribution in question. The following Reference sections develop these relations and their limits.

In actual work, a test result, an available component or a missing production capability can be the first thing known. The mantra can also expose an unanswered question before any difficulty is felt. A.1.STM:4.0 connects these entries: recover or reuse the grounds in intended use and wider functioning, then investigate the consequential construction or creation dependency. New evidence can reopen those grounds, and alternatives may develop together. A team needs enough compatible answers for the decision now being made, not a completed answer to every possible project question. Keep supported local results while revising the premises that no longer hold.
