## RMP.8 - Use Research Results to Retain or Revise an Episteme's Claims and Return Bounded Evidence

> **Type:** Research Method Practice practitioner method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### RMP.8:1 - Problem Frame

Use this pattern when research analysis, criticism, a credibility result or a synthesis changes what a knowledge account may assert, or when its recipient needs a bounded evidence return. Begin by locating **the exact claim, model assumption or explanatory relation that the supplied result can retain or change**.

A new laboratory synthesis can support a seal comparison while leaving its field-transfer premise unresolved. A dated archive record can defeat an attribution about a decision without explaining its cause or later implementation. A correction becomes useful when the earlier statement, new contribution and receiving use concern the same identified subject and conditions.

The result is an **EpistemeChangeAndEvidenceReturn**: the retained or changed knowledge contribution, its basis and limits, and the return needed by its actual use. A corrected sentence and short linked explanation can carry this result. A model with several dependent claims or a correction already relied on elsewhere may need a fuller account.

An episteme here is the identified knowledge account being revised: for example a claim, model, interpretation or pattern describing a Method and its use. RMP.8 concerns the asserted content of that account. A changed filename or publication label does not identify what its recipient should now believe or rely on.

Enter directly with a compatible supplied result from analysis, synthesis or criticism. An already sufficient RMP.5, RMP.6 or RMP.7 answer may finish at its own return. Use RMP.8 when a broader account or consequential receiving reliance needs reconciliation. The receiving practice supplies its decision criteria and authority; the same person can perform both contributions without merging them.

### RMP.8:2 - Problem

Research conclusions often change in transmission. A limited finding becomes a general recommendation; a criticism of one assumption is read as rejection of the whole model; a correction about one event silently replaces a claim about another. Conversely, an amended source can leave a known receiving use dependent on the earlier claim.

Another failure is to demand a new investigation whenever uncertainty remains. That postpones useful qualified answers and treats a proposed examination as evidence already obtained. A lack of feasible inquiry does not make the current account more credible.

The practitioner needs to identify the warranted change, carry it through the relevant account and return it with the limits that can change reliance.

### RMP.8:3 - Forces

| Force | Tension |
| --- | --- |
| Exact correction | A concise conclusion is useful, while a small change of subject, context or time can alter what was established. |
| Coherent models | A failed premise can affect several outputs, while unaffected claims should remain usable. |
| Receiving use | Evidence can change a practical choice, while the choice also depends on values, constraints and authority outside the research result. |
| Recoverability | Existing reliance may need the earlier statement and its correction, while every draft edit does not need a full change dossier. |
| Disclosure | A recipient needs enough basis to judge reliance, while source rights, confidentiality and community authority constrain access. |
| Presentation and content | Different publication forms can serve different readers, while a concise or familiar template can conceal a missing problem, rationale, operation or applicability condition. |
| Further inquiry | A valuable unanswered question may justify work, while useful completion and unobtainable acquisition remain possible. |

### RMP.8:4 - Solution

Match the supplied contribution to the earlier assertion, revise only what it warrants, reconcile the affected account and return the current answer to its actual use. Preserve the support, uncertainty and limits that matter to that return.

#### RMP.8:4.1 - Locate the assertion and its receiving question

Recover the earlier wording or model relation, its referents, comparison, context and time. State what result has arrived, what examination actually produced it, and which part of the account it bears on.

Ask whether the two sides concern the same claim. A factory's decision to close and its later closure are different events. A laboratory parameter and a field-use parameter can denote different conditions. A new finding about another population can be useful without contradicting the original bounded claim.

For an emerging account, identify the proposed contribution and what existing material it would add to or qualify. If there is no earlier claim to correct, state an addition rather than inventing a prior error.

Recover the receiving question when it changes how much explanation or assurance is needed. The question may be a scientific explanation, a reusable knowledge contribution or an engineering, public or personal use. Immediate operating benefit is not the only legitimate value of research.

If the source, subject correspondence or actual result is missing, return that specific gap. A selected credibility examination and a proposal for synthesis cannot serve as their performed results.

#### RMP.8:4.2 - State the warranted retention or change

Separate the contribution from a stronger claim the receiver might infer.

| Warranted disposition | What to state |
| --- | --- |
| Retain | The existing assertion still has its stated support under the examined conditions; identify the relevant new examination and its limits. |
| Narrow | Restrict the population, context, time, relation or transfer to what the result supports, preserving the supported part. |
| Qualify | Keep the identified assertion with the uncertainty, alternative explanation or reliance condition the result makes consequential. |
| Reject | Remove a defeated claim or inference and explain what defeats it. Rejection of one account does not establish a particular rival by default. |
| Add | State the new contribution and its relation to the existing account, including whether it is supported information, an interpretation or a hypothesis for further inquiry. |

Several dispositions may be needed for different claims in one model. Keep their distinct reasons. An increase in confidence needs an actual supporting contribution; repetition of the assertion, a newer document date and unavailable criticism supply none by themselves.

Retain method-specific uncertainty. A numerical interval depends on its quantity and assumptions. A qualitative confidence judgment has its own grounds. “Uncertain” is too vague when the recipient needs to know that the unsupported step is causal attribution, extrapolation, prevalence or another particular inference.

An inconclusive result can leave the proposed effect unresolved. It does not automatically establish no effect or equivalence. Similarly, lack of access can limit what was examined without supplying evidence against the underlying claim.

#### RMP.8:4.3 - Reconcile the affected account

Follow the changed premise through the claims or outputs that actually rely on it. Identify which conclusions survive, which must change and which now need another supplied relation.

For a model, distinguish a represented target, structure, parameter, assumption, calculation and interpretation when the change bears on them differently. Correctly computed output can lose its warrant for a use when a transfer assumption fails. A locally supported parameter may remain useful within its calibration conditions.

Make consequential changes in the account itself, rather than leaving an unqualified conclusion beside a detached caveat. Preserve the earlier statement when an existing citation, correction or reliance requires recovery. For a draft that nobody has relied on, an ordinary edit with enough source connection can suffice.

A replacement explanation is a new assertion with its own support. If criticism only defeats the former explanation, retain the remaining alternatives without selecting one by elimination that the evidence does not warrant. Return a new research question or design choice through RMP.1 or RMP.2 as needed.

Use the existing records and source links that make this reasoning inspectable. RMP adds no compulsory change ledger for every account.

#### RMP.8:4.4 - Return evidence to the actual use

Lead with the current answer and what it changes for the named receiving question. Give the source or examination that supports it and the scope, uncertainty, dependence, transfer or access limit that can alter reliance.

When someone has already relied on the affected assertion, identify that actual use and provide the correction or limitation through its responsible owner and permitted route. FPF A.10.1 governs the general recovery and revalidation of affected uses; this Method supplies the research content to carry through that relation. A citation list may help locate reliance but does not prove that every citation used the changed claim.

The author of one account cannot silently replace another owner's record. A museum editor, model maintainer or receiving practitioner may need the corrected statement and its basis before changing their own account or decision. A known consequential return is not complete merely because the source text was edited. If required communication or access is unavailable, state the unfinished return and what would enable it while retaining the completed local correction.

Respect confidentiality, source rights, participant protection and applicable community authority. Use the least disclosure that supports the receiving question. A permitted redacted account may carry the conclusion; if it omits material necessary for a requested examination, state that examination limit. Do not imply that a public reader inspected protected originals.

The receiving practice then evaluates its available actions, values, constraints and authority. Research can narrow an action's evidential premise without selecting or authorizing that action.



##### RMP.8:4.4.1 - Make a research Method contribution recoverable across publication forms

Use this branch when the contribution includes a Method that another reader should understand, repeat, adapt or combine. First identify the knowledge contribution and the use to be supported; then choose how to present it.

A practitioner Method pattern is an episteme about a Method and its use. Its content includes the recurring difficulty, the situation in which that difficulty matters, competing forces, the solution and its grounds, worked applications, limits, consequences and relations. FPF's SoTA-Echoing adds the comparison with the current best-known line and serious alternatives that can change the Method. These are substantive claims and explanations, not decorations around an operation list. Apply A.3.2 when claiming that an episteme is a U.MethodDescription; filling a pattern template does not establish that membership.

Keep that content distinct from its publication form: the selected section template, natural language, notation, reading order, file arrangement, medium and place of publication. Translation or moving text between files may preserve the contribution; omitting a force that changes the choice of solution may not. A short entry can direct a reader to the developed account, but cannot supply an explanation that the account never contains.

An article can present several connected claims and Methods, their construction, evidence and limits. Its contributions may be developed into one pattern, several related patterns, an amendment of an existing language, or another suitable knowledge product. A pattern language organizes substantive problem-solution relations; a DPF has its own field of practice, promised coverage and maintenance boundary. Neither is merely an article layout or a collection of files. Do not infer one article–one pattern or one article–one DPF. E.4.DPF selects the framework contribution when that architectural choice matters.

For a pattern contribution, check the recurring problem–conditions–remedy relation as well as whether a suitably prepared recipient can reproduce its use. State what makes another situation an instance of that problem, which parts of the way of acting recur, which adaptations are permitted, what preparation and support are needed, and what result would count as successful use. Applying the Method again may produce a different artifact or numerical value while preserving its intended effect under the stated conditions. Recomputing one reported number alone does not establish this reusable reach. A correctly recognized limit is a useful boundary result; it is not a successful reproduction of the promised positive effect.

For a Method contribution, make the following connections usable:

1. **Recover the construction.** Explain the problem and frame, the objects and relations through which it is understood, the relevant forces and the resulting way of acting. Conceptual synthesis can combine, adapt or reinterpret already known Methods. Show both the horizontal organization—sequence, alternatives, feedback and returns—and the vertical relation between constituent actions and the larger Method where they change use. E.4.CM develops this composition; a bibliography of constituents does not establish their compatibility or supply missing joins.
2. **State what another reader is to repeat.** Reconstructing a derivation, rerunning a computation on the same inputs, obtaining new measurements and applying the Method under changed conditions test different claims. Say what is held comparable, what changes, the result to compare and what agreement means. Exact equality may be warranted for a derivation or deterministic calculation; new measurements usually require a justified uncertainty or agreement criterion. Use RMP.6 for the relevant credibility decision and for interpretation of an actual return. The word “reproduction” alone does not choose the examination.
3. **Provide the consequential means.** Supply or locate the definitions, assumptions, input preparation, reasoning, operations, data or material, comparison and interpretation needed for that examination. Specify versions, computational environment, randomization, calibration, timing or performer capability when a difference there can change the claim. For a reused Method, identify the usable source passage and the conditions supplied by it. A source title is insufficient when the reader still has to invent a missing operation. RMP.4 governs the actual trace; it cannot recover conduct for which no evidence survives.
4. **Choose and connect the presentations.** An empirical article may separate question, Methods, results and interpretation; a theoretical or Methods article may center a construction and its argument; a protocol or Stage 1 Registered Report describes proposed work, while a later results report states what was actually done and obtained. Use the applicable venue and study-type reporting requirements. A pattern presentation organizes the same supported contribution around recurring use, including problem frame, forces, solution, cases, boundaries and SoTA. Preserve the reasoning and evidence needed for that use even when section names and order change.
5. **Expose what is available and what remains untested.** Connect each presentation to the identifiable source contribution and the materials needed for its stated examination. State permitted access and consequential omissions. A protocol is not performed Work, an available artifact is not a successful rerun, and a repeatable calculation alone does not establish that its model applies under changed conditions. Correct affected presentations and known receiving uses through 4.3–4.4 when their relied-on claims change.

Use the smallest explanation and access arrangement that supports the receiving use. A theoretical contribution need not invent an empirical dataset; protected participant material need not be made public contrary to its conditions of use. Conversely, a disclosure restriction can leave a particular public examination unavailable. State that limit rather than presenting the publication format as evidence of reproducibility.

#### RMP.8:4.5 - Finish the answer and select further inquiry only when warranted

Finish when the identified account has the warranted retention or change and the return required by its actual use is supplied, or when a precise input or delivery limit has been returned. A broader unresolved question does not automatically hold this bounded result open.

If further inquiry could materially improve an explanation, reusable finding or receiving decision, compare the attainable contribution with its whole burden. Include preparation, access, performance, interpretation, protection, shared resources and displaced work. An attractive experiment that cannot be obtained supplies no new evidence.

Use RMP.2 for a research-design choice and RMP.6 for a relevant credibility choice. A still-needed selection prevented by a missing input differs from a completed no-new-study allocation. Preserve that distinction and the current evidence limit. A worthwhile new possibility can later reopen the choice.

A recurring failure in how research Methods are selected or performed may identify an RMP.9 or Core Method-development question. State that need and any available practice able to address it; naming the need neither supplies a new Method-improvement result nor prevents the present knowledge contribution from being returned.

### RMP.8:5 - Archetypal Grounding

These constructed cases illustrate the Method rather than reporting actual research or completed communication.

#### RMP.8:5.1 - Keep the laboratory result, withdraw the unsupported field prediction

An engineer receives a synthesis supporting a positive mean difference in seal lifetime under specified accelerated laboratory conditions. The existing model account contains three contributions:

| Existing contribution | Supplied criticism and revision |
| --- | --- |
| The fitted wear relation describes the dry laboratory material over the tested range. | The supplied analysis still supports that bounded relation; retain its parameter, fit and uncertainty for that scope. |
| The same wear rate applies in the wet service environment. | The supplied synthesis concerns dry accelerated tests and does not establish this transfer; remove the account's claim that the research has demonstrated it. The transfer remains unresolved. |
| A service replacement time follows from the laboratory rate and the engineering wear limit. | The calculation may remain arithmetically correct, but its service interpretation relies on the unsupported transfer. Withdraw that field prediction as an established research conclusion. The supplied engineering limit remains the engineering owner's criterion. |

The return says: “The laboratory relation remains supported in its tested range. This research does not establish the wet-service wear rate or the replacement time derived from treating that rate as unchanged.” It includes the source result and the exact missing transfer premise.

A model with separate dry and wet rates is a possible next hypothesis, not a validated replacement supplied by this criticism. A useful and obtainable field comparison can be considered through RMP.2/RMP.6. The reliability owner receives the bounded evidence and retains the maintenance decision. A short correction to the model account can complete the research return even when the operational choice still needs more input.

#### RMP.8:5.2 - Correct the same historical event

Several later accounts claim that a public protest prompted the decision to close a factory. They share one retrospective memoir. An authenticated record dates that decision before the protest.

RMP.8 rejects the protest as the cause of that earlier decision, retains its supported timing and leaves the cause unresolved. Possible effects of the protest on later implementation remain a different question.

A museum description actually attributed the decision to the protest. Its responsible editor receives the corrected decision chronology, the source reference and the unresolved causal limit. The correction does not silently turn into a claim about why or when the physical closure occurred. Protected source detail stays within its access terms; if a public summary cannot support independent authentication, it says so.

If the same dated evidence were supplied against the broader claim “the protest affected the closure,” the return would instead distinguish the defeated decision-causation component from still-open implementation effects. Matching the event changes the warranted revision.

#### RMP.8:5.3 - Return a situated finding without inventing prevalence

A community synthesis links distrust in monitoring to withheld feedback in its examined settings. An earlier briefing says that this is the principal reason most local residents do not participate.

The supplied interpretive finding supports neither a population denominator nor the comparative frequency implied by “most” and “principal.” RMP.8 replaces the briefing's prevalence assertion with the situated contribution: withheld feedback contributed to distrust in the examined accounts, with the noted exceptions and contexts. The account distinguishes the source interpretations from the synthesist's broader explanation.

The community group can use that qualified insight when considering how to return monitoring information. The evidence does not establish how many residents would participate after a proposed change or authorize that change. A frequency survey is a separate research option if its attainable contribution warrants its burden.

#### RMP.8:5.4 - An adequate answer and a later correction

A requester receives an existing synthesis that already answers the specified comparison. A short linked answer with its applicability and uncertainty completes the return; there is no need to create a separate episteme-change form.

Later, an erratum reverses one result that materially supported the synthesis. Recover the affected finding and the known decision or account that relied on it. Recompute or reinterpret that finding through its supplying Method, revise its dependent claim and carry the correction to that actual use. Retain unaffected results. A newly observed date alone would not justify the same reopening.

#### RMP.8:5.5 - Valuable uncertainty without an obtainable study

A historical synthesis resolves a chronology but leaves a scientifically valuable mechanism unexplained. The only currently proposed inquiry requires records that cannot lawfully be accessed, and no worthwhile obtainable substitute is supplied.

The historian returns the supported chronology and unresolved mechanism and selects no further study for the current allocation. The explanation remains valuable and incomplete. If an accessible archive later supplies material capable of discriminating the live alternatives, its contribution and burden can justify a new research choice. An immediate commercial payoff is not a prerequisite for the present answer.



#### RMP.8:5.6 - One calibration contribution, an article and a pattern

Consider a constructed contribution using the instrument relation y = a + bx, where x is the reference temperature and y the displayed temperature, both in degrees Celsius. Assume a separate instrument characterization supports an affine relation over 0–20 °C under the named operating conditions. The contribution does not establish that premise by fitting two points.

The reported reference pairs are (0, 2) and (20, 24). The reasoning gives b = (24 − 2)/(20 − 0) = 1.1 and a = 2; the correction is x = (y − 2)/1.1. A displayed 13 °C therefore gives 10 °C. A reader can reconstruct that derivation and recompute that result from the stated pairs. This repeats the calculation, not the original observations.

An article can present the instrument problem, the characterization it relies on, the derivation, actual observation procedure and data, and the scope of the conclusion. A pattern for correcting readings would explain when offset and scale errors defeat use, when the affine frame is justified, the tension between a simple correction and an unmodeled nonlinear response, how to obtain qualified references and apply the correction, and what to do when an independent comparison disagrees. Its cases, consequences, relations and SoTA comparison remain content. Retitling the equation “Solution” does not supply those missing explanations.

Now suppose the examination uses a newly obtained 10 °C reference and the instrument displays 13.2 °C. The corrected value is 10.1818… °C. Under a previously selected total agreement allowance of ±0.3 °C, with reference uncertainty included in that allowance, this observation agrees. That finding concerns this comparison; it does not establish every reading in the interval. RMP.6 retains the examination's actual scope.

This distinction also makes the pattern's repeatability testable. The same correction rule applies to another qualified reading; its output changes with that input. Recomputing 10 °C from the original display of 13 °C repeats the reported calculation. Applying the rule to the new display of 13.2 °C gives 10.1818… °C; copying the earlier answer would fail to repeat the Method. Recognition of the qualified affine frame and access to its calibration basis remain necessary on each use.

The pattern may be published in English and Russian, in separate files or in a single framework publication. Those choices need not change the calculation or its qualified frame. A shortened version that removes the 0–20 °C condition does change what a reader may infer. A proposed 40 °C application needs a new applicability basis; agreement at 10 °C cannot supply it. If a later investigation defeats the affine premise, RMP.8 preserves the arithmetic but revises the calibration claim in the article correction, affected pattern account and known receiving uses.

### RMP.8:6 - Bias-Annotation

A short correction is easiest to demonstrate in an account with one identifiable recipient. Distributed reliance, conflicting editions and protected sources can require more recovery and communication. Scale that work to the actual consequence instead of assuming that every research result needs a universal recipient register.

The engineering examples make decision support visible. Basic explanation, interpretation and reusable knowledge also have value. Preserve their legitimate questions when comparing future inquiry.

### RMP.8:7 - Conformance Checklist

Use these questions to examine the revised account and return:

- Do the earlier assertion, supplied contribution and claimed correction concern the same identified subject, context, comparison and time, or explicitly distinguished ones?
- Is each retained, narrowed, qualified, rejected or added contribution supported to the extent stated?
- Have affected model outputs or explanations been reconciled while unaffected contributions remain usable?
- Can the recipient recover the current answer, its basis, uncertainty and the limitation that changes reliance?
- Has a known consequential receiving use received the permitted correction, or is the exact unfinished return visible?
- Are obtained evidence, proposed inquiry and the receiver's decision authority distinct?
- When a Method contribution is returned for repetition or adaptation, can its recipient recover the substantive problem-solution account, the relevant examination and its means, independently of the chosen publication layout and language?

The working situation selects this Method. These questions examine the warranted content and actual return; recognizing a need for revision proves neither.

### RMP.8:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Failure | What changes in practice |
| --- | --- |
| A new label substitutes for a changed assertion. | Locate and amend the actual claim, assumption or explanatory relation. |
| An article is converted into pattern headings and declared reproducible. | Preserve the substantive problem, frame, forces, construction, grounds and limits; provide the means for the named repetition and distinguish its actual result from mere availability. |
| A decision date is used as proof about physical closure. | Match the event or distinguish the decision from its later implementation before drawing the causal conclusion. |
| One failed premise erases the entire model. | Follow its actual dependencies, retaining supported parameters, observations and calculations within their remaining scope. |
| An unqualified conclusion survives beside a caveat. | Make the conclusion itself carry the warranted scope and reconcile the dependent outputs. |
| A local edit is assumed to correct known reliance. | Return the change through the actual receiver and authority, with an unresolved delivery limit when necessary. |
| Uncertainty automatically commissions research. | Compare the attainable contribution and whole burden; finish with the current limit when no further inquiry is selected. |

### RMP.8:9 - Consequences

The practitioner can preserve a useful research contribution while changing only what the evidence warrants. The recipient can distinguish a supported result from a stronger extrapolation, a defeated explanation and a future hypothesis.

The cost depends on the account's dependencies and actual reliance. A short current answer may suffice; an established model or consequential published correction can require recovery of prior meaning and a responsible return.

### RMP.8:10 - Rationale

A research result and its use are connected by particular assertions, assumptions and interpretations. Revising those connections prevents both overclaim and indiscriminate withdrawal.

Keeping the research contribution separate from receiving authority permits a useful answer even when the practical choice is unfinished. Keeping future inquiry separate from obtained evidence permits honest completion without denying the value of a longer research programme.

### RMP.8:11 - SoTA-Echoing

**What can this evidence change for its receiver?** The selected line returns a claim with the applicability and uncertainty that govern its use. The serious default is to turn a positive research result directly into a recommendation, or an inconclusive result into no effect. Cochrane [chapter 15](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15) supplies a best-known-line candidate because its interpretation distinctions expose both unsupported moves. **Adapt:** 4.2 and 4.4 carry the warranted assertion and its remaining inference limit while the receiving practice supplies further judgments. A short qualified answer retains the useful research result at lower burden than an unnecessary new study, but a consequential transfer limit requires explanation. A changed target, uncertainty, actual result or receiving use reopens that interpretation. Intervention-review guidance does not decide every domain's action.

**How should historical correction preserve its claim?** The selected line keeps evidence and interpretation connected to the same event and preserves contrary material and legitimate confidentiality. The attractive default is to update the broad narrative once a compelling dated source appears. That can substitute a decision for its implementation or treat a corrected date as a discovered cause. **Adapt:** 4.1–4.4 and 5.2 distinguish those events and return only the warranted correction. AHA [professional standards](https://www.historians.org/resource/statement-on-standards-of-professional-conduct/) supplies the best-known-line candidate for source integrity and evidence/interpretation discipline; the worked event comparison applies those principles rather than receiving a verdict from the standard. Recovering the event costs a small comparison and avoids a different unsupported historical claim. New dating, provenance or a changed asserted event reopens the inference.

**Which account and reliance should change?** The selected line identifies the affected claim or model relation, follows its actual dependencies and carries a consequential correction to the use that relied on it. Two plausible defaults are withdrawing the whole model after one failed premise or editing only the local source. They can respectively destroy supported knowledge or leave a receiving account dependent on the defeated claim. FPF C.2.1, A.10 and A.10.1 supply the governing episteme, evidence-use and affected-use distinctions and are the best-known-line candidates for this comparison. **Adapt:** 4.1, 4.3, 4.4 and 5.1 apply them to the research contribution, preserving the laboratory relation while correcting its unsupported field interpretation. The trade-off is dependency and receiving-use recovery proportional to actual reliance. This does not supply a universal registry or replace the generic Methods. Changed relied-on content or a newly identified consequential use reopens the relevant return.

**When is further inquiry worth obtaining?** The selected line compares attainable epistemic or practical contribution with whole burden and permits a bounded completed answer while uncertainty remains. The default of studying every open question can select unobtainable work; a rule requiring immediate operating payoff would discard valuable explanation. FPF C.11.DUA supplies the best-known-line candidate for this contribution/burden distinction, with RMP.2/RMP.6 supplying the actual research choice. **Adapt:** 4.5 and 5.5 preserve scientific value, shared resources, access and protection while keeping insufficient evidence distinct from no selected acquisition. This comparison costs judgment but avoids treating either resources or a proposal as evidence. Reopen when a worthwhile contribution becomes obtainable or an input changes a selection still needed.

**How can a reader repeat a published Method contribution?** The selected line couples a usable account of the Method to the particular examination and its obtainable materials. The tempting alternatives are to treat a paper's publication as sufficient for reproducibility, or to convert its steps into a pattern template while losing the problem, grounds and scope. Nature Portfolio's [reporting and availability policy](https://www.nature.com/ncomms/editorial-policies/reporting-standards) supplies a current publication-practice comparison: consequential design details and the access conditions for data, code, materials and protocols must be recoverable. The [Registered Reports description](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/registered-reports) supplies the specific distinction between assessment of proposed Methods and reporting obtained outcomes. **Adapt:** 4.4.1 and 5.6 connect those reporting concerns to RMP.4's actual trace, RMP.6's distinct credibility questions and FPF E.8's substantive pattern functions. The additional explanation serves the intended repetition or adaptation; it does not require every possible artifact for every inquiry. Neither reporting policy nor a complete template demonstrates a successful repetition. A missing consequential input, changed Method condition or failed actual comparison reopens the affected claim and presentation.

The external guidance contributes to bounded interpretation and research reporting; it does not empirically validate this cross-domain revision Method. A failed actual use or stronger methodological source can require a local repair.

### RMP.8:12 - Relations

- **RMP.5, RMP.6 and RMP.7** can supply compatible analysis, actual criticism or synthesis directly. Their sufficient short answers retain their own completion.
- **RMP.1 and RMP.2** receive a changed research question or design-selection need. **RMP.3 and RMP.4** retain the distinction between intended inquiry and actual material when it matters to the correction.
- **FPF C.2.1, A.10 and A.10.1** supply the underlying episteme, evidence-use and affected-use relations. **C.11.DUA** supplies the general useful-evidence and obtainable-inquiry comparison.
- **Receiving domain and practice Methods** supply action alternatives, values, safety, access, confidentiality and authority. A bounded research result informs those decisions.
- **RMP.9 and Core Method development** receive a distinct research-Method improvement difficulty through their actual available contributions.
- **FPF E.8** supplies the substantive functions of a pattern account and their publication conventions; **E.4.CM** supplies development of a composite Method contribution. **E.4.DPF** governs whether those contributions warrant a new or revised DPF; article layout alone cannot answer that question.
