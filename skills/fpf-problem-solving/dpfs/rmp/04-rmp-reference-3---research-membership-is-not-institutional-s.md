## RMP.Reference:3 - Research membership is not institutional status

A company, public body, university, archive, community, independent practitioner or citizen group can conduct research. Ask whether the knowledge question remains open under the relevant sources, whether the inquiry as a whole faces a non-routine task of establishing knowledge under genuine uncertainty, whether a critic can follow the evidence from question to result, and what bounded contribution can be communicated. A routine operation or a previously justified protocol can be part of such an inquiry. Affiliation, publication plans and tool choice do not settle membership.

When it matters who conducted the inquiry, follow the work that produced the finding. In the stream study, residents may choose the comparison and interpret the result. A laboratory commissioned to measure their samples can contribute that part of the investigation. It might instead supply records from earlier monitoring. Those records can support the residents’ inference. Crediting the laboratory with choosing the comparison would require evidence of that choice. [RMP.4](34-rmp-4---preserve-a-criticism-ready-research-trace.md) helps recover who did what when the answer depends on it.

The same operations can serve different tasks. A seal test can qualify a product or test the relation that makes acceleration informative. A fully specified replication can examine a disputed claim. In [RMP.1’s paired assay case](31-rmp-1-decide-whether-a-question-needs-research-and-when-to-s.md#rmp157---one-supplied-protocol-two-different-epistemic-tasks), an established procedure also supplies a routine lot verdict under an accepted rule. Stream samples may support monitoring or an inquiry into rival causes. Reading sources can provide the answer directly or furnish material for historical source criticism.
