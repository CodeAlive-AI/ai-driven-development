## RMP.Reference:12 - Conditions for a usable combined result

For a combination of methods, work back from the intended answer and its stopping condition. Contributions may answer different subsidiary questions. Identify the relation the recipient’s conclusion requires between the findings. A conjunction about one person may require matching that person across records. A total across disjoint groups uses the separate results and the grounds for adding them. For separate answers, each may stand on its own. Establish any connection needed to carry a result from another time, population or scale into this conclusion. Compare their source basis, rival accounts, scope and specialist results. A design for another material or a different temperature mechanism may not answer a question about field seal degradation. Identify the missing relation. Requalify the question or obtain suitable evidence if the stronger answer is needed and attainable.

For a mixed design, [RMP.2's family comparison](32-rmp-2-choose-a-criticism-bearing-research-design-or-stop.md#rmp245---preserve-design-family-differences) asks why each component is needed and how its result can be interpreted with the others. Check the participants’ shared workload, access windows, specialist time, resources and constraints over the same period. Explain what failure or disagreement in a component would mean for the claim. An additional method can resolve uncertainty, but agreement arising from shared data or assumptions cannot be counted as independent corroboration.

Consider a mixed inquiry into the stream anomaly with twelve investigator-days available. Field comparison requires six, archival comparison five and computational sensitivity analysis four. Suppose the three demands cannot overlap or share work. Each pair fits, but the three together need fifteen days. If all three results are necessary and all the work is still required, the proposed design exceeds capacity. An adequate existing result could reduce the work remaining. Otherwise compare a feasible arrangement when one is worth considering, or finish without a new study and state the evidence limit. Extra capacity requires its own decision. Dropping a component that supplies a necessary challenge changes the design. Lack of time leaves the adequacy of the evidence a separate question.

If different plausible capacity values prevent a needed selection, give the ResearchDesignHold under RMP.2. If the current allocation ends without a study, report the unresolved capacity as a limitation. Where multiple inputs are unknown, apply [RMP.2's joint-input test in RMP.2:4.3](32-rmp-2-choose-a-criticism-bearing-research-design-or-stop.md#rmp243---expose-selection-changing-inputs) before treating them as PostSelectionNeed. The [RMP.2 result rule](32-rmp-2-choose-a-criticism-bearing-research-design-or-stop.md#rmp247---return-exactly-one-researchdesigndisposition) identifies what to record and how to revisit the choice when an actual value falls outside the admitted range.

First establish that the selected design can answer the question, then use RMP.3 to examine the joint conditions of its proposed operations. RMP.4 records what was done and changed. A completed protocol or extensive trace can show how a comparison was lost, but cannot supply its missing evidence. Keep the supported question, design and unaffected material. Identify the failed relation and return it to the relevant work. RMP.5 can give a bounded claim and RMP.6 a credibility finding. Use RMP.7 or RMP.8 for synthesis or wider revision when needed, and RMP.9 for a distinct Method problem.

**Constituent actions in ongoing work.** Classifying an observation may be part of constructing an analytical comparison while an inquiry into an explanation is under way. If the question changes from describing cases to explaining their occurrence, a classification that ignores selection may no longer support the answer. Recording skill and statistical calculation can both be available while the operationalization between them fails. Use RMP.3 for that relation. B.1.5.EW helps explain which actions are part of the ongoing inquiry and what access, specialist capability and shared observation effort their combination requires. A later calculation can use earlier observations after collection has ended.
