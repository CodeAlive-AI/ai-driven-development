## PSDA.Advising:1 - Use this when

Use this profile when a distinct adviser helps a recipient consider what a specified person, organization or technical arrangement could develop. Identify the contribution the recipient needs, the horizon and the premises that bear on it. A useful return may be a direction, a set of alternatives, a proposed probe, a well-grounded request or a limit on advice.

Development-direction work also has an earlier, independently useful branch: constructing an opportunity when the worthwhile problem or possible contribution is not yet settled. Enter [Construct a Bounded Development Opportunity (FPF C.40.OCN:1)](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/111-c-40-ocn---construct-a-bounded-opportunity.md#c40ocn1---problem-frame) directly for that question. A practitioner can use it independently to explore future engineering Work. It can return an opportunity, its dependencies and the question that remains open. A separate advising relationship has its own participants and purpose.

Enter this profile at the contribution the question needs. An opportunity under consideration, the subject that could develop, a recommendation and the subsequent choice each have different evidence. Use qualified work in human development, organization change, strategy, operations, AI engineering, safety or another relevant practice for its own conclusion.

A two-sentence return may suffice. “The available comparison covers internal development and configuration A. Ranking the external provider as well would require evidence about that provider under the same result, security boundary and horizon.” The recipient can use the bounded comparison now.

For a chooser with adequate alternatives and a settled decision question, use C.11 or the direct domain rule. Use a single qualified domain method when it answers the whole question. An unmanaged population or lineage without a local recipient or chooser is a subject for evolutionary inquiry. A research team considering an intervention has its own question, authority and required evidence.

When the recipient asks which programme to design for future participants, identify the design question. Use [PSDA.11](27-psda-11---bound-the-decision-support-engagement-and-authorit.md) where the engagement boundary is unresolved and the learning-product design practice where a design result is needed. Keep the audience description as a design assumption. For personal advice, identify the prospective participant and use the premises about that person's later work, starting performance and support that bear the answer. Missing premises limit the advice; obtain more only when their contribution warrants acquisition. The [programme-design case (Engineering.Reference:11.2)](../engineering-dpf-suite/suite-reference/13-engineering-reference-11---how-do-we-recommend-a-development.md#engineeringreference112---recommend-a-programme-before-the-learners-are-known) shows the two uses.
