## PSDA.Application:7 - Choose development support without assuming an office

The following example supplies the staffing, permissions and time available for the comparison.

Four project teams repeatedly revise commitments after discovering an unchecked premise in the estimates. The director proposes a development office. The six-week undertaking has two aims, improving the estimation method and helping the leads apply and develop it while delivery continues. The teams need to form, test and revise the premises behind their estimates.

Using [PSDA.1](18-psda-1---generate-plural-problem-formulations.md) and [PSDA.2](19-psda-2---set-and-reopen-the-problem-boundary.md), the adviser distinguishes missing technical knowledge, too little opportunity to challenge a commitment, and difficulty turning a weak signal into a useful inquiry. Each points to a different possible need. Records or a participant’s correction can establish what occurred. Attributing the repeated failure to one mechanism needs evidence for that causal claim.

With [FPF C.40.JD:4.5](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/118-c-40-jd---develop-a-way-its-holder-and-its-provision-togethe.md#c40jd45---synthesize-the-organization-from-the-work-and-conditions), identify the work the teams need to retain locally and the specialist help, common resources and wider decisions they require. Use [FPF C.32.MWA.PP](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/83-c-32-mwa-pp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-p.md) and [OCE.8](../management-dpf-suite/oce/30-oce-8---compare-human-ai-robotic-and-provider-arrangements-f.md) to compare proposed ways to perform and supply the whole result, testing their feasibility. Here the evidence supports the managers’ coaching competence, and each has one protected hour per week for local work. The common subject question still requires specialist help.

Compare two arrangements for six weeks. Distributed support uses the existing managers and a shared specialist. The proposed office initially uses the same specialist and local practice and adds two weekly coordination hours. No additional specialist capacity has been secured. The table gives the recurring specialist work.

| Specialist contribution | Managers with distributed help | Proposed office using the same specialist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing commitments | 2 hours | 2 hours |
| One genuinely shared inquiry session | 2 hours | 2 hours |
| Four distinct case reviews, half an hour each | 2 hours | 2 hours |
| Additional office coordination | 0 hours | 2 hours |
| Total against six available hours | 6 hours | 8 hours |

A single session can serve all four teams because they need the same distinction and may share the material. The local reviews remain separate. This case supplies protected team time, permitted material, management authority and the ability to receive the result. Given those other conditions, the six-hour total fits the specialist’s available time.

The adviser recommends distributed support for the six weeks. The proposed office needs eight specialist-hours against six available. Concentrated demand, a reusable service, another qualified provider or a different coordination cost could change the comparison. An external provider whose access and availability are unknown remains conditional. With [PSDA.9](26-psda-9---test-robustness-and-sensitivity-of-decision-alterna.md), test consequential changes when needed. Use [PSDA.14](30-psda-14---prepare-and-return-a-decision-support-recommendati.md) to return the recommendation to the director for the separate choice.

If confidentiality later prevents a common session, four separate two-hour sessions require eight hours. With two hours for case reviews and two already committed, the total is twelve against six available. The distributed proposal no longer fits. Revisit its provision or scope. The previously established technical distinction is still available.

When the chosen arrangement is authorized for use, assess its performance from the work carried out. In a contrasting case, examine how a lead identifies a consequential premise, obtains the missing subject answer and forms a justified commitment. This may support a conclusion about that lead’s contribution under the observed conditions. Evaluate the revised method and any stronger capability claim on their respective grounds. Use [PSDA.15](31-psda-15---arrange-decision-follow-up-or-reassess-advice-afte.md) and [FPF C.40.RQ](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/116-c-40-rq---requalify-changed-opportunities.md) to revisit advice or opportunities that depended on a changed premise.
