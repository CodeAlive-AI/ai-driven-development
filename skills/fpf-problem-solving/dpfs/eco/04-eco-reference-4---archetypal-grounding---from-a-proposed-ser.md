## ECO.Reference:4 - Archetypal grounding - From a proposed service to a viable arrangement

Suppose a firm proposes a monitoring service. A customer has a use for it, a supplier can prepare equipment and a funder can supply finance. The supplier needs dedicated tooling, the customer may cancel early and the funder requires an order. The technical and financial accounts leave open how the parties can meet these conditions together.

Use [ECO.3](13-eco-3---discover-an-exchange-between-different-plans.md) to identify what each party can contribute and which plans the exchange would serve. Use [ECO.1](11-eco-1---recover-the-basis-for-an-economic-calculation.md) to examine prices and attainable alternatives, and [ECO.4](14-eco-4---form-resource-commitments-for-an-uncertain-venture.md) to identify what can be done first or which commitments can be made together. A small demonstration can answer a technical question. Whether the customer can pay requires financial grounds.

Use [ECO.5](15-eco-5---choose-exchange-terms-for-private-information-and-ef.md) to assess whether the proposed terms make the required quality and effort worthwhile for those providing them. With [ECO.6](16-eco-6---arrange-adaptation-in-a-dependent-trading-relationsh.md), compare protection for dedicated tooling and a less dependent standard process. Include an organizational alternative if it offers a credible response. Use the relevant MA, FIN, OPS and EAM results in the comparison.

The standard process may serve the customer at an acceptable cost with less dependence. If it misses the required quality, assess whether the parties can undertake the dedicated arrangement on the stated terms. A demonstration can finish an order for evidence about the equipment. To recommend an offer for the whole service, follow the same proposal through the customer's conditions, the supplier's investment and the funder's release of money. A signed order for one specification and a financing offer for another can both be genuine while leaving this service unfunded. Locate the unfulfilled condition. If asked to design a way forward, develop a proposal within your remit and check that the participants’ available contributions and effective terms can sustain it. Obtain their input where the proposal depends on information they hold or the design requires their participation. To put changed commitments into effect, obtain the decisions their terms require.

Reusable tooling and interchangeable suppliers change the parties’ dependence. Recheck the affected conclusion and use the earlier calculations where their costs, quantities and terms still hold. Reorganization requires its own grounds.
