## ECO.Reference:4 - Archetypal grounding - From a proposed service to a viable arrangement

A constructed firm proposes a monitoring service. A customer has a use for it, a supplier can prepare equipment and a funder can provide money. The supplier needs dedicated tooling, the customer may cancel early and the funder requires an order. Existing technical and financial accounts leave these economic connections unresolved.

[ECO.3](13-eco-3---discover-an-exchange-between-different-plans.md) identifies the reciprocal contributions and the different plans they serve. [ECO.1](11-eco-1---recover-the-basis-for-an-economic-calculation.md) tests the relevant prices and available alternatives. [ECO.4](14-eco-4---form-resource-commitments-for-an-uncertain-venture.md) looks for a first action or jointly attainable commitments instead of treating interest as resources. A small demonstration may settle a technical question, but not a customer's inability to pay.

[ECO.5](15-eco-5---choose-exchange-terms-for-private-information-and-ef.md) asks whether the proposed terms make the required quality and effort worthwhile. [ECO.6](16-eco-6---arrange-adaptation-in-a-dependent-trading-relationsh.md) compares protection for the tooling, a less dependent standard process and an organizational alternative only if it is serious. Relevant MA, FIN, OPS and EAM results remain inputs to that comparison.

A participant may choose the standard process if it supplies the required result with less costly dependence. If it does not, a conditional dedicated arrangement can remain preferable. If no party can supply the next contribution at acceptable exposure, the useful result is the failed condition and a redirected proposal.

Changing the case to reusable tooling and interchangeable suppliers changes the dependence question. It need not alter an adequate calculation or create a reorganization. The method language lets the practitioner change only the affected contribution and carry the result forward.
