## PHY.Reference:3 - Solution - Connect physical methods through their results

### PHY.Reference:3.1 - Construct and constrain the account

[PHY.4](09-phy-4---constrain-an-unknown-physical-law-by-transforming-th.md) begins while the response law is still unknown. Recover the participants, transform the relevant situation, and derive the restrictions supplied by symmetry, dimensions, balances and dissipation where their physical grounds hold. The remaining unknown function or state directs further work.

[PHY.5](10-phy-5---choose-an-effective-physical-description-by-scales-a.md) chooses the physical detail needed at the requested scale and duration. An omitted state can leave memory or fluctuations; a fast response can matter during a transient. Its result tells mathematical reduction what must be retained or bounded.

[PHY.6](11-phy-6---construct-physical-evolution-from-balances-and-respo.md) combines physical participants, exchanges, response laws and compatible preparation into an evolution. A balance alone can leave a response undetermined. Locating that missing contribution is useful before any solver is selected.

[PHY.7](12-phy-7---obtain-motion-from-a-physical-variational-principle.md) offers another construction when a physically justified variational principle applies. The interactions, allowed comparisons and boundary freedoms determine what is varied. Mathematical variation then derives a consequence. A direct balance can suffice, as it does for the rod’s angular acceleration in PHY.7:5.1.

[PHY.8](13-phy-8---infer-macroscopic-physical-behavior-from-microscopic.md) constructs collective predictions from physically admissible alternatives and their weighting grounds. It retains correlations, preparation and the difference between sampling a distribution and following physical evolution. A distribution or sufficient bound can remain useful when a particular moment or equilibrium approximation is unavailable.

### PHY.Reference:3.2 - Compare, observe and construct another use

[PHY.1](14-phy-1---construct-physical-similarity-across-changed-conditi.md) determines which effects and scale relations let a physical result travel to changed conditions. PHY.5 deepens the question when a neglected effect changes that transfer.

[PHY.2](15-phy-2---construct-a-physical-analogue-from-interactions.md) constructs an arrangement whose interactions supply a wanted physical consequence. The construction must include preparation and interpretation. Similar-looking equations can suggest an analogue while leaving those physical tasks unresolved.

[PHY.9](16-phy-9---construct-a-measuring-interaction-for-a-physical-dis.md) makes the wanted difference affect an indication. It develops the coupling, reference or preparation, including disturbance and ambiguity. The result supplies the observation relation used in subsequent inference or comparison.

[PHY.10](17-phy-10---construct-a-physical-test-that-separates-rival-acco.md) constructs a physical contrast between accounts that agree under earlier conditions. It follows the changed preparation through the response and readout, retaining parameter freedom and alternative apparatus explanations. A failed comparison returns to the particular premise that can change it.

[PHY.3](18-phy-3---derive-a-physical-limit-from-permitted-transformatio.md) derives a bound or impossibility from the complete transformation and an admissible comparison. That result may finish the question or show which physical premise a different construction would have to change.

The starting point and stopping point depend on the question. A known response can enter at measurement; an unknown interaction can enter at law construction; a useful impossibility can end work before a device or computation is built.

### PHY.Reference:3.3 - Constituent actions in ongoing work

A measuring instrument can acquire a signal during the test, preserve part of it and be read later. Start with the difference between the physical accounts and trace it to what the instrument retained. Suppose both accounts predict zero final deflection but peak deflections of 3 and 7 mm. A final reading of the deflection leaves both accounts possible. A peak indicator that tracked the motion of this specimen throughout the test and retained 7 mm with a total error bounded by 0.2 mm excludes the 3 mm prediction. The stored peak can decide that comparison after the specimen settles. If the record contains only final values, look for another observation or physical relation already available. Change the acquisition timing or coupling when the distinction still matters and the available evidence leaves it open.

Choose the physical arrangement and the acts of measurement separately. Consider two internally uniform blocks of fixed composition and volume with the same known, constant heat capacity. Their mean temperature determines their combined change in thermal energy from a common reference temperature. Suppose instead that the decision is whether either block exceeds a temperature limit at a specified instant. Redistribute the temperatures at fixed mean. If the verdict can change, retain a sufficient bound on the hotter block or information that distinguishes the distributions. Bringing the blocks into contact to read their equilibrium temperature can erase the difference that matters. Earlier readings can serve both decisions if their times, uncertainties and the intervening evolution support the required instant. FPF B.1.5.EW helps trace which operations produced the readings. B.1.5.RS examines a proposed replacement.
