## Foundational.Reference:2 - What this Suite connects

Knowing a formula can leave its use unresolved. A mathematically valid calculation can concern the wrong objects. A computation can return a value after the opportunity to act has passed. The Suite develops ways to locate and repair such difficulties while retaining the contributions that still work.

| Contribution | The work it does | What another contribution can use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mathematical Thinking** | Construct objects and operations; establish consequences; compare and change constructions. | An object with usable maps, an interpreted expression, an argument, a witness, an obstruction or a qualified approximation. |
| **Physical Thinking** | Identify relevant phenomena, preparation, interactions and constraints; develop and challenge an account of what can happen. | Physical premises, possible changes, a distinguishing observation or a limit on realization. |
| **Computational Thinking** | Construct, understand, analyze and transform algorithms within computer science, including their semantics and interaction. | An algorithm, its representation and correctness, progress and resource conditions, or a limit that changes the requested answer or available operations. |
| **Mathematical Modeling** | Formulate how the subject question, supported relations, unknowns and observations enter mathematics. | A mathematical question whose answer has a stated use in the subject, with the conditions and losses of that use. |
| **Notational Engineering** | Develop expressions and interpretation through which participants can recognize and perform the needed operations. | A usable notation, correspondences between representations and the preparation their readers need. |

Section 5 describes the current repertoire. Choose a method by the contribution the working question needs; combining methods adds value when one result supplies what another requires.

**Methodology connects the contributions to ways of working.** A mathematical construction can describe how operations combine. A physical or computational result can make a different working arrangement possible. Core supplies the general requirements, construction and comparison of ways of working; [MMP.21](../mmp/27-mmp-21---compare-interacting-operations-through-mathematical.md) and [MMP.22](../mmp/28-mmp-22---construct-a-way-of-working-through-mathematical-tra.md) develop the mathematical comparison and transformation when those are needed. Together these contributions support changes in observation, action and division of work without making general methodological development a sixth constituent discipline.

There are two connected questions throughout. How is a claim obtained and warranted? How can its obtaining method and result be used, taught, distributed, changed and continued? Mathematical assumptions, physical premises and computational resource claims need their respective grounds. Their interaction makes a useful inquiry possible.

Here *foundational* means helping enter and develop problems across branches of these fields. A method's conditions still matter: a symmetry method needs a relevant transformation, for example. Further theoretical inquiry can itself be a useful continuation. A problem need not have an immediate commercial application to open a consequential new line of work.



### Foundational.Reference:2.1 - Five subjects and equally legitimate entries

The subject of this composite field is the construction, justification and development of mathematical and physical accounts, computational ways of obtaining results, modeling correspondences and operative expressions. Here an *operative expression* is a formula, diagram, table, program, score, gesture or other structured expression with rules for reading or changing it. Such an expression may be read by a person or interpreted by another system; it need not be executable software. A construction can be developed for its own explanatory or theoretical consequence. Physical application, engineering realization and commercial benefit are not universal entry conditions.

The five branches retain their complete declared subjects. Their DPFs describe selected repertoires within those subjects, not complete textbooks for the academic fields their names may suggest.

- **Mathematical Thinking** constructs mathematical objects, operations and representations; develops arguments, witnesses, countermodels and bounds; and changes axioms or conjectures when the question requires it. Its result can be a theorem, a new construction or an impossibility argument. A proof can finish the work without a physical example. A computational search may help obtain a witness, but the mathematical claim retains its own warrant.
- **Mathematical Modeling** constructs and revises a mathematical question that retains the subject distinctions needed by the intended answer. Its subject may be a physical situation, a way of working, a computation or another mathematical construction. Formulation, observation models, inference, reduction, coupling and return to the subject are work in their own right. A model is not complete for a use merely because an equation has a solution, and the DPF does not cover every kind of modeling.
- **Physical Thinking** constructs and challenges an account of interactions, preparation, constraints, scales, evolution and observation. A physical explanation, discriminating observation or limit on possible transformation can be its primary result. Particular material laws and specialist physical knowledge can be supplied from outside the repertoire. Obtaining a numerical prediction is one continuation, not the definition of the branch.
- **Computational Thinking** develops algorithmics within computer science: effective operations, their representations and semantics, correctness and progress, resource demands and interaction. An algorithm, interpreter, lower bound or changed computational representation can be the result. A mathematical definition of an answer does not by itself supply a way to obtain it. This subject is wider than numerical calculation and narrower than all computer science or software engineering.
- **Notational Engineering** constructs and changes conventions for structured expressions: formation and binding, interpretation, transformation, translation, coordinated representations and the operations their readers need to perform. A usable notation or an improved reading and editing procedure can be the primary result. Its subject includes temporal and embodied notation, not only formulas and programs. Notation supplies timing and reference conventions; musical or movement performance and its required learning come from the corresponding practice. General semiotics, perception and learning remain outside this declared subject.

These are equal entry points, not stages of one mandatory workflow. Their conditions determine which further contribution is needed. A mathematical question may lead to a more suitable representation; a notational ambiguity may lead to an algorithmic or physical question; a failed inference may require another observation rather than more computation. A familiar adequate contribution can be used directly, without opening the other four languages.

### Foundational.Reference:2.2 - Relations that form the field

The discipline parts are the knowledge and reusable practices described by the five DPFs. The relations here connect what a construction is permitted to mean and do with how a result is obtained and how a failure changes the available knowledge or practice.

| Connected contributions | Knowledge constrains the operation | Results or failures return to the construction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mathematical Thinking and Mathematical Modeling | The mathematical objects, operations and preservation laws limit which formulations and reductions retain the required consequence. The subject correspondence determines which mathematical distinctions matter. | A counterexample or failed answer recovery can expose an invalid reduction; non-identifiability can show that the formulation cannot determine the requested subject answer. MATH.2/.18 and MMP.10/.12/.14 develop these different repairs. |
| Physical Thinking and Mathematical Modeling | Interactions, preparation, conservation or response premises constrain an admissible physical model; the observation procedure constrains what its variables and data can mean. | A failed prediction, unresolved scale interaction or changed physical constraint can require a different physical account, observation or mathematical formulation. PHY's prediction-and-observation construction and MMP.14/.16/.20 retain those returns. |
| Computational Thinking and mathematical construction or modeling | The required mathematical answer and allowed operations constrain correctness; access, representation, interaction and resource limits constrain what can be obtained. | An effective construction can make an existence result usable. A lower bound or unattainable resource demand can instead require another representation, weaker answer or changed formulation. CMP.1/.8/.10/.11 and the answer-under-limits case expose these differences. |
| Notational Engineering and the other four branches | Binding and interpretation determine which object, observation or operation an expression denotes. Mathematical laws, physical measurement meanings and computational semantics constrain permissible transformations. | A changed interpretation can invalidate a rewrite while leaving the mathematical identity intact. Difficult reading or editing can require a different expression, while the intended operation constrains what that redesign must preserve. NOT.2–.7 and CMP.12 supply the relevant constructions. |

These relations persist beyond a single Work occurrence. A proof establishes a transformation condition that later formulations and algorithms can reuse. A physical experiment can change the premises admitted by later models. A notation repair can change how later readers construct or inspect an argument. The field therefore includes the continued development of the contributions and their connections.

**A notational entry exposes a substantive difference.** In the [formula, graph and table case (NOT — NT-CHANGE-TOGETHER - Carry a changed request through several representations)](../not/_index.md#nt-change-together---carry-a-changed-request-through-several-representations), `q + q + b` and `2*q + b` agree when q denotes one supplied real value. If each occurrence becomes a fresh sensor read, successive readings 4 and 5 with b=1 give `read() + read() + b = 10`, whereas `2*read() + b = 9` using the first reading. The arithmetic law has not failed. NOT.3/.4 must distinguish the new interpretation and transformation conditions; CMP.12 can give the expression an effective interpreter, and the measurement account supplies what one read obtains. Binding one sample once or retaining two observations answers different questions. The returned distinction can change the notation, algorithm or measurement request.

**A mathematical entry need not proceed to physics.** The [two-element selection case](04-foundational-reference-3---working-combinations.md#foundationalreference35---continue-and-distribute-thinking) asks for a deterministic selection that respects renaming and proves the obstruction. That proof is already a mathematical result. Introducing an order or changing the guarantee to equal probabilities changes the construction; it does not repair the original theorem by a better implementation. The later dispatch example shows how the new construction can then alter a way of working without confusing the mathematical and organizational conditions.

**An algorithmic entry can change what mathematics must retain.** In the [answer-under-limits case (CMP — CP-ANSWER-UNDER-LIMITS - Obtain the answer the work needs within available resources)](../cmp/_index.md#cp-answer-under-limits---obtain-the-answer-the-work-needs-within-available-resources), asking for every optimum rather than one optimum changes the permitted pruning and the contents of a shared table. The optimum value may stay the same. The needed witness information and computational representation do not. This is a connection among answer meaning, representation and effective construction.

The physical prediction and operating-flow cases in Section 3 show further entries. Their calculations illustrate particular connections and retain their own conditions.

### Foundational.Reference:2.3 - The whole, its continuity and its boundaries

The characteristic obtained through these relations is the ability to construct and revise a jointly interpretable, warranted and obtainable result while keeping its different grounds recoverable. *Result* includes a construction, explanation, proof, algorithm, model or notation, not only a number returned to an application. The appropriate grounds depend on what is claimed: mathematical validity does not establish physical adequacy; a correct algorithm may be too costly; a physical account does not supply a usable expression; and an intelligible expression does not establish the truth of its contents. A pure proof makes no physical-adequacy claim unless one is actually introduced.

This characteristic belongs to the connection. Several individually adequate results can fail to compose because they concern different observations, discard different distinctions or permit incompatible operations. Conversely, a changed contribution need not defeat the whole: its consequences can be followed to the affected model, algorithm, expression or premise while unaffected work remains usable. For example, an oscillator's numerical error can call for another approximation without rejecting its physical law; a measurement that defeats an idealization can require a new physical or modeling premise that more accurate computation cannot provide. The branches supply the distinct constructions and returns.

The field's identity rests on continuing these five kinds of contribution and their constructive relations. New axioms, physical theories, model families, algorithms, notations, media and specialist suppliers can change without changing that identity. They must still let users recover the relevant meanings, warrants, obtaining conditions and consequences of revision. A replacement for a constituent contribution has to do that work in the connected field. Removing the ability to develop models or notation as primary results, or retaining them only as unexamined inputs to calculation, would change the declared whole. So would replacing this subject by all intellectual work or by the realization of engineered systems.

Field membership does not require every branch to contribute to every task. It also does not require one person to master every method, one publication to duplicate every supplier, or each constituent discipline to belong to only one larger field. A particular use establishes a use relation. Membership in another composite discipline would additionally require that discipline's own subject, constituent knowledge and practice, and obtaining relations; a link or folder location cannot establish it.

The same field is compatible with a larger assembly for formal and empirical inquiry. Consider an assembly whose subject is constructing, examining and revising knowledge claims and the means by which they can be expressed and obtained. It combines this complete five-branch field with research design and evidence criticism, and with the specialist investigative practices needed for its questions. Its boundary includes a proof, a revised physical account, a new model, an algorithm or a notation as a research result; it does not require an engineered realization.

This assembly needs Foundational Thinking's connected contribution, not just five independently supplied answers: a changed claim or observation must remain connected to what its expressions mean, what its model retains, what warrants its conclusions and how a result can be obtained. Research design determines which rival accounts an inquiry can distinguish; these formal, physical and computational conditions can show that the proposed design cannot answer its question. Conversely, actual research material can defeat an observation model, physical premise or inference and return the revision to the affected construction. The Research Method Practice contributions to design, operationalization, criticism and revision explain the other side of this connection.

Compatibility preserves the whole described here. All five primary subjects, their constructive relations and the ability to develop each contribution remain available; a particular inquiry need not use all five. The exposed interfaces include interpreted constructions, warrants and limits, model correspondences, obtaining procedures and the consequences of changing them. They preserve the distinction between mathematical validity, physical adequacy, computational obtainability and usable expression. Changes of theory, technique or supplier can preserve the assembly when these meanings and return relations continue. An arrangement that accepts only computed numbers, subordinates every model and notation to physical prediction, or turns research results directly into authority to intervene does not admit this same whole unchanged. This establishes compatibility with the described possible assembly, not an assertion that another established discipline or published Suite already contains it.

A narrower relation is also useful. Computational mechanics can obtain selected mathematical, modeling, physical, computational and notational contributions together with specialist mechanics and experimental practice. Such a profile can return a failure to the contribution it defeats without importing the entire field. That supply relation is not evidence that Foundational Thinking in its full scope is a part of computational mechanics or Engineering.

The boundary also leaves useful neighboring fields distinct. Research Method Practice can supply empirical inquiry; Explanation Design can supply an explanation for a particular recipient; Human Capability Development can supply representative practice and transfer work; general methodological development in Core can change how contributions are obtained. Financial modeling, operations management, music and movement can provide subject questions or use the results. These relations do not absorb the complete supplying or receiving fields into Foundational Thinking. In particular, general argument recovery in Section 3.11 is a Core contribution used here, not a sixth discipline or a claim to cover all reasoning.
