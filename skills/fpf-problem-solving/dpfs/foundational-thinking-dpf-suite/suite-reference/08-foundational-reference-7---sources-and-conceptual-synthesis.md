## Foundational.Reference:7 - Sources and conceptual synthesis

[Fong and Spivak's *Seven Sketches in Compositionality*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05316) develops category-theoretic constructions through several applications. The available MATH bodies use particular constructions, including paths and interpretation; they retain direct finite calculation as an alternative when it suffices. The Suite uses composition across practices without requiring one mathematical foundation for every inquiry. The MATH Preface supplies further source comparisons for universal algebra, constructive proofs, symmetry and variation.

[Sussman and Wisdom's *Structure and Interpretation of Classical Mechanics*, Preface](https://mitp-content-server.mit.edu/books/content/sectbyfn/books_pres_0/9579/sicm_edition_2.zip/preface001.html), connects physical investigation, mathematical notation and executable procedures. Its functional notation exposes operations hidden by conventional expressions. This supports an important direction here: recover what each expression asks its interpreter to do. Mechanics and Scheme are the book's worked setting; they do not set the Suite's subject or language requirements.

[Frigg and Hartmann's *Models in Science*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/models-science/) distinguishes questions about what models are, how they represent and how they support knowledge. The MMP Preface develops its adopted source comparisons further, including Nguyen and Frigg, SIMULA history and equation-based modeling. The Suite consequently asks which use of a model is intended and how its result is obtained and interpreted, instead of prescribing one carrier for every model.

[Rodin's *Axiomatic Method and Category Theory*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-00404-4) compares different ways of forming axiomatic theories, including constructive and categorical approaches. Its methodological question helps explain why this architecture includes operations as objects, comparison of interpretations and changes of axioms. A difference in permitted constructions or equality can itself be investigated. The selected methods let the reader examine such a difference while retaining the grounds specific to each mathematical account.

[Dutilh Novaes's *Formal Languages in Logic*](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/formal-languages-in-logic-a-philosophical-and-cognitive-analysis) studies formal languages as tools used in human reasoning, including their historical and cognitive effects. This contributes a different question from whether a string is well formed: which reasoning operations does the notation make accessible, difficult or implicit? Notational Engineering therefore includes interpretation and reader-use changes, alongside expression rules. Extending these methods to AI or embodied carriers is part of this Suite's synthesis; claims about human cognition retain their own scope.

[Blackwell and Green's chapter on the cognitive dimensions of notations](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~afb21/publications/BlackwellGreen-CDsChapter.pdf) examines notations together with the activities and environments in which people use them, including musical notation. It makes the costs of particular reading or changing operations visible rather than treating one notation as uniformly better. This supports retaining notation design as a subject in its own right and comparing concrete trade-offs. Its account of human activities is not evidence that every AI interpreter or embodied carrier has the same cognitive properties.

[Marletto, Deutsch and Vedral's *Tests of Constructor Theory*](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07352v1), especially §§1.4 and 3.2, compares allowed evolution with repeatable performance of a transformation. This informs the separate attention to preparation, physical means, attainable transformations and their limits. Its new physical principles are proposals accompanied by tests. A physical method here must state the principles its consequence uses, so that a different physical account can be compared at the affected premise. This preserves the source's methodological contribution without making acceptance of its whole physical programme a prerequisite.

[Risi, Tang, Ha and Miikkulainen's *Neuroevolution: Harnessing Creativity in AI Agent Design*](https://neuroevolutionbook.com/) develops complete constructions for obtaining, combining and continuing neural behavior. Section 3.10 connects those constructions to their receiving uses and the existing FPF, computational and modeling methods. The book provides the sustained applied explanation; particular primary accounts supply algorithm details and comparisons. The application retains corrections where they change the work, including the walking environment's observations and reward, the complete CMA-ES update, stochastic action, bounded retention and the salamander model's actual construction. Evolutionary search, gradient learning, direct design and their combinations are alternatives whose task, information and complete costs must be compared. The cited newer constructions change particular available choices; neither the book nor this synthesis establishes an exhaustive current ranking or a verified implementation of every method.

The organization here is a conceptual synthesis: retaining each contribution's conditions, connecting knowledge with its application, and making the methods themselves available for criticism and change. These sources develop particular constructions and couplings; they do not provide a canonical five-branch taxonomy named Foundational Thinking that this Suite merely reproduces. The subject and whole account in Section 2 explain the architectural synthesis adopted here. The small cross-practice cases explain this construction; they do not establish a general learning effect. A changed source matters when it changes an operation, premise, cost, interpretation or useful result. Consult the affected framework's or pattern's source discussion when deciding whether and how to revise the contribution.




### Foundational.Reference:7.1 - Investigate how results become obtainable and how their obtaining ways change

Use this connection when a successful construction raises a further question about how it can be reproduced, changed or made available in a different situation. A robot may lift one object while its designers cannot explain the limits of the grip. A generator may produce one useful controller while failing to produce another from a fresh start. An account of adaptation may fit observed behavior while leaving its proposed mechanism untested. The useful result is a research programme whose questions distinguish these possibilities and whose proposed investigations can change an explanation or a way of working. A sufficient established method can answer the immediate practical question without resolving the wider programme.

Such a programme can combine physical inquiry, mathematical construction, computation, engineering and inquiry into learning or organization. This Suite supplies the five contributions described in Section 2 and their connections. The subject practice supplies the phenomena, consequential distinctions and requirements of the intended use. Research practice supplies the further means of obtaining evidence. These contributions can support one programme while retaining their own subjects and grounds.

The starting connection is between what happens and how it is obtained again under changed conditions. A state description can tell us what an object is like; a model of interaction can explain how it behaves. Reproducing that behavior also requires the preparation and means that realize it. When those means use a retained description, changing the description can change later realizations. Developing a better way to produce that description introduces another object of inquiry. Keeping these objects distinct makes the dependency useful:

| Question | Object whose change matters | What the result must explain or supply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How does this system produce the effect during use? | The system during operation, interacting with its surroundings | Relevant state, interaction and conditions of continued behavior |
| How is a further instance or increment obtained? | The materials and existing system changed by a creating arrangement | The realized increment and the preparation, resources and means needed to obtain it again |
| How does the design change across realizations? | The retained design from which instances or increments are obtained | An interpretable change, its realized consequences and the useful earlier properties that it preserves |
| How can better designs or acting procedures be obtained? | The method used to construct, learn or select them | The changed obtaining operation, its application and the resulting performance under its stated starting conditions |

The second and third questions explain a useful engineering difference. Replacing a gripper on an existing robot changes that robot. Altering the retained gripper design changes what can be built for later robots, provided their creators can interpret and realize the revision. Neither change alone develops a better design method. Conversely, a changed search procedure can produce a better design even while the current robot remains untouched. [Section 3.10.4](04-foundational-reference-3---working-combinations.md#foundationalreference3104---develop-the-way-that-learns-or-obtains-a-result) and [FPF C.40:4.7](../../../sections/03-first-principles-framework-readme/_index.md) develop the latter relation through actual applications of the candidate way.

These are distinctions among changes, not a requirement for four stages or four fixed time scales. Operating adjustments, production and design work can overlap. Their characteristic times and information exchanges are questions for the chosen account. A retained description may reside in a creator's tools rather than in the resulting system; identify its carrier and interpretation when that difference determines what can be copied, changed or resumed.

### Foundational.Reference:7.2 - Connect the questions through their consequences

Build the programme around a consequential uncertainty: *which result becomes possible, reproducible or affordable when a particular construction, representation or obtaining operation changes?* Name the proposed change and the result that would make its investigation worthwhile. [FPF C.39:4.1–4.6](../../../sections/03-first-principles-framework-readme/_index.md) develops the missing way from the receiving result, connects available contributions and returns from failed conditions. The following connections explain how the foundational contributions enter that work; they can be pursued independently when the other needed results are already available.

**Physical possibility constrains construction, and construction exposes omitted conditions.** Specify the transformation, permitted inputs, resources and what must remain usable afterward. [PHY.3:4.1–4.5](../phy/18-phy-3---derive-a-physical-limit-from-permitted-transformatio.md) derives a restriction that covers the relevant class of mechanisms and distinguishes that restriction from realizing an admissible mechanism. A failed prototype can reveal an omitted resource or an incorrect model. Failure of one prototype leaves other mechanisms open unless the argument actually covers them. For repeated use, include the preparation and restoration that make the next performance attainable. These requirements let a programme ask both why a transformation is limited and how a useful construction approaches that limit.

**Composition makes a larger result possible only through the right interpretation and interfaces.** A mathematical construction can supply an operation or a preservation theorem. The corresponding physical, computational or organizational composition needs its own identification of operands and interactions. Specify what the formal objects represent and which operation realizes the formal connection. Then derive the claimed consequence through that interpretation. A formal operation constructing a description of a group, for example, obtains that description; the group's ability to act together still depends on the participants' actual interactions. The mathematical work can remain valid when the proposed interpretation fails. The construction-and-interpretation connections in :2.2 and :3.1–3.2 retain this selective return.

**A representation and its changing operations determine which developments can be attempted.** A parameter vector, a graph, executable code and a learned generator make different alterations available. Determine what is inherited, decoded, learned during an application and retained for the next one. Then compare what those alterations can actually produce and what they cost. Sections :3.10.3–:3.10.6 develop these operations. For example, changing an encoded network can change many interacting connections, while editing one symbolic instruction can identify a specific operation; either representation can make a needed change easy or obstruct it. Compare the actual relation between the edit and behavior.

A proposed correspondence between symbolic expressions and learned representations must likewise identify the task, interpretation and operations it preserves. If the question is whether two representations support the same decisions, compare those decisions on the relevant inputs, including consequential changes. If it is whether the learner uses the same internal mechanism, behavioral agreement leaves a different question. NOT's interpretation and translation methods, MMP's subject correspondence and CMP's realization can supply their respective contributions. A general theory unifying these representations remains a research proposal wherever those correspondences have not been supplied.

**Persistence and changes of organization connect local performance to longer development.** Identify the units that persist, the interactions that sustain them and the changes through which a later configuration is obtained. When local improvements conflict, derive the conflict from their actual shared conditions. Ask whether a changed interaction, boundary or organization makes a previously unavailable result attainable, and compare the added burden with a simpler adequate construction. Sections :3.10.5 and :3.10.8 show such questions for interacting and continuing behavior. A new level of organization, a simpler arrangement and ending an unproductive line are different possible outcomes. The result of this inquiry determines which continuation is justified.

The connections also generate discriminating investigations. Two accounts can agree about one successful performance yet disagree about a fresh start, a disturbance, a changed input family or repeated operation. Derive the records each account predicts under a feasible comparison, including what the observation procedure retains. [MMP.16:4.1–4.6](../mmp/22-mmp-16---design-observations-to-separate-mathematical-model.md) constructs that comparison. Return the obtained result to the uncertainty it can resolve: an interpretation, physical premise, changing operation or claimed scope. A result that leaves the practical choice unchanged may still advance a stated explanatory question; that value needs its own reason for the further work.

### Foundational.Reference:7.3 - Keep the strength of each ground visible

A programme can use several kinds of support together. Preserve the inference each supplies.

| Ground | Supported consequence | What remains to establish for a stronger use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A derivation from stated premises | The consequence holds if the premises and derivation hold | Whether the subject satisfies the premises; whether the required construction is available |
| A constructed model or simulation | The implemented account produces the calculated behavior under its conditions | The correspondence to the subject, omitted interactions and adequacy for a further use |
| An observation or performed intervention | A record was obtained through a particular procedure and bears on the compared accounts through its interpretation | How far its conditions and uncertainty support another setting or an explanation of origin |
| A conjecture or analogy | A candidate account or connection worth developing | Its precise correspondence, distinct consequences and grounds for relying on them |

The hypotheses can be substantial. [Constructor theory](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07352v1) proposes physical principles about possible and impossible tasks, including repeatable performance. This suggests asking whether the means retain the ability to perform again and what makes an inverse transformation available. PHY.3 carries that useful distinction while retaining the particular physical premises of each argument. Accepting the complete proposed theory is a further physical question.

[Fields, Glazebrook and Levin's minimal physicalism](https://chrisfieldsresearch.com/min-phys-NC-2021.pdf) proposes an information-based account extending cognition across physical scales. Its subject and assumptions matter: the proposed extension can guide questions about interaction, memory and persistence, but adopting those questions does not settle attribution of cognition or experience to every system. A programme investigating an adaptive machine can identify its observation, internal update and action without first resolving that broader attribution.

[Vanchurin and colleagues' multilevel-learning account](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.14602), especially its stated principles and treatment of conflicting interactions, motivates questions about learning across different temporal scales and changes of organization. The account assumes a loss function, scale separation and further conditions on information flow and change. A proposed application must supply those counterparts or state the altered hypothesis. An engineering conflict alone does not establish an inevitable increase of complexity or an obligation to continue development indefinitely.

These are productive lines of inquiry with different premises and targets. Similar equations can suggest a correspondence, but the correspondence must explain which quantities, operations and consequences are preserved. In particular, an objective called free energy needs its own definition and units before it can enter a physical energy account. Keep a successful conditional model or engineering method usable while a wider unification remains unresolved. A failure of use then reopens the implicated implementation, correspondence or premise; it does not identify the ontology as the unique cause.

### Foundational.Reference:7.4 - Work a programme from a gripping problem

Consider a deliberately simplified robot-gripper model. Two jaws hold a load of weight 8 N. Each jaw applies normal force F over contact area A. Assume equal forces, uniform contact pressure, quasistatic motion and Coulomb friction with coefficient mu. Let the allowable pressure be 0.5 N/mm² and the actuator permit up to 25 N per jaw. The model requires:

- enough friction: 2 mu F >= 8 N;
- acceptable pressure: F / A <= 0.5 N/mm²;
- available actuation: F <= 25 N.

With A = 20 mm² and mu = 0.5, the first two conditions give 8 N <= F <= 10 N. A command realizing F = 9 N is therefore admissible in this model. That is a conditional calculation. Its physical use requires the pressure distribution, friction and force realization to support the assumptions.

Now suppose wet contact gives mu = 0.2. Holding the same load requires F >= 20 N, while the original contact area still permits only F <= 10 N. No force adjustment satisfies both constraints within this gripping model. Searching more controller parameters cannot remove that contradiction. Relevant continuations include a larger contact area, a different supported friction coefficient, a smaller load or another load-support mechanism.

Take the larger-area branch. At A = 50 mm², the pressure condition permits F <= 25 N. Choosing F = 22 N gives friction capacity 8.8 N and pressure 0.44 N/mm², within the stipulated limits. The calculation obtains a candidate region and design change; manufacturing a compatible pad and verifying its contact remain engineering contributions. A fixed force of 22 N already fits both stipulated friction cases with the new area. Developing an adaptive learner needs an additional benefit, such as a warranted reduction of damage or energy over a larger specified family.

The distinctions in :7.1 now generate different investigations. Replacing the pad changes one robot. Retaining the dimensions, material specification and interpretation needed by a fabricator enables production of further pads. Developing a generator that chooses pad geometry and control settings changes the obtaining method. Compare that generator by the realized designs and their performance across the declared loads and contact conditions, including the cost of producing and trying them. The direct inequality calculation is a substantive alternative. Use :3.10.4 when evaluating a proposed learner or generator, preserving its initialization and inherited state.

For an explanatory question, suppose wet trials fail and two accounts remain: lower friction or failure of the actuator to realize the commanded force. A slip record alone can fit both. Derive their predicted records when actual jaw force and load response are observed together. MMP.16 guides the design; the instrument and subject specialists must supply the attainable force observation and its uncertainty. If adequate force is reached while the load slips, that result bears differently on the accounts than a force shortfall. A mixed result can require both contributions. The useful return is the implicated physical or realization condition, not merely a worse score.

Repeated operation asks another question. A grip can work once while heating, wear or unavailable reset prevents the next lift. Specify the repetitions and disturbances the receiving use needs and include their effects in the model or trials. Retaining a successful trained state answers continuation from that state; reproducing performance from a new robot and fresh initialization requires the corresponding construction. These differences direct further inquiry without discarding the dry-contact calculation or claiming that any described trial has already been performed.

### Foundational.Reference:7.5 - Carry a result back to the programme

An obtained result should change a named question or continuation. A bound can eliminate a design family; an interpretation can make a formal construction applicable; a comparison can retain a simpler obtaining method; a failed prediction can expose a missing interaction. Preserve the conditions of that contribution and revisit only the uses that depend on what changed. This is how particular methods remain connected to the programme's reasons for using them.

The wider programme remains open where its proposed explanation exceeds these results. A demonstrated engineering capability need not explain its biological origin. A model of changing organization need not establish a universal direction of evolution. Conversely, an unresolved broad explanation need not prevent a bounded construction supported by its own premises. Section :3.10.7 develops the engineering–mechanism distinction, and :8 supplies the return from a changed answer or condition.

Place further work with the subject whose contribution is missing. A physical law, a mathematical operation and a theory of learning have different development needs. Engineering must obtain and support the resulting system; investigation of people or organizations must retain their actual practices and evidence. Their use of this Suite's models or notation does not make their full disciplines constituents of the Suite. The programme connects their results through explicit questions, interpretations and returns.
