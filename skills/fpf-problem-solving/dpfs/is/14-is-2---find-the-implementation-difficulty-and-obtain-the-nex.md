## IS.2 - Find the Implementation Difficulty and Obtain the Next Useful Contribution

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable
>
> **Primary working result:** a supported response to an implementation difficulty, with the contribution to obtain, its receiving action, consequential uncertainty and a workable return or stop.

### IS.2:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** intended practice cannot begin, later use fails or current help is ineffective, and different explanations would lead to different action. The target can be established or still being examined. What matters is that a concrete intended use and its difficulty can be investigated.

Begin with one episode: what result was needed, what was understood and attempted, what was available, and where the receiver could no longer continue. Ask the performer to show the difficult point. A missing operation, inaccessible input, unavailable time and an unsuitable target call for different contributions.

The governed subject is the implementation difficulty and the proposed response. An interview, observation or small comparison helps understand it; none is compulsory when a current qualified result already settles the action. The Method returns useful help or a request that can actually be fulfilled.

Use an adequate known response directly. When an unexpected result after an intervention needs explanation before adaptation, A.3.1.MI:4.5 supplies that developed comparison. When the question is already a human capability diagnosis or an organizational participation difficulty, use HCD.3 or OCE.10 rather than recreate their work.

### IS.2:2 - Problem

Labels such as resistance, low readiness and missing capability can conceal different failures. A person may know the Method but lack permission, begin correctly but lose access, or complete the prescribed work without receiving any useful result. A capable provider may answer too late to help.

An indiscriminate diagnostic exercise can make matters worse. Long inventories consume scarce participant time, produce categories without obtainable repairs and delay an already supported response. Conversely, an attractive quick fix can repeatedly move the difficulty elsewhere because it repairs a symptom without following the receiving work.

The problem is to distinguish only what changes the next useful action, and then obtain the contribution that action needs.

### IS.2:3 - Forces

| Force | Consequential tension |
| --- | --- |
| Several plausible explanations | The same missed action can have different causes and several can operate together. |
| Local knowledge and authority | Participants know important conditions, but their explanations do not alone settle empirical claims or commitments. |
| Timely help and uncertainty | A useful response may be justified before one cause is established. |
| Diagnostic depth and interruption | More detail can improve a decision or consume the window in which help matters. |
| Target integrity and local adaptation | A local repair can enable the intended practice or silently replace a decisive operation. |
| Adequate expertise and scarce supply | The needed answer may exist while no qualified provider is available at the required moment. |

### IS.2:4 - Solution

Reconstruct a receiving episode, compare explanations through their different practical consequences, and obtain the smallest contribution that changes the next use. Retain uncertainty at the place where it limits the decision.

#### IS.2:4.1 - Follow the episode through the intended result

Recover the actual occasion, input, interpretation, action, output and receiver. Ask what the participant believed they were doing and what was expected to happen next. Compare that account with permitted task material: a case, input, tool response, work product or receiving decision.

An effective question is “Show me where you could no longer continue”. Follow the answer both ways. Backward, identify the missing condition or earlier contribution. Forward, ask what result the next person or system needs and why the current result is insufficient. B.1.5.EW helps when the action's contribution to the encompassing work is unclear.

For example, finding a source edition can be part of judging support for a claim, which is part of preparing a release recommendation. Correct retrieval is not sufficient if the retrieved source cannot support the claim. Equally, debating the recommendation cannot repair an unavailable source.

Include a successful contrast when it can distinguish the failure. The same performer using a different source route may reveal an access problem; success with an expert present may instead reveal the expert's missing contribution. Preserve that assistance in the account rather than treating both episodes as independent performance.

If the intended event has not occurred, use an explicit prospective case and keep its status clear. A walkthrough can identify a contradiction or unavailable input without proving that later work will succeed.

#### IS.2:4.2 - Construct the action-changing alternatives

State explanations as working claims about the episode. “The lookup route cannot retrieve this edition” is more useful than “technology barrier”. Explain what would differ if the claim were true and which response it would warrant.

| Possible explanation | Useful contrast when it can change action | Different response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The needed source or tool cannot be used. | Can the qualified performer proceed when effective access is supplied? | Repair access or select another qualified obtaining way. |
| An operation or judgement is missing. | What contribution does an expert make that is absent from the ordinary attempt? | Obtain that contribution, explain the Method or develop the intended performer's capability. |
| The target does not fit the receiving case. | Which relied-on condition differs, and does the known alternative address it? | Reconsider the target or qualify a variant through the subject practice and A.3.1.MV. |
| Correct work conflicts with current commitments. | What occupies the necessary interval, and who can change the allocation? | Obtain a feasible coexistence or workload decision. |
| Correct participation brings adverse consequences. | What actually follows the action, compared with the stated rule? | Obtain an effective change to those consequences; instruction alone is insufficient. |
| A completed action returns no wanted value. | What did the receiver need, and what remained unresolved after completion? | Repair the contribution or reconsider the desired action. |

The table is illustrative. Form another explanation when the case requires it. Several can survive: an unclear instruction and unavailable time can both matter. An available determinant inventory can prompt questions about the relevant operation and its grounds.

Do not diagnose a person from silence or non-use. Ask for their account where it can be obtained appropriately. Refusal can reveal an unacceptable burden, a valid objection or a legitimate choice. When a clinical or specialist judgement is actually needed, return it to a qualified professional instead of extending implementation advice into that domain.

#### IS.2:4.3 - Decide whether another observation is worth obtaining

First ask whether existing grounds already support a response. If all live explanations support repairing the same unavailable input, the current decision may not need a unique cause. If one explanation requires training and another requires changing sanctions, a discriminating observation can prevent substantial wasted work.

Describe the possible answer before commissioning the inquiry. Name how it would change the action, who can obtain it, the conditions required and when it can arrive. Compare its whole burden with an adequate current response, a small reversible start, narrowing the promise or stopping. C.11.DUA supplies that comparison.

A low-burden contrast can be useful: try the same lookup through an authorized working route; inspect one task with and without the existing aid; ask the receiver to show what made the result unusable. Preserve the relevant conditions and the limitation of the contrast. A successful assisted attempt can show what the combined performance obtained; it leaves independent capability and the assistance's isolated effect unsettled.

When the proposed explanation and causal reliance require more than the local contrast can establish, use A.3.1.MI:4.5, C.28 and the appropriate RMP design. Do not promise an effect estimate from a convenient before/after count. Conversely, unresolved causality does not prevent every useful local repair.

#### IS.2:4.4 - Obtain the response through its actual supplier

Convert the chosen response into a contribution someone can supply. State the case and receiving result, the condition to be changed or question to be answered, the needed timing and the qualification on which use depends.

For a missing judgement, ask the expert to demonstrate the relevant comparison on the actual kind of case, including the condition that changes the answer. For a learning need, give the learning provider the later-work action and the observed difficulty rather than request generic training. For an allocation conflict, show the simultaneous commitments and the decision required from their owners.

Choose the professional route from the question:

- HCD.3 and the appropriate HCD development Method distinguish and address a human learning target.
- OCE.10 supplies organizational participation diagnosis and intervention; OCE.11 supplies coexistence with continuing service.
- A.15.8.MS supplies a method-use support configuration. A.3.1.FT, A.3.1.MV and C.39 handle a target that needs qualification or change.
- CB.12 supplies a wanted repeated participation action; CB.14 supplies a shared repertoire contribution when those are the actual problems.
- RMP receives a research question when existing knowledge does not supply an adequate answer and its research conditions hold.

These routes can supply the whole bounded response. Use the returned contribution with its conditions of use and next recipient.

#### IS.2:4.5 - Make the returned contribution usable in the next episode

Inspect the returned result against the question that required it. A claim that access has been repaired needs an actually usable route. An explanation needs to settle the disputed interpretation or preserve the exact unresolved question. An agreed allocation needs to be effective when the work occurs.

Arrange the receiving action. Place the corrected reference at the point of use, obtain the permitted next case, connect the expert response to the decision or make the protected interval available. IS.3 constructs a larger strategy only when several contributions or alternatives still require it.

Observe the next use only as far as it can change the response or the claimed result. Keep failed entry, missing observation and justified non-use distinct. If the response did not supply its promised contribution, repair that connection or return it to the provider. If it did supply the contribution but the larger result still failed, continue from the next consequential difficulty.

#### IS.2:4.6 - Reconsider the target when support is the wrong repair

A support investigation can reveal that the target is no longer appropriate, that a new possible result is more valuable, or that a needed Method does not exist. Preserve that discovery. Do not make the support service's original scope a reason to retain an unsuitable target.

Return the changed target question to its decision owner with the evidence, useful existing results and receiving consequence. C.39 and the subject practice can construct or qualify a way. C.40.OD can help compare development directions; B.5.PI/C.40.CD can help form a question when the initial interpretation fails. Implementation support can continue independent useful work while the dependent branch remains open or stopped.

The completed result states the supported response, what it made possible, what remains uncertain and which next action is available. A precise stop is a useful return when no permissible contribution can meet the current promise.

### IS.2:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.2:5.1 - Distinguish access, capability and consequences

Continue the constructed Harbor Design case. The helper reconstructs three episodes:

| Episode | What happened | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A source check with a working direct link | The analyst identified the relevant revision and explained its effect. | This episode supports use of the known operation with that access; it does not prove every analyst's general capability. |
| A check through the usual shared portal | The needed revision was unavailable, and the analyst used an older one. | The portal condition is a concrete repair target. |
| An early challenge with the correct revision | The manager deferred the challenge to preserve the release date; the analyst received an adverse response for delay. | Correct detection is insufficient while the receiving consequence defeats its use. |

The source owner repairs the portal; the responsible manager and technical lead settle how a legitimate concern reaches the release decision and how its time is provided. OCE.10 supplies that organizational response.

Suppose the next supported episode succeeds with one analyst, while a second still cannot compare the two revisions. That different finding opens a bounded HCD question about the second analyst's operation. It does not retrospectively turn the portal failure into a learning defect.

If the manager refuses the needed change, the helper reports that the promised early-challenge practice remains unsupported. The source repair can still be useful.

#### IS.2:5.2 - An independent carrier needs a different input, not a community

A freelance repairer obtains a diagnostic procedure from a publisher. The new instrument exports a different identifier format, and the procedure's lookup fails.

The support practitioner asks for an allowed sample and follows it through the lookup. A subject expert can map the new identifier unambiguously; the existing instruction assumed the older format. The immediate response is a qualified input adapter and an explanation of its boundary.

If two new identifiers map to the same older code, the mapping is no longer sufficient. That changed condition returns to the target Method and subject expert. The helper must not choose arbitrarily merely to keep the implementation moving.

#### IS.2:5.3 - Preserve uncertainty when the response does not depend on it

A service's help link has moved, and users cannot reach the support page. Records do not distinguish whether each user forgot the old route or followed a broken bookmark. Restoring the correct route and communicating it is supported under both explanations.

The local response can proceed on this basis. If a later decision concerns reducing reminders, the memory and cue question becomes material then.

### IS.2:6 - Bias-Annotation

The helper may favor explanations that match their own service: trainers see learning gaps, tool providers see configuration gaps and managers see motivation. Use the episode, recipient account and actual contribution to challenge that preference.

A successful contrast can also mislead when it changes several conditions. Keep expert assistance, case difficulty and effective access visible; their combined result does not isolate one cause.

### IS.2:7 - Conformance Checklist

- A concrete intended or actual receiving use grounds the difficulty.
- Live explanations differ in the action or warranted conclusion they imply.
- An adequate current response can end diagnosis without another observation.
- A selected inquiry has a possible answer that changes the decision and warrants its burden.
- The response names an obtainable professional contribution and receiving action.
- Capability, organizational conditions, target fitness and support are not treated as interchangeable.
- The target can be reconsidered when help would preserve the wrong practice.

### IS.2:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Score a barrier inventory and call the highest score the cause. | Recover the case and compare explanations through their different expected observations and actions. |
| Diagnose a missing skill whenever a person does not perform. | Inspect access, task conditions, consequences and the actual operation. |
| Ask for more evidence without saying what it changes. | State the possible answer and next action before selecting the inquiry. |
| Forward a problem to “an expert” without a usable request. | Supply the case, needed result, timing and receiving condition. |
| Repeat an adequate OCE or HCD diagnosis under a new label. | Consume its current result and act on the remaining question. |

### IS.2:9 - Consequences

The next intervention can address the contribution that actually limits use. Some requests become smaller repairs; others correctly return to a target decision or a professional supplier.

The Method requires access to enough task evidence and participant understanding. Where those are unavailable, its conclusion remains limited. A narrow useful response can still be possible without a complete causal account.

### IS.2:10 - Architectural Rationale

The unit of diagnosis is a receiving episode because implementation difficulties arise at connections among an occasion, action, means and result. A label about an individual or organization can hide those connections.

The Method separates explanation from response selection. More than one explanation can support the same response, while one generic symptom can require very different actions. That distinction makes the work useful under uncertainty and limits unnecessary inquiry.

### IS.2:11 - SoTA-Echoing

OCE.10 supplies a developed comparator: reconstruct a consequential gap, distinguish explanations, obtain the actual intervention and examine receiving use and burden. Its sanctions and workload distinctions are retained. HCD.3 supplies the human diagnosis when that is the question. A.3.1.MI:4.5 supplies the more demanding explanatory comparison after an implementation result.

The pattern uses those whole Methods where their subject fits. Its distinct connection is from an implementation-help request across different receiving settings to an obtainable next contribution.

The simpler alternative is a known adequate response. Retaining it avoids spending participant time on uncertainty that cannot change the current action. Reopen when another episode defeats its premise or a stronger claim makes the unresolved distinction material.

### IS.2:12 - Relations

IS.1 supplies the intended benefit and help boundary. IS.3 consumes the response when a support strategy remains to be constructed. A.3.1.MI returns failed entry, adaptations and later-work observations. IS.5 reopens diagnosis when transfer or continuation loses a necessary contribution.

B.1.5.EW explains the constituent contribution in its encompassing work. C.11.DUA governs the worth of further inquiry. C.28 and RMP supply stronger causal or research work.
