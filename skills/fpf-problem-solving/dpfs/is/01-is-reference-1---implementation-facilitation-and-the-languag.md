## IS.Reference:1 - Implementation facilitation and the language that describes it

Implementation facilitation is the practical work of helping practitioners, organizations and affected people make an evidence-informed practice usable in their circumstances, examine its consequences and improve their ability to conduct subsequent changes. The facilitator works on that connection. The service practitioner performs the target practice; its recipient experiences its benefit, burden or exclusion; the responsible authority decides matters within its remit. One person may occupy several of these positions, but an agreement to facilitate does not supply another position's competence or authority.

This Architectural Reference addresses users of the framework: facilitators, the people receiving their help, and those deciding what help to provide. It explains what the Methods obtain, why they are connected, and where another practice must supply a result. The main sources concern human and social services. Transfer to a professional community, independent practice or an AI-assisted arrangement requires the actual recipient, intervention, authority and evidence conditions to be recovered. It is not an effectiveness conclusion inherited from those sources.

The field combines knowledge about how a proposed practice works in a setting with reusable ways of helping its participants change that practice. Research evidence, professional judgement and the experience of people affected constrain different claims; disagreement among them is a reason to examine those claims, not to average them into consensus. Facilitation selects and adapts a response, participants enact the changed work, and observations return to the practice account and its facilitating Methods. The whole can be judged by how well its combined knowledge and facilitating practices enable a justified, usable change in the receiving setting, and by how observations from that change improve later action. To examine this, recover why the change was selected, what participants actually did, what happened to the receiving work, and what the result permits next.

Different evidence models, facilitation strategies and provider arrangements can serve this field when they preserve that connection and its limits. A new target profession, a different organizational home or a changed tool does not by itself create another field. Replacing the purpose with research alone, direct service delivery alone or unrestricted organizational management changes the promised whole. Implementation research remains a source and a possible receiver of qualified observations; this framework does not absorb its complete research practice. Organizational change, human development and community management likewise keep their own subjects.

One possible larger field assembly is implementation science and practice: connecting warranted practice knowledge with its use in receiving settings and returning the consequences to inquiry. Evidence synthesis or translation makes the relied-on practice account and its limits usable; implementation research investigates how delivery, mechanisms and context affect results; the target profession supplies actual delivery and its professional judgement. Facilitation connects those contributions to an obtainable change with the people affected. Their combined contribution is the ability to obtain a justified, usable implementation and revise its grounds from receiving evidence. Neither a sound research account nor successful delivery alone supplies that whole answer.

Within that possible assembly, this same facilitation field receives qualified practice accounts and relevant knowledge of recipients and settings. It supplies adapted, obtainable help and bounded observations of what was delivered, used or prevented. Those observations can challenge a relied-on assumption or identify a research question; the receiving research or synthesis practice determines what broader conclusion they support. Different professions, evidence models or providers preserve the facilitation contribution when this knowledge–help–observation connection, the recipient's practice, and the limits of professional judgement and authority remain. Replacing the helping contribution with research alone or direct delivery changes that constituent. Select the contributions needed by the undertaking.

The language has two useful levels. IS.1–IS.3 and IS.5 explain agreement, diagnosis, strategy and continuation of help. The general Core Method [FPF A.3.1.MI](../../sections/05-part-a---kernel-architecture-cluster/28-a-3-1-mi---introduce-a-method-into-practice-and-decide-what.md) supplies introduction and interpretation of later use, including an implementer's direct use without a helping relationship. The broader applicability of these Methods does not make every instance implementation facilitation. IS.6–IS.8 develop three recurring facilitation constructions: make cross-party knowledge affect a practice despite unequal influence; act on the interaction of the proposed practice, participants and context; and develop the recipients' ability to facilitate another change. The latter construction uses human-development and organization-capability Methods.

For an uncomplicated access failure, use IS.2 or [FPF A.15.8.MS](../../sections/07-cluster-a-v---constitutional-principles-of-the-kernel/23-a-15-8-ms---configure-methodological-support-for-work.md) and stop when the missing contribution is supplied. For a disagreement whose resolution depends on who can affect the practice, use IS.6. For a practice that must change with its setting, use IS.7. For recipients expected to lead a later change, use IS.8. Existing adequate results can enter any of these Methods directly.

This field is narrower than all implementation practice and wider than chairing a discussion. A facilitation-only meeting can be enough when discussion is the missing contribution. An undertaking involving evidence interpretation, changed provision, protected participation and later capability needs the corresponding connected work. Obtain the required contributions from the relevant practices: [FPF C.32.MWA.PP](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/83-c-32-mwa-pp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-p.md) compares whole arrangements, [OCE.6](../management-dpf-suite/oce/28-oce-6---establish-holder-assignments-and-enabling-relations.md) supplies effective organizational relations, and the target profession supplies its technical judgement. A common office, a specialist, an internal practitioner, an external partner and distributed provision remain alternatives.

Implementation can finish while development continues. A useful result may expose a more demanding attainable problem, a changed aim or a missing Method. [FPF C.40.OD](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/117-c-40-od---construct-and-continue-open-ended-development.md) constructs the development opportunity, [FPF C.40.JD](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/118-c-40-jd---develop-a-way-its-holder-and-its-provision-togethe.md) relates changes in the way, its holder and its provision, and [FPF C.40.NP](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/119-c-40-np---continue-from-a-result-to-new-attainable-problems.md) continues from a result to another worthwhile problem. Facilitation helps a selected change become useful.
