## CUST.7 - Evaluate Growth and Customization with Full Resource Obligations

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### CUST.7:1 - Problem frame

Use this method when a customization, common contribution or provider change is justified by growth, repeatability or economics, but the full work and resource consequences are unclear.

Start by naming what is expected to grow, the useful result that must remain available and the boundary of comparison. Growth in customers, variants, execution volume, partners and support years can create different burdens.

Use an already adequate accounting and operating result directly when it covers the same alternative, period and obligations. This method joins those results to the customization decision; it does not replace accounting, finance or capacity analysis.

### CUST.7:2 - Problem

A local price can hide common investment, support and migration. A successful deployment can conceal scarce expert time. Outsourcing can move work without reducing it. A proposed productized offering may therefore appear economical while consuming more of the resource that limits total delivery.

The decision needs a continuation for which the required resources can be obtained and the obligations met.

### CUST.7:3 - Forces

| Tension | Effect on the comparison |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate delivery and shared investment | Separate costs and benefits without charging the same common work repeatedly. |
| Revenue and obtainable means | A profitable promise can still lack cash, time or a qualified performer when needed. |
| Local efficiency and total burden | Include work moved to customers, partners, support and future releases. |
| Volume and variety | More similar cases can share preparation; more interacting variants can increase qualification. |
| Commercial and public provision | Use an appropriate funding and value basis without imposing local profit as a universal condition. |

### CUST.7:4 - Solution

#### CUST.7:4.1 - Fix the comparison boundary and growth claim

Name the customer result, customer population or case mix, period and service conditions that the alternatives must support. Include current obligations affected by the choice. Comparing a one-time prototype with a maintained service without their different horizons gives a misleading answer.

State the growing quantity. “Scale” might mean more customers with the same mix, more variants, greater throughput, more operating locations, longer support or more delivery partners. If several grow, keep them distinct and explain their interactions.

State the proposed mechanism. Shared preparation may reduce setup per case. Automation may reduce a particular operation. A partner may add capacity. Standard interfaces may reduce change work. Each mechanism predicts a particular burden change; none follows merely from calling the result productized.

#### CUST.7:4.2 - Recover complete work and resource obligations

Start from the constructed alternatives, not from an assumed price. Trace preparation, local execution, adaptation, qualification, acceptance, assistance, coordination, updates, support, transition and ending. Include the recipient's work when the alternative transfers effort to them.

Use [MA.8 — Account for Customer and Product Economics Over Time](../ma/15-ma-8---account-for-customer-and-product-economics-over-time.md) for the complete accounting method over customer and product economics. Supply the alternatives and time boundary; receive the relevant cost, capacity, allocation, support-tail and economic results under their stated assumptions. Describe the whole relevant work for each customization or family alternative, then use the returned account to choose its disposition.

Keep unlike resources distinguishable. An hour of a scarce specialist is not automatically substitutable by an hour of general delivery work. Record when a resource is needed, not only its total amount. Separate committed obligations from optional investment and from work whose funding or provider is still unknown.

Avoid double counting common development across customers while still making its actual financing visible. A contribution can be valuable over a portfolio even when one case cannot bear the whole preparation cost. Conversely, allocating costs away from a customer does not remove the underlying burden.

#### CUST.7:4.3 - Test the claimed burden change

Compare what changes in the intended growth condition.

| Growth | Candidate mechanism | Observation that can defeat the claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More comparable customers | Reuse preparation and ordinary delivery. | Each case still needs the original author for the decisive operation. |
| More variants | Shared invariants and qualified variation rules. | Interaction testing and support grow faster than the saved preparation. |
| More execution volume | Added capacity, automation or delayed differentiation. | A shared queue or unavailable specialist determines delivery time. |
| Longer support and more releases | Common updates and controlled compatibility. | Old branches require continuing separate work or expensive migration. |
| More partners | Transfer of obtainable delivery work. | Central exceptions and partner coordination retain or worsen the bottleneck. |

Distinguish capacity expansion, economies of scale, economies from shared variety and gains from learning. Hiring proportionally more performers may support more customers without reducing work per customer. That can still be a good decision if its costs and conditions fit the purpose.

When evidence is uncertain, identify the uncertainty that can change the decision. A small, permitted receiving trial may establish whether ordinary delivery still requires author intervention.

#### CUST.7:4.4 - Join economic attractiveness with operating feasibility

Use [OPS.10 — Qualify Operating Capacity Under Variability](../ops/40-ops-10---qualify-operating-capacity-under-variability.md) for capacity under variability and [OPS.13 — Align Commitments, Resources, and Service Outcomes](../ops/46-ops-13---align-commitments-resources-and-service-outcomes.md) for simultaneous commitments, resources and service outcomes. Receive the supported workload, timing, variability and capacity conditions. [OPS.14 — Relate Throughput, Cash, and Operating Consequences](../ops/47-ops-14---relate-throughput-cash-and-operating-consequences.md) helps relate throughput, cash and operating consequences when that connection matters.

Compare those results on the same boundaries as the accounting answer. If the cost estimate assumes six deployments in a period but capacity supports only two, do not carry the six-case saving into the commitment. If a specialist is available only after the promised date, funding the hours does not repair the timing.

For an outcome arrangement, use the responsibility and operating concept obtained through SYSE.8. Include observation, corrective action, customer contributions, risk exposure and continuing work. Do not price an outcome from installation cost alone.

#### CUST.7:4.5 - Choose an attainable continuation

Choose among the complete alternatives: accept the supported scope, invest in a common contribution, charge or fund local work, retain assistance, add a qualified provider, change timing, narrow variants, separate offerings or decline the unsupported part.

In commercial provision, use the appropriate economic and cash consequences. In public or nonprofit provision, establish the useful result, available funding, allocation priorities and resource limits. A recipient need not pay a positive local margin, but someone must actually provide the needed contributions.

Identify who can commit the resources and who can change the promise. The case can proceed locally while a separately funded investment remains open. Avoid hiding that investment inside a delivery estimate or treating a hoped-for future sale as already committed support.

#### CUST.7:4.6 - Retain the conditions that make the decision true

State the relevant volume, mix, period, support conditions and uncertain assumptions. Keep the comparison recoverable in ordinary working material. Reopen it when demand mix, assistance, variant interactions, update frequency or provider availability changes materially.

Use actual repetition to test the mechanism. If shared preparation saves setup but adds a larger support tail, revise the claim rather than reporting only the favorable measure. Return a false family assumption to CUST.3, a hidden delivery dependency to CUST.6 and an unaffordable migration to CUST.8.

### CUST.7:5 - Archetypal Grounding

Six comparable deployments each require six hours of one named qualified resource, for 36 hours. A proposed shared method requires twelve hours of preparation and three hours per deployment, for 30 hours at six cases. At two cases, the comparison is twelve hours versus eighteen: the common preparation has not yet reduced the total.

Now include ten hours of transition work for existing customers in the same period. The proposed arrangement requires forty hours, not thirty. It may still be worthwhile over a different supported horizon, but the original six-case claim no longer justifies this release. These are supplied scenario values, not empirical estimates.

The provider then proposes partner delivery. Ordinary work moves to the partner, but every exception still needs the same central specialist. CUST.7 separates the useful increase in ordinary delivery capacity from the unresolved specialist constraint and includes coordination. A partner invoice does not show that total work fell.

For a publicly funded literacy programme, compare preparation, facilitator work, translation, devices, ongoing help and participant burden. A shared lesson plan may save preparation while local facilitation remains proportional to attendance. The decision concerns attainable public value within funding and capacity.

### CUST.7:6 - Bias-Annotation

The comparison can favor monetized effects and recorded hours. Include meaningful recipient burden, noninterchangeable capabilities and longer support obligations. Use qualified valuation where needed rather than converting every effect into an unsupported monetary figure.

Forecast precision can conceal uncertain demand. Keep the decision-sensitive assumptions visible.

### CUST.7:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Are alternatives compared for the same useful result, population, period and conditions?
- Is the growing quantity and proposed burden-reduction mechanism explicit?
- Are common preparation, local work, assistance, support, migration and ending included?
- Are transferred work and scarce resources visible?
- Do accounting, cash and capacity assumptions fit together?
- Is the funding basis appropriate to commercial, public or nonprofit provision?
- Does the conclusion distinguish supported growth from a demonstrated reduction in burden?

### CUST.7:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**Revenue proves scale.** Inspect the operation and resource whose burden is supposed to change.

**A paid exception has no cost.** Payment changes funding, not the work required.

**A partner removes the work.** Count coordination and remaining central contributions.

**All hours are interchangeable.** Preserve the identity, timing and limits of scarce capability.

**One cheap pilot predicts the support tail.** Bound the inference and obtain the evidence needed for continuing obligations.

### CUST.7:9 - Consequences

The method can justify growth, investment, a narrower offering or continued bespoke provision. It can also reveal that a proposed common release is premature.

Its accounting and operating detail has a cost. Obtain the precision needed for the decision.

### CUST.7:10 - Architectural Rationale

The burden belongs to the complete provision arrangement over time. CUST uses accounting and operations as full suppliers because their calculations are not specific to customization. Its distinct move is to construct and preserve the family, customer and transition boundary on which those calculations must operate.

### CUST.7:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The method combines service-productization work with the full MA.8 and Operations methods. Compared with pricing only the initial customization or counting internal hours after outsourcing, it preserves shared investment, moved work, constrained resources and continuing support.

Mass-customization and platform sources support the potential value of common preparation and controlled variation. They do not establish that every added variant lowers cost. Naldi, Galizia and Bortolini provide a bounded example of competing time and storage objectives.

CUST adopts the complete-boundary comparison and rejects growth claims based only on a route, fee or provider change. Reopen the answer when actual repetition changes the proposed mechanism or the horizon needed to support it.

### CUST.7:12 - Relations

CUST.7 can evaluate a local service without productization. Within CUST.1 it constrains every consequential promise and shared investment. CUST.2 supplies linked differences, CUST.3 supplies family alternatives, CUST.4 supplies differentiation choices, CUST.6 supplies actual delivery work and CUST.8 supplies transition obligations.
