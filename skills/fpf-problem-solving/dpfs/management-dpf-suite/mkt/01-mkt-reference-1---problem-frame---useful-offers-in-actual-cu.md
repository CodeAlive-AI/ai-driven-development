## MKT.Reference:1 - Problem frame - Useful offers in actual customer situations

Marketing examines and changes the relations through which people discover, choose, obtain and use offerings, and through which providers learn what to offer and how to sustain that provision. Its practical object is larger than a message or a sale: a useful contribution has to connect a customer's circumstances, a provider's capabilities, a meaningful choice and actual provision. Marketing knowledge concerns these relations and their conditions; marketing practice investigates them and constructs, performs and revises ways of working with them.

This language develops that account through customer and market inquiry, offers and positioning, communication and advertising arrangements, acquisition, experience, relationships, commercial distribution and repeatable customer work. A founder, service team, learning provider or business-to-business programme owner can enter with one bounded problem. The reader can investigate the domain, work with customers and obtain engineering, operating or economic contributions when needed. The language does not supply every specialised method of brand strategy, price research, media buying, logistics or public policy. It explains how their required contributions enter the customer work and when a result must return to its responsible practice.

Use the language when misunderstanding a customer's work, overextending evidence, disconnecting a promise from provision or confusing one kind of result with another could change the next decision. A known suitable offer with a workable ordinary purchase may need only its existing operating method. A personal learning problem, a community mandate, an engineering design or a liquidity decision keeps its own professional owner even when commercial work depends on it.

### MKT.Reference:1.1 - Form the objects from the work

Begin with an episode in which somebody is trying to obtain a result. Recover what the person is doing, what makes the result useful, what currently happens, what alternatives are available and which conditions matter. A requested product feature is one possible means. A difficulty completing the person's work can survive its delivery. MKT.12 investigates the consequential question; MKT.1 constructs the customer problem and the differences between situations that would change a decision.

The people who use, choose, pay, permit, maintain and bear consequences can be different. These are relations to a particular activity, not permanent kinds of people. A team member can use a service while an employer pays; a retailer can buy goods upstream and sell them downstream. Identify the organisation or person and the actual relation whenever a claim about “the customer” would otherwise change meaning. A role description alone supplies neither a holder nor the authority, capability or time to perform its work.

Form a customer group from differences relevant to the intended decision. For an offer decision, ask whether one proposal remains suitable across the included situations under the stated conditions; a relevant difference can require separate treatment. Investigate cases that could defeat that grouping. A market account then relates such customer situations to available alternatives, providers, access conditions and exchanges over a stated place and period. A list of contacts, an advertising audience and the people who can both choose and obtain the contribution may cover different populations.

Construct an offer by connecting the intended result to a contribution a provider can supply, the recipient's required work, consideration, conditions and continuation. MKT.2 obtains these parts and tests their uncertain connections. An opportunity exists for the present work when a particular situation could support a worthwhile continuation; interest alone does not qualify its conditions. A customer programme becomes useful when several such continuations share consequential preparation or resources. MKT.14 constructs that larger arrangement without erasing their separate decisions and obligations.

These distinctions allow the same statement to be tested at the right object. “They want it” may report expressed interest; “they can buy it” requires a possible choice and acquisition; “it is available” requires provision under the relevant conditions; “it helps” concerns use and benefit. Retain the differences that can change what to investigate, offer, promise or perform.

### MKT.Reference:1.2 - Connect the functions without prescribing departments or a funnel

Work backwards from useful use to the contributions it requires, and forwards from each proposed contribution to its receiving activity. A person may need to discover an unfamiliar alternative, compare it, obtain permission, acquire access, prepare for use and receive help. Another person may already know exactly what to buy. The first situation can need several kinds of work; the second can need only a functioning ordinary purchase. Their relationship is conditional, not a compulsory sequence of stages.

Marketing contributes the investigation and construction that connects these activities: which situation to serve, what useful difference to offer, how people can encounter and judge it, and what arrangements make it obtainable and sustainable. In an advertising arrangement, a sponsor obtains a medium's contribution to presenting a message. The media work determines available contexts, expressions, placement controls and observations. MKT.3 and the intermediary application connect that contribution to the supported offer and the work receiving a response. Exposure supplies an occasion for attention; it does not supply the customer's decision.

Sales work helps a particular customer compare, decide and complete an acquisition on usable terms. MKT.4 distinguishes interpretation, suitability, authority, concern and transition because each can require a different response. In a major account, choosing criteria, negotiating a package and enabling implementation are consequential constructions. In an ordinary repeat purchase, those questions may already be settled. A commercial agreement can establish obligations while leaving the work, access or capability needed to fulfil them absent.

Distribution makes the offering obtainable where, when and on the conditions that the intended use requires. It can include selecting intermediaries, locating availability, allocating selling and support work, arranging transfer or access, and connecting orders, payment, information, returns and recovery. Physical delivery is one possible contribution. A digital service also needs an effective route to authorised access and support. MKT.11 connects these commercial requirements to an actual provider arrangement; MKT.2 and MKT.4 receive the resulting offer and acquisition conditions.

A channel therefore needs its object stated. The route by which somebody encounters a claim can differ from the route through which they buy and receive the contribution. One platform can provide both; one retailer can advertise, sell, hold stock and support use. Recover the actual relations instead of assigning all these functions from its name. The provider and a merchant intermediary have one exchange; the intermediary and its customer can have another. Each exchange needs its own offer, feasible performance and grounds for interpreting observations.

These functions need not become separate departments, firms or levels of a system. One person can perform several, and several organisations can supply one. Decide that arrangement from the required results, authority, capability, timing and conflicts in the work. Marketing also depends on engineering, economics, operations, learning and community practice. Their contributions retain their own objects and conditions; commercial importance does not turn their Methods into sales techniques.

### MKT.Reference:1.3 - Ground knowledge and change ways of working

A useful account explains what is known, how it was obtained and what decision it supports. A customer's account can reveal a purpose, interpretation or reported difficulty. Observation can establish that an activity or event occurred. A provider can answer for a capability or commitment within its authority. Calculation can expose consequences under stated assumptions. A causal conclusion needs evidence and assumptions that distinguish the intervention's contribution from competing explanations. MKT.12 obtains a qualified answer; MKT.10 handles the causal and full-cost comparison when the decision requires it.

A reusable method needs more than a successful instance. Explain the relation that made the result possible and the conditions on which it depended. For example, local availability helps an urgent replenishment need only if the right item is present, can be identified and can be obtained in time. More exposure cannot supply missing stock. An assisted trial can support meaningful choice only if the promised assistance and required customer participation are available. Its purchase count cannot by itself establish the participant's later competence or benefit.

Develop alternatives from what the situation leaves unresolved. MKT.6 can help construct a useful result; MKT.2 compares proposed contributions and positioning; MKT.9 obtains or constructs a fitting variant; MKT.11 obtains the necessary joint provision. Use existing adequate results directly. Where no supported arrangement exists, engineering constructs alternatives with the receiving use and provider conditions together. Economic reasoning compares exchanges and consequential terms; the relevant authority establishes permissions. Select an arrangement by the whole result and burden, then obtain the decisions and work that make it effective.

Practice returns evidence to the account. A failed acquisition may expose an inaccessible payment route rather than an unattractive offer. A successful sale followed by unused access can expose missing preparation, a changed purpose or an unsuitable contribution. Investigate the difference before generalising. It may require a local remedy, a changed method, a revised customer group or a narrower claim about where the method works. Repeated use and maintained counterexamples improve the reusable account; repeating the same procedure does not establish its generality.

The purpose of an intervention also remains open to judgement. Provider return, customer benefit, voluntary participation, learning and effects on others are distinct concerns. Evidence that a technique increases purchase cannot decide whether its burden, disclosure, permission or treatment of refusal is acceptable. The ethical distinctions in §6 constrain the alternatives throughout this work.

### MKT.Reference:1.4 - Return the whole account to different situations

Consider a few changes that require different continuations. A known consumable needed today makes verified availability and a workable purchase decisive. If the local seller loses stock, MKT.11's changed supply answer returns to MKT.2's promise and MKT.3's invitation; buying more attention cannot preserve “available today”. A complex service with several decision makers can instead require MKT.4's comparison and negotiation, followed by actual assignments and access. When permission is missing, another explanation of the already understood benefit supplies no substitute.

A platform that changes which parts of a message it can omit changes the advertising arrangement. The intermediary application returns to the family of possible meanings and actual controls before choosing a route. A learning offer raises another relation: the buyer's agreement, the learner's participation and acquired capability are different results. The learning application obtains HCD and community contributions where needed and allows an informed refusal or a separate personal route.

Finally, success in a few supported cases can leave ordinary repetition unresolved. MKT.13 asks what acquisition, provision, benefit and economics depend on, including exceptional help. MKT.14 may make common preparation worthwhile, but a new organisation's access restriction or a competing specialist obligation can defeat the larger programme. Return that fact to the whole comparison and revise the affected promises. These returns are how a connected marketing account changes decisions in its parts.
