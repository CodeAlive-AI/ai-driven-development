## MKT.8 - Construct a Journey Map for a Specific Decision

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.8:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when understanding an activity across several contacts, people or periods could change a marketing decision. A customer may understand an offer but fail to obtain access, encounter conflicting promises from two providers, or return to an earlier question after beginning use. The map helps locate the connection that matters and choose an attainable change or a precise inquiry.

A journey map is a selective representation of the relevant activity and encounters, including their order, conditions and possible continuations. Its result is useful when someone can act on the represented difference. It can describe an observed history, compare possible future experiences or support a proposed intervention; keep these uses distinguishable.

The intended reader knows the activity and can obtain accounts from the relevant participants and providers. A known local defect with a sufficient direct remedy does not require a map. The method does not make the marketer responsible for a person's whole life, learning, employment or activity with other organisations.

### MKT.8:2 - Problem

A familiar stage diagram can hide the very difference that matters: the purchaser is not the user, contacts occur outside the provider's channels, several conditions coexist, or the customer returns and changes direction. A map can also mix what happened with what the team hopes will happen. An arrow then appears to explain a transition whose enabling work is absent.

Build the representation from a decision and qualified accounts of the relevant activity. Keep actors, observations, interpretations and proposed work recoverable. Use the map to obtain a change in the actual arrangement or an answer to the unresolved question.

### MKT.8:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the map must retain |
| --- | --- |
| A concise view is easier to use; omitted work can determine the result. | The connections that can change the decision and the consequential unknowns. |
| A shared account helps coordination; people's histories differ. | Differences that defeat a common intervention. |
| Sequence is visible; causation and feasible transition need more. | The conditions and actual work connecting events. |
| Providers can improve some conditions and only observe others. | Control, negotiated influence and conditions outside their reach. |
| A plan gives direction; customers can take another route. | Possible return, refusal, pause and alternative continuation. |

### MKT.8:4 - Solution

#### MKT.8:4.1 - Begin with the decision the map should change

Name the useful result at issue, whose activity receives it and which decision can change that activity. For example: should the provider change an invitation, obtain an access contribution, redesign the support handover or stop an unsuitable offer? Determine who will use the answer and what difference would lead them to another action.

Choose a sufficient reach in time and participants. Start far enough before the apparent problem to include its prerequisites, and continue far enough afterward to see the receiving use. Include a third-party approval, a customer's preparation or a later support need when it can change the decision. Stop expanding where an added contact cannot affect the present choice.

Select the intended use of the representation. A history asks what occurred. A possible route asks what could occur under stated conditions. A plan asks which actions someone will undertake. These accounts can be compared, but do not let the proposed route fill gaps in the historical evidence.

#### MKT.8:4.2 - Recover activity, encounters and consequential differences

Use sufficient existing accounts first. When evidence is missing, [MKT.12](27-mkt-12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-dec.md) supplies the decision-specific inquiry. Ask relevant participants about concrete occasions and connect their accounts to available work or contact evidence. Preserve who reported what, when, and what remains uncertain.

Recover the customer's own actions as well as encounters with the provider. Ask what the person was trying to accomplish, what they used, who else contributed, what had to become available and what happened when a condition failed. An activity need not produce a contact visible to the firm. Advice from a colleague or an internal budget decision can matter more than another visit to a page.

Distinguish the people doing, choosing, paying, approving and receiving the result. A purchaser's approval can occur while a user's access is still absent. For example, having the prerequisite skills, having access to instruction and wanting to buy can occur in different combinations. Do not assign a single stage to the whole customer when that would conceal the needed condition.

Compare more than one history when the planned intervention depends on a common route. Group histories only when their differences leave the proposed intervention suitable. Keep a separate branch or a narrower population when another person's circumstances change the work required. An account assembled from several people is a synthesis, not the observed history of one typical person.

#### MKT.8:4.3 - Choose a representation that exposes the live connection

Use the smallest form that makes the decision understandable. A short story can recover a sequence; a table can compare actors, conditions and evidence; a diagram can show alternative continuations or overlapping work. A graphical template is useful only if it preserves the distinctions needed here.

Make the meaning of the representation explicit. Identify the actors or activities represented by rows or nodes, what an arrow means, and how a reader can distinguish observed, reported, inferred and proposed content. An arrow for time order does not assert a cause; an arrow for a planned handover does not establish that the receiving team can perform it.

At each relevant connection, retain the following content in whatever form serves the reader:

- the activity or encounter and the participant concerned;
- the useful result or condition sought there;
- what is observed, reported, inferred or proposed there, with the relevant sources and limits;
- the prerequisite, response or contribution needed for a continuation; and
- the party able to change that condition, or the fact that it lies outside the provider's control.

Add reported experience or evaluation when the decision concerns it. Keep these separate from an observer's account of performance. A long wait can be observed; whether it felt reassuring, frustrating or acceptable needs an appropriate account from the person. If recollection concerns the whole service, do not distribute that answer across individual contacts as though each had been measured.

#### MKT.8:4.4 - Check the proposed explanation at the consequential connections

Read the representation from the receiving activity, not only along the provider's preferred sequence. Look for an unexplained transition, missing participant, external contribution, simultaneous condition or return to an earlier question. Compare the picture with the underlying accounts and ask the relevant participants to correct consequential errors where possible.

Challenge the connection that would justify the intended intervention. If the map suggests that a customer stopped after a message, ask what establishes the reason and what else changed. Temporal proximity can direct an inquiry without identifying a causal effect. If an arrow implies a handover, recover what is transferred, who receives it, what they need and what happens if they cannot act.

Distinguish a missing observation from evidence that an event did not occur. A provider's record can omit contact with another firm or the customer's own work. Where that unknown can change the intervention, obtain the needed answer or qualify the decision. Filling the gap with a standard funnel stage would conceal it.

When the representation is about possible futures, test whether each proposed continuation has the needed contributions. Include an ordinary case and an action-changing variation, such as absent access, a changed goal or unavailable support. Retain a feasible current arrangement among alternatives. A route that requires a new service returns a construction question to MKT.9 and its engineering suppliers.

#### MKT.8:4.5 - Select an intervention, inquiry or justified non-change

Locate a change the responsible people can actually make. Describe the failed connection and compare suitable alternatives: revise the explanation, change an interface, obtain an approval, arrange help, alter supply, narrow the offer or leave an adequate arrangement in place. Include the customer's effort and the burden moved to other contributors.

Name the receiving method and result. [MKT.3](18-mkt-3---reach-a-suitable-audience-and-obtain-a-meaningful-re.md) can change communication and its response path; [MKT.4](19-mkt-4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible.md) can make a choice or transition feasible; [MKT.5](20-mkt-5---develop-and-align-a-customer-experience-concept.md) can align the experience conditions; [MKT.6](21-mkt-6---jointly-shape-a-useful-result-and-help-achieve-it.md) can reshape the useful result and its support. MKT.11 coordinates the required contributions. Use the located problem and its supporting evidence to make the needed change with the selected method and responsible contributors.

If a consequential cause or condition remains unknown, return a focused question instead of an intervention presented as a known remedy. If the decision requires a causal or resource comparison, MKT.10 develops it. An adequate map can also support retaining the existing arrangement when the apparent defect disappears under a better account.

#### MKT.8:4.6 - Revise from actual use without rewriting the past

After a selected change, obtain evidence of the altered arrangement and the receiving result at appropriate times. Compare it with the intended continuation. Keep the earlier history and the new observation distinguishable; a revised plan does not change what the earlier customer encountered.

Update only the reach affected by new evidence, a different customer situation or changed provider ability. State what the map can now support and what remains outside. Retire a view that no longer serves a decision rather than maintaining every contact indefinitely.

### MKT.8:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.8:5.1 - An apparently abandoned trial

In a constructed reporting-service case, a dashboard shows that Sever's purchaser read an invitation but no trial was booked. The team considers sending another invitation. Its live decision is whether to change communication or resolve a different condition before inviting again.

The provider reconstructs the relevant interval from the purchaser, operator and available correspondence. The purchaser understood the assisted scope and forwarded it internally. The operator found a suitable sample but could not obtain the required permission in time. A colleague recommended waiting for the next reporting period. That advice occurred outside the provider's channels.

| Relevant part of the account | Kind and grounds | Consequence for the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invitation received and forwarded | Correspondence supports these actions. | Another copy of the same explanation may add little. |
| Operator prepared a candidate sample | The operator reports the preparation; the sample's suitability is separately checked. | The operator's preparation and the purchaser's contact with the provider were separate actions. |
| Permission remained unavailable before the session window | The responsible authority confirms the unresolved condition. | The provider cannot offer that trial as immediately feasible. |
| Colleague advised waiting | Purchaser's account of an external encounter. | The observed pause is not evidence that the offer was misunderstood. |
| Later approved sample and session | Proposed continuation, not part of the observed history. | Its feasibility depends on actual permission and available support. |

The provider clarifies the permission question and offers a later session if the necessary contributions become available. MKT.4 handles the practical transition; MKT.11 obtains a support answer. If the purchaser instead confirms that unattended integration is essential, the receiving problem changes to offer suitability through MKT.2; resolving the sample permission would no longer rescue this proposal.

The provider records whether the later trial actually occurs and what its result establishes. It does not label the earlier pause as a completed trial or treat this one account as a universal route for all customers.

#### MKT.8:5.2 - A learner's activity exceeds a supplier's course

A learner combines a course, workplace practice, advice from colleagues and a later change of project. A provider wants to decide whether to add a practice consultation. The relevant map includes the work opportunity and the learner's own attempts because they determine whether consultation can help. It does not turn the course sequence into the person's whole learning history. HCD supplies the learning intervention; if the actual obstacle is absence of suitable work, another lesson may be the wrong contribution.

### MKT.8:6 - Bias-Annotation

Provider records favour visible contacts and successful completions. Teams can also make a synthesized persona unusually cooperative. Seek the action-changing work outside those records and preserve refusals, returns and unresolved conditions. Use a different history to challenge a claimed common route where that difference could alter the intervention.

### MKT.8:7 - Conformance Checklist

- A named decision determines the map's people, time reach and useful detail.
- Observed history, interpretation, possible continuation and planned work remain distinguishable.
- Different actors, customer work, relevant external encounters and unknowns are represented where they affect the decision.
- Arrows and grouping have explicit meanings that do not silently assert cause or control.
- A selected change has a responsible contributor and an attainable receiving result, or the missing question is returned.
- Later observations revise the applicable account without rewriting earlier events.

### MKT.8:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Fill a standard funnel before finding the problem. | Start from the decision and retain the activity that could change it. |
| Treat several people's accounts as one observed customer. | Mark the synthesis and preserve consequential differences. |
| Turn a missing record into proof that nothing happened. | Preserve the unknown and obtain a needed external account. |
| Infer a cause from the order of boxes. | Establish the needed causal basis through MKT.10 or return a narrower explanation. |
| Announce an improved journey after redrawing its map. | Obtain the actual changes and evidence of their receiving use. |

### MKT.8:9 - Consequences

A decision can use a more accurate account of the customer's activity and locate the right contributor. The map can also reveal that the provider cannot control the relevant condition. Recovery and maintenance cost effort; the representation is worthwhile only while it helps a live decision. Selectivity remains necessary, so its limits should be visible to later users.

### MKT.8:10 - Architectural Rationale

A journey representation connects accounts of activity to a particular use. History, possible route and plan answer different questions, while the customer remains able to choose and act outside the provider's arrangement. The method therefore joins representation with a receiving decision and actual specialist work rather than making one diagram govern every practice.

### MKT.8:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For the reach of the account, **adopt** the broader contextual view in [Becker and Jaakkola, 2020, Premise 2](https://pure.hanken.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/42844202/2020_Becker_Jaakkola.pdf), together with the distributed activity discussed in [the journey source](https://ailev.livejournal.com/1740317.html). These contributions challenge provider-only contact sequences. They do not prohibit all advance planning or establish that a pictured sequence causes a result.

For turning a representation into useful work, **adapt** the journey-representing and subsequent action connection in [Customer experience orientation, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-024-01031-y). The present method makes the receiving decision, evidence distinctions and actual intervention explicit. A simple sufficient account is cheaper when no wider connection changes the decision. Reopen the map when a changed participant, condition or goal defeats its intended use.

### MKT.8:12 - Relations

MKT.12 supplies qualified accounts; MKT.1 distinguishes customer situations; MKT.2 supplies the proposed offer. MKT.3 receives communication questions, MKT.4 choice questions, MKT.5 questions about experience conditions, and MKT.6 questions about the useful result and help. MKT.7 receives continuation questions, MKT.9 questions about the offer variant, and MKT.11 provision questions. MKT.10 handles causal contribution and cost, while MKT.14 can connect several opportunities within an organisation.

A map can locate the needed learning, engineering and operating contributions. Obtain and check their results through the responsible practices.
