## MKT.12 - Investigate a Customer or Market Question for a Decision

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.12:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a product, offer or customer decision depends on something you do not yet understand about people's work, choice, use or experience. Perhaps customers praise a service but stop using it, apparently similar buyers choose differently, or a team cannot decide which difficulty to address. Start with the receiving decision as far as it is understood. If its relevant alternatives or distinctions are not yet known, develop them from an actual customer episode through §4.2–§4.3 before asking which answer would change the choice.

The useful result is an answer with a stated reach, or a precisely bounded unanswered question, delivered to someone who can use it. That can come from existing information, ordinary information gathering, or a deliberately designed inquiry. A new survey is not the default result.

The intended reader understands the product or service and can arrange a competent conversation, observation and basic analysis. A specialised sampling, measurement or causal claim needs the corresponding expertise. This pattern supplies the marketing question and the connections between inquiry and action; it does not confer interviewing skill or access to private material.

Use an existing sufficient answer directly. Repair an already established service error through the responsible operating practice. If the decision concerns the effect caused by an intervention, qualify that causal question separately; a customer's account of an experience can inform it without answering it.

### MKT.12:2 - Problem

An organisation can collect abundant feedback without learning what its next decision requires. A buyer's stated preference may concern acquisition rather than use. A score after delivery may omit a difficulty that appears later. A striking interview may reveal a possible explanation while leaving its prevalence unknown. Combining these observations into one customer metric loses the question each can answer.

Construct the inquiry from the receiving decision and the available customer material. When the decision is still poorly understood, examining an actual attempt can first reveal the relevant difficulty and alternatives. When those are established, work backward to the answer needed, then forward from obtainable observations to a warranted answer. Keep the work being studied, the person reporting it, the observation and the inference distinct.

### MKT.12:3 - Forces

| Tension | Consequence for the inquiry |
| --- | --- |
| A decision needs an answer soon; the full situation may be extensive. | Seek the smallest answer that can distinguish the live actions, and state what it leaves unresolved. |
| People can describe their situations; memory, incentives and question wording affect their reports. | Keep an attributed report separate from an observed event and an inferred explanation. |
| Common measures permit comparison; unlike meanings make the comparison misleading. | Establish a comparable activity, role, period and interpretation before aggregating. |
| Broad coverage costs attention and access. | Include a contrasting or missing case when it can change the conclusion, rather than collecting everything available. |
| Evidence must reach action; the investigator may not own that action. | Arrange the recipient and decision before collecting more data. |

### MKT.12:4 - Solution

#### MKT.12:4.1 - Define the receiving question and try the existing answer

Name who must decide and when. If the relevant difficulty or alternatives cannot yet be stated, select an accessible episode through §4.2 and examine the actual attempt through §4.3, or the matching observation route. Recover what the person tried to achieve, the operations and conditions involved, and a relevant contrast. That work can produce the previously unknown distinction and candidate alternatives. Bound it by the material, attainable contribution and participant burden; it need not promise a particular discovery. Return what it establishes to this comparison.

When alternatives can be stated, name which remain live and what answer would change the choice. Replace “understand our customers” with, for example, “decide whether the next offer needs an easier acquisition route or a different way of performing the work”. Ask what observation would distinguish those alternatives. If the obtained grounds leave no plausible answer that could change the bounded move, revise the question or stop collecting. Not yet having grounds to formulate alternatives is the earlier construction question, not evidence for this stop.

Inspect available service records, previous inquiries, current customer accounts and relevant external sources. Check their subject, period and conditions before reuse. An old answer about individual buyers may not answer a current question about organisations with a separate access authority. Conversely, age alone does not defeat an answer whose conditions still hold.

Use sufficient information. When a missing fact can be obtained by a known suitable operation, obtain that fact and check its meaning. For a surviving non-routine knowledge question, take the question and existing grounds to [RMP.1](../../rmp/31-rmp-1-decide-whether-a-question-needs-research-and-when-to-s.md). Its qualification can return an existing answer, adjacent work, a missing-input hold or a research brief. Calling an activity customer research does not determine which result is appropriate.

#### MKT.12:4.2 - Choose the object, people and kind of answer

Distinguish the person performing the work, selecting a solution, paying, permitting access and living with its effects. They may be one person or several. Name the relevant episode or continuing activity as well as the people. “The company wants less effort” hides whose effort and which decision can establish a commitment.

Choose the inquiry from the unresolved distinction:

| Question that can change the decision | Material and inquiry | Permitted first result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which results of an understood activity are poorly served? | Observe and discuss performance with its executors; establish desired results, then compare their importance and present attainment where comparable measurement is possible. | Candidate improvements and differences between customers; use §4.3. |
| Why did someone change, decline or remain with an alternative? | Reconstruct an actual choice episode, including circumstances, considered alternatives and the difficulty of transition. | An explanatory hypothesis about that choice and a question for contrast; use §4.4. |
| What happens at a contact, during continued use or around a new possibility? | Select observation, conversation and measures over the appropriate interval, with a named recipient. | A bounded account of experience or a new possibility; use §4.5. |
| How widespread is an established difference? | Define the population and a suitable sampling and measurement design. | A population claim with its uncertainty, if the design warrants it. |
| Did our intervention produce the change? | Distinguish the intervention, outcome, comparison and competing explanations; obtain the causal design needed for that claim. | A bounded causal conclusion, or a descriptive result when attribution remains unsupported. |

These routes can inform each other. A choice history can expose the wrong activity boundary; an outcome comparison can reveal a difference worth exploring in a new conversation. The result of one route becomes a question or input for the other, not automatic evidence for its conclusion.

#### MKT.12:4.3 - Investigate results of performing the work

Start with an executor and an activity described independently of a preferred product. Ask the person to walk through a concrete attempt: what started it, what they did, what they tried to achieve, what made success difficult and what happened afterward. Observe the relevant work or inspect its permitted traces where that can challenge the account. Separate a requested feature from the result it is expected to improve.

Use the attempt to sketch what must be accomplished for the activity to succeed. Start with its central work, then recover the preparation and completion it depends on. Ask what each part achieves and whether it is merely a feature of the present solution. "Open spreadsheet A" may serve "establish which source values are valid"; another way of performing the activity can obtain that result. A small map or list helps retain these relations. Keep the boundary tied to the decision instead of expanding into everything the person does.

Challenge the sketch with a materially different attempt, including an interrupted or unsuccessful one when it could expose a missing part. Examine handovers and combinations of tools that the initial account treated as incidental. Ask what was omitted, repeated or repaired and what outcome mattered there. Test a proposed addition against an actual attempt or relevant participant; a suggested possibility alone does not establish an unmet need. If the addition describes another activity, either revise the inquiry's boundary explicitly or leave it outside this answer.

Turn a vague desire into a result that can be interpreted consistently. “Make reporting easy” might conceal time spent locating missing entries, risk of sending an incomplete report, or effort correcting discrepancies. State the direction of improvement, the thing affected and the relevant circumstances. Ask participants to explain the wording in their own situation; split an item when people are rating different things. A product-independent statement helps compare solutions, but it may still omit an important concern.

Check coverage before ranking the surfaced results. For each relevant part of the activity, determine whether the inquiry has a usable account of success, an unresolved question or a reason it cannot affect this decision. Ask what a plausible missing outcome could change: the target group, proposed contribution or next inquiry. Pursue the smallest missing observation when it could alter that choice. If it cannot be obtained, return the affected choice as unresolved or narrow the recommendation to what the evidence supports. When the remaining uncertainty cannot change the bounded next move, stop at that scope; more interviews are not required merely to lengthen the list. Existing sufficient material can supply this comparison without a new mapping exercise.

Once meanings and coverage support the intended comparison, compare importance and present attainment for the people and work under consideration. Keep the two questions distinct. A result can be important and already adequately attained, or poorly attained but irrelevant to the current decision. Inspect combinations within and across cases before using an aggregate. A priority score is a decision aid under its assumptions; it does not prove demand, causal benefit or a stable segment.

Return candidate target results, the activity and contrasting attempts they cover, the relevant differences between executors and their evidence. Identify any unresolved omission that can change the receiving decision and the smaller answer needed to settle it. [MKT.1](16-mkt-1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-cus.md) uses this to qualify customer groups; [MKT.2](17-mkt-2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning.md) uses it to construct an offer. If the activity or meaning of a measure changes, revisit that part of the comparison before carrying forward its ranking.

#### MKT.12:4.4 - Reconstruct a choice and the transition it involved

Select an actual episode rather than a hypothetical favourite. Ask for the sequence from the earlier situation through first concern, search, comparison, decision and what followed. Where did the person first consider a change? What was happening then? Which alternatives were genuinely available? What made one acceptable and another difficult? Preserve uncertainty about dates or reasons instead of silently completing a smooth story.

Use concrete episodes and permitted documents to probe the account. “We chose it because it was modern” may lead to a more useful account of an upcoming deadline, a trusted colleague's advice, a procurement restriction or fear of losing a valued way of working. Functional, emotional and social considerations can coexist. Keep each attributed to the participant and supported at the strength available; do not diagnose motives that the inquiry cannot establish.

Include a contrasting case when it can defeat the explanation: someone who stayed, delayed, chose another supplier or did not complete a transition. Compare the circumstances that differ, not only the words people use. A possible explanation of several episodes can guide an offer or acquisition change. Estimating how often it applies needs additional grounds.

[MKT.4](19-mkt-4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible.md) consumes the actual transition difficulty. If the episode shows that the person seeks a different result altogether, return to the problem and offer rather than treating every objection as a barrier to remove.

#### MKT.12:4.5 - Match the observation window to the experience question

For a specific transaction, collect information close enough to recover the relevant event and send an actionable failure to the operating recipient. For a continuing relationship, observe use and changes over a period that can reveal the issue; a pleasant purchase does not settle later usefulness. For a new possibility, use a bounded exploration whose observations can change the proposed contribution. These are different choices of cadence and recipient, not obligatory successive phases.

Select the combination of observation, conversation and measures from what each can expose. A log may show that an action stopped without showing why. A conversation may explain a perceived difficulty without measuring its frequency. A rating records a response under particular wording and conditions. State what each contributes before joining them.

Schedule inquiry around the decision and the participant's burden. Combine requests when their meanings remain clear; stop repetitive questioning when it no longer changes the answer. Give urgent operational failures their own response rather than making them wait for a periodic research report.

#### MKT.12:4.6 - Obtain and interpret enough evidence

Choose cases that can distinguish the live explanations, including access failures or non-users when their absence would bias the answer. An initial purposive set can discover differences. A population estimate needs a defensible population and sampling design; a convenient set of willing customers does not acquire representativeness by growing larger.

Arrange permitted access, understandable participation and competent inquiry. Pilot consequential questions on a small attainable case to find ambiguity before wider collection. Keep the question wording, relevant context and source of observations recoverable in the ordinary working material. Retain only information needed for the stated use under the applicable conditions. Obtain specialist advice where access, rights or a statistical claim depends on it.

During analysis, separate observed events, participant reports and your inferences. Compare cases and examine contrary observations. Ask whether a difference could come from the way information was obtained: selective response, changed wording, interviewer prompting, a different period or an incentive to give a favourable answer. A plausible explanation is useful as a hypothesis when it guides the next discriminating move; it need not be inflated into a general law.

#### MKT.12:4.7 - Return an answer that changes the work

Deliver the answer in the recipient's decision terms: what was learned, about whom and under which conditions; which alternative it supports or defeats; what remains unknown; and what action or smaller inquiry is now warranted. Show the evidence needed to challenge a consequential inference. A concise note can suffice; no particular report format makes the inference valid.

The answer can support proceeding, changing the offer, repairing supply, retaining an existing choice or stopping. If the receiver cannot act, resolve the missing recipient or authority instead of maintaining an unused measurement programme. Reopen only the affected question when customer work, roles, circumstances, measurement meaning or the decision changes.

### MKT.12:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.12:5.1 - Positive onboarding scores, little subsequent use

Consider a stipulated supplier of a file-transfer service. The team wants to choose between simplifying its purchase form and changing the service. A support record already shows that one customer cannot obtain an access approval. That case goes to the responsible access and service participants; another satisfaction survey cannot supply the approval.

For the remaining question, the investigator follows permitted reporting attempts with an operator and reconstructs two contrasting buying episodes. Operators in both cases want less repeated entry. One purchaser can approve a short trial; the other needs a purchasing window and is reluctant to replace a familiar review process. The first observation concerns execution, the second set concerns acquisition and transition. They do not support one undifferentiated “easy to use” score.

The investigator also checks the work preceding entry against a month-end attempt with corrected source values. "Copy the spreadsheet" had hidden a preparation task: establish which values remain valid. The operator and the correction record confirm that a later revision can make an apparently complete report wrong. The additional desired result is reducing the chance of using a superseded value. It could change the service choice, so the inquiry adds that question rather than ranking duplicate-entry effort alone. The delivery owner must now establish whether the proposed operation can recognise a revised source or requires a separate customer check.

The returned answer is bounded: retain reduced entry and valid source selection as offer hypotheses, provide a separate acquisition route for the purchasing window, and investigate whether the familiar review can be retained. It does not estimate the market proportions of these cases. MKT.1 can now keep the source-validation and purchasing conditions visible, MKT.2 can construct a trial around those unresolved contributions, and MKT.4 can make its next decision feasible.

#### MKT.12:5.2 - Change the decision and the needed inquiry

Now management asks whether the trial reduced total reporting time. The earlier accounts do not identify that causal effect: workload or another process change may have altered the result. Define the comparison and competing explanations before choosing an evaluation design. Existing observations remain useful for constructing that question; they are not upgraded into its answer.

In a learning-service case, the paying employer's reason for enrolling staff can be studied through the same question discipline. Whether each learner can subsequently perform the work needs an HCD task and observation under stated assistance conditions. A favourable buying story cannot supply that capability judgement.

#### MKT.12:5.3 - Find the missing question before comparing offers

Suppose the same service team has only praise after onboarding and little subsequent use; it cannot yet formulate useful alternatives. With permission, it follows an operator's reporting attempt. Entry itself is quick, but the operator spends most of the attempt checking which source values have been superseded. A contrasting attempt uses already confirmed values and finishes without that repeated checking. These episodes reveal a candidate difficulty: obtaining valid inputs, rather than entering them faster.

The team can now compare supplying source validation with retaining the existing validation arrangement, and ask which version information each requires. The observations justify that local question; they do not establish its market prevalence or the causal effect of a new service. If the relevant source history cannot be obtained, retain the discovered difficulty and leave the affected offer comparison unresolved. The specification or a demonstrated use of an existing validation service may already show which version information it requires; use that answer without another interview.

### MKT.12:6 - Bias-Annotation

Easy-to-reach customers, recent complaints and vivid successful transitions can dominate the account. Look for the case whose absence could reverse the decision. A supplier can also prefer the question its existing dashboard can answer. Begin with the decision before deciding which available measure to use.

An AI-generated synthesis can help find passages or propose contrasts. Keep its claims connected to obtainable source material and inspect meaning, disagreement and omitted cases. Fluency supplies no missing observation.

### MKT.12:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The receiving decision, live alternatives and answer condition are recoverable.
- Existing sufficiency, ordinary information gathering and non-routine inquiry remain distinct possible outcomes.
- The selected people, activity, period and evidence can answer the declared question at the strength claimed. Outcome comparison includes a challenge for omitted parts of the activity and a decision-relative sufficiency or unresolved return.
- Result investigation, choice history, experience observation and causal evaluation are not silently substituted for each other.
- Contrary evidence, relevant missing cases, participant burden and conditions of data use can change the inference or next action.
- A named recipient receives the answer, its limits and an actionable continuation or stop.

### MKT.12:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Ask what features people want, then call the list their underlying work. | Recover the actual attempt and expected result before deciding which feature could contribute. |
| Explain a choice from the first answer to “why?”. | Reconstruct the episode and compare an action-changing contrast. |
| Add more post-transaction ratings to explain long-term non-use. | Choose an observation window that includes use and the relevant outside conditions. |
| Turn a few interviews into a market percentage or intervention effect. | Keep the explanatory hypothesis; obtain the distinct design required for the stronger conclusion. |

### MKT.12:9 - Consequences

The team can obtain a useful answer without automatically commissioning a study. When inquiry is needed, its effort goes toward a distinction that changes work. This can also reveal that no answer is presently obtainable within the access, competence or time available. Preserve that limit in the decision rather than reporting certainty because a questionnaire was completed.

### MKT.12:10 - Architectural Rationale

The decision-to-evidence-to-action connection belongs together because an otherwise competent data-collection operation can answer the wrong question. Different inquiry routes remain available because their units and warranted conclusions differ. They can be composed through explicit new questions without a universal sequence or a single meaning of “job”.

A full research method, specialist measurement or causal design remains with its supplier. The local contribution is selecting and interpreting those results for a marketing decision, including the sufficient-answer stop and the return to a changed offer or service.

### MKT.12:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For choosing an inquiry, the selected line is question-specific construction with explicit limits. **Adapt** [Strategyn's public ODI account, steps 1–5](https://strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation/) for comparing results of an identified activity, and the [Christensen Institute's current JTBD account](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) for reconstructing choice in circumstances. They are useful rival entries for different questions, not interchangeable definitions. This changes §§4.2–4.4. The linked [needs explanation](https://strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation/understanding-customer-needs/) and [job-map construction](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/customer-centered-innovation-map/) inform the coverage challenge in §4.3; its stopping rule remains relative to the receiving decision. Supplier success claims, permanent outcome stability and population-wide causation are not adopted. Reopen the choice when the activity or explanation no longer fits the observed cases.

For cadence and receiving action, **adapt** [Meyer and Schwager, 2007, pp. 6–11](https://zimmer.csufresno.edu/~sasanr/Teaching-Material/MIS/SIS/understanding-customer-experience.pdf): transaction, relationship and exploratory questions call for different arrangements. This historical construction remains useful in §4.5 because a uniform survey misses their different time spans. Keep the later [Becker and Jaakkola, 2020, premises 1, 2 and 4](https://pure.hanken.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/42844202/2020_Becker_Jaakkola.pdf) distinction between provider conditions and a customer's response. The added design effort is justified when that distinction changes the decision; adequate ordinary information is the lighter alternative. These sources do not establish the effectiveness of this combined method.
