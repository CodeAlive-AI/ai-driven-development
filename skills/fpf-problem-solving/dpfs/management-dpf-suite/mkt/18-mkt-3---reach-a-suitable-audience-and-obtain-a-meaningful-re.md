## MKT.3 - Reach a Suitable Audience and Obtain a Meaningful Response

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.3:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a useful offer or invitation is not reaching the people for whom it could matter, when recipients infer a different proposal, or when attention produces no usable continuation. It covers selecting where to meet those people, constructing an appropriate message and invitation, making the response possible, and learning from a bounded attempt.

The result is a feasible way to reach a specified audience with a truthful proposal, together with the observed response and a justified continuation. A meaningful response supplies something relevant to that continuation: a request, an answer about a condition, an informed choice or a refusal. A click can be useful evidence of an action without establishing any of these meanings.

The intended reader knows the proposed contribution and can arrange its receiving work. A qualified situation from [MKT.1](16-mkt-1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-cus.md) and an understood offer from [MKT.2](17-mkt-2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning.md) are sufficient starting points. A known customer who has requested a quotation usually needs that quotation through the agreed means; a new audience study or communication campaign would add unnecessary work.

### MKT.3:2 - Problem

A large audience may contain few people able to use the offer. A suitable person may encounter the message at an unsuitable moment, misunderstand its category, lack a feasible response, or reply to a team that cannot continue. Treating all these failures as insufficient exposure leads to more messages without resolving the broken connection.

Construct the connection from the recipient's situation through the message and possible response to the work that receives it. Test the uncertainty that could change that construction. Keep exposure, understanding, response and later use distinct.

### MKT.3:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the construction must preserve |
| --- | --- |
| Reach can grow faster than relevance. | The circumstances that make a person an appropriate recipient. |
| A short message must compete for attention. | The contribution and any condition that would change its meaning. |
| Different places support different forms of participation. | The recipient's expectations and the permitted form of contact. |
| A response can arrive before delivery capacity is available. | An attainable next step and an honest account of its timing. |
| A small attempt gives useful clues but incomplete evidence. | What was observed, what remains unknown and the decision that can use the result. |

### MKT.3:4 - Solution

#### MKT.3:4.1 - Choose whose response could change the next action

Start with the activity, difficulty and conditions that make the contribution useful. Identify the people who encounter that difficulty, the people who can authorize a relevant next step, and the people who might introduce them. These can be different audiences with different invitations. An introduction from a trusted colleague does not establish that the recipient needs the offer.

State what response is useful now and why. A new service might need to learn whether a proposed input can be made available; an established offer might need an appointment with a qualified purchaser. Asking for a purchase before the parties understand a necessary condition can conceal the very information needed to construct the offer. If the useful result itself is still contested, use MKT.6 before making a definite promise.

Define the boundary of the attempt: which recipients, situation, period and volume can be served, what preparation is available, and what would warrant continuing or stopping. Base the boundary on the consequential uncertainty and capacity, not a standard number of messages. An existing answer can justify direct execution without a new probe.

#### MKT.3:4.2 - Find a fitting place of attention and an attainable route

Locate where the intended people already seek help, exchange relevant information or make the decision. Ask how the subject is encountered there: a requested email, professional meeting, search result, public explanation, referral or another appropriate contact. Recover evidence from actual customer accounts, existing communication or a bounded inquiry; the team's preferred platform is only a candidate.

Compare candidate routes on the same receiving situation. Consider whether the relevant people can be reached, whether the context makes the message intelligible and credible, what explanation the format permits, what contact is allowed, and what effort a response requires. Include preparation, distribution and the work of receiving replies. A route with fewer contacts can be preferable when it reaches the responsible people at a usable moment.

Select one adequate route or a small set whose roles are clear. A public explanation may enable later recognition while a requested conversation resolves a specific condition. Do not force every recipient through both. If no available route reaches suitable people, return a concrete access question or reconsider the audience; broader distribution does not repair missing access by itself.

Distinguish the route of attention from the route of acquisition and provision. A useful advertisement may direct a customer to a retailer or service intermediary whose availability, terms or support differ from the provider's own offer. Obtain that arrangement's supported answer through MKT.11 and communicate the attainable next step. A media placement and a distribution agreement can involve the same organisation while requiring different contributions and observations.

#### MKT.3:4.3 - Construct a message whose inferred promise is supportable

Build the message around a recognizable situation and a supported contribution. Give the recipient enough context to understand why it may matter, why the claim deserves consideration and what to do next. Keep a consequential limitation with the claim it qualifies. A short invitation can link to details, but it must not depend on those details to reverse its main promise.

Check the actual expression in its intended format. A heading, illustration, category or omitted condition can imply more than the literal sentence. Compare what the recipient is likely to infer with the offer and supplier's actual ability. Replace an overstated implication or change the contribution through its responsible practice. Presenting an assisted trial as unattended automation changes the inferred offer even when the small print mentions assistance.

When an intermediary combines, omits or generates material, use [the intermediary application](32-mkt-application-2---app-mkt-media---keep-a-supportable-offer.md) to construct a supportable family of expressions with actual controls, observations and receiving work. Approval of separate elements or one preview does not qualify every permitted combination.

Adapt the explanation to the role without inventing different commitments. A purchaser may need scope and consideration, a user may need the required work, and an access authority may need the conditions of a trial. Each should be able to recover a compatible proposal. When an important distinction is unfamiliar, use a concrete comparison rather than more promotional adjectives.

#### MKT.3:4.4 - Make the invitation and its receiving work usable

Specify one proportionate next action and what follows it. Explain the information actually needed, an attainable way to provide it, the expected effort and timing, and who will respond. Provide an appropriate way to decline or defer. Do not request sensitive working material in a public reply when an agreed protected means is needed for the next stage.

Arrange the receiving contribution before distributing the invitation: the responsible person, access to the necessary information, capacity, and a fallback when the proposed next step cannot occur. A message promising a consultation needs available consultations. If only a later place is available, state that timing and let the recipient decide whether it is still useful.

Test the response path in the form the recipient will encounter. An inaccessible form, missing appointment or unexplained account requirement can defeat an otherwise suitable message. [MKT.4](19-mkt-4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible.md) develops the subsequent choice and acquisition; this method must at least make the offered transition possible.

#### MKT.3:4.5 - Observe the attempt without merging different answers

Before the attempt, decide which observations can answer the live question. Keep the following distinctions even when a single conversation supplies several observations:

| Observation | What it can support | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery or display | The material reached an address or surface under the recorded conditions. | That the intended person noticed it. |
| Notice or attention | A specified person attended to some material. | That they understood the contribution and its limits. |
| Account of meaning | The person can describe the relevant proposal or distinguish its consequences. | That they prefer it, can acquire it or will benefit. |
| Reply, request or refusal | A particular response occurred, with its stated grounds when available. | The unstated reason for everyone else's silence. |
| Later action | The named appointment, acquisition or use occurred. | That the message caused it or the promised benefit followed. |

Observe meaning by asking a fitting recipient to explain what would be supplied and what they would need to do, or by using another suitable direct task. Do not teach the missing explanation during the probe and then credit the original message with the repaired understanding. A friendly colleague familiar with the product can help with drafting but may not expose the intended audience's inference.

Retain the unit, denominator, period and selection conditions of any count. Distinguish ten replies from ten suitable recipients, and unique people from repeated exposures. If two routes reach different populations at different times, their response rates do not isolate the effect of the route. Use [MKT.12](27-mkt-12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-dec.md) for a missing evidence question and MKT.10 when a causal contribution or cost comparison is needed. A bounded communication attempt does not automatically require either inquiry.

#### MKT.3:4.6 - Repair the connection indicated by the evidence

Locate the consequential mismatch. Evidence of delivery without meaningful attention can change the route or moment; an incorrect account of the promise can change its expression or positioning; a well-understood but unsuitable contribution can reopen the offer or audience; an attempted response blocked by access can change the response operation. Several failures can coexist. Investigate a consequential unknown rather than assigning every silence to the most convenient cause.

Continue, revise or stop at the scale the evidence supports. Retain working parts and change the failed connection. An informed refusal can settle the present invitation; repeated contact is not a remedy for an understood decision to decline. Return the selected route and expression, the feasible next action, the observed result and the limit on its interpretation to the people who will continue the work.

### MKT.3:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.3:5.1 - An invitation to an assisted reporting trial

A provider can help an operations team map one approved file into a periodic report. A staff member at Sever has asked about reducing repeated entry. The provider wants to establish whether an approved sample and an operator's time can be available before offering a trial date. Its first draft says, “Automate your reporting; book a demo.” A purchaser reads that as a promise of unattended recurring reports.

The team changes the invitation to an assisted file-mapping trial for a periodic report. It states that the trial uses one approved sample, needs an operator for one agreed session and does not include continuous integration. The requested reply is whether those conditions can be met or which one needs discussion. A named person can receive the answer and offer one of the sessions actually available. A public upload is unnecessary; sample transfer will use the agreed means if the trial proceeds.

In a constructed probe, the provider uses requested email so contacts can clarify the conditions with colleagues before scheduling a session. It sends individually relevant invitations to eight contacts who have agreed to receive this information. Delivery is confirmed for six addresses. Four people send substantive replies: two can provide the required contributions, one needs access approval, and one declines because continuous integration is essential. Two intended recipients who discuss the message can explain the assisted scope. The remaining contacts' understanding and reasons for silence are unknown. Four substantive replies out of eight invitations describe this attempt; they do not establish market demand or a superior channel.

The provider can arrange the two feasible trials, help clarify the access condition and close the unsuitable invitation. It retains the qualification in later messages. If the operator at Vector is unavailable until the next reporting period, changing the calendar is a possible next step; calling the same trial “effortless automation” would instead conceal a required contribution.

#### MKT.3:5.2 - Learning to recognize an altered promise

A team preparing messages repeatedly adds unsupported implications to a factual service description. An instructor uses [HCD.6.1](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/23-hcd-6-1---help-a-learner-discern-a-needed-distinction-in-pra.md): the learner compares the original with a promotional rewrite, identifies the changed expectation and decides what must be corrected before use. The examples make the consequential difference visible, such as assistance becoming a guarantee of independent success.

The instructor then changes a superficial cue and presents another pair, so success cannot depend solely on the word “guaranteed.” The learner must explain the consequence for this offer. Producing the exercise is different from demonstrating that the learner can make the distinction; success here does not establish general immunity to persuasion, retention or transfer to every kind of message. When no learning problem exists, a competent author can simply correct the expression.

### MKT.3:6 - Bias-Annotation

Teams can overvalue the channel they know, treat every reply as interest, or attribute silence to insufficient pressure. Start with a response that would change a real decision and include informed refusal among useful outcomes. Existing familiarity, prior contact and self-selection can all help an invitation work; preserve those conditions when interpreting the attempt.

### MKT.3:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The receiving situation and the useful response are specific enough to select an appropriate audience and route.
- The message's material implications agree with the supported offer and its conditions.
- A recipient can take, decline or defer the proposed step, and someone can receive the result.
- Delivery, attention, understanding, response and later action remain distinct.
- Observations retain their unit, period, selection and interpretive limits.
- Continuation addresses the supported mismatch or an explicit unknown.

### MKT.3:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Select a popular platform before identifying a receiving situation. | Compare where the relevant people can encounter and use this invitation. |
| Hide a material qualification behind an attractive main claim. | Repair the inferred promise where the expectation is formed. |
| Count any click as demand or meaningful understanding. | Obtain the observation needed for the actual decision. |
| Increase distribution while replies remain unanswered. | Restore the receiving work or reduce the invitation to available capacity. |
| Treat nonresponse as an informed refusal or permission to keep contacting. | Preserve the unknown and the applicable conditions of further contact. |

### MKT.3:9 - Consequences

Attention can lead to a useful, serviceable continuation instead of an unexamined count. The work can reveal an unsuitable audience, offer or route early. Preparation and observing meaning take effort, and a small attempt leaves population and causal questions open. A direct requested communication remains appropriate when those uncertainties do not matter.

### MKT.3:10 - Architectural Rationale

Communication connects people and their situations with a proposed contribution and a possible action. The connection fails in different places, so one exposure measure cannot govern all repairs. Keeping the receiving work inside the construction prevents an invitation from creating a promise that no one can honour.

The method can be used for one message or for repeated communication. Neither use requires a universal sequence of stages: a recipient can ask for details, introduce another responsible person, return later or decline. The continuation follows that response and the supported offer.

### MKT.3:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For intelligibility, **adapt** the alternatives-and-value construction in [Dunford's positioning guide, 2021](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning), with the [2025 distinction between preference and exclusion](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/value-vs-objection-handling). It improves message construction when a familiar category implies an unavailable capability. It does not select a universally best channel or establish that an intelligible message causes acquisition; an existing suitable explanation is cheaper when the comparison is settled.

For the teachable distinction in §5.2, **adapt** the original-versus-promotional-rewrite contrast in [the source discussion](https://ailev.livejournal.com/1738906.html), using HCD.6.1's developed construction and action-changing second example. This gives a bounded learning task instead of promising a fixed number of repetitions or general resistance to influence. Reconsider the exercise when the actual mistake or receiving use changes.

For the whole communication attempt, **synthesize** these contributions with MKT.12's decision-specific evidence and the actual response operation. A distribution-only account leaves meaning and continuation unanswered. The additional work is warranted when either can change the result.

### MKT.3:12 - Relations

MKT.1 supplies the receiving situation; MKT.2 supplies the supported offer; MKT.4 receives a possible choice or acquisition. MKT.6 can establish a useful result before a definite invitation. MKT.12 supplies missing evidence, while MKT.10 addresses causal contribution and cost. MKT.11 can change what the receiving team can promise, and MKT.14 can combine common preparation with different invitations to named organisations.

HCD.6.1 supplies actual instruction when making a distinction is a learning problem. Communication does not establish the learner's capability, the operational result or a customer's later benefit.
