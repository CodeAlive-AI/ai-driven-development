## MKT.11 - Align Promises, Partners and Execution

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.11:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a customer promise depends on several people, teams or organisations, or when customer evidence reveals a mismatch between a promise and actual delivery. Examples include a consultation advertised without available specialists, a trial that needs another organisation's access decision, a service improvement that staff understand but lack the time or authority to perform, or an offering whose intermediary cannot support the promised acquisition, availability or help.

The result is a supported promise joined to effective contributor arrangements and an executable continuation, or a precise limit on what can yet be promised. It includes what happens when a contribution fails and who receives the customer evidence that can change the arrangement.

The intended reader can recover the offer and engage the people responsible for its contributions. A settled ordinary offer with sufficient current supply can use that arrangement directly. This method does not replace specialist engineering, operations, organisational design or the authority to commit another party.

### MKT.11:2 - Problem

Separate teams can each perform a local task while the customer receives an incompatible whole. Marketing describes a result, a partner provides only part of it, and support inherits a problem it was never equipped to solve. Recording a responsible person or sending feedback does not establish the missing contribution.

Trace the promise to the actual work and conditions that make it attainable. Obtain the relevant provider decisions and enabling contributions, resolve conflicts in the whole arrangement and return the supported scope to every expression of the offer. When evidence calls for change, connect it to a responsible decision and performed work.

### MKT.11:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the arrangement must preserve |
| --- | --- |
| A simple customer promise can require many contributions. | The dependencies that determine whether the whole result is attainable. |
| Teams have local goals and authority. | Effective decisions and commitments without treating a meeting as consent by every provider. |
| A provider can be capable but unavailable at the needed time. | Capability, access, assignment and capacity as separate conditions. |
| New experience insight can justify change while current service must continue. | The complete burden of both and a feasible way to recover. |
| Flexibility helps exceptions; unrestricted promises can defeat the service. | Bounded discretion, actual support and a known return when limits are reached. |

### MKT.11:4 - Solution

#### MKT.11:4.1 - Recover the whole promise and the conditions it implies

Take the offer, relevant communication and customer's understood result. Identify what is promised, to whom, when and under which conditions. Include access, support, customer work and later obligations where they change the result. [MKT.2](17-mkt-2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning.md) supplies the offer; [MKT.5](20-mkt-5---develop-and-align-a-customer-experience-concept.md) can reveal conflicting experience conditions; [MKT.6](21-mkt-6---jointly-shape-a-useful-result-and-help-achieve-it.md) supplies the agreed useful contribution.

Read the promise from a concrete use. What would have to happen for the customer to receive the supported result? Recover the required work and contributions, including those performed by the customer or another provider. Include what must happen before a later action can begin and what must remain available while it occurs.

Keep a proposed new offer distinguishable from an existing commitment. A proposal can be narrowed before agreement; an existing obligation requires its applicable change or remedy decision. If the whole service arrangement is missing, obtain its construction through MKT.9 and SYSE.8 before treating a list of willing contributors as sufficient.

#### MKT.11:4.2 - Obtain a real supply answer for each consequential contribution

Ask the responsible contributor what result can be supplied, under which inputs, capability, access, timing, volume, terms and support conditions. Identify the person or organisation that can make the commitment. Separate a possible contribution, a decision to arrange it, an effective arrangement and work already performed.

For a small known service, this can be a short confirmation of the changed condition. For a new or disputed arrangement, recover the needed relations explicitly. [OCE.6](../oce/28-oce-6---establish-holder-assignments-and-enabling-relations.md) establishes actual assignments and their enabling conditions: a named person still needs suitable capability, available time, permission, access and the authority relevant to the work. Obtain missing conditions from their responsible sources rather than assigning them by implication.

Use a competent operating answer for the supported service volume and response conditions. An apparently free calendar does not establish that a team can handle variable arrivals, exceptions or a required service level. If the provider cannot yet answer, keep the dependent promise conditional or choose a smaller contribution whose supply is known.

Do not assume the customer's work merely because it appears in the offer. Obtain the needed participation, preparation and decisions in a form appropriate to the relationship. A payer's approval may not provide a user's time or an access authority's permission.

##### MKT.11:4.2.1 - Construct a distribution arrangement when the route is unsettled

Start from where, when and under what conditions the intended recipient must be able to obtain the contribution. Recover the needed assortment or configuration, means of identifying a suitable option, acquisition, availability, support and recovery. Include customer travel, waiting, preparation and later work where they change usefulness. A route that attracts the intended people can still leave the required purchase or use unavailable.

Construct a few arrangements that could satisfy the same receiving situation. The provider might sell directly and obtain a delivery contribution; a merchant might acquire an offering upstream and make its own downstream offer; an intermediary might arrange a transaction without acquiring the offering; or several parties might combine these contributions. These descriptions name possible relations. Establish which actually hold for the selected arrangement. A marketplace name or the fact that a party collects payment does not by itself identify the seller, the authority to promise support, or the provider of access.

Give [SYSE.8](../../engineering-dpf-suite/syse/29-syse-8---develop-an-integrated-offering-and-provider-concept.md) the needed use, proposed offering, transfer or access conditions and candidate providers. Obtain coherent alternatives whose contributions cover availability, acquisition, use and recovery. For material goods, custody, ownership and exposure to unsold or damaged stock can be consequential different relations. For services, access rights, reserved capacity, activation and continuing support can play the decisive part.

For each exchange, establish who offers what to whom, who can accept or vary it, the relevant consideration and timing, and the obligations that continue afterwards. Use [ECO.3](../../eco/13-eco-3---discover-an-exchange-between-different-plans.md) to connect the parties' different plans. The final user's useful result can make an arrangement worth developing while leaving the intermediary's work or exposure unsupported. Compare terms through [ECO.5](../../eco/15-eco-5---choose-exchange-terms-for-private-information-and-ef.md) when a participant can alter a consequential action or has relevant private information. For example, payment for accepted upstream deliveries can reward stock accumulation without securing availability at the point of use. Investigate the actual alternatives and information before selecting different terms.

Connect the exchanges through actual contributions. Who makes the item or capacity available, receives and confirms an order, supplies it, communicates a changed condition, resolves an unsuitable result and receives useful evidence from use? Establish what each participant can observe and is permitted to share. Distinguish an upstream order, a downstream acquisition, an activation, a return and a useful customer outcome. Settle the necessary meaning and receiving use of each observation before joining counts.

Compare the complete alternatives with the customer's usable result and the full provider burden in view. A nearby intermediary can improve access while adding preparation, stock, coordination and support costs; direct supply can remove one exchange while increasing waiting or customer work. Obtain the availability and capability answers that determine feasibility. Use MKT.13 when ordinary repetition and economics remain in question. Select the supported arrangement, obtain its effective commitments through §4.2, and join them through §4.3. A settled satisfactory route needs only the affected condition checked.

#### MKT.11:4.3 - Join the contributions and resolve the whole conflict

Check the connections from the receiving result through the actual work. For every consequential handover, establish what the receiving party obtains, what makes it usable, who notices an absent or unsuitable result and what continuation follows. Include responsibility for helping the customer when the failure spans teams.

Compare the combined demand at the times and resources that can constrain the promise. Count preparation, customer-specific work, coordination, help, exception handling and recovery as well as the visible delivery. Keep unlike resources separate. Two teams' total hours do not establish availability of the one specialist needed at a particular moment.

When contributions conflict, compare feasible alternatives: change scope or time, choose another provider, obtain a missing capability or access condition, change the arrangement, or decline the unsupported promise. Use [FPF C.32.MWA.PP](../../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/83-c-32-mwa-pp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-p.md) when the choice concerns incompatible combinations of methods, work or allocation. Use the returned comparison where it is sufficient instead of repeating it as another marketing exercise.

Obtain the decisions that make the selected arrangement effective. Then align the offer, invitation, customer-facing interface and staff explanation with its actual scope. A contradictory old invitation can continue to create expectations after the internal plan changes; revise the affected expression and address already made commitments through their applicable terms.

#### MKT.11:4.4 - Connect customer insight to a bounded change in work

When a customer account, journey inquiry or observed failure exposes a mismatch, state the specific condition that should change and the receiving result it is meant to improve. Give the people who can act the relevant evidence and its limits. They must be able to distinguish a reported experience, a failed operation and an inferred cause because these can require different changes.

Choose the level of change that the result needs. A known exception can be handled within current authority; a repeated missing contribution may require a changed assignment, resource allocation, staff capability, product feature or provider agreement. Obtain that contribution through its responsible practice. A general instruction to care more does not supply any of them.

Where staff need discretion, establish what they may decide, within which scope and resource conditions, and where they return a case outside that scope. Obtain the knowledge and capability needed to exercise that discretion. Where a recurring cross-team decision is needed, arrange a usable means for the responsible people to receive the evidence and resolve it. A new standing committee is unnecessary when an existing decision and communication route can do the work.

Give the change actual time, support and an attainable receiving operation. [OCE.11](../oce/33-oce-11---coordinate-organization-change-work-with-continuing.md) coordinates preparation, learning, trial, recovery and hand-back with continuing service. If the total burden is not feasible, reduce or reschedule the change, obtain support or return the decision. Do not finance the new promise with unacknowledged work by staff who must still serve current customers.

After the change, recover what was implemented, what occurred in use and whether the relevant customer result changed. Use that distinction to retain, revise or reverse the arrangement. Repeated useful changes can alter the organisation's ordinary methods, assignments and support; the responsible practices must establish those changes. The insight record alone does not institutionalize them.

#### MKT.11:4.5 - Arrange the actual continuation and failure response

Before a dependent customer action begins, confirm the necessary contributions for that case and interval. Use [OPS.6](../ops/34-ops-6---continue-cases-and-handle-exceptions.md) to obtain the executable continuation with a performer, effective conditions, expected result, stop or fallback, and a return to continuing operation. A sufficient existing result can be reused while its conditions hold.

Make failure handling intelligible to the people who encounter it. Identify what the customer can do, who receives an unresolved case, what temporary alternative is available and what cannot honestly be promised. A customer should not have to discover which of two providers owns an internal disagreement before obtaining the agreed response.

Observe performance where a difference changes action. A provider's delayed input, a lost permission or an exhausted support interval can invalidate a dependent continuation. Pause or revise that branch; restore its required conditions or return the supply limit to the people maintaining the promise. Preserve independent useful work where its conditions remain valid. MKT.7 handles restoration or ending when the relationship decision is affected.

#### MKT.11:4.6 - Return the supported promise and maintain its dependencies

Return what can now be promised and performed, its material conditions, the actual continuation and any unresolved contribution. Communicate changes to the people maintaining the offer and arranging work. Keep a condition visible for as long as another contribution relies on it. When a team's task finishes, identify which obligations remain under the applicable terms, who must fulfil them and which receiving contributions depend on them. Include obligations retained by the supplying team as well as those of receiving teams.

Review the arrangement when a provider, customer requirement, support capability or relevant demand changes. Reuse current sufficient confirmations. A recurring mismatch can reopen the service construction or the commercial model through MKT.9/13; a changed scale of work with organisations can reopen MKT.14. The result remains an account of supported contributions, not a claim that the entire customer's outcome is under provider control.

### MKT.11:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.11:5.1 - An evening registration promise with daytime support

In a constructed registration service, a page promises that every submitted application will receive a same-day decision. Ordinary cases can be completed automatically, but exceptions require a qualified operator. The operator works during the day, while many customers apply in the evening. An automated receipt message is being interpreted as confirmation of acceptance.

The team recovers the whole result: customers need to know whether registration is complete or what remains unresolved. Engineering supplies which cases can be decided automatically. Operations supplies the supported exception hours and response conditions. The responsible manager identifies who may decide exceptions and obtains the necessary assignment and access; staff availability by itself does not confer that authority.

The team compares narrower next-working-day decisions for exceptions, additional supported evening coverage, and reducing the conditions that require manual decisions through product changes. The first is presently feasible. The page and receipt then distinguish a received application from a completed registration, explain the remaining condition and give the attainable response time. The changed explanation follows the actual service arrangement.

Customer accounts later show that the exception timing still prevents some useful participation. That evidence goes to the people who can alter the product, allocation or offer. They select a bounded trial of an additional supported decision window. OCE.11 accounts for training, supervision and current daytime commitments, rather than counting only the new customer-facing hours. The trial separately records the supplied window, actual decisions and customers' accounts of whether the timing helped.

If the qualified substitute becomes unavailable, the trial's dependent promise must be withdrawn or rescheduled before new customers rely on it. Already submitted cases receive the applicable continuation or remedy. Publishing a reassuring message without restoring a qualified performer would leave the missing contribution intact.

#### MKT.11:5.2 - Permission governed by the customer's organisation

A reporting trial needs a sample supplied under the customer's access rules. The provider can supply a session and mapping assistance, but it cannot grant the customer organisation's permission. It obtains a precise return from the relevant authority, keeps the trial conditional while permission is pending and arranges a later session if the condition becomes effective. The customer's payment or the salesperson's commitment cannot substitute for that separate contribution.

#### MKT.11:5.3 - A local seller makes the urgent offer possible

In a constructed replenishment case, a workshop needs a compatible printer cartridge before the afternoon dispatch. The manufacturer's direct service can supply it tomorrow. A local retailer can supply it today from held stock and can identify compatibility from the printer model. The customer's problem is an interrupted activity with a deadline; finding the manufacturer's lowest unit price does not settle that problem.

The team compares direct delivery tomorrow, a supported urgent delivery service and local stock with collection. The urgent carrier cannot meet this destination's deadline. The retailer confirms the required item, reservation until noon, compatibility assistance and a workable collection. The resulting offer is conditional on those contributions; “available today” is not an unrestricted manufacturer promise across all locations.

The manufacturer and retailer also need a workable upstream exchange. In this example they agree replenishment timing, payment, the retailer's exposure to unsold stock and the handling of faulty or misidentified goods. The retailer's downstream terms identify the seller and the support the buyer can actually obtain. The provider construction connects an unsuitable purchase to the party who can resolve it; forwarding every case to the manufacturer would leave the retailer's compatibility contribution without a usable continuation.

During the trial, the manufacturer supplies twenty units to the retailer. The retailer records eight downstream acquisitions and two subsequent returns. Those observations concern different events. They do not establish twenty end-customer purchases, six successful uses, or an effect caused by advertising. The team investigates the two returns because their reason can change compatibility help, the offering or stock selection. MKT.13 uses the relevant stock, acquisition, support and cash timing evidence for repetition.

If the reserved stock becomes unavailable, the retailer returns that fact immediately to the current offer and attention route. The team removes the unsupported same-day invitation, handles any existing commitment through its applicable continuation and offers tomorrow's direct supply only where it still helps. The chosen distribution arrangement was part of the value of the offer; changing it changes the customer choice.

#### MKT.11:5.4 - Payment through a reseller does not activate a service

In a constructed software-service arrangement, a reseller explains and sells a supported subscription while the service provider controls activation. The customer's administrator must authorise the relevant account. A paid order therefore cannot support the message “ready to use” while that authorisation is absent.

The parties connect the reseller's accurate offer and acquisition work to the provider's activation result, the administrator's permission and named first-use support. They compare a conditional order with a later activation window and a supported preparation service. The customer chooses the conditional order; its confirmation states what remains to be obtained and what happens if it cannot be supplied. No warehouse operation is needed, but the commercial distribution problem remains real.

Repeated failed activations return evidence to MKT.2/4/11: change the prerequisites, preparation or arrangement that is failing. More paid orders would increase unresolved obligations. If activation is supplied but people still cannot perform their work, investigate the different use or capability question through MKT.6 and its competent suppliers.

### MKT.11:6 - Bias-Annotation

People can interpret willingness as capacity and the presence of an owner as evidence that the work is enabled. Marketing may also favour the broadest promise while Operations favours the easiest local service. Compare each position with the customer's receiving result and the full burden of the arrangement. Preserve a qualified refusal from a provider instead of translating it into a communication problem.

### MKT.11:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The promise and its material implications are recoverable from an actual customer use.
- Each consequential contribution is either supported by a provider and effective conditions or retained as an explicit limit on the promise.
- Capability, assignment, access, authority, timing and actual performance are distinguished where they affect the work.
- Handover and simultaneous-demand conflicts are resolved or returned as explicit limits.
- Customer insight reaches a responsible decision; any selected change is enabled and its result observed.
- Where intermediaries participate, their exchanges and contributions support the final acquisition and use; observations retain the events and parties they actually concern.
- Failure handling and remaining obligations are usable by the customer and contributors.
- Dependent expressions and future starts change when the supported promise changes.

### MKT.11:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Name an owner and assume they can deliver. | Obtain the specific assignment, capability, access and resources the work requires. |
| Add several locally feasible promises without checking their combination. | Compare their shared demand and receiving conditions. |
| Send customer feedback with no one able to act on it. | Connect the evidence to the responsible decision and enabling contribution. |
| Count only visible delivery in a new-service trial. | Include preparation, learning, support, recovery and continuing work. |
| Keep a public promise after its provider contribution fails. | Restore supply or revise the promise and handle affected commitments. |

### MKT.11:9 - Consequences

Customers can receive a more coherent contribution, and missing supply becomes a concrete repair or a smaller honest promise. Cross-team work and maintained dependencies cost effort; existing adequate arrangements should be reused. The method can expose that no feasible combination supports the proposed offer, which is a useful result before additional commitments are made.

### MKT.11:10 - Architectural Rationale

An offer can depend on several separately governed contributions. Marketing connects their results to the promise, while their responsible practices establish the capability, authority and performance. This division makes a missing contribution actionable and prevents an attractive description from being treated as implementation.

Customer evidence becomes practically useful when it informs a decision to retain or change the work. The connection may require a local remedy or changes to ordinary resources and assignments; its scale follows the problem rather than a universal transformation programme.

### MKT.11:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For carrying experience knowledge into work, **adapt** the organisational-learning question in [Customer experience orientation, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-024-01031-y). Its nine-case managerial account connects insight with discretion, resources and organisational support; outcome effects remain propositions requiring further testing. This sharpens §4.4 without making every customer issue require a new organisation-wide programme.

For actual enabling contributions, **adopt** OCE.6's establishment of effective assignments and its separate checks of effective access and the performer's required capability. OCE.11 coordinates change with continuing service, and OPS.6 supplies an executable continuation. These developed constructions resolve the gap left by naming responsible departments or circulating feedback. C.32.MWA.PP supplies a needed comparison of incompatible work arrangements. A current sufficient result is cheaper to reuse; reopen only dependencies defeated by a changed promise, provider, customer condition or burden.

For distribution, the working question is how to construct an arrangement that makes the promised contribution obtainable in the customer's situation. **Adapt** the intermediary-contribution distinctions in [OpenStax, Principles of Marketing, §17.1](https://openstax.org/books/principles-marketing/pages/17-1-the-use-and-value-of-marketing-channels). A serious alternative is to choose among channels by buyer requirements, coverage, product conditions and profitability, as in [§17.3](https://openstax.org/books/principles-marketing/pages/17-3-factors-influencing-channel-choice), then select, support, motivate and evaluate the partners as in [§17.4](https://openstax.org/books/principles-marketing/pages/17-4-managing-the-distribution-channel). This approach can settle the decision when an existing route already specifies and supplies the required availability, exchanges, information and support. Reuse that route and check the changed condition; reconstructing it would add work without improving the answer.

When those relations remain unsettled, select the end-use, exchange and provider construction in §4.2.1. **Adopt** SYSE.8's offering/provider alternatives and ECO.3/5's constructions of exchange between different plans and terms for consequential information or effort. Join their results through actual contributions and commitments in §§4.2–4.3. The additional result is a supported connection from the customer's required use to each party's offering, acceptance, provision, information and continuation. Channel-choice factors guide the alternatives, but do not supply an unanswered stock commitment, activation condition or agreement about support. A comparison using those factors can remain sufficient by consuming an already obtained answer to these questions.

Compare the ways of obtaining that answer for the same receiving use, demand, deadline and available contributors. The fuller construction adds inquiry, engineering and economic judgement, negotiation, coordination and testing of the receiving connections. Include the customer's preparation and waiting, each provider's transition and continuing work, exception handling and recovery. Accept this extra burden when an unresolved connection can change the feasible promise or selected arrangement; otherwise prefer the adequate existing route or its smallest repair. The deliberate trade-off is more preparation for an inspectable, supportable arrangement, not a claim of lower total effort in every case.

The stock and activation cases in §§5.3–5.4 show what this choice changes: the urgent offer depends on actual reserved supply and separate upstream/downstream terms, while a subscription needs an authorised activation and first-use support beyond payment. These are constructed consequences of the chosen method. OpenStax supplies a substantive instructional comparator, not evidence of effectiveness in a particular market; the selected synthesis also needs actual supplier answers. Reopen the comparison when a provider loses a required contribution, information no longer reaches its user, a change of terms rewards a different action, or the combined burden defeats the promised timing or volume.

### MKT.11:12 - Relations

MKT.2/3/5/6/9 supply promises and receiving uses and consume the resulting supply limits. MKT.4 makes the supported transition feasible; MKT.7 handles relationship recovery or ending; MKT.8 locates consequential connections; MKT.12 supplies qualified customer evidence. MKT.10 evaluates a claimed intervention effect, MKT.13 tests repeatability, and MKT.14 coordinates common and individual work with selected organisations.

SYSE constructs the offering and provider arrangement; C.32.MWA.PP compares how methods are related and whether their performance creates conflicts; OCE establishes assignments and workable change; HCD supplies capability; Operations performs the service. Their results retain their own conditions when used in a marketing promise.
