## OCE.3 - Generate and Compare Organization Concepts

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable
>
> **Primary working result:** a **bounded organization-concept comparison** stating how alternatives differ in contribution, Work, assignment eligibility, authority, resource access, information use, service, coordination, capability, provider involvement, coexistence and burden; which participant contributions or missing voices matter; the decision or honest stop; and evidence that reopens the comparison.

### OCE.3:0 - Use This When

Use this pattern when one incumbent arrangement, target operating model, topology, reporting-line change, centralization/decentralization slogan, or provider proposal is being treated as inevitable. Enter when the organization and intended contribution are bounded and enough current Work and relation evidence exists to generate serious alternatives.

Begin with a compatible `OCE.1` focus and `OCE.2` current account or equivalent content. State which gaps are tolerated and which block comparison.

A bounded question can be how to let contributors act independently while their results still fit, how to give a unit an economic mandate, or how to organize inquiry when the tasks and best methods are not yet known. Recover the intended contribution or worthwhile problem, the permitted scope of inquiry and the conditions that any candidate must respect. Enough knowledge to compare a next arrangement need not mean a complete future task plan. Use the coordination derivation in OCE.3:4.1.1.1 when the current arrangement leaves this choice unexplained; an already adequate division and coordination of work needs no redesign.

The first useful result is a small decision set of materially different possible organization concepts. Describe each concept with its assumptions, participant input, supporting evidence, trade-offs and authority conditions. A rejected concept remains useful when it exposes a hidden contribution or burden.

Do not use OCE.3 to realize the proposed configuration, establish a position, assign holders, establish capability, authorize change Work, or prove adoption or effectiveness. Send selected design questions to `OCE.4`–`OCE.8` and realization to `OCE.9`.

#### OCE.3:0.1 - Working Distinctions

| Name used here | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| organization concept | A possible organization configuration described far enough to answer one decision. Its Work and relations remain possible until realized and observed. |
| concept alternative | A materially different way to satisfy or revise the intended contribution, including repairing the incumbent configuration. |
| selected structure | Constituents and relations selected for a comparison use, such as contribution, Work, assignment, authority, resource-access, information-use, service, coordination, or capability structure. |
| view | A description produced under a viewpoint. Project, process and case viewpoints ask different questions; charts, maps or models can present the resulting descriptions. |
| bounded generation move | The OCE.3 move that varies decision-bearing relations through incumbent repair, changed specialization/contribution/authority, and boundary/provider or human–AI alternatives, then stops at a small serious set. |
| participant contribution | Evidence or a proposed alternative supplied by people or other Agents whose knowledge of actual Work, burden, authority, safety, service or providers can change the set or comparison. Participation is not consensus or veto. |
| concept assumption | A claim that must hold for feasibility or worth but is not established. |
| coexistence and change burden | The demands and risks of changing while continuing Work shares the same conditions. These include learning and migration effort, provider and governance demands, service interruption, opportunity costs, and the effort or risk of reversal. |
| organization-concept comparison | Alternatives, criteria, evidence, uncertainty, consequences, decision or stop, and reopen conditions. It is not a target organization by itself. |

A concept describes an organization and its possible relations. Name the people or other systems expected to enact the relevant roles, the capabilities they need, the methods they can use, and the agreements or decisions that would make their contributions available. A role name does not supply a capable holder; knowledge of a rule does not establish commitment to act under it; an agreement does not perform the work. A shared aim or belief can guide the selection of different methods without itself becoming one method.

### OCE.3:1 - Problem Frame

Organization concepts arrive with persuasive forms. Charts foreground reporting; team topologies interaction; provider proposals service boundaries; process views recurring contributions. Each can expose a question, but none contains every authority, capability, resource, contribution, or consequence relation.

Concept generation must vary decision-bearing relations rather than redraw one configuration. It must include incumbent repair and obtain design knowledge from participants whose Work or burden is otherwise hidden.

### OCE.3:2 - Problem

A favored concept turns evidence collection into confirmation. Practitioners compare names instead of relations, infer capability from a proposed position, infer authority from hierarchy, and hide the burden of continuing service.

Several diagrams may still be views of one concept. Without a serious alternative and participant contribution, the decision cannot expose which assumption makes the preferred concept better.

Coordination can also remain unexplained inside an apparently complete concept. A common goal, trained team, interface specification or manager is named, yet no one has established which decisions it makes compatible and which still require clarification. More meetings may then repeat known information while a resource conflict or incompatible result goes unresolved. Conversely, a blanket demand for agreement can suppress useful independent initiative.

### OCE.3:3 - Forces

| Force | Tension |
| --- | --- |
| Diversity | Serious alternatives reveal assumptions, while decorative variants waste attention. |
| Comparability | Shared concerns aid comparison, while concepts allocate contributions and burden through unlike structures. |
| Participation | Actual Work knowledge can change alternatives, while participation can be burdensome, unsafe, or outside authority. |
| Novelty and continuity | New relations may unlock capability, while incumbent relations carry memory, authority, and service continuity. |
| Product/organization alignment | Mirroring can reduce some coordination, while one-to-one correspondence is contingent. |
| Decision speed | A bounded selection is useful, while scalar scores create false precision. |
| Independence and fit | Local choice uses situated knowledge, while its effects must remain compatible with other contributions and shared constraints. |
| Preparation and adaptation | Prior rules, knowledge and commitments can reduce later coordination, while their applicability can change during the work. |
| Joint discovery | Combining different knowledge can make a new contribution possible; removing every dependency can remove that possibility with its coordination cost. |

### OCE.3:4 - Solution

Generate a small decision set by changing the relations that matter to the contribution. Begin from the incumbent and current relation account; vary specialization, supplied-result, authority, assignment, access, coordination, provider, and human–AI relations; include affected participants whose knowledge can change a candidate; and stop when the set contains serious alternatives that challenge the favored concept's assumptions, or a generation gap is explicit.

Use `C.17` only after candidates exist, and only for novelty, diversity, value, or comparison characteristics actually judged. Use `C.18` only when retained or open-ended exploration -- including its Archive, Front, descriptors, telemetry or lineage -- is part of the working question. Use `C.11` or another applicable decision Method after alternatives, non-negotiables, uncertainty, and authority are explicit.

#### OCE.3:4.1 - Pattern-Use Unfolding

1. **Bind the comparison.** Name organization, contribution, decision authority, horizon, current account, non-negotiables, and tolerated or blocking gaps.
2. **Explain the difficulty before choosing a form.** From OCE.2, state which needed contribution is delayed, unusable, missing or unnecessarily burdensome, under which conditions, and which explanations remain possible. Name the relations that a change could affect. Preserve the product/service, authority, protection and continuing-work conditions against which a candidate must be compared.
3. **Choose bounded participants.** Invite or otherwise obtain contributions from participants whose knowledge of actual Work, burden, local adaptation, authority, safety, service or providers can alter the candidate set or comparison. Record missing participation and protection limits; preserve decision authority separately.
4. **Construct combinations from that difficulty.** Use §4.1.1 to connect contribution, specialization and allocation with the information, decisions, participation and support that would make each candidate work. Include incumbent-plus-repair; a changed specialization, contribution or authority configuration; and a boundary/provider or human–AI alternative when plausible. Explain why each combination could change the difficulty and what could defeat it. Stop generation when another variant changes no live assumption or criterion, or record the unmet generation need.
5. **Decide whether retained exploration is current.** For a one-time small decision set, proceed without Archive/Front apparatus. Use `C.18` only for retained or open-ended exploration; use `C.17` only to characterize candidates already present.
6. **Describe the whole candidate at the needed grain.** Use C.32:4 for the candidate palette, with the organization-specific reasons developed below. Name the selected structures, changed relations, expected gain and loss. Use charts, maps, scenarios, prototypes or mathematical lenses for the question each can answer and state their losses.
7. **Expose feasibility assumptions.** State required capabilities, effective authority, resource access, provider commitments, product/service claims, participation conditions and continuing-service constraints. Use the direct suppliers in §4.1.2 for a still-missing combination, replacement or coexistence result.
8. **Compare contributions, interactions and burdens.** Follow §4.1.2 through a condition that can defeat the proposed mechanism. Retain useful parts, revise the combination or reject it. Compare intended contribution, coordination, resilience, affected-System consequences, participant burden, service interruption, reversibility and uncertainty without hiding unlike outcomes in a score.
9. **Choose, narrow, probe, or stop.** Select under named authority, retain ties, or request the specialist result that changes the comparison. Selection does not establish organization relations.
10. **Return constraints and reopen evidence.** Supply the selected questions and assumptions to downstream patterns and name observations that reopen them.

##### OCE.3:4.1.1 - Derive an organizational combination

Begin with a short explanation of the work: the receiver needs a particular result under certain conditions; current contributors attempt to obtain it in a known way; a recovered difficulty prevents or burdens that contribution. Keep an explanation that remains uncertain as a hypothesis. If the difficulty is merely a missing permission or an isolated execution error, repairing that condition may be the serious incumbent option. Larger regrouping needs a reason that the local repair leaves unanswered.

For open inquiry, the current arrangement may concern how participants notice difficulties, choose investigations or obtain help, while the method for the future contribution remains unknown. Explain that current work and the unanswered question. The candidate then compares ways to make the inquiry and subsequent method construction attainable, without inventing a complete task sequence in advance.

**Find the contribution that must change.** Follow the receiving result back through the necessary subject work, using `B.1.5.EW` if its constituent connections are unclear. Obtain the relevant professional explanation when “prepare evidence”, “support users” or another label conceals work the designer does not understand. Identify where the current way creates delay, repeated interpretation, loss of information, incompatible demands or another supported difficulty. This yields a concrete design question, such as how Electrical's changing compatibility evidence can reach package assembly early enough, rather than an instruction to become more cross-functional.

**Give a reason for grouping and allocation together.** Use the specialization reasons and contribution-crossing specification in `OCE.4:4.0–OCE.4:4.1` to develop the candidate. Close, frequent mutual adjustment can favor bringing contributors together around a result. Scarce expertise, shared equipment, professional development or independent judgement can favor retaining a specialization and improving its contribution to several receivers. Compare those reasons in this case. Name who would supply each needed result, who would receive it, and how an unusable result returns. A proposed group with an unfilled contribution remains incomplete. C.32:4 supplies functional allocation, retargeting and bearer-feasibility repairs when needed. The draft concept can be refined through OCE.4; a full contribution-architecture decision need not precede generating it.

**Make information and decisions fit that allocation.** Ask what each deciding participant would need to know, when it would arrive and who could respond to an exception. If knowledge stays distributed, alternatives include supplying it to an integrator, enabling local decisions under compatible conditions, or changing the work so that fewer decisions need joint information. Choose a candidate reason, not a universal preference for centralization. Keep safety acceptance, release, resource allocation and other independently governed decisions separate where their bases require it. Grouping people together does not change those bases. Use `OCE.7` and `C.32.CONWAY` when product/service and organization choices constrain one another; retain a justified mismatch.

**Make participation and support fit the expected contribution.** Ask which accepted work a contributor would have to interrupt, which local success criterion the contribution might conflict with, and what access, capability, response or protection it requires. Obtain OCE.10's account when a participation difficulty needs explanation. Use that result to change the candidate: for example, pair an earlier evidence-return obligation with an accepted allocation and recognition of useful challenge, rather than expecting extra hidden work. The relevant owner must establish any changed condition. Required capability may be supplied by an existing contributor, development or another arrangement; it is not supplied by naming the group. OCE.11 provides the shared service/change conditions when the same people or support would be occupied.

**State the mechanism of the combination.** Explain how the changes would affect the recovered difficulty: “earlier electrical input lets Integration detect an incompatible claim before assembly closes; a reserved response interval lets Electrical correct it; separate Safety acceptance preserves the required judgement.” Name the expected observable difference and a condition under which it would not appear. This explanation shows which parts depend on one another and gives the comparison something to challenge. It remains a proposed mechanism; the attractive configuration and its eventual causal effect are different claims.

Generate another serious combination by changing a consequential premise of that mechanism. Retaining specialists with a reliable crossing, grouping recurring contributors around a receiving result, and obtaining a contribution from a provider can respond differently to scarcity or delay. A provider/AI branch is useful only when a plausible supplied contribution exists; OCE.8 develops the complete work arrangement. A cosmetic reporting change that leaves the causal difficulty and relevant relations untouched adds no alternative.

###### OCE.3:4.1.1.1 - Derive coordination and the scope of independent decisions

Coordination makes separately undertaken contributions fit in the receiving work. Start from the decisions and dependencies that need this fit, then construct the combination of advance preparation and interaction during performance. The same organization can use different combinations for different contributions. A familiar name for a coordination mechanism helps recall a possibility; it does not determine the combination.

**Find what another contribution can change.** Follow one consequential receiving use through the current or proposed work. Ask which result, timing, resource use or decision by one participant can change what another must do, whether their result can be used, or whether a shared condition remains satisfied. Obtain that answer from the participants and relevant subject methods. A product interface may hide a shared calibration state; separate work packages may compete for one specialist; two autonomous local decisions may together exceed a common resource. Preserve the direction and occasion of each material dependency instead of assuming that everyone depends on everyone in the same way.

Distinguish a dependency of results from a participant's need to anticipate another participant. If a receiver can wait for an accepted input and choose its own action afterwards, the input relation need not require continuous negotiation of both parties' work. If choices must be made simultaneously, information arrives too late, or a participant must commit before another's action is observable, the arrangement needs a usable expectation, a shared decision, or a change that removes the need to decide blindly. Changing the sequence, separating a shared resource or making a result independently usable are candidate changes, with their own delay, cost and consequences. A claim that the dependency has disappeared needs the relevant technical and resource grounds.

**Recover the knowledge and agreement needed before each decision.** For a materially dependent decision, ask what the participant must know, be able to rely on, or be entitled to change at that time. The answer might be an input's meaning and tolerances, another role's expected contribution, a confirmed capacity interval, an applicable response rule, or a limitation that requires a joint decision. Knowledge of all other participants' internal methods is usually unnecessary. Complementary expertise can remain different while the participants understand enough of one another's contributions to combine them.

Separate knowing what another might do from having their commitment, and separate both from knowing who can authorize a change. An accurate prediction of refusal does not make a contribution available. Likewise, communicating an incompatible demand more clearly does not reconcile it. Use OCE.4's result crossing to obtain the required supplied result, receiving conditions and exception return; use the relevant authority and resource owners to obtain the conditions on which reliance would depend. Keep proposed conditions distinguishable from conditions already effective.

When the decision-specific knowledge is missing or inconsistent, use OPS.4:4.1 in the Operations Management Principles Framework to obtain current claims, grounds, uncertainty, permissions and participant views for those decisions. Bring that account back to the candidate. Different views may suffice; copying one dashboard to everyone neither creates the necessary interpretation nor gives everyone the same decision rights.

**Test the scope left for independent choice.** Identify the proposed range of decisions left to each participant. Use the relevant subject and resource grounds to establish where the resulting contributions remain usable together and the shared conditions remain satisfied across that range. Challenge the account with a consequential different choice that another participant is actually permitted and able to make. If it defeats the result, identify what needs to be constrained, known or jointly decided. If the grounds support only a narrower range, retain independence there. The task is to justify the proposed freedom, not to enumerate every imaginable action; a successful example alone does not establish the whole range. A consequential unknown remains an assumption to investigate or a limit on the permitted use.

State the independence in terms of decisions: a producer may choose its internal technique while preserving agreed output and timing; a local organizer may choose a useful subject within an accepted purpose while lacking permission to promise another group's venue. Physical compatibility, applicable protection, resource availability and authority can each limit independence for a different reason. Retain the freedom that remains rather than treating one shared constraint as a reason to centralize every decision.

**Construct advance conditions together with a way to handle what remains uncertain.** For recurrent situations that participants can recognize, a shared procedure can specify actions and handovers. It reduces repeated choice when its conditions hold. Use OPS.2:4–OPS.2:5 to obtain a usable operating method and a bounded exception rather than extending the procedure to a situation it cannot handle. An agreed result specification instead leaves more choice of internal method when compatibility can be established from the supplied result and its conditions. Obtain the needed result and return conditions through OCE.4 and the product/organization compatibility through OCE.7; a specification does not erase a shared state, resource conflict or regulated process condition.

Where experienced participants need to anticipate complementary contributions rapidly, preparation can make their interaction intelligible without detailed instructions on every occasion. Use HCD.22:4.2.1 in the Human Capability Development Principles Framework to obtain preparation for the whole episode, the different role contributions and a consequential exception. Bring the attainable capability and its support conditions into the concept. Similar certificates or identical vocabulary do not establish that these particular holders can interact under the proposed conditions. Training need not make their professional knowledge interchangeable.

For work whose next significant choices cannot be specified in advance, provide interaction where the relevant knowledge can meet. Participants may adjust directly, or an integrator with suitable information, time and decision power may coordinate particular decisions. Give that interaction an actual purpose: resolve a compatibility question, reconcile two uses of a constrained resource, interpret a new observation or revise a shared expectation. Determine which information must reach whom and in time for which decision. A shared meeting is one possible means; an established exchange can be sufficient. Keep independent acceptance and other separately governed decisions with their legitimate owners.

These possibilities can operate together. Trained contributors can follow a routine handover, choose their internal methods, and temporarily clarify an unusual result. An integrator can resolve one conflict without directing every task. Compare changing the dependency itself with supporting more interaction around it. Count preparation and maintenance as well as communication during performance; moving effort earlier does not make it free.

**Use shared purposes and norms to support initiative where tasks remain open.** A common direction can let participants notice opportunities, choose a contribution and devise a method without waiting for a central task list. Make the direction usable through its reasons: which receiving need or possibility matters, why a proposed contribution could help, which consequences are unacceptable, and which uncertainties call for inquiry. STR.5:4.3–STR.5:4.4 in the Strategy Principles Framework supplies this direction and its use in discriminating proposals. Bring back the grounds for a particular initiative, its open questions and the conditions under which it would no longer fit. Shared slogans alone cannot perform this selection.

Allow a participant to propose an unanticipated task or a different way of obtaining the contribution. If the way is not yet known, use FPF C.39 to construct or obtain it. Where the subject is pursuing continuing development, use FPF C.40.OD for noticing difficulties and opportunities, problematizing them, developing worthwhile challenges and continuing from results. The present organizational comparison asks what participation, knowledge, authority and support make that development attainable. It does not replace development with the execution or acquisition of an already selected method.

Initiative can proceed with the participant's available capability and resources under the effective conditions on their use. When it would change a shared interface, consume another party's resource, create a joint obligation or affect protected work, obtain the corresponding contribution or decision before relying on it. Shared purpose does not require one employer or a common budget, but neither does it establish another participant's consent. In wider exchange, ECO.8:4–ECO.8:5 in the Economic Reasoning and Coordination Principles Framework supplies the boundary between direct cooperation and contractual or rule-based contributions; use its adjustment to avoid promising voluntary help where a paid commitment is needed, or removing discretionary help on which the work depends.

For a norm to support such initiative repeatedly, participants need a way to learn its meaning from actual choices, challenge a poor application and maintain it when conditions change. Use OCE.10 to obtain the participation and cultural support that this arrangement needs. The result may preserve a norm, alter its supporting conditions or expose an incompatible incentive. Agreement on a desirable direction, willingness to contribute, competence and available means remain distinct questions.

**Make the combination actionable and revisable.** For each serious candidate, explain which prior conditions allow which independent decisions, which remaining decisions require interaction, and what observation or exception returns a decision for reconsideration. Reuse ordinary agreements, established practices and available preparation that already provide these conditions.

Compare the combination with a credible incumbent repair at the same receiving use and horizon. Include displaced work, specialist scarcity, preparation, latency, lost local information, upkeep and the cost of a wrong assumption. Shared goals can coexist with conflicting rewards; correctly anticipated actions can still fail to produce the required whole. Bring those failures back to participation, contribution and resource design rather than prescribing more communication by default. Use OCE.3:4.1.2 to challenge the whole combination and retain ties or a justified stop.

After selection, LDR.4:4.5–LDR.4:4.7 in the Leadership Principles Framework can obtain the effective decisions, actual commitments, mutually visible start and first useful handover when those contributions are missing. Return unresolved support or authority to its owner; OCE.9 governs realization of the organizational concept. Neither the comparison nor its shared explanation makes the proposed arrangement actual. During later use, a changed dependency, failed expectation, new capability or changed receiving need reopens the affected choice. Successful delivery can also reveal a new development possibility; it does not require freezing the arrangement or automatically launching another change.

###### OCE.3:4.1.1.2 - Make an economic mandate work through actual authority

Use this branch when a proposed arrangement changes who may shape an offering, commit resources or bear its economic consequences. Begin with the product uses and obligations from OCE.1 and the actual rights recovered through OCE.2. A familiar label is optional; the needed result is a workable allocation of decisions and means.

**Recover the mandate by decision.** Establish who can form or change the offering, accept a request, promise its result and timing, choose means, incur costs, and decide continuation or termination. Identify who can accept the supplied result and who can amend an existing obligation. Rights can be divided among a unit, product leaders, procurement, finance and the containing organization's governing body. State the limits and the way their decisions combine. A missing decision right remains a missing condition; it is not repaired by calling someone the owner or entrepreneur.

**Connect means and incentives to that mandate.** Compare what the unit is expected to provide with the people, equipment, information, funding and provider commitments actually available. Trace a conflict to the affected uses and consequences. For example, rewarding a laboratory solely for internal bench utilization can discourage an outside test that preserves two product deliveries. Include the useful receiving result and displaced burden when designing measures and recognition. Use OCE.10 if a participation difficulty needs explanation; changing a reward formula alone does not establish its effect.

For economic comparison, distinguish shared costs from costs caused by a particular product, attribute material receipts and risks, and identify who bears them. An internal payment finances a contribution; changing its allocation does not create additional customer receipts. Obtain the needed Finance or economic analysis rather than infer profit from a saved departmental budget. A permitted subsidy or exploratory loss retains its purpose, limit and decision-maker.

**Test a feasible decision under divided rights.** Work through one ordinary request and one consequential conflict. Ask whether the proposed holder can make the promised choice with the available means while preserving other commitments. Use §4.1.1.1 to establish the scope of independent action. A manager may book an approved outside laboratory within a reserve while lacking authority to change a product's promised delivery or test requirement. In the concept comparison, show which choice that holder could make and which decision would need another holder's authority. General agreement by every department is unnecessary when the required rights are already effective. For a service promise in actual operations, use OPS.13 to obtain the supported commitment or the decision still needed, and return any changed organizational condition to this comparison.

**Choose the arrangement and its local acceptance.** Compare an ordinary service unit, an internal enterprise and an outside provision where they are serious alternatives. Apply OCE.1:0.2's definitions to the resulting relations. A unit with assigned work and a budget can remain the sufficient design. An internal enterprise needs the actual offering, economic obligations, permitted means and attributed consequences together; it can have bounded rights and share decisions. Where several organizations must act together, recover the mutual commitments that would make their joint work an extended enterprise while preserving each participant's boundary and other obligations.

Specify the capability to realize, its acceptance and the continuing obligations. OCE.6 supplies missing effective assignments and enabling relations; OCE.9 realizes and qualifies the capability. A proposal or selected concept remains such until those conditions obtain. Retain a precise unresolved right, resource or product-use assumption when it prevents the preferred arrangement.

**Separate the contributions called entrepreneurship.** Identify the work actually needed: product vision and strategy, appraisal of the firm's economic future, organization design, customer development, financing or fulfilment of commitments. Obtain the relevant contribution and its authority from the responsible practice; Management.Reference:3.2.2 explains these connections. One person may perform several, but "Entrepreneur" is not a single assignable role that supplies them all. An initiative can lead to an inquiry, a temporary project, an internal enterprise or a separate firm; a sponsor or founder title does not decide which arrangement exists.

##### OCE.3:4.1.2 - Challenge the combination and change it

Bring each candidate's prospective result path and assumptions to the general method that answers the unresolved question. `C.30.ASV:4.5a` distinguishes Method composition, work, information, support and other structures when that distinction changes the design; `C.32.MWA:4.1–C.32.MWA:4.4` combines those accounts and exposes conflict or burden moved between them. Enter C.32.MWA with a prospective case for a proposed organization of Method use. Existing observed relations keep their own evidence; missing Work identity does not become an invented obtaining occurrence.

For the finite change from the incumbent to a candidate, use `C.11.CRC:4` to compare effects under the same horizon, scenarios and protected conditions, including interactions. Use `B.1.5.RS:4` when replacing a contribution: follow it through every receiving whole that can change the choice, including an adapter or fallback the replacement needs. Reuse a sufficient comparison. OCE.11:4.1–OCE.11:4.3 supplies the time and resource overlap with continuing service; the concept uses those conditions instead of constructing another capacity account.

The organization question supplied to these methods is whether the proposed specialization, allocation, decisions, information and participation can jointly deliver the contribution. For example, a common integration queue may reduce duplicated interpretation yet delay local corrections. Keeping local output targets while adding a shared evidence obligation may make the apparently available contributors unavailable in practice. Such an interaction gives a reason to change a relation or support condition, retain a bounded exception, reduce the demand, or reject the candidate. A compensating policy is part of the candidate, with its burden and authority conditions, rather than a free remedy added after comparison.

Challenge the claimed independence as part of that interaction question. A new shared state can defeat an output interface while leaving professional preparation useful. A changed role holder can invalidate an expectation without changing the technical interface. A new resource commitment can block the next occasion without disproving the earlier method. Reopen the condition that changed, including a need for new subject knowledge, instead of replacing the entire coordination combination by habit.

Retain an incumbent repair when it satisfies the needed result at acceptable burden. Retain a tie when the observations do not distinguish the serious configurations. Request only the next result that could change the choice, and return a specific feasibility or authority gap when no candidate can meet the current conditions. Concept comparison can finish with that honest stop; it does not require a workshop, organization-wide agreement or an experiment for every alternative.

#### OCE.3:4.3 - What Changes in Practice

Teams compare possible organization relations rather than fashionable names. The incumbent can be repaired, product and organization structures can differ deliberately, participants can change the alternatives without receiving an automatic veto, and provider or AI contributions can be designed without pretending that outsourcing or automation supplies authority, capability, or results.

The manager can also explain why particular decisions may be made independently, what the participants must know or agree before acting, and what still needs coordination during performance. This makes a proposed reduction in meetings, an investment in shared preparation or support for independent initiative examinable through its contribution and conditions.

### OCE.3:5 - Archetypal Grounding -- PumpWorks and Other Coordination Concepts

The comparison uses `PumpWorks-EngineeringOrg`, weekly evidenced AI-inspection releases, the `OCE.2` account, and non-negotiable safety authority, evidence traceability, and continuing service.

Product, Electrical, Software, Safety, Field Service, platform, provider, and service-liaison participants contribute knowledge of actual Work, scarcity, burden, authority, exceptions and provider conditions. Missing customer-use evidence remains visible; participation does not transfer the release decision.

**Derive the repair from the current difficulty.** OCE.2's R42 reconstruction shows that Electrical's evidence is used during package challenge, that a planned rig interval can be displaced by service, and that field-service information can arrive too late. The cause of the last delay remains unresolved. The design therefore starts with timely usable evidence and a working exception return. It cannot yet justify a blanket claim that departments cause the delay.

For `OC-PW-FUNCTIONAL-REPAIR`, keep specialist contribution in Electrical and assembly in Integration. Use OCE.4's crossing to specify which configuration the evidence concerns, when Integration needs it and how a challenged claim returns. Pair that contribution with a prospective rig-access arrangement and a known escalation path for missed information. Safety still accepts evidence; the director still decides release. The mechanism hypothesis is that an explicit earlier request and response can remove avoidable coordination delay while preserving expertise and independent judgement. This candidate is serious even if no reporting line changes. Its limit is that a crossing agreement cannot create an unavailable expert or rig interval.

**Construct and challenge a different combination.** If frequent mutual correction is the unresolved pressure, propose a stable release integration group with recurring Electrical and Software contributions and platform support. This is the reason for the stream/enabling candidate: participants can resolve a changing compatibility question in the same working interval. It does not require moving independent Safety into the group's release authority or abandoning functional capability homes.

Suppose the bounded participant discussion now adds that Electrical is judged on its own completed design tasks and that its specialist also covers service diagnostics. The service owner confirms that the proposed response interval is already committed to protected diagnostics and no substitute is available. Moving evidence preparation into a common queue while retaining that commitment defeats the assumed response interval; the local output criterion creates a further participation question. The group could improve package assembly while leaving Electrical's answers later or displacing protected service. Apply C.32.MWA to expose this conflict and OCE.11 to obtain the service/participation conditions; the unrepaired stream candidate is rejected for this use.

A revised candidate would pair the recurring group with a different specialist response interval under qualified service coverage, recognition of the cross-group contribution by the relevant manager, and a fallback to the existing exception route when service interrupts. These changes still need the relevant owners' decisions and effective conditions. Its extra coordination, setup and displaced work belong in the C.11.CRC comparison. If the service owner cannot supply the required coverage, narrow the release demand or keep the functional repair; a nominal allocation cannot make the revised candidate feasible. B.1.5.RS governs any proposed replacement of the old evidence-return contribution, including its other service users. OCE.10 is used only if the participation explanation still changes the intervention.

#### OCE.3:5.1 - Preserve independent methods while revising a shared condition

Suppose two language teams prepare versions of the same technical explanation. For this constructed case, an accepted source text, a shared glossary for the consequential terms, the release date and separate review commitments are already available. Each team has the relevant translation capability and its own working time. The receiving publisher needs both versions to convey the same accepted claims, but does not need the teams to use identical drafting tools or sentence construction.

The incumbent requires a joint meeting for every drafting decision. The manager traces what the other language team's choice could change. Routine local phrasing within the accepted meaning does not alter the other team's usable contribution. The candidate retains local drafting, the agreed source and glossary, the review obligations and a return for disputed meaning. Shared preparation supports recognition of that return; it does not require translators to possess the technical reviewer's authority. OPS.4 can make the applicable source revision and unresolved claims available to each team. LDR.4 is needed only if the changed commitments or beginning are not yet established. Under these stipulated conditions, repeated joint approval of routine phrasing adds no required coordination contribution.

Now a source ambiguity is discovered: the same expression could assert either a measured property or a condition assumed only for the calculation. The teams cannot independently choose different readings and still deliver the same accepted claims. They return that subject question to the technical author and authorized reviewer. A temporary joint clarification establishes the intended assertion and its limits; the source owner issues the resulting revision and the affected parties receive it before relying on it. Local drafting then continues on that basis. If the subject question cannot be answered in time, a scoped release or delay must be decided under the actual publication authority.

The new dependency changes the coordination of meaning, not every sentence decision. A shortage of review time would require a different repair: an attainable review commitment, reduced scope or another date. Neither another glossary discussion nor common enthusiasm supplies that time. The case illustrates a changed selection under stated conditions; it does not establish a measured benefit of fewer meetings.

#### OCE.3:5.2 - Support initiative before the tasks and method are known

Consider a volunteer archive group whose accepted direction is to help visitors understand how uncertain historical claims are supported, while preserving the source records and avoiding unsupported certainty. Contributors have different expertise, their own means and no power to allocate one another's work. The group currently waits for a coordinator to list and assign every improvement. The comparison concerns whether bounded independent initiatives could expose useful problems and produce contributions sooner without creating unsupported promises.

A volunteer notices that visitors repeatedly treat a record's entry date as the date of the event described. The problem is not yet a specified correction task: the group does not know how often the distinction matters, which cases have both dates, or what explanation would help. Using the direction's grounds, the volunteer proposes an inquiry into this difficulty rather than simply adding another transcription target. Another contributor can distinguish the archival fields; a third can examine how visitors use a proposed explanation. Combining their knowledge might reveal a problem and a method that none could have specified alone.

The candidate permits the volunteer to examine the permitted public records with their own available time and tools. It keeps participation by the other contributors conditional on their actual offers. The volunteers use C.39 to develop a way to distinguish the claims and try an explanation; C.40.OD supplies the continuing development question rather than presuming that this first idea is the final problem or method. A short exchange is warranted where their interpretations affect one another. There is no reason to require a central decision for every observation or to standardize a procedure before the relevant distinctions are understood.

Suppose the inquiry produces a useful distinction between two dates and a proposed explanatory note, but publishing it would require the archive editor's time and permission to change the public description. The volunteers can develop their proposal independently; they cannot promise that publication. The editor can offer one review interval for a limited set of notes. The candidate therefore combines independent inquiry, optional collaboration, a bounded editorial commitment and an agreed return for unsupported claims. If another initiative requests the same interval, the participants use the actual allocation rule or obtain a decision from its holder. A shared aim cannot make the two commitments fit by itself. Paid specialist work, if needed, enters through its own agreed terms using ECO.8.

The group compares this arrangement with keeping central assignment. A coordinator may still be useful for a shared queue or consequential conflict. What changes is that proposing and investigating a suitable contribution no longer waits for a complete task list. The comparison includes the editor's burden and the cost of discovering that an initiative does not fit. OCE.10 supplies support for the norm of proposing grounded inquiries, returning limitations and recognizing useful challenges; recounting a successful initiative can help later contributors learn that norm.

On these stipulated facts, the participants select a bounded trial of independent inquiry with the editor's offered review interval. They retain the actual allocation rule and decline to require a complete central task list before inquiry can begin. If the editor withdraws the interval, the inquiry and its learning can remain useful, but the planned publication loses its support and must be reconsidered.

After the first note is accepted, further records may expose a different uncertainty, or participants may discover a more valuable way to help visitors. C.40.OD then supplies the next development judgement. The completed note remains a useful finite result; it neither completes development nor obliges the group to pursue every new possibility. This candidate preserves initiative and the growth of knowledge while keeping shared commitments bounded.

#### OCE.3:5.3 - From a pump-service initiative to an internal enterprise

An engineer at PumpWorks proposes condition-based servicing of customers' installed pumps. The proposal is an initiative. Inquiry first establishes the relevant outages, operating conditions, user result and buyer's willingness to pay. Product strategy, engineering and economic appraisal supply different grounds for deciding whether to proceed. A launch project can finish with a tested possibility and a continuation decision, or with the agreed ability to service a first class of installations.

Compare keeping a service department with creating an internal enterprise. Under the first arrangement, another product authority defines the offer and commitments; the department performs assigned work within its budget. Under the second, the unit receives a bounded mandate to form or change its service offer, enter economic commitments through the firm's authorized arrangements, choose permitted means and bear attributed consequences for the direction. Its manager can commission approved diagnostics within a reserve; a new customer guarantee still needs the holder of that right. The split preserves a real mandate without pretending that the unit is independent of the firm.

In this service use, the maintained or changed pump installations in operation are the SoI. The service unit is their creator in the relevant work. Its launch project can close while the continuing unit performs service operations and further projects. A sensor supplier, the service unit and a customer operator can form an extended enterprise for agreed joint work if their actual obligations and resource arrangements support it; their other products and commitments remain outside that arrangement.

The internal payment makes the unit's work fundable. Usefulness to the operator, customer receipts and the distribution of costs and risks need their own grounds. If the evidence supports only a subsidized trial, authorize that bounded trial rather than claim a profitable continuing service. If a consultant performs the same organizational setup, the product basis and local capability acceptance remain. A substantive change in the offered service reopens those grounds.

#### OCE.3:5.4 - Keep two product lines in a shared laboratory decision

Suppose one laboratory serves water-supply pump stations and seasonal-irrigation installations. In the selected applications, the first line needs tests under frequent starts and the second under sustained load. Each has its own deliveries, result requirements and dates. The laboratory's design must support both. These stipulated regimes illustrate the distinction; real regimes come from the intended uses.

Tomorrow the internal bench has eight available hours, already allocated to one four-hour test for each line. An unexpected failure leads the first line to request another four hours of diagnosis. The laboratory manager recovers what delay would change: diagnosis is needed for the agreed commissioning decision, while postponing the second line's test would miss its promised delivery. The resulting twelve-hour demand cannot fit the same eight-hour interval. Bench utilization alone does not select the useful response.

The manager compares postponement, outside testing and an extra shift. No qualified person is available for an extra shift tomorrow. A previously qualified outside laboratory confirms an appropriate method, a free interval and a result by the required date including sample transport. The second line's agreed conditions permit that site and preserve its result requirements. The manager has authority to commission these tests from a reserve, and the confirmed price fits it.

The manager orders the second line's outside test and obtains confirmation that the sample and result can be handled as agreed. The internal bench now supplies four hours for the first line's planned test and four for its diagnosis. Both receiving teams obtain the revised schedule; their requirements and dates remain intact. This choice uses existing rights. Changing a promised date or requirement would instead need the holder of that obligation's amendment authority.

The order resolves this shortage. A recurring pattern of shortages can justify a different capacity arrangement, but first compare the expected requests, conflicts, delays, costs and alternatives. If an organizational change is warranted, frame a finite project to obtain the ability to provide both test families under an agreed demand range. Acceptance examines the regimes, equipment, qualified performers, result delivery, exception handling and capacity limits. It can finish before the future pumps operate; a test report, laboratory capability and a pump's fitness in use retain different acceptance grounds.

Attribute shared and product-specific laboratory costs to the parties that bear them. Charging everything to the first line would hide what supporting the second costs; changing an internal allocation would not create more customer revenue. An investment decision needs both product uses, expected obligations, attainable capacity and economic assumptions. The manager can do this work with ordinary names for the tests, promises, people and means. Whether the laboratory is an ordinary unit or an internal enterprise matters only when its economic mandate changes the decision.

### OCE.3:9 - Consequences

A target organization is no longer a picture selected by familiarity. Practitioners obtain alternatives with visible participant knowledge, feasibility, consequences, and transition burden.

The cost is that a favored concept can remain unresolved. More evidence or a smaller probe may be required before selection.

### OCE.3:10 - Rationale

Organization concepts are possible relation structures for a named contribution. Several views can help, but the decision turns on relations and consequences. A bounded domain generation move avoids importing open-ended search apparatus into every design while keeping that option available when retained exploration is real.

Preparation and interaction are alternative or complementary ways of obtaining particular coordination conditions. Their adequacy depends on what the participants must decide, know and contribute, not on how many messages they exchange. The design can change those dependencies as well as accommodate them. Yet useful joint discovery can depend on combining different knowledge, so reduced coordination effort is not the sole objective. The concept comparison preserves the receiving contribution together with its feasibility, authority and burden.

#### OCE.3:11.1 - Coordination under partial knowledge

**Practice question.** Which advance conditions and interactions allow these contributors to act independently where useful and still obtain the intended contribution? OCE.3:4.1.1.1 selects a dependency- and decision-based construction. Its advantage over choosing a familiar mechanism name is that the manager must explain the knowledge, commitments and remaining choices that make the proposed combination work. Its cost is the needed subject inquiry and participant contribution. A sufficient existing procedure, output arrangement or professional collaboration remains an adequate incumbent without reconstructing the whole theory on every occasion.

**Historical foundations.** [Galbraith (1974), *Organization Design: An Information Processing View*, pp. 28–36](https://paperzz.com/doc/8715024/organization-design--an-information-processing-view) explains reducing information-processing demand and increasing capacity under task uncertainty, including the costs of slack, self-contained tasks and lateral relations. **Adapt** the choice between changing a dependency and supporting its coordination; do not adopt an exhaustive strategy list or fixed progression. [Mintzberg and Glouberman (2001), pp. 72–74](https://mintzberg.org/sites/default/files/article/download/4c2.pdf) describe six familiar mechanisms and the limits of relying on standardized professional knowledge. **Adopt** their combination question; **adapt** the healthcare argument as a reason to examine the present preparation and exception, not as a universal prescription.

**The explanatory refinement.** [Puranam, Raveendran and Knudsen (2012), *Organization Design: The Epistemic Interdependence Perspective*](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2010.0535), with an [accessible author manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228295013_Organization_Design_The_Epistemic_Interdependence_Perspective), distinguishes task dependence from a participant's need for predictive knowledge. **Adapt** that distinction in the decision-timing and independence questions. The model's aligned-incentive and stable-environment assumptions limit its sufficiency here: OCE retains commitment, capability, changing knowledge and resource conditions separately. [Okhuysen and Bechky (2009), pp. 482–491](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232913984_10Coordination_in_Organizations_An_Integrative_Perspective) explain how different mechanisms support accountability, predictability and common understanding. **Adopt** examining what a mechanism actually supplies; **reject** treating those conditions as a universal sufficient checklist or requiring identical knowledge among specialists.

**Contemporary extension and bounded alternative.** [Raveendran, Silvestri and Gulati (2020)](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4qd2j3xz/qt4qd2j3xz_noSplash_416b2b66ccb3da74c5c7356718aeebed.pdf) distinguishes task, goal and knowledge interdependence and examines how agents can shape work when a complete division is unavailable in advance. **Adapt** that account in the initiative and joint-discovery branches; do not identify common rewards, common goals and technical dependencies. Its open research questions limit general predictions about emerging arrangements. [Worren and Cammelli (2025)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41469-025-00189-1) supplies a serious bounded alternative for grouping decisions with given roles and explicit dependencies. **Retain** that experimental comparison where those inputs and its coordination-cost objective fit. **Reject** using its optimized grouping as an answer to the broader question of creating dependencies, establishing commitments and choosing the whole coordination combination.

These sources justify distinctions and conditional design moves, not the effectiveness of the constructed examples. Reopen the selection when new knowledge changes a dependency, a different arrangement supplies the same contribution at lower total burden, or a recurring use requires a coordination construction these branches do not provide.
