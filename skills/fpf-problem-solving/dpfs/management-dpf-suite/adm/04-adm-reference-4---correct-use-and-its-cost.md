## ADM.Reference:4 - Correct use and its cost

In an ordinary request or note, name the action or condition, the participants it concerns, the relevant time, applicable rule and available grounds. Identify the next action when one is needed. Add a timeline, comparison or explicit event identity when the receiving question depends on it. For a service-level inquiry, identify the population and the consequence to be assessed.

For a consequential use, examine whether the result distinguishes the requested condition from its evidence, identifies who may resolve a missing condition, and makes clear any limitation on the conclusion or the next action. A claim of completed provision needs the promised usable effect. A supported relation claim needs the applicable rule and facts. A common event account needs enough correspondence to distinguish it from another event. These are different questions. An adequate answer can be reused for the question it resolves.

Elapsed delay, labor and money also answer different questions. The laboratory's T+N shorthand needs an explicit starting event and administrative result; reducing that delay can still worsen correctness or shift effort to the applicant. Include relevant unfinished and pre-entry cases before treating a portal indicator as the service outcome. A bounded observed defect may support a correction while a general effectiveness or causal claim remains unresolved.

This approach can reduce repeated checking, opaque refusals and unnecessary reconciliation. Use the sources that bear on the question, including participants' evidence when the portal omits their failed attempts or the extra work they did. A participant's account can remain unresolved when a needed source or qualified decision is unavailable. State the missing result and who can supply it; useful administrative framing can finish with that actionable gap.
