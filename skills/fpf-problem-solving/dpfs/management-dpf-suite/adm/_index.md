# Organization Administration Principles Framework

**DocID:** `ADM` within **FPF Library**.

**Author:** Anatoly Levenchuk, with AI-assisted development and review\
**Edition date:** 20 September 2026\
**Status:** Eternal alpha — an evolving framework, revised as methods and evidence improve.

ADM helps administrators examine administrative requests, give justified refusals and provide usable results under the organization's rules. Its fifteen patterns also help improve the arrangements supporting this work by connecting organizational rules, participants, permissions, work and records. Use the permission that applies to the action. Obtain a missing specialist judgement or decision from its competent source or authorized holder.

# Organization Administration Readme

## Practical entries

Organization Administration connects the condition a participant needs for its work with the rule that governs it and the provider able to supply it. For archive access, an eligibility result can support a grant. The grant specifies what the provider can enable. Use evidence of the effect to establish what the recipient obtained and, under the applicable terms, what the provider still owes. If provision fails or its conditions change, return to the question affected.

The examples below show how to start from different questions and connect the needed contributions. An ordinary request with adequate inputs can use its existing instruction directly. For a small ambiguity, [ADM.1 - Frame the Administrative Condition Needed for the Work](11-adm-1---frame-the-administrative-condition-needed-for-the-wo.md) may suffice: arranged transport and accommodation can be complete even when the traveller's negotiations fail. The next negotiating decision belongs to the person responsible for that work.

Use the Table of Contents for other questions. You can ask an assisting agent to explain a pattern or comment on your case in ordinary language. In `ADM.15:4.3`, `15` identifies the pattern and `4.3` its section; the Parts and `§` positions give publication order.

### ADM-E2 — A future appointment is being used for today's access request

- **Situation:** A researcher needs archive access by a particular date. The appointment, read grant, technical configuration and provider capacity concern the same request but establish different conditions.

- **Question:** Which condition is already established, and what must the next participant supply for access to become usable?

- **First useful result or blocker:** An answer about the supplied eligibility basis, followed by the applicable service response; when the institutional conditions hold, usable authorized access or the unresolved provision task.

- **Start with:** [ADM.3 - Determine What Changed in Administrative Relations and When It Takes Effect](13-adm-3---determine-what-changed-in-administrative-relations-a.md) for the date question. Use [ADM.2 - Establish the Relevant Participants and Relations for an Administrative Action](12-adm-2---establish-the-relevant-participants-and-relations-fo.md) if the beneficiary, decision holder, grant or provider responsibility is unclear.

- **Stop or return:** Apply the service rule to an established positive or negative condition. Keep adequate results. If a condition remains unresolved, direct the question to the source, provider or authority able to settle it.

1. **Match the basis to the intended use.** In the constructed archive case, the supplied appointment begins on 1 October and the rule requires an effective appointment. It therefore does not satisfy that condition for 25 September. A directory entry made earlier does not advance the effective date. This settles whether the appointment satisfies the eligibility condition on 25 September. To decide whether reading was permitted that day, consider the applicable grants and standing rules. A prohibition covering every permitted route can settle that question. Whether the researcher could open D also depends on the access provided.
2. **Pass the established conditions to provision.** Change the requested use to 2 October. Suppose the appointment now covers the work, an accepted identity result exists, and the custodian has granted reading of document D that day. [ADM.8 - Establish and Exercise the Required Permission for an Administrative Action](18-adm-8---establish-and-exercise-the-required-permission-for-a.md) supplies the bounds for the actual performer and action. The appointment supports eligibility. The credential is one technical means the researcher uses to attempt the read. Whether D opens depends on the provision under the custodian’s grant.
3. **Find the failed provider contribution.** If the researcher cannot open D, [ADM.9 - Provide a Usable Administrative Result](19-adm-9---provide-a-usable-administrative-result.md) uses the grant and failed attempt to direct recovery. In its case, the provider corrects an account mapping and the researcher then opens D. That result supports the named use; an administrator's successful login would not establish the same fact. A permitted alternative reading channel may suffice, while a download falls outside a read-only grant.
4. **Return a changed condition to the decision it affects.** If recovery moves use to 4 October, the 2 October grant no longer answers the permission question. If both cases are entitled to separate two-hour performances in one two-hour window and currently depend on the same specialist, [ADM.11 - Join Providers Around the Administrative Result](21-adm-11---join-providers-around-the-administrative-result.md) exposes the four-hour demand. Changing priority can choose which request gets the two hours. Additional qualified help can cover both only if substitution is allowed and the help is available in that window. When each case is entitled to the named person, the parties need an agreed change to the work or its timing. For an existing obligation, follow the applicable terms for performance and remedy. The valid appointments and grants need not be reconstructed unless that change affects their conditions.

### ADM-E3 — One party says “payment” and the other says “receipt”

- **Situation:** Participants' records describe a payment differently, and a closed case or lost provider response leaves fulfillment uncertain.

- **Question:** What was owed, what effect occurred, and which correction is now justified without paying twice?

- **First useful result or blocker:** A correspondence between the participant accounts and a supported fulfillment conclusion, or the particular unknown effect that the provider must recover.

- **Start with:** [ADM.4 - Reconcile Participant-Relative Administrative Accounts](14-adm-4---reconcile-participant-relative-administrative-accoun.md) when meanings or event identity differ. If those are already adequate, proceed directly to the outstanding effect or fulfillment question.

- **Stop or return:** Retain both correct accounts of one event. Correct a record through its authorized procedure, supply established missing performance under the applicable permission, or preserve an unknown effect before a potentially duplicate action.

1. **Determine what each account asserts.** An outgoing payment and an incoming receipt can describe one transfer from different positions. An issued instruction, a credited amount and discharge of an obligation are different facts. ADM.4 establishes the correspondence using the participants, event evidence, amount, time and relevant local meanings; the shared word “paid” cannot supply it.
2. **Ask for the fact that can change the next action.** In the constructed case of 1,000 units owed, 600 are confirmed credited and a second instruction for 400 has an unknown effect. [ADM.7 - Check the Claim Needed for This Administrative Decision](17-adm-7---check-the-claim-needed-for-this-administrative-decis.md) isolates the missing claim: whether that second attempt credited the intended account. Another receipt upload or a changed status label would not answer it. Use a sufficiently reliable confirmation tied to this attempt and account if one is available. Otherwise use ADM.9 to take the unresolved question and original reference to a provider able to answer it.
3. **Use the recovered effect under the actual terms.** [ADM.10 - Reconcile Administrative Obligations, Performance and Records](20-adm-10---reconcile-administrative-obligations-performance-an.md) applies the supplied rule that credited amounts discharge the corresponding obligation. Confirmed credit of the full 400 establishes fulfilment. If the second attempt credited only part, subtract that part from the 400 remaining after the first credit. Confirmed failure before credit leaves 400 outstanding and permits the applicable authorized continuation. Continued uncertainty establishes neither full fulfillment nor a definitely unpaid 400.
4. **Preserve the history needed for correction.** A provider report arriving on 12 September can establish that credit occurred on 10 September. [ADM.13 - Keep Administrative Records Fit for Their Uses](22-adm-13---keep-administrative-records-fit-for-their-uses.md) preserves the earlier grounds, later evidence and their correspondence. Correcting the account neither repeats the credit nor changes its event time. A new fee or allocation dispute returns to the terms and competent interpretation it affects.

### ADM-E7 — A new policy starts while earlier cases remain open

- **Situation:** A service owner is introducing an allowance while receipt-based journeys, unsettled payments and disputed claims remain.

- **Question:** How can the authorized change become usable without losing the conditions that still govern earlier cases?

- **First useful result or blocker:** A supported decision about retaining or changing the arrangement, or the specific missing policy, provider or evidence contribution. If a change is to take effect, establish usable handling for the affected cases.

- **Start with:** [ADM.16 - Decide Whether and How to Change an Administrative Arrangement Without Losing Open Cases](25-adm-16---decide-whether-and-how-to-change-an-administrative.md), using the actual retention or change question and one case whose treatment could differ.

- **Stop or return:** Finish the selected change with the promised provision and continued support for the earlier cases that still need it; identify any unresolved cases and their next responsible action. A new form, successful data import or quiet old queue does not settle a grant, obligation or required historical use.

1. **Use service consequences to judge the proposed change.** [ADM.15 - Judge Administrative Consequences](24-adm-15---judge-administrative-consequences.md) separates usable provision, correct and mistaken refusal, unresolved effects and work shifted to applicants. Its access-service case has 70 closures among 80 registered requests, but only 60 confirmed provisions; twenty additional requests failed before registration. Such an account can justify correcting an established defect without waiting for a general effectiveness study. A proposed policy change still needs its own grounds and competent decision.
2. **Apply the adopted transition to the fact that selects the regime.** In ADM.16's constructed allowance case, the ordinary rule assigns receipt-based terms to journeys authorized before 1 November and the allowance to those authorized from that date. Authorization on 28 October places the journey in the earlier cohort, even if performed on 3 November and claimed on 6 November. A valid election before disbursement, with the required confirmation, can change the terms for that journey. Unknown authorization or election facts remain specific questions, not guesses from the form version. If the permitted regimes give the same authorized payment under the present facts, the handler can decide that payment. Identifying which regime governed, or completing a separately required check, can still require the missing date or election.
3. **Turn that decision into usable handling.** [ADM.5 - Design Reusable Instructions for Administrative Requests](15-adm-5---design-reusable-instructions-for-administrative-requ.md) uses the policy and its transition conditions to specify the minimum request, necessary checks, competent decision, provider action, completion evidence and exceptions. ADM.11 obtains the provider contributions and resolves their joins. If a control gives the wrong response, [ADM.14 - Connect an Administrative Control to Its Purpose and Competent Decision](23-adm-14---connect-an-administrative-control-to-its-purpose-an.md) distinguishes a defective test or implementation from a policy requiring authorized change.
4. **Establish usable handling and preserve unresolved effects.** Establish which uses change under the new terms. For this allowance transition the selected rehearsal covers an old-regime case, a new-regime case and a consequential election or unknown-payment branch. For another change, use sufficient earlier evidence and the applicable rule, and perform a trial when it is required or can settle a consequential uncertainty at a justified cost. The recipient must obtain the promised result; a converted record is only one input. For an unchanged use, reuse adequate existing observations. Use a new staff exercise when its attainable result about independent handling or required support justifies the work. ADM.9 guides recovery of an earlier payment effect before another payment or conversion that could duplicate it; ADM.10 guides reconciliation with the obligation and records when that question remains open. ADM.13 retains the grounds needed for active disputes and legitimate historical use. Ending new requests through an old form does not by itself authorize disposal of those records.

The [Architectural Reference](_index.md#organization-administration-principles-framework-architectural-reference) explains the common boundaries and the wider provider relationships. When ordinary handling cannot resolve a missing rule, authority or effect, [ADM.6 - Resolve an Administrative Exception](16-adm-6---resolve-an-administrative-exception.md) helps the handler put an answerable question to the competent participant. Work that does not depend on the answer can continue within its permission.
