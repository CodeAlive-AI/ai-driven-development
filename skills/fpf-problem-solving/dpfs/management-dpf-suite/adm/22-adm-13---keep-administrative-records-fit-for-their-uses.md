## ADM.13 - Keep Administrative Records Fit for Their Uses

**Type:** Architectural

**Status:** Stable

### ADM.13:0 - Use this when

Use this pattern when a reader needs to establish an administrative relation, decision or provision, and the retained records may not support that use. A handler may need to explain an earlier refusal, recover the terms that governed an open claim, correct a mistaken account or decide what can be disclosed or disposed of.

Start with the later question and its legitimate reader. The first useful result is an adequate record arrangement for that use, including its interpretation, access, correction and retention conditions, or a precise account of the source, capability or authorized decision still missing.

Use an adequate record directly. Apply the retention, disclosure and priority rules governing the case. If their application is unclear and matters to the question, obtain a qualified interpretation or state the limit on use.

### ADM.13:1 - Problem frame

The practitioner is maintaining records used in administration. A record may contain a request, rule edition, decision, grant, provider instruction, observed effect or participant account. It may be stored in one system or recovered from several adequate sources.

What is recorded and what occurred have different conditions. An appointment may already be effective before a directory is updated. A rule may instead make a particular registration act part of constitution. For the relation or event at issue, establish what legal or institutional effect the applicable rule gives to the record.

A recipient needs an intelligible response; a dispute resolver needs the relevant grounds; a provider may need only the current authorized instruction. Fit for one use does not imply unrestricted access or fitness for every other use.

### ADM.13:2 - Problem

A current status overwrites the earlier decision and its grounds. Later staff cannot explain why an action was taken, which terms applied or whether a payment was actually made. Alternatively, an organization keeps every document indefinitely while losing the interpretation or access route needed to use it.

Correction, restricted use and disposal are often treated as one operation. Someone deletes an old account to fix a current display, or keeps exposing information because another legitimate purpose requires retaining it. Correction and disclosure each need their own grounds and authority.

### ADM.13:3 - Forces

| Force | Tension |
| --- | --- |
| Future use | Enough grounds must remain recoverable without collecting every available fact. |
| Historical accuracy | A corrected account should improve the answer while retaining material earlier decisions and their basis. |
| Access | A record needed for one authorized use may contain information unnecessary for another reader. |
| Retention | Continuing obligations or disputes can require records while other information has reached its disposal condition. |
| Representation | Several correct participant accounts can coexist; a common storage location does not make their meanings identical. |

### ADM.13:4 - Solution

#### ADM.13:4.1 - Name the uses the record must support

Identify who must establish what, for which subject, purpose and time. Include only uses that the applicable service, authority, obligation, recovery or record rule requires.

For an earlier refusal, the needed answer may be the request assessed, the applicable rule, the grounds then used and the response given. For a current provider action, the necessary record may be much smaller: an adequate current authorization and its relevant conditions.

Distinguish a required future use from speculative convenience. If an existing source can reliably supply the needed grounds within its authorized access and retention conditions, a second full copy may add no useful result.

#### ADM.13:4.2 - Retain the content and correspondence needed by those uses

Keep the identity and meaning of the source, decision or event, and its effective and recording times where those distinctions change the answer. Identify the material rule or instruction edition used in an earlier decision.

Separate what the source asserts, what the handler established and what action occurred. For a claim about the effect of an instruction, use evidence of that effect, such as the credit to the beneficiary’s account. A copy of a decision carries the scope supported by the original authority and conditions.

Use [FPF A.10][A10] when the source-to-use account must remain recoverable and contestable. Use ADM.3–ADM.4 or SIE for a material temporal or cross-source correspondence question. An ordinary case reference and relevant source can suffice.

#### ADM.13:4.3 - Make authorized retrieval and interpretation workable

Provide an authorized way for each required reader to retrieve the record and interpret it for the intended use. Establish that it works from relevant evidence or by trying the retrieval where necessary.

Apply the access and disclosure rules for the record. Give a participant the information needed for the legitimate use through an appropriate protected channel. Separate the ability to retain a record from permission to disclose all of its contents.

Where an old format or system is being retired, identify the reading capability, context and identity that must survive. A conversion that preserves visible words but loses the relation between decision, beneficiary and effective date may defeat the needed use. Where conversion or restoration is needed, use a method appropriate to that technical work.

#### ADM.13:4.4 - Correct the right thing

Determine whether the defect concerns a description, an absent evidential link, an institutional act or missing provision. Select the corresponding authorized correction.

For a mistaken description, preserve enough of the original and correction to support the required historical use. State what was corrected and the effective facts the new account describes. A spelling correction does not constitute another appointment or move its effective date.

If the governing rule makes a registration act constitutive, establish whether and when that act took place. If its occurrence remains unknown, retain that limit on the institutional conclusion. If the payment is still outstanding, use ADM.9–ADM.10 to provide or reconcile it.

A later source may change what can now be established about an earlier event. Preserve both the earlier decision's actual grounds and the later qualified account. The responsible participant applies the case procedure to determine whether revision or a remedy is due.

#### ADM.13:4.5 - Separate new use, disclosure, retention and disposal

For a proposed change, identify which action is being considered. Stopping new requests through an old form, restricting access, correcting a record, ending reliance on an obsolete rule and disposing of retained data are different actions.

Recover the applicable retention and disposal conditions, including a live dispute or hold when one exists. Match them to the record class and intended use. Apply an established precedence rule or obtain a qualified interpretation if a material conflict remains.

Apply authorized disposal only to the eligible scope. Retention for one continuing purpose does not authorize unrelated new use or unrestricted disclosure. After ordinary use stops, retain records still required for a continuing obligation, an authorized later use or a historical enquiry.

#### ADM.13:4.6 - Establish what the reader can recover

Use an applicable retrieval result, or try the question that made the record necessary. Recover the earlier grounds, establish the effect of a correction or supply a permitted extract, as needed. If a changed identifier, an unavailable reader or a disputed source could defeat that use, check the affected connection.

Return the record arrangement and its applicable limits, or the missing capability, source or decision. A representative retrieval establishes the tested use under its conditions.

Reopen the affected arrangement when a required use, rule, reader, record meaning, dispute or provider capability changes.

### ADM.13:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### ADM.13:5.1 - Explaining the earlier access response

On 25 September a researcher requests archive access using an appointment effective on 1 October. Policy P requires an effective appointment at the requested time. The handler establishes that the supplied appointment does not meet that condition and gives P's prescribed response.

For a later explanation, the relevant record retains the assessed request and date, appointment decision and effective date, policy P, the negative temporal result and the actual response. The finding concerns the appointment presented. Other appointments and a separate read grant were outside this assessment.

A later directory correction fixes the spelling of the researcher's name while preserving identity and the appointment's effective date. The authorized record maintainer corrects the current display. The historical account still explains the September response, using the original appointment and effective date.

Now suppose the researcher presents a different, earlier appointment decision. That new source may change the current eligibility account. The handler retains the earlier assessment's actual basis and sends the new source through the applicable case procedure. ADM.3 establishes the temporal claim; ADM.8 supplies any still-required read-permission question. The record distinguishes what informed the September response from what the handler can now establish.

#### ADM.13:5.2 - A dispute hold and an obsolete service form

Suppose the organization's supplied record rule requires the decision and material grounds for a disputed claim to remain available to the authorized resolver until the hold is released. A separate instruction ends new requests through an old form on 1 November. The old system also contains routine diagnostic records with their own disposal conditions.

The service owner can stop new use of the form under that instruction. The active dispute's decision and grounds still need their permitted retrieval route. The resolver receives the relevant protected account; other readers do not receive unrestricted access merely because retention continues.

Before withdrawing the old reader, the provider demonstrates that the retained decision, source meaning and correction history can be recovered through the authorized replacement. The service owner applies the separate disposal conditions to eligible diagnostic material. If the scope of a restriction or hold is unclear, seek an interpretation from its competent source and keep the disputed scope open until resolved.



### ADM.13:6 - Bias-Annotation

Current-system designers tend to privilege present fields over historical explanation. Future-use advocates can make the opposite error and retain everything without a defined legitimate purpose. Begin with the concrete uses and rules.

Records also reflect the perspective of their producer. Preserve participant correspondence and material disagreement instead of assuming the most centralized account is correct for every question.

### ADM.13:7 - Conformance Checklist

**Recognition.** The record arrangement names the required use and reader, necessary content and meaning, applicable access, and the distinct correction and retention conditions. Where the sought answer cannot be recovered, it identifies the missing source, access, interpretation or decision. It separates a description from any constituting act or actual provision.

**Assurance for reliance.** To support a claim of retrievability, use an applicable earlier retrieval or check that the intended reader can recover and interpret the answer through the authorized channel. Cover any correction, identity change, hold or reader withdrawal material to that use. If the answer is unavailable, establish the source, access or interpretation limit that prevents retrieval. For disclosure or disposal, establish the authority for that action.

### ADM.13:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**Current status replaces the past** destroys the grounds for an earlier decision. Preserve the material earlier account and the correction needed by later uses.

**The record creates the event** treats an edited field as appointment, authorization or payment. Identify whether the actual rule makes a recording act constitutive and whether that act occurred.

**Retain means disclose** extends a legitimate retention purpose into unrestricted access. Apply the disclosure rule to the reader's particular use.

### ADM.13:9 - Consequences

Participants can explain decisions, recover obligations and correct descriptions without inventing events. Service changes can preserve necessary history while allowing eligible obsolete material to be removed.

Maintaining the record requires usable access and interpretation as well as storage. If a restriction is unresolved or a needed historical source is unavailable, identify which conclusion or action this prevents.

### ADM.13:10 - Architectural Rationale

Records connect participants to claims about relations, decisions and performance. Their usefulness depends on recoverable meaning, legitimate access and the time at which the claim matters. This is why correcting a description, ending new use and disposing of the record can require different acts and permissions.

### ADM.13:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The May and June laboratories and the qualified ADM source pack supply the relation/record, correction and execution distinctions. [FPF A.10][A10] and [SIE.4 through SIE.6][SIE] supply bounded source recovery and meaning correspondence.

[ISO 15489-1:2016][ISO]'s public abstract connects records, metadata, responsibilities, business context and controls. That conceptual scope informs §§4.1–4.5. The full paid standard and its clauses are not used here; no retention period or compliance conclusion is derived from the abstract.

Use an existing case database directly when its history and retrieval are adequate for the required uses. Choose the access, correction and disposal arrangements by the intended institutional use and its governing rule.

Reopen the affected arrangement when its legitimate use, governing restriction, source interpretation or recovery capability changes. The examples use the specified rules. Apply the rules governing the case for a claim about an actual record.
