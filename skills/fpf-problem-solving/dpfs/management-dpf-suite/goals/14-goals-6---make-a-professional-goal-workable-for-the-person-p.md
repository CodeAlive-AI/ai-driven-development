## GOALS.6 - Make a professional goal workable for the person pursuing it

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.6:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** a person wants or is expected to pursue a professional goal, yet adopting it, beginning, continuing or changing course remains difficult. The object is that person's goal pursuit under actual work and participation conditions. The result is an intelligible and feasible next action, support arrangement or revised aim.

The method concerns professional and work-related learning aims. Clinical treatment and a general diagnosis of personality require other qualified practice. An established routine that already works needs no additional personal-goal programme.

### GOALS.6:2 - Problem

A goal can be clear without being workable for its performer. The person may disagree with it, lack an essential skill or means, face competing obligations, miss an opportunity to act, or use a response that no longer helps. Treating each of these as weak motivation directs effort at the wrong cause and can conceal an incoherent assignment.

### GOALS.6:3 - Forces

Personal purposes and role obligations can differ legitimately. Demanding aims can justify development while current delivery needs reliable provision. Sustained attention has opportunity costs across work and life. Help can enable a useful result while changing the claim about independent performance. Persistence and adjustment each have value under different conditions.

### GOALS.6:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.6:4.1 - Relate the person's aim to the actual undertaking

Ask what the person wants to obtain or protect through the work and what the assignment requires. Use their account; avoid inferring private purposes from a score. Distinguish voluntary adoption, an effective work obligation and a negotiated development opportunity. LDR.1:4.4.1 supplies the role conversation when that connection is unclear.

Work through a real next episode. What action is required, what discretion and help are available, what others rely on, and what other work competes? If the person legitimately declines a voluntary contribution, choose another contribution or revise the undertaking. If an obligation governs the work, use its actual decision and challenge route. Labelling an assignment “your aspiration” does not settle its participation conditions.

#### GOALS.6:4.2 - Locate the difficulty that changes the remedy

Recover a recent attempt and enough of its conditions to distinguish the live alternatives. If the person knows the action but lacks time, access or equipment, obtain or change that provision. If the goal is unclear, use GOALS.1. If the action itself is unknown, obtain the subject method. If the person cannot yet perform a required operation, use HCD to define and develop that contribution. If capable action occurred but failed to help, examine the means hypothesis through GOALS.4.

For a learning goal, state the later task, conditions, allowed assistance and evidence of the human contribution. Compare learning with obtaining a competent result from a colleague, provider or agent, preserving any independent value placed on personal mastery. HCD.2 supplies the full programme comparison. Count practice, feedback and correction alongside current work; adding a course cannot create spare capacity.

#### GOALS.6:4.3 - Bridge an adopted intention to suitable action

When the person accepts the goal and the difficulty is acting on a suitable intention, an implementation intention can connect a recognizable situation to an instrumental response: “When the review request arrives, I will identify the claim and its evidence before proposing a verdict.” Choose a cue that can be noticed and a response that can actually help. Rehearse or clarify the connection if needed. Revisit it when conditions require another response. Gollwitzer and Sheeran's planning review supports this conditional human strategy; it does not supply missing competence, means or a reason to adopt the goal.

Use a simpler arrangement when adequate: a ready work space, an agreed protected interval, a direct reminder or an existing routine may answer the difficulty. The choice follows the observed problem and the person's feasible conditions. Keep the next action small enough to be performed but large enough to make a useful contribution; a tiny activity that avoids the hard operation is no substitute for it.

#### GOALS.6:4.4 - Reconcile competing aims and adjust deliberately

Compare the relevant goals together, including current obligations, recovery and unfinished learning. Ask whether they compete for the same time, attention or opportunity, or whether one action can genuinely serve several. A shared action can reduce duplicate effort while the result claims remain distinct. Use GOALS.3 for joint organizational consequences and HCD.2:4.2a for concurrent learning.

When pursuit becomes less worthwhile or feasible, consider a change of means, a narrower next contribution, a pause or relinquishing the goal. Distinguish the specific target from the wider purpose it serves. Ending one route can preserve another; persisting can remain justified when the purpose and available way still warrant it. Include the remaining opportunity costs and the person's values. Use GOALS.4 to make any affected commitments and continuation explicit.

Return a concrete next action and the help or decision it needs. Arrange feedback from the receiving work at an occasion when it can change the next attempt. Retain successful support when independence is unnecessary; test independent performance only when that is the claim. Recognition identifies the kind of difficulty. Assurance grounds the proposed remedy and the actual capability or outcome claimed.

### GOALS.6:5 - Archetypal Grounding

An analyst wants to learn how to frame ambiguous client questions while delivering this month's reports. An agent currently frames the questions and writes the reports. The company receives a useful output, but that arrangement provides little practice of the intended human operation.

The analyst and provider identify a suitable forthcoming case. The analyst frames it before seeing the agent's proposal, explains which decision the answer must support, compares the alternatives and receives feedback on a missed assumption. The delivery arrangement retains qualified help. A later materially different case can support a transfer claim under its stated assistance. The week includes time for framing and feedback; the existing delivery allocation changes accordingly.

Now change the condition: the only current need is an accurate report by tomorrow, and no independent human mastery is required. The competent agent's result with the necessary assurance can be sufficient. Requiring the analyst to learn and duplicate every operation would change the task and its cost. A later voluntary development aim can be considered separately.

### GOALS.6:6 - Bias-Annotation

People differ in purpose, constraints, available help and the cost of disclosure. Ask about conditions relevant to the work and respect the person's participation and privacy. Evidence from one population or task does not establish a universal motivational prescription.

### GOALS.6:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Does the action fit the person's actual participation and the role's reliance?
- Has the remedy addressed the difficulty that can change performance?
- Are learning, assisted delivery and independent performance distinguished?
- Do competing goals and the required help fit the available conditions?
- Can the person recognize when to keep, adapt, pause or end this pursuit?

### GOALS.6:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**More pressure for every failure:** distinguish missing means, method, skill, acceptance and changed purpose before choosing help.

**Practice time counted as mastery:** obtain evidence of the intended human contribution under the specified conditions.

**A personal goal used to hide incompatible assignments:** take the conflict to the actual allocation and commitment owners.

### GOALS.6:9 - Consequences

The person obtains a feasible way to proceed or a justified change in the undertaking. The arrangement may use assistance, learning or a different performer. It can improve practical clarity without establishing a measured psychological or learning effect.

### GOALS.6:10 - Architectural Rationale

The method combines the common goal account with human participation, action and development. Those conditions determine which intervention is appropriate. A purely numeric target lacks this construction; a comprehensive personal-development programme is excessive when a specific next action and available help suffice.

### GOALS.6:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The human branch adapts conditional planning where intention-to-action is the actual problem and retains LDR/HCD for purpose, support and capability. Goal-adjustment research makes withdrawal and reengagement legitimate questions with distinct grounds. The practical choice remains specific to this person's work, values and alternatives. Reopen the arrangement when those conditions change.

### GOALS.6:12 - Relations

GOALS.1 interprets the aim, GOALS.3 addresses shared demands and GOALS.4 revises continuation. LDR.1 supplies usable help and role conversation; HCD supplies the learning method and evidence. GOALS.7 applies to an agent's separate contribution, including where it changes the human's task or learning opportunity.
