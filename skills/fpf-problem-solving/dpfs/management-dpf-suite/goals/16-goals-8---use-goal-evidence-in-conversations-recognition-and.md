## GOALS.8 - Use goal evidence in conversations, recognition and personnel decisions

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.8:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** goal information will support help, reflection, recognition or a decision about a person. The object is that receiving use of evidence and the interaction through which it affects work or the person. The result is useful feedback or recognition, or a properly grounded return to the actual personnel decision.

A routine factual update with an understood use need not become a formal assessment. When the question is already a personnel selection, promotion or pay decision, use HRM's full method; this pattern supplies the goal evidence and the distinctions needed at that connection.

### GOALS.8:2 - Problem

The same goal score can be used to decide help, discuss progress, recognize a contribution or allocate pay. Those uses need different grounds. A team result may depend on shared resources and agent work; an ambitious shortfall may differ from failure of a supported promise. Treating the score as a universal judgement can distort both the work and the conversation.

### GOALS.8:3 - Forces

Candid reporting supports useful action while real consequence rules influence what people disclose. Recognition should make valuable contributions visible, including less visible help. Privacy and legitimate personnel use must be intelligible. Timely informal help should remain possible, and consequential decisions need the evidence and authority they actually require.

### GOALS.8:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.8:4.1 - State the receiving question and permitted use

Identify what this interaction must produce: clarify a result, understand an obstacle, supply help, learn from an episode, recognize a contribution, or inform a personnel decision. Tell participants the material purpose, audience and use of observations. Reuse established arrangements when they are sufficient. A later new use of private learning data needs its own grounds and permissions.

Recover the actual goal mode, evidence, starting conditions, allocation, changes and material contributions. OPS.22 supplies an operating performance interpretation where needed. Distinguish the observed result from an explanation of it and from a judgement about a person. An unfavorable forecast should expose a response decision; if the actual consequence rule penalizes truthful reporting, obtain the relevant change or acknowledge the constraint rather than offering a false assurance of safety.

#### GOALS.8:4.2 - Conduct a useful conversation and feedback exchange

Use LDR.1's smallest sufficient working conversation. Begin with the concrete episode and the participant's account. Ask what happened, what they were trying to do, which conditions helped or hindered, and what next contribution would change the work. Supply a clear observation and its consequence rather than an unexplained personality label. The participant can correct factual mistakes or offer another supported explanation.

Agree the next useful action, help and response occasion. The needed contribution may be a clearer assignment, resource decision, subject answer, changed method or learning opportunity. Assign it to someone able to provide it. A manager's advice cannot on its own change another team's commitment or supply a teacher's missing competence. If the conversation already settles the work, end there.

For development, distinguish supported practice from performance used for consequential appraisal. Use HCD when the person needs a new capability and agree the use of its observations. Feedback should let the learner change an operation and try again under suitable conditions. A conversation about learning is not evidence that learning occurred.

#### GOALS.8:4.3 - Recognize an evidenced contribution

Name the contribution, the result or continuation it helped and the available basis for that account. Acknowledging someone who surfaced an unworkable promise, helped a neighbor or repaired a bad assumption can make useful work visible alongside headline outputs. Keep the claim proportional: contribution to a team result is different from sole causation, and ownership of an OKR is different from performing all its work.

Choose recognition appropriate to the participant and setting, including preferences about publicity. Explain enough for colleagues to understand the practice worth retaining. Formulaic praise or a ranking detached from the contribution provides little guidance and can misattribute credit.

#### GOALS.8:4.4 - Return consequential people decisions to their complete grounds

Goal evidence can inform a personnel decision when its interpretation fits that decision. HRM.2 supplies the actual work analysis, assessment inference, comparison and limits. HRM.3 supplies remuneration and progression through their applicable bases and terms; HRM.1 checks the coherent people/work arrangement. Include opportunity, assistance, scope, attribution and relevant changes when comparing contributions.

Avoid a direct universal score-to-pay or score-to-promotion conversion. This preserves legitimate use of achievement evidence: a defined, agreed contribution can be relevant under a qualified reward rule. The rule's authority, validity, fairness and actual terms still govern. Obtain the applicable professional and institutional judgement where it is required; this method does not issue an employment or legal determination.

Return the conversation result or qualified input to its receiving owner. Recognition detects an unjustified change of use. Assurance establishes the evidence, permitted use, inference and authority needed for the actual consequence.

### GOALS.8:5 - Archetypal Grounding

Two employees have scores of 1.0 and 0.6. The first had a committed goal with established means and completed the defined result. The second pursued an aspirational search, discovered that a proposed solution would fail and helped two teams avoid wasted work. Their scores answer different goal comparisons. A direct ranking would lose that difference.

In a CFR conversation, the second employee's evidence supports discussion of the search quality, the useful finding and the help provided. The team can recognize those contributions and decide what to pursue next. A promotion panel separately asks whether the employee can perform the proposed wider role. HRM.2 receives appropriate evidence, including relevant goal work, but needs the role-specific inference and comparison.

Change the condition: a valid reward arrangement already grants a specified payment for an evidenced contribution, and its terms are met. The separation of CFR and raw scores does not remove that obligation. HRM.3 makes the payment decision and implementation effective under the actual terms.

### GOALS.8:6 - Bias-Annotation

Visible output can conceal helping, care, coordination and unequal opportunity. Interpret the evidence in the actual work configuration. Participants' preferences about recognition and disclosure differ. AI-generated work changes attribution and the relevant human contribution; it does not erase that contribution or establish it automatically.

### GOALS.8:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Is the purpose and material use of the conversation or evidence clear to participants?
- Does feedback concern an observable, changeable contribution with usable help?
- Is recognition supported and appropriately attributed?
- Does a personnel conclusion use the full decision-specific evidence and authority?
- Have changed data uses and applicable terms been handled through their actual grounds?

### GOALS.8:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**A private learning discussion becomes an undisclosed assessment:** establish the new use's grounds and permitted evidence before relying on it.

**Score equals person:** recover the goal mode, work conditions and relevant contribution; use HRM for the personnel inference.

**Separation interpreted as total exclusion:** keep legitimate achievement evidence available under the actual decision rule while rejecting unsupported raw-score substitution.

### GOALS.8:9 - Consequences

Conversations can produce usable help and recognition without pretending to settle every personnel question. Consequential decisions receive better-qualified evidence. Clear use conditions can require an uncomfortable discussion about existing incentives or an unavailable authority; concealing that conflict would leave the practice unreliable.

### GOALS.8:10 - Architectural Rationale

The receiving decision determines the meaning and adequacy of evidence. This pattern develops the connection among goal work, CFR and personnel practice while retaining their different objects. It avoids both the automatic score conversion and the blanket rule that goal performance can never inform a people decision.

### GOALS.8:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Doerr's CFR account and resource guide distinguish ongoing conversation and recognition from mechanical compensation through OKR scores. LDR and HRM supply fuller present methods for assistance, assessment, attribution and terms. This construction adapts CFR to the actual relationship and permits justified evidence use. Changes in work, tools, terms or data purpose reopen the affected inference.

### GOALS.8:12 - Relations

GOALS.2 supplies the achievement evidence and GOALS.4 its changing context. LDR.1 supplies conversation and help, HRM.1–3 supply people decisions, and HCD supplies development. GOALS.5 incorporates the appropriate interactions into the organizational practice; GOALS.9 repairs missing capability or support.
