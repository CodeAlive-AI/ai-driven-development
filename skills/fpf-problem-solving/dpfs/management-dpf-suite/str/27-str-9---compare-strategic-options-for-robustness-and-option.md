## STR.9 - Compare Strategic Options for Robustness and Option Value

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### STR.9:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern to find where a strategic option meets a stated condition, how options compare as conditions vary, or what a feasible later choice contributes. Start with the required result, the conditions to test and any alternatives the question compares.

The result states where the options satisfy the conditions tested, any preference or reversal the evidence supports, relevant costs of flexibility and what remains unresolved. The comparison can support a recommendation or a narrower commitment. Binding resources or conduct requires the authorization that applies to that commitment.

Robustness means that a stated performance or comparison condition holds across a declared range. Option value concerns the contribution of preserving a feasible later choice. State the range tested and the effort or rights needed to keep the choice available.

Reuse a sufficient comparison for the same options, configuration, horizon and criterion. A direct comparison or a justified probability-based model can be enough. Match the domain evidence, model adequacy and assurance to the consequences of relying on the result. Stable outputs can come from an inadequate model.

### STR.9:2 - Problem

An option can win a weighted score while failing a protected condition. Another may be called robust because it wins under several nearly identical futures. A retained possibility may be treated as currently best even though it survives only for later exploration.

“We can switch later” can hide expired rights, unavailable capacity, slow observation or the work needed to preserve a fallback. An option description is not itself the ability to exercise that option.

### STR.9:3 - Forces

A common comparison basis makes alternatives intelligible, while genuine differences can resist one scalar ranking. Keep protected conditions, differences between beneficiaries and unresolved authority visible where they affect the comparison.

A bounded commitment can preserve future choices, but carrying and switching costs may make immediate commitment preferable. More analysis can expose a reversal while delaying the action that made the option valuable. The comparison therefore includes the cost and timing of both evaluation and realization.

### STR.9:4 - Solution

Compare each option against the stated criterion or protected condition. When the question compares alternatives, apply the same question to each. Test the conditions that could change acceptability, relative preference or the grounds for the conclusion. Include the full change to the current configuration and the conditions for exercising a future choice where relevant.

#### STR.9:4.1 - Fix the comparison being claimed

Identify the strategic subject, current configuration, option or alternatives, horizon, criterion and protected conditions. Use existing accounts of the options and their conditions when they fit. STR.6 can supply missing alternatives and STR.8 can identify supported conditions and gaps. Apply a common question to the alternatives being compared.

Distinguish an option excluded by a current condition from one with a low expected benefit or an unresolved premise. Required permission is an eligibility question; uncertain demand is a consequence question. Leave an unestimated effect open.

Use a qualified PSDA.9 robustness account when it already covers the relevant configuration and variation. Strategy adds the effect on direction, commitment timing, capability and future exercise. Check the horizon and configuration before transferring a result.

When another participant's response can change the result, use STR.4's qualified response account for each candidate move. A common comparison basis can contain the same conditional response rule applied to different own moves, producing different responses. Compare the induced outcomes, timing, full burden and protected limits. Where the response premise is unresolved, state the conditional result.

In STR.4:5.3 the provider has grounds to restrict the interface after integration under the original arrangement. A qualified prior transfer removes that move for a stated window. An enforceable change in incentives could instead make maintenance preferable while leaving restriction available. Compare outcomes under the arrangement actually assumed. When no other participant’s response affects the question, an ordinary comparison under external conditions is enough.

#### STR.9:4.2 - Choose the criterion for the comparison

Identify the condition, value or protection the comparison addresses. Use the applicable rule or state the results under competing rules if none has been selected. Expected value requires defensible probabilities and a value model suited to the use. Under deep uncertainty, conditional performance, worst-case loss, regret or an acceptable region may answer the question.

These criteria answer different questions. Worst-case comparison emphasizes the largest loss in the declared conditions. Regret compares an option's result with the best available alternative in each condition, under a common justified measure. Apply protected obligations before judging preference by regret.

Keep material value disagreement explicit. A scalar can help only when its trade-offs are warranted for this use. Explicitly assumed probabilities and scales yield a conditional calculation. A claim about expected outcomes under real conditions requires probabilities and scales justified for that use. Identify a changed criterion as a new comparison.

For a dominance claim, specify the characteristics, their preferred directions, the conditions and the evidence. Under Pareto comparison, an eligible rival that is at least as good on each characteristic and better on one dominates the proposal. A supported advantage for the proposal on one characteristic rules out domination by that rival. Compare with every eligible rival to establish membership of the non-dominated set. A proposal may be ruled out even while the evidence leaves the identity of its dominating rival open. Establish that at least one joint estimate fits the evidence for this decision. If the estimates conflict, resolve which evidence supports the comparison. Exclude the proposal if every admissible joint estimate gives it a dominating rival. Choosing which rival to back can require further evidence. Where the estimates change membership itself, retain that uncertainty. A chooser can use established trade-offs and state the priority behind a selection. A higher weighted average alone establishes no Pareto dominance.

#### STR.9:4.3 - Compare the whole finite change with the current configuration

Identify what the option would add, remove or replace. Include transition, support, recovery, common bottlenecks and displaced work. An indivisible training package or service commitment cannot be valued by multiplying an isolated local gain without checking its joint effect.

Use FPF C.11.CRC, *Configuration-Relative Contribution Comparison*, when this finite comparison is missing. It makes the current configuration, changed configuration, result and resource coordinates, interactions and option effects explicit. Use an existing account when it already covers the whole change.

When money is material, identify the offer or obligation behind each claimed change: who would pay or receive what, when, and under which demand and acceptance conditions over the chosen horizon. Use an adequate OPS.14 operating account for a bounded operating choice, or the qualified financial result needed for another use. Reconcile shared receipts, avoidable payments and displaced contributions once for each whole configuration. Disjoint feature lists or unchanged salaries do not establish those differences. Identify estimates still missing.

Before using a rate or priority score to compare whole financial results, recover its underlying quantity, denominator, interval and completion conditions through OPS.15. Determine delay loss from the receipts, payments or contributions changed by the delay. Charge a postponed receipt according to the effect of its later timing.

Compare the attainable addition with the current arrangement. Include the cost of obtaining the comparison separately from the cost of realizing each option examined. Information that opens a later choice is not yet operating revenue or delivered benefit.

#### STR.9:4.4 - Find holding regions and meaningful reversals

Test a decisive assumption when a further test would improve the answer enough to justify its cost. Use the least demanding adequate test. Vary demand, cost, availability, model assumptions or a justified value judgement where those changes can alter the result. Test joint changes when shared causes, thresholds or interactions matter; changing one input at a time can miss them.

Separate the uncertain input, such as future demand, from the response created by a particular service or resource arrangement. If a central-input estimate could hide a material nonlinear response, use PSDA.9 section 4.3.1's paired test or reuse a sufficient qualified analysis. Carry back the response values, tested domain, threshold result and limits on extrapolation. Equal weights describe the test. Event probabilities need a separate basis, and two arrangements can respond differently to the same demand.

State where the option is acceptable, where a rival is preferable under the criterion if one is compared, and where the evidence leaves the result open. Include a condition that defeats all present options when one is material. The least bad candidate is not necessarily admissible.

A new beneficiary, criterion or horizon may define a different comparison rather than a numerical perturbation of the old one. Preserve the earlier basis and make the change explicit. If a missing serious response could affect the comparison, use STR.5 or STR.6 to find or construct it.

#### STR.9:4.5 - Price the ability to decide later

For a staged or retained option, identify who could exercise it, any observation needed first, its expiry and the time required to respond. Determine what must remain available and what carrying it displaces.

Compare the retained flexibility with a serious immediate-commitment or continuation alternative. Include monitoring, coordination, switching and temporary exposure where they matter. An informative signal arriving after a contract becomes irreversible cannot preserve that decision.

When a failure condition suggests narrower exposure, specify what would actually limit the obligation or loss: changed admission terms, available support or a smaller complete service scope. If that arrangement is under consideration, compare it through section 4.3. Identify any claimed protection that still lacks support. A four-day preparation budget limits that preparation, not the service obligations of a later agreement.

Financial option valuation belongs to its qualified method and inputs. Compare reversibility and future choices in the terms the available evidence supports. A retained archive entry preserves reasoning; maintaining a working fallback can require an additional real contribution.

#### STR.9:4.6 - Return the comparison at its supported scope

State the supported comparison under the chosen basis, any justified preference over a serious rival, any reversal conditions and the limits of the conclusion. A retained set, qualified recommendation, infeasibility result or worthwhile next inquiry can be complete.

Use STR.7 when an attainable inquiry could improve the requested conclusion or decision enough to warrant its burden. Use STR.10 for unresolved commitment consequences and STR.11 when an authorized choice is required. Identify any actual resource commitment separately.

Reopen when a changed option, condition, source result, criterion, timing or carrying cost can alter this comparison. Follow an updated source only to the conclusions that rely on it.

### STR.9:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### STR.9:5.1 - SensorCo: preparation beats a viable rival only under stated conditions

All facts in this case are constructed. SensorCo can sustain its current device and service contracts for twelve months. The Board seeks a repeat paid contribution less exposed to generic-inspection price competition while protecting service and incident reserve.

Two prospective customers may discuss a bounded paid service trial next quarter at a price ceiling, subject to agreement and permitted use. Four funded internal days before the next Board consideration can complete costing and specify a possible trial. They could instead improve device diagnostics.

SensorCo compares spending the next four days on service preparation, which could inform the twelve-month choice, with spending them on device diagnostics.

For the exit comparison, assume that orderly exit and device continuation both satisfy the protected-service requirement under the three conditions below. The supplied comparison for this twelve-month decision includes the exposure avoided, net disposal proceeds, the best attainable use of released resources and any later consequence material to the choice. Under the Board's criterion, exit's supported advantages are outweighed by the continuing device contribution forgone and the full customer-transition burden.

| Condition at the relevant horizon | Comparison with device-only continuation and diagnostic work | Supported return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commoditization continues and an accountable-service opportunity remains attainable | Preparation can establish whether a relevant service arrangement is economically plausible; diagnostics improve the viable current direction but do not answer that question | Prefer the bounded preparation at the Board's accepted four-day sacrifice; keep the twelve-month service direction open |
| Purchasing freezes throughout the horizon, preventing a useful paid trial | The preparation no longer has the stated opportunity to inform this choice; device continuation supports existing obligations | Prefer device-only continuation and retain the four days for diagnostics |
| Data reuse is restricted | A permitted customer-local service might still be possible, while cross-customer licensing requires its own right | Continue preparation only if an attainable determination leaves a relevant permitted option; otherwise prefer device continuation |

The Board supplied this trade-off. Under restricted reuse, only a permitted service arrangement could keep preparation worthwhile. Licensing is not currently selectable without its required reuse right. Under the supplied whole-consequence comparison, continuation is preferable to exit. Reopen that comparison if an exit benefit, transition burden, continuing contribution or protected-service condition changes enough to reverse it; continuation need not become infeasible for exit to become preferable.

Costing can also settle the service question. If no relevant permitted configuration can cover delivery cost at attainable customer terms, the recommendation is device continuation without a trial. A bound covering all relevant configurations can rule them out despite an unknown cost. Seek that cost when its possible values leave the choice undecided.

Later capacity arithmetic answers another question. A proposed 78-person-day configuration fits the 80-day ceiling including protected service and reserve. Strategic merit and delivery capability require supporting evidence of their own. Within its six platform days, two preserve device-compatible interfaces and instructions, postponing a two-day service-diagnostic enhancement. That is the cost of the fallback through the first staged decision. It differs from the four-day device improvement forgone in the present preparation choice.

#### STR.9:5.2 - The criterion can change the answer even when the numbers do not

In a separate constructed public-service comparison, assume two configurations satisfy the supplied protected-access condition. A model stipulated for this use gives the waiting times below for two demand conditions. No probabilities are assigned.

| Configuration | Condition 1: waiting minutes | Condition 2: waiting minutes | Worst waiting time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 10 | 12 | 12 |
| B | 4 | 16 | 16 |

Minimizing the worst wait favours A, with a maximum of 12 minutes against B's 16. For maximum regret, compare each option with the best wait in each condition, 4 and 12 minutes respectively. A's regrets are 6 and 0 minutes. B's are 0 and 4. Minimizing maximum regret therefore favours B, with 4 minutes against A's 6.

Return these two conditional results if no public value rule has been selected. The responsible body determines which rule governs a choice. If B breaches the protected-access condition, apply that condition before comparing regret. The example uses stipulated waiting times.

#### STR.9:5.3 - SensorCo: two improvements can share one receipt

Consider a constructed later costing comparison, separate from the current four-day preparation and its authority. It concerns a device-health report offer, an alert offer and their combination for one prospective customer over the same twelve months.

Assume that either offer, or both together, would produce one incremental payment of 10,000 currency units at month 12 under the stated acceptance and payment conditions. The complete future additional payments within the horizon are 6,000 for reports, 5,000 for alerts and 11,000 for both. Existing receipts and payments remain unchanged, and the supplied account establishes no other material displaced contribution. The calculation assumes funding on the payment dates, resources and permitted service conditions.

| Compared future arrangement | Incremental receipt | Future additional payments | Incremental net cash over twelve months |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Continue without either new offer | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Reports only | 10,000 | 6,000 | 4,000 |
| Alerts only | 10,000 | 5,000 | 5,000 |
| Reports and alerts | 10,000 | 11,000 | −1,000 |

Adding the two standalone net returns would give 9,000 by counting the same possible receipt twice. Under these assumptions, alerts have the strongest net-cash result. This conditional cash result can inform a strategic comparison. Authorization of a commitment and revenue received require their own grounds.

Now change one premise: a qualified customer basis supports a further 8,000 payable only for the combined offer, with the other cash consequences unchanged. Its receipt becomes 18,000 and its net cash 7,000, reversing the financial ranking. If that extra payment is unsupported, keep it as an unresolved premise rather than adding it or silently setting it to zero. The extra receipt changes the value of combining the offers.

A supporting rate needs the same care. In a separate constructed account, two distinct results each pay 100 units on completion and take two and four hours sequentially on the same resource, with no other delay. Together they yield 200 over six hours, or 33⅓ units per hour, not the sum of their standalone rates, 75. By contrast, independent streams paying 100 and 50 in the same one-hour interval can jointly yield 150 in that interval. Recover the quantities, conditions and interval before aggregating.

These are undiscounted cash comparisons under supplied conditions. For a choice that depends on earlier funding, financing costs or later effects, establish those consequences over the relevant horizon. The current preparation can obtain missing costing or customer answers without claiming that this later comparison has already occurred.

#### STR.9:5.4 - SensorCo: a central workload can hide the service exposure

This constructed extension examines the proposed 78-engineer-day first-month configuration, separately from the earlier four-day preparation decision. Fix its service scope, team and thirty-day window. A stipulated operating model gives total engineer-day demand, including the protected eight-engineer-day incident reserve, when zero, one or two additional trial requests arrive. These three request conditions are the model's stated test domain; none has an assigned probability.

| Additional trial requests | Total required engineer-days, including reserve |
| --- | ---: |
| 0 | 78 |
| 1 | 79 |
| 2 | 84 |

The central response is 79 engineer-days. The two endpoint responses average 81, two engineer-days higher. These are test values under PSDA.9. An expected workload would require probabilities. With two additional requests, demand is 84 engineer-days against 80 available, four too many. The proposed scope therefore fails the capacity limit in that condition. All three calculations include the protected reserve.

One proposed response is to limit trial admissions to one and defer additional requests. That only narrows exposure if the actual customer terms permit deferral, the remaining service is a useful complete contribution, and the required admission and support arrangement is feasible. Those premises are not supplied here. The two-request condition fails. If an alternative admission arrangement is requested, return the three open conditions before relying on a one-request limit.

The Board can use permitted preparation to clarify that narrower arrangement and its full cost if their answers would materially improve the comparison or choice. Existing device continuation remains a serious alternative. The earlier preparation authorization neither establishes the response model nor funds or limits a future service promise. A sufficient current capacity and terms account would be reused without another test.

### STR.9:6 - Bias-Annotation


A favoured strategy can determine the scenario set, weights and baseline before the comparison begins. For a comparison with a serious rival, use the same horizon, categories of burden and treatment of evidence.

Flexibility can be overvalued by people who enjoy exploration and undervalued by those rewarded for early commitment. Attribute the choice criterion and include the real cost of keeping later action possible.

### STR.9:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The option is tested against the stated question and conditions. A comparison among alternatives uses the same configuration boundary, horizon and basis for each.
- Eligibility, missing support and consequence uncertainty remain distinct.
- The criterion or conditional criteria are explicit. Any probabilities and scalar trade-offs have a suitable basis.
- A finite change includes interactions, transition, displaced work and protected results.
- The comparison identifies where the tested conditions hold or fail and any supported reversals or common failures.
- When the comparison relies on a future choice, it identifies who can exercise it, when, and any cost of carrying or switching.
- A dominance claim is supported under its stated rule and is not treated as selection.
- The return states the supported comparison, any justified preference over a serious rival, and the conditions or limits that could change the conclusion.

### STR.9:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**The average winner.** Good mean performance hides failure of a required condition. Separate eligibility from the comparison among eligible options.

**Robust in three versions of the same future.** Repeated similar scenarios supply little challenge. Test the materially different mechanism that could reverse the choice.

**Free option value.** The fallback requires maintained interfaces and scarce support that the plan omits. Include them or state the option's expiry.

**A new rule disguised as new evidence.** Changed preferences reverse the ranking, but the report says the old observation was wrong. Preserve both comparison bases.

### STR.9:9 - Consequences

The recipient can use the conditions under which an option is acceptable, any supported preference or reversal, and the cost of keeping a later choice available. These results can guide a recommendation or a further inquiry when either is needed.

The result can remain set-valued or conditional. Its limits can follow from the model, evidence or an unsettled value rule. It can still support a bounded current commitment.

### STR.9:10 - Rationale

Robustness, sensitivity, dominance and selection answer different questions. Establish what the analysis supports before using it to choose under the applicable rule.

The current configuration matters because a strategic addition interacts with what already works and with what must continue. The same nominal initiative can be attractive in one configuration and infeasible in another. A future option likewise has value through attainable exercise, not through its name.

### STR.9:11 - SoTA-Echoing

**How should strategy compare commitments under deep uncertainty?** The robust-decision and adaptive-pathway methods discussed in [Lempert and colleagues' 2024 analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/climate/articles/10.3389/fclim.2024.1380054/full) use consequential information even at lower confidence to identify conditions of failure and feasible adaptations. Sections 4.2–4.5 apply this to strategic options. The serious rival is a qualified expected-value comparison, retained when its model and probabilities suffice. The source concerns climate assessment and methods, not evidence that one corporate strategy will succeed.

PSDA.9 supplies the operational tests of holding regions, reversals and adaptive limits, including the bounded paired-response check when a central input conceals nonlinear exposure. A compatible existing test can supply the result for the same comparison. C.11.CRC supplies the finite configuration-relative contribution when an isolated score or marginal-rate argument misses interactions and indivisibility. This changes section 4.3 and the separate treatment of SensorCo's preparation, capacity and fallback costs.

For an operating gain's financial contribution, OPS.14 follows accepted demand through the actual incremental receipts, payments, timing and displaced use; OPS.15 recovers the quantities and event meanings behind an account. These methods can expose a shared customer payment or unlike rates that distort the return assigned to an initiative. An adequate existing financial account remains the cheaper alternative. The constructed cash comparison demonstrates the distinction, not a general financial advantage of one service design.

Reopen the method choice when defensible probabilities or better model evidence change the appropriate comparison, when a serious omitted option appears or when the timing and cost of future exercise defeat the flexibility claim.
