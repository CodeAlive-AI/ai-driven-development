## Management.Reference:8 - Field basis and further reading

### Management.Reference:8.1 - Recognition of the field and its boundaries


Management is a recognized field of scholarship and practice with differentiated intellectual traditions. The Academy of Management's [divisions and interest groups](https://www.aom.org/divisions-and-interest-groups/) provide an institutional entry to that diversity. The Suite's particular composition is argued through its constituent subjects and working connections above.

The [Strategic Management domain statement](https://www.aom.org/divisions-and-interest-groups/strategic-management/) connects knowledge of organizational strategy with decision processes, resources and consequences. [Organization and Management Theory](https://www.aom.org/divisions-and-interest-groups/organization-and-management-theory/) studies organizations, organizing and their wider relations, joining theoretical inquiry with practical implications. [Operations and Supply Chain Management](https://www.aom.org/divisions-and-interest-groups/operations-and-supply-chain-management/) explicitly includes transformation processes in profit and nonprofit organizations and encourages connections across functions. These statements support recognition of the field and its breadth. They do not determine the membership of this Suite or validate a particular method.

The organization-change field has a constructive management basis in [Galbraith's organization-design practice](https://jaygalbraith.com/services/organization-design/) and an engineering connection explained by [SEBoK's Enterprise Systems Engineering account](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Enterprise_Systems_Engineering). The [AOM Organization Development and Change domain](https://www.aom.org/Divisions-and-Interest-Groups/Organization-Development-and-Change/) is broader than OCE's deliberate changes to organizational relations and capability. Those sources support related practices and their boundaries, not an equation of their complete subjects.

In §2, the [GFMAM Maintenance Framework, second edition](https://www.gfmam.org/sites/default/files/2021-02/GFMAM%20Maintenance%20Framework%20-%202nd%20Edition%20Final.pdf) distinguishes maintenance engineering from maintenance management; §§6.4–6.9 connect strategy, resources, work, performance, information and programme. This connection supports keeping the bounded maintenance field complete when Management uses it. It does not make maintenance identical to all Asset Management.

The full DPFs carry the disciplinary sources and limits needed for their own Methods. [FPF C.20](../../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/39-c-20---composition-of-u-discipline.md) explains discipline composition; [FPF E.4.PFAD](../../../sections/11-part-e---the-fpf-constitution-and-authoring-guides/08-e-4-pfad---principle-framework-architecture-decision.md) distinguishes that question from the choice of a framework or publication arrangement.

### Management.Reference:8.3 - What the comparison establishes

The synthesis favors comparing complete applicable arrangements over importing a fixed organization form. It preserves more than one scale of judgement: the quality and cost of a particular contribution, the supportable flow of work, the resulting commitment, and its value in use. New capabilities can change the work worth attempting; STR.17 reopens the strategic question when a local efficiency comparison no longer represents that change.

For example, when AI changes which tasks are attempted and several agents share reviewers or laboratory equipment, a time-per-task comparison is insufficient. A useful comparison must keep the original needs and declined work visible, distinguish comparable tasks from the changed undertaking, account for shared resources and retain checking and subsequent losses. Its design needs the relevant measurement and causal-inference expertise. An observed improvement, a locally useful arrangement and an established causal effect are different conclusions.

The older source is retained where its construction remains adequate; a newer source reopens the affected decision when it supplies a better alternative or defeats a premise. Conversely, a new date, a leading company's name or institutional endorsement is not a sufficient selection rule. Reconsider the arrangement when its result, task mix, technology, limiting resource, authority or consequential horizon changes. These comparisons justify specific questions and alternatives.
