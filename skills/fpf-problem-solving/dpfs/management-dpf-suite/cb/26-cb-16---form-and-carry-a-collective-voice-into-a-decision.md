## CB.16 - Form and Carry a Collective Voice into a Decision

> **Type:** Community-building method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### CB.16:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a community needs to contribute to a decision that affects its practice, but individual comments do not adequately carry the shared concern. A profession is invited only after consequential design choices have been made. A provider asks a network for its view on a proposed change. Several local groups want to make one request while preserving different interests.

The governed object is a collective contribution to a specified receiving decision: the position, whom it represents, the authority to carry it, and the returned response. This is one branch of collective action. CB.13 constructs the decision mandate when needed; this pattern develops and carries the substantive position within it.

**First useful result:** participants can identify the receiving decision, the useful contribution they seek, the positions and uncertainties that matter, and who may present which position on whose behalf.

**What changes in practice:** a spokesperson can offer a grounded, authorised contribution and bring back an actionable answer. Members can distinguish what they asked for, what was agreed and what remains unresolved.

An individual's sufficiently useful expert answer can remain an individual contribution through CB.5. A community's internal allocation belongs to its existing decision arrangement. Use this pattern when representation of a shared position and its return from another decision are material to the result.

### CB.16:2 - Problem

An organiser may speak confidently for a community without knowing whether its members support the position. A popular statement may omit the practitioners most affected. Conversely, waiting for complete agreement can prevent any useful contribution before the decision closes.

Even a properly authorised message can fail because it asks only for recognition, gives the recipient no feasible action or reaches someone who cannot decide. A meeting may then be reported as influence although the receiving decision and working conditions remain unchanged.

The task is to build a contribution that both the represented participants and the decision-maker can use, carry it under a valid mandate, and return its actual effect and remaining work.

### CB.16:3 - Forces

| Force | Tension |
| --- | --- |
| Shared voice | A joint position can make a common concern intelligible while concealing real differences. |
| Expertise and authority | Knowledge supports the content; representation depends on the group's decision arrangement. |
| Timing | A decision window can be short while affected people need a usable opportunity to contribute. |
| Feasibility | An ambitious request may matter but require contributions that the community cannot provide or promise. |
| Negotiation | A representative needs enough discretion to respond while preserving the limits members accepted. |
| Influence | A received or accepted proposal still needs effective decisions and later observation of the practical result. |

### CB.16:4 - Solution

Locate the decision that can change the practice, develop the useful contribution with the people concerned, obtain the mandate to carry it and engage the actual recipient. Return the response and its consequences to the represented participants before treating the contribution as complete.

#### CB.16:4.1 - Locate the receiving decision and the practical gain

Recover the proposed change or recurring exclusion from an actual source: a decision proposal, current arrangement, invitation or account of the work. Identify who can decide, what is still open, the constraints and the time by which a contribution can matter. Confirm uncertainty with the recipient when access permits.

Ask practitioners what difference they need. Describe the receiving task or result and how the current arrangement helps or defeats it. “Include the technical writer before the interface is fixed so that operating instructions can influence its design” gives a recipient more to assess than “recognise the importance of writers”.

Use STR.5 when the contribution itself is unclear. It connects a recipient's need with a worthwhile result and the capability and resources that could sustain provision. Compare a serious alternative, such as an earlier bounded review, a later specialist service or a different provider. Keep the conditions each proposal needs.

Separate the community's aspiration from the facts on which it relies. Cases may demonstrate a recurring missing input without proving the effect of a proposed new arrangement. Obtain a sufficient source answer or the needed inquiry through the applicable guidance in CB.14 and RMP where that stronger claim determines the choice.

If the decision is already closed, establish what can still change: implementation, correction, a later review or a new decision. Do not offer participants a choice that the recipient has no authority or opportunity to make.

#### CB.16:4.2 - Recover positions and affected differences

Define whose position is needed for this contribution. Use actual membership and constituency arrangements where they exist. Include people affected by the proposed action even when they do not attend ordinary meetings. CB.1 and OCE.2 help recover missing participants and different interests.

Provide enough of the proposal and its consequences for people to respond. Ask for the change they want, the case or reason behind it, what they can accept and what would make the proposal unworkable. Choose accessible routes and timing proportionate to the decision; one open meeting may not reach dispersed or less confident contributors.

Separate agreement, disagreement and absence of a response. An unanswered invitation supplies no assent. A well-attended discussion supplies useful positions, but its participants do not automatically represent every practitioner in the field.

Investigate differences that could change the request. Some may concern the same aim under different conditions; others involve competing priorities or a cost one group is asked to bear. Preserve the consequential difference in the proposal or in a clearly attributed dissent.

Agree what may be quoted, summarised or disclosed. A confidential case can inform the question without being identifiable in the public position. Obtain permitted evidence sufficient for the claim, or narrow it to what the participants can support.

#### CB.16:4.3 - Develop a position the recipient can act on

Construct the contribution around the actual decision. Explain the current difficulty, the proposed change, why it helps the receiving work, the evidence and limits, and the support needed to make it feasible. State what response is requested: a decision, a change to a proposal, a trial, information or a further conversation.

Compare materially different options when the choice remains open. Include the useful part of the current arrangement and the loss each change introduces. A narrower feasible request may obtain a needed contribution sooner; a broader request may be necessary if the narrow one leaves the real problem intact. Explain the choice to the represented participants.

Make the resource implications visible. State what the community can offer within its actual commitments and what must come from the recipient or another owner. A group of practitioners can propose protected review time; it cannot create that allocation by promising enthusiasm.

Prepare a joint position that can carry meaningful disagreement. Depending on the group's rules, it may be a supported majority proposal with a minority account, a common minimum with stated unresolved questions, or several attributed alternatives. Use a joint position only at the scope the participants have authorised.

If the contribution is advice, say what it can establish and which decision remains with its receiver. A request to be consulted may be justified by a useful professional contribution; it does not by itself establish an exclusive right to represent the domain.

#### CB.16:4.4 - Obtain and bound the representation mandate

Use the existing decision arrangement or obtain the needed one through CB.13. Establish who may approve the position, which participants it represents, the authorised spokesperson, the subject and duration, and how members can correct or withdraw the mandate.

Give the representative workable discretion. State what they may clarify or negotiate, the conditions they must preserve and which changes must return for a further decision. A mandate that only says “speak for us” leaves both the speaker and members exposed to incompatible expectations.

Distinguish approval of the position from approval of commitments. The community may authorise a request while each employer, site or individual retains the right to provide its own time or resources. Obtain those decisions when the negotiated action depends on them.

Prepare the spokesperson to use the mandate. They need the reasons, evidence limits, different positions, likely questions and a way to return an unexpected proposal. LDR.1 supplies the working brief and explanation. Where the task needs more than one kind of knowledge, obtain a supporting contributor.

When a collective mandate cannot be obtained within the decision window, choose a truthful narrower contribution. Named willing contributors may offer their own joint view under their own authority. State whom it represents; a community name or large mailing list does not enlarge that scope.

#### CB.16:4.5 - Engage the recipient and handle a changed proposal

Confirm that the recipient can receive the contribution in time and through a usable route. Present the request with its practical reason, represented constituency and mandate. Provide the evidence needed for the decision at a level the recipient can inspect.

If the first contact cannot decide, ask for the appropriate receiving route. Where no accessible route or timely decision remains, return that limit to the participants so they can choose a later opportunity or end this episode.

Use the exchange to test understanding. Ask the recipient to identify the question they can decide and any missing condition. Recover objections from the work: an unavailable resource, another affected interest, an unsupported claim or a different decision boundary may each require a different response.

Work within the authorised discretion. A representative can clarify the case or compare an allowed option. A materially different commitment or claim returns to the people who can approve it. State the pending condition to the recipient so that a provisional discussion is not mistaken for an agreement.

Keep disagreement intelligible. The recipient may decline the request for a reason members contest. Return that reason and the remaining possible action; do not replace it with an unsupported account of the recipient's motives.

If the recipient offers a trial or accepts a change, identify the actual decision, owner, scope and next contribution. Obtain the assignments, access and resources through their owners. An invitation to discuss, a favourable comment and an effective decision remain different results.

#### CB.16:4.6 - Return the answer and make the next action usable

Report the contribution and response to the represented participants. Explain what the recipient accepted, rejected, changed or left open; the reason and evidence supplied; any commitment made within the mandate; and any new question requiring the group's decision.

Give dissenting or previously absent participants a usable opportunity to correct a misrepresentation. Correct a materially inaccurate account with the recipient as well as within the community. CB.13 supplies reconsideration or replacement when the representative exceeded the mandate.

Return implementation work to its actual owners. Use OCE.14 for a bounded revision of an existing arrangement and OCE.6 for needed assignments and enabling conditions. CB.14 supplies a new practice contribution; HCD supplies a needed learning result. If the community has only obtained an invitation to further work, preserve that bounded gain and the unfinished result.

Agree an observation that can tell whether the expected practical change occurred. For example, check whether writers were actually included before the specified decision and whether their contribution was usable. CB.10 can connect those observations with the community's value question. A causal claim about the whole intervention still requires suitable evidence.

Close the representation episode when the answer, effective commitments and remaining work are understood by their recipients. A refusal or a bounded agreement may complete this episode even though the community's wider aim remains open.

#### CB.16:4.7 - Reconsider the position and its continuing mandate

Reopen the affected position when the external proposal changes, new evidence defeats a reason, a previously missed constituency matters, the required provision is absent or the mandate expires. Recover which part of the earlier contribution remains useful.

For continuing representation, agree how the community will keep the representative informed, obtain returns and replace them. The burden may justify a standing arrangement through CB.13; occasional external requests may need only bounded assignments.

Preserve independence among the represented groups. They may support one shared request while retaining different practices and other positions. A cross-community response through CB.15 needs each side's actual mandate at the scope attributed to it.

Independent carriers of a practice can use a published argument or contribute an attributed response without joining a common organisation. Their use or silence supplies no authority for a spokesperson to claim that they endorse the collective position.

### CB.16:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### CB.16:5.1 - Technical writers ask to enter a design decision earlier

In a constructed engineering firm, a writers' community finds three cases where documentation began after the operating interface had been fixed. The writers had to describe confusing actions that an earlier review might have changed. They recover the cases with the relevant project staff and obtain permission to use the examples internally.

The community compares two proposals. A stronger final documentation service preserves the current development schedule but leaves interface changes late. A bounded writer review before the interface decision could expose explanation problems earlier; it requires time from both a writer and the design team.

Members support trying the early review on two projects. Some prefer a permanent writer on every team, while others cannot provide additional time under current allocations. The position retains those differences and requests a limited trial with protected time, a defined review question and a return after the two project decisions.

Under its member decision arrangement, the community authorises a spokesperson to request that trial. The mandate permits clarification of timing and review material; it does not permit promising staff from every department. The spokesperson brings the two alternatives and the cases to the engineering manager who owns the relevant process decision.

The manager proposes involving all writers in a weekly meeting. That exceeds the offered contribution and lacks time provision. The spokesperson returns the proposal for discussion. The members retain the bounded review, and the manager instead approves two project-specific sessions. The participating managers allocate the agreed time and the two writers accept the work.

The community receives the decision, conditions and named project returns. In the first session, the writer's explanation of an operating sequence prompts the team to clarify an interface label before its decision. Before the second session, its assigned writer becomes unavailable. The project reaches the interface decision before a replacement is obtained and reports that the early review did not occur. The community returns that provision failure to the participating manager for the next proposed use, retaining the useful first contribution. The two returns do not establish the programme's overall effect.

#### CB.16:5.2 - A shared response can preserve a minority concern

A provider proposes moving a network's support sessions to a new time. Most participating groups prefer the new slot; a smaller group would lose its only accessible session. The network gathers each group's reason and confirms which service decisions the provider can make.

Its authorised position supports the new slot only if a usable alternative remains for the affected group. The spokesperson is permitted to compare equivalent alternatives but must return any proposal that removes access. The provider offers a recorded presentation.

The smaller group explains that it needs live questions about its cases, so a recording does not supply the missing contribution. The spokesperson returns that answer within the mandate. The provider agrees to test a second live session if an appropriate host can be assigned. The network reports the conditional result and the unfilled host requirement; access is not described as secured until that condition is met.

#### CB.16:5.3 - Named contributors speak without claiming the whole field

A platform invites comments from repair practitioners on a proposed information form. Several members of a loose network have useful experience, but the network has no authorised common position and many independent repairers are outside it.

The willing contributors submit a joint technical account in their own names, stating the settings their examples cover and the limits of their agreement. They make the permitted account available for others to use or challenge. The response is a real contribution; it is not presented as the view of all repairers.

A later proposal to maintain an ongoing representative relationship would require a mandate and provision through CB.13.

#### CB.16:5.4 - A request already within the organiser's assignment

A club needs its existing room booking corrected. The authorised organiser contacts the venue, obtains the correction and informs participants. The task is complete under the existing assignment.

### CB.16:6 - Bias-Annotation

A spokesperson may overrepresent confident contributors, people available at the consultation time or the position most convenient to a sponsor. Invitations from a prestigious recipient can also encourage overclaiming the community's mandate. Recover affected differences, show the scope of support and preserve useful dissent. Keep represented people able to challenge the speaker's account and the claims open to contrary evidence.

### CB.16:7 - Conformance Checklist

For reliance on a collective contribution, establish:

- The receiving decision, authorised recipient and opportunity to affect it are known or their uncertainty is explicit.
- The practical gain and proposed action are supported at the claimed scope, with a serious alternative where it changes the choice.
- Affected positions, meaningful dissent and missing responses remain distinguishable.
- The position is approved and the spokesperson has a valid mandate with usable discretion and return conditions.
- Promised resources and commitments have their actual owners' decisions.
- The response is returned accurately, with the effective result and unfinished work.
- Misrepresentation, new conditions and expired authority can reopen the affected contribution.

A recurring exclusion or an external invitation is enough to recognise the opportunity. It does not supply the authority or evidence required for reliance.

### CB.16:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Failure in practice | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Ask for recognition without a contribution the recipient can assess. | Explain the receiving work, proposed change, benefit, cost and needed support. |
| Treat the most visible members as the whole constituency. | Recover affected positions and state the actual representation scope. |
| Wait for unanimity while hiding the alternatives. | Use the applicable decision rule and carry meaningful disagreement explicitly. |
| Give a speaker an unlimited mandate because they know the subject. | Separate expertise from representation and bound negotiable commitments. |
| Promise other people's time in order to obtain approval. | Request and obtain the actual allocation before relying on the promise. |
| Count access to a meeting as a changed practice. | Return the specific decision and observe whether its conditions and contribution become effective. |
| Report only the favourable response to members. | Include refusals, changed terms, unsupported conditions and needed decisions. |
| Extend one episode's authority into permanent representation. | Obtain a current continuing mandate with returns and replacement. |

### CB.16:9 - Consequences

The community can make a contribution that a decision-maker can act on while keeping its members' positions and authority visible. A representative can negotiate useful details and return a changed question without inventing consent.

The work takes time to recover positions, prepare a feasible request and obtain the return. It may expose differences that prevent a single position. A narrower attributed contribution or an explicit refusal can be more useful than apparent unanimity.

### CB.16:10 - Rationale

A collective voice gains practical value through the work it helps change and the constituency it can legitimately represent. Evidence, mandate and receiving authority answer different questions. Keeping them distinct lets a technically strong proposal remain advice when authority is absent, or an authorised position remain uncertain where evidence is weak.

The return completes the connection. Without it, participants cannot distinguish influence from visibility, correct a misrepresentation or decide whether the effort remains worthwhile. Bounded discretion lets a representative act between meetings while members retain the ability to revise or withdraw the mandate.

### CB.16:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The practice question is how a community can make its expertise and shared interests count in another party's decision. The selected line joins an actionable contribution, a position that preserves differences, a valid mandate and a returned answer. Personal expert comment remains sufficient when no collective representation is needed.
