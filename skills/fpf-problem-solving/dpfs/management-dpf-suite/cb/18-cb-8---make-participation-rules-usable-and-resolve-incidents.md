## CB.8 - Make Participation Rules Usable and Resolve Incidents

> **Type:** Community-building method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### CB.8:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when participants cannot tell what conduct is expected, when different organisers apply a rule differently, or when an incident requires a response that people can understand and challenge. A useful disagreement is being treated as disloyalty. Advertising crowds out requested help. A participant can no longer access the only channel through which they could appeal their exclusion.

The governed work is making rules of participation usable and handling an incident under a valid decision arrangement. It includes clarification, proportionate protection, fact finding, response, appeal and return. Establishing who may make shared rules or review the decision-makers is the separate CB.13 question.

**First useful result:** the people concerned know the applicable expectation, the action being taken and its reason, and a reachable way to obtain correction or review. Where an incident is open, someone capable and authorised owns its next action.

An organiser needs the relevant activity and its conditions, a person able to receive concerns, and the actual scope of the available decision authority. A simple clarification can start with one unclear expectation. A consequential restriction needs grounds and authority proportionate to that action.

**Ordinary non-use:** a civil disagreement about the practice can continue through subject reasoning with CB.4 or CB.5 without becoming a conduct case. Existing understood rules may already be sufficient. Use the relevant qualified service for a danger or allegation beyond the community's handling capability.

### CB.8:2 - Problem

A rule can be visible but hard to apply. "Be respectful" may leave participants unable to distinguish sharp criticism, repeated personal attack and refusal to provide private information. A long code may still omit who can decide or what happens when the moderator is involved.

Incident handling fails in several ways. An organiser can rush to judgement, demand an unsafe confrontation, repeatedly tolerate an action that prevents participation, or hide a correctable mistake behind the rule's authority. A public explanation can itself disclose the information that should have been protected.

The task is to preserve useful participation while resolving the specific conduct question with an intelligible basis, an effective response and available correction.

### CB.8:3 - Forces

| Force | Working consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate protection and a considered judgement have different timing. | A temporary measure can be needed before the facts supporting a final restriction are settled. |
| Common expectations meet different circumstances. | Language, accessibility, role and prior conduct can change what a fair response requires. |
| Explanation and privacy interact. | People need reasons without unnecessary exposure of reports or personal details. |
| Familiarity helps and can bias judgement. | A moderator's relationship to the people or dispute can require another decision-maker. |
| A rule needs real handling capacity. | An appeal promise fails when the same unavailable or conflicted person receives every route. |
| Criticism can be uncomfortable and useful. | Distinguish the subject claim from conduct that prevents others participating. |

### CB.8:4 - Solution

Connect each needed rule to the activity it supports and an action people can perform. Make the ordinary route usable before an incident, then handle a particular incident through its actual facts, available protection and applicable decision arrangement.

#### CB.8:4.1 - Explain the expectations needed for the activity

Begin with the useful participation and a concrete way it can fail. In a case exchange, participants need to challenge the proposed explanation and protect confidential case details. In a shared feed, repeated promotional posts may make requests hard to find. At a workshop, access or equipment rules may be necessary to carry out the activity.

State what a participant should do, the relevant boundary and why it matters. Include a short example where the distinction is easy to misread. For instance: disclose an affiliation when recommending one's own tool, explain its relevance to the question, and use the designated offer space for unrelated promotion. A blanket restriction may still be appropriate for a particular activity; choose it for that activity's conditions.

Separate subject standards from conduct rules. A technical claim can be wrong and need correction; a person can challenge it strongly while following the participation rules. Conversely, a correct claim does not justify repeated personal intimidation. Domain judgement and incident handling may both be needed, with different recipients.

Make expectations available where people enter and act. Explain the few consequential conditions before an event, link the relevant guidance at a posting point and provide an accessible way to ask. A participant should be able to understand an expectation before making a conspicuous contribution. Explain real limits on privacy instead of promising that anything said in a group will remain confidential.

Retain adequate existing rules. Revise an unclear or ineffective rule through its actual decision arrangement, with affected participants able to explain the practical consequences. State when the revision takes effect. Clarifying future conduct and judging past conduct are different decisions; do not silently treat a newly invented expectation as an earlier agreement.

#### CB.8:4.2 - Provide a workable receiving and decision route

Identify who receives a concern, what that person can do immediately and who may make a consequential decision. State how someone can report a problem privately and how urgent problems are routed while the usual receiver is absent. Give a realistic response window.

Test access from the position of a person who has been excluded, has left the venue or does not use the main platform. A route outside the restricted space can be essential. State who sees a report and the practical limits of confidentiality before inviting sensitive details.

Choose people able to understand the context and make the relevant judgement. Provide preparation, time, backup and permission to step away through CB.7. A person receiving hostility should be able to transfer the case.

Provide a way to disclose and handle a consequential conflict of interest. The challenged moderator, their close collaborator or an opposing party in the dispute may have useful evidence while being unsuitable to decide the case. Identify a replacement or an agreed external route under CB.13.

Keep the process small enough to operate. A small group may need two named contacts and an agreed alternate. A large or high-consequence setting may require trained staff and a more formal arrangement. If no adequate route exists for the claimed level of protection or review, change that promise and obtain the missing provision before relying on it.

#### CB.8:4.3 - Establish what happened and what needs action now

Receive the concern without deciding its truth from the reporter's status or tone. Recover the relevant action, context, time, affected participation and requested help. Use the available evidence when it suffices for the next decision; otherwise obtain the missing evidence and context, such as a complete exchange rather than one isolated sentence, an account from the people present, or a relevant observation.

Separate what was observed, what someone reports, their interpretation and what remains disputed. Retain enough context for later review. Restrict access to sensitive material and avoid reproducing it in a public discussion of the case.

Ask what must be protected while the issue is examined. Where the available authority permits it, a temporary pause, removal of exposed private information, separation of an interaction or alternate contact can preserve participation. State the scope, reason, responsible person and next review point. A temporary protection measure does not by itself establish responsibility for the alleged conduct.

Choose the next contribution from the question that needs to be settled:

| What remains to be settled | Appropriate next contribution |
| --- | --- |
| People understood different meanings or lacked relevant information. | Clarify the expectation and context, then check whether the difficulty remains. |
| People disagree about the practice or a proposed result. | Obtain the subject discussion or qualified judgement through CB.4 or CB.5 as the contribution requires. |
| An observed action may breach an applicable participation rule. | Compare the action and context with that rule, hear the relevant response and decide within the available authority. |
| People dispute the rule, decision-maker or mandate itself. | Preserve any currently justified protection and return the governance question to CB.13. |
| The matter exceeds the community's capability or authority. | Obtain the appropriate service or institutional response and limit the community's own promise. |

Several questions can coexist. A useful criticism can contain an impermissible disclosure; preserve the criticism's subject while acting on the disclosure.

#### CB.8:4.4 - Select and carry out a proportionate response

Give the person whose conduct is in question enough of the concern and relevant basis to respond, subject to the protection needed for others. Ask for missing context and correct factual mistakes. Where an immediate action had to precede this hearing, provide the opportunity as soon as the conditions permit.

Choose the response from the actual difficulty, seriousness, recurrence and foreseeable effect. Useful responses include clarification, a request to change or repair the action, a warning with an understandable next expectation, a bounded restriction or ending access to the activity. Follow the applicable decision rule. A serious incident may justify a stronger permitted response immediately; a fixed ladder of warnings cannot decide every case.

Consider whether a support or design defect contributed. An inaccessible posting rule, an unclear boundary between critique and personal comment, or an overloaded channel may need a repair alongside any individual response. A communication difficulty alone is not evidence of hostile intent.

Explain the decision to the people concerned. State the conduct or uncertainty on which it rests, the rule and scope, what changes now, how long a restriction lasts or what condition ends it, and how review can be requested. Give practical instructions for continuing permitted participation where that remains possible.

Use direct conversation when it can help and the people concerned can take part freely. Do not make a person confront the subject of their complaint as the price of receiving a response. A written exchange, an intermediary or separate conversations may be more workable. Choose the form that preserves the needed account and usable participation.

Make the selected response effective. Confirm the actual change to access, event arrangements or material, including the owner needed to perform it. For an in-person event, that may be the venue's responsible host; on a platform, it may be an administrator whose scope differs from the community moderator's. An announcement alone does not alter the operating condition.

#### CB.8:4.5 - Make appeal and correction possible

Offer a review route proportionate to the decision's consequence. Explain the grounds that can change it, such as relevant new facts, mistaken application of a rule, an excessive response or a conflict in the original judgement. Provide the person with enough reason to formulate the request and a route they can still reach.

Have a suitably authorised person who can reconsider the case examine the challenge. For a consequential disputed judgement, obtain someone other than the challenged decision-maker. The original moderator can supply their reasoning and evidence while the reviewer makes the reconsideration. CB.13 establishes who can fulfil that function if the arrangement is missing or contested.

Compare the decision with the relevant rule, facts and protected interests. The outcome may retain, change or reverse it, or return a missing inquiry. Explain the result and any further route that actually exists. Repeated identical submissions need not reopen settled facts indefinitely; a material new fact or a newly established conflict can reopen the affected question.

Correct what the mistake changed. Restore appropriate access, remove an unsupported public implication, revise an incorrect record and inform the people who relied on it. Restoring a post does not require redisclosing private material; provide a corrected permitted version when necessary.

Where return after a justified restriction is offered, agree the next expected conduct and support from the participation problem that led to it. Obtain the authorised access or activity change and inspect the next participation against that expectation. Retain the original reason where it still holds; a useful return need not reverse the earlier judgement. If access ends, state the scope and settle remaining practical matters through the applicable arrangements.

#### CB.8:4.6 - Learn from cases without making the case public

Keep a proportionate account sufficient to explain consequential decisions, support review and recognise recurrence. Use the normal permitted record and its access and retention conditions. An informal clarification may need no separate incident file; a consequential restriction needs a recoverable basis.

Inspect whether the response restored the wanted participation and whether it displaced the burden. A quieter feed may reflect restored usefulness, people leaving, or inability to report. Ask affected people through a workable route; absence of public complaints is not enough to distinguish those outcomes.

When cases reveal an unclear rule, incompatible decisions or missing support, repair that condition through its owner. Compare similar relevant cases while preserving their consequential differences. Prepare moderators on the revised expectation and confirm that participants can discover and use it.

Explain a community-wide change at the level people need: the working difficulty, the changed expectation, when it applies and how to ask or challenge. Protect case details that are unnecessary for that explanation.

Close the incident when the selected actions and any review are complete, the people concerned have the usable result, and outstanding work has a recipient. Reopen for material new evidence, failed protection, an inaccessible review route or recurrence that defeats the earlier remedy.

### CB.8:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### CB.8:5.1 - Correct a mistaken removal without losing the rule

In a constructed online repairer group, a rule permits a relevant answer about one's own tool when the affiliation is disclosed; unrelated offers belong in a separate space. A moderator removes a member's linked answer as self-promotion and temporarily restricts posting from the member's account. The member can still reach the independent appeal contact by email.

The appeal includes the original question and the complete answer. The member disclosed their connection, explained the tool's relevant limitation and answered the requested comparison. The moderator who removed it also sells a competing tool and supplies their account without judging the appeal.

The alternate reviewer finds that the removal misapplied the rule. The answer and the member's posting access are restored, the moderator's public implication of an undisclosed interest is corrected, and the member receives the reason. The group keeps the rule that separates relevant disclosure from unrelated promotion.

A later post from the same member advertises a discount without answering a current question. Its different content supports moving it to the offer space.

#### CB.8:5.2 - Receive an offline complaint after the event

At a volunteer workshop, the host repeatedly interrupts one participant's account of a scheduling problem. The participant leaves and later reports through the alternate contact printed on the invitation. They do not have to attend another meeting chaired by the same host to be heard.

The alternate obtains accounts from the participant, host and a willing witness. The scheduling concern is real and returns to the organiser responsible for access. The interruption also prevented the participant from making that concern intelligible. Under the group's existing decision arrangement, the alternate obtains an authorised organiser's assignment of another facilitator for the next discussion. The host receives concrete feedback on receiving concerns.

The participant can use the new route without a private reconciliation with the host. The contact explains what will change and checks whether that route is usable. If the authorised organiser cannot obtain a replacement, the contact explains the remaining gap and obtains a different workable arrangement before promising the change.

#### CB.8:5.3 - Protect first, then settle a disputed judgement

A member posts a screenshot containing another person's private contact details. Under a previously established moderation rule, the on-duty moderator can hide that material immediately and preserve a restricted copy for review.

The screenshot is hidden and the affected person receives an explanation and a contact. The moderator then hears the poster's account. It is still disputed whether the exposure was deliberate. The immediate protection is justified by the exposed information; a further sanction is decided from the applicable rule and evidence, without treating the unresolved intention as a proven fact.

If the moderator accidentally hid an accompanying technical correction as well, they restore a permitted version of that correction. The useful subject contribution and the privacy problem receive distinct outcomes.

#### CB.8:5.4 - A disagreement needs a subject answer

Two members disagree about which maintenance interval applies to a particular device. Both state their reasons and leave room for a response. The organiser asks for the relevant configuration and source, then obtains a qualified comparison through CB.5. There is no conduct restriction to impose merely because the discussion is uncomfortable or ends without agreement.

#### CB.8:5.5 - Offer return while retaining a justified restriction's reason

In another constructed repairer group, a member repeatedly inserts advertisements into requests for technical help. A moderator explains the rule and shows the separate offer space. The member repeats the advertising, displacing the requested comparisons, and posting is suspended under the group's stated rule. The decision gives its reason and an email contact for review.

The member later asks to return. An authorised alternate confirms the original basis and offers a supported return within the existing moderation mandate. The member wants to answer questions about work they also sell. Together they examine a proposed answer: it needs to address the question, disclose that interest and keep unrelated offers in the offer space. A willing moderator can check the next two technical answers within an agreed response time. They agree to resume ordinary posting once the member has prepared those two answers to meet the stated expectations.

The administrator enables submission for approval. In the first answer, the member explains which repair their service cannot perform and supplies a relevant alternative; the moderator approves it. For the second answer, the member removes an unrelated discount before submission; the moderator approves the technical comparison. The questions receive usable answers. After these two exchanges, the moderator confirms the agreed condition and the administrator restores ordinary posting. The earlier advertising and its justified restriction remain in the proportionate account of the case.

The two approved answers meet the stated return conditions. Further unrelated advertising would reopen the response from the new conduct and its effect. If the promised moderation support becomes unavailable, the reviewer and member need another workable arrangement before relying on it.

### CB.8:6 - Bias-Annotation

Public posts and articulate complaints are easier to inspect than private exclusion or the experience of someone who has already left. Moderators can also identify with familiar contributors or mistake an unfamiliar communication style for misconduct. Recover context and affected participation, make alternative reporting possible and separate consequential conduct from agreement with the organiser. The amount of public approval is evidence about expressed reactions, not a complete measure of fair or effective handling.

### CB.8:7 - Conformance Checklist

For the present rule or case, ask:

- Can a participant understand what to do and obtain the relevant guidance at the point of participation?
- Does the concern have an accessible receiver with the capability and authority for the next action?
- Are observed conduct, reported experience, interpretation and unresolved facts kept distinct?
- Is any temporary protection limited and subject to a real next review?
- Does the selected response address the actual difficulty, and has it taken effect?
- Can the affected person obtain an intelligible reason and a reachable, suitably independent review?
- Has an error been corrected in the access, material or understanding it changed?

Recognising a possible participation difficulty can be quick. The evidence, hearing and review needed for a consequential restriction depend on its actual consequences and governing arrangement.

### CB.8:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Treat disagreement with an organiser as a conduct breach. | Preserve the subject question and identify any separate action that actually violates a rule. |
| Add rules after every discomfort without checking their use. | Recover the practical failure and test whether clarification or a direct repair is sufficient. |
| Apply a new rule as though everyone had already accepted it. | Explain its basis and effective time; judge earlier conduct under the applicable conditions. |
| Publish the complaint in order to demonstrate transparency. | Explain the decision while restricting unnecessary case details. |
| Require the reporter to negotiate directly with the person complained about. | Offer a feasible receiving and handling route with the needed protection. |
| Place the appeal inside the space from which the person is excluded. | Supply an accessible route outside that restriction. |
| Let a conflicted moderator decide the challenge to their own action. | Obtain the authorised alternate and retain the original moderator's account for consideration. |
| Call an announced restriction or correction complete. | Confirm the actual change and return it to the people who rely on it. |

### CB.8:9 - Consequences

Rules become instructions people can use, and an incident can end with a specific correction or supported restriction. A mistaken decision can be repaired without abandoning a useful expectation. Useful disagreement can continue while harmful conduct receives an effective response.

Handling and review require time, judgement and protection of sensitive information. A community that cannot supply them must narrow its promise or obtain the missing service. More elaborate procedure is worthwhile only where it improves the relevant decision and participation.

### CB.8:10 - Rationale

An expectation helps when a participant can recognise its application and act on it. Enforcement adds interpretation and consequences, so the decision-maker's actual authority, the evidence and the possibility of correction matter.

Temporary protection and final judgement answer different questions. Separating them permits timely action without turning uncertainty into guilt. Separating rule quality, actual handling and public reaction makes subsequent improvement more informative than adding another rule or counting approval.

### CB.8:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The practice question is how to maintain workable participation and correct mistakes in incident handling. The useful line connects clear expectations with accessible reporting, contextual judgement, proportionate response and effective review. It accepts the cost of hearing and correction where a mistaken response could materially exclude people or damage the activity.

The alternative of informal clarification remains sufficient for an ordinary misunderstanding with limited consequences. A standing incident process becomes useful when consequences, recurrence or multiple decision-makers make that informal response insufficient.

Reopen the method's local use when reporting becomes inaccessible, an appeal lacks an independent decision-maker, a rule defeats useful participation or later evidence contradicts a consequential decision.

### CB.8:12 - Relations

- **CB.3 and CB.4** make entry and shared activity usable; **CB.5** supplies subject contributions that a conduct process cannot judge merely by agreement or tone.
- **CB.7** prepares and supports organisers. **CB.13** establishes the authority to make rules, assign decisions and review challenges to decision-makers.
- [LDR.1:4.4](../ldr/08-ldr-1---obtain-and-continue-the-leadership-contribution-need.md#ldr144---perform-the-smallest-sufficient-working-conversation) supplies a task-focused conversation when that is the needed contribution.
- [OCE.14:4](../oce/36-oce-14---decide-whether-and-how-to-revise-the-organization-f.md#oce144---solution) supplies a bounded revision of an established organisation; its owner and mandate must already be available.
- [ECO.7:4](../../eco/17-eco-7---coordinate-use-and-renewal-of-a-shared-resource.md#eco74---solution) supplies combinations of use, upkeep, observation and dispute rules for an actually constrained common resource.
