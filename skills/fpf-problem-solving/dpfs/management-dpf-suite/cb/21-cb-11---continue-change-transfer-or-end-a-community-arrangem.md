## CB.11 - Continue, Change, Transfer or End a Community Arrangement

> **Type:** Community-building method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### CB.11:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when the purpose, people or support of a cultural or community arrangement changes enough that its continuation needs a decision. A founder is leaving, a sponsor withdraws a platform, participants need different forms of participation, or the useful work has largely been completed.

The subject is the arrangement that people can actually use: its activities, relationships, organising work, resources and commitments. The move is to choose a feasible continuation or ending, carry the useful contributions into the chosen arrangement, and settle what will no longer be provided. The wider cultural practice can continue among independent carriers even when an organised activity ends.

The first useful result is an agreed continuation or ending, with the provision and remaining actions needed to carry it out. Where implementation is part of the work, the result includes receiving use under the new arrangement and settlement of the old commitments. An announcement or copied archive alone establishes neither.

The intended user is an organiser, a person taking over a contribution, or someone who can obtain the relevant decisions from participants and resource owners. They need access to the commitments and means of work that the change affects.

**Ordinary non-use.** A continuing arrangement that remains useful and adequately provided needs no reinvention because a lifecycle diagram labels it mature. A routine rota change within effective existing terms can use those terms. Use this pattern when the change affects what people can obtain, who can provide it or which commitments remain valid.

### CB.11:2 - Problem

People often identify a community with its current host, founder or meeting format. That makes loss of one component look like the end of everything, or lets a claim that “the relationships will continue” hide the loss of access, support and practical opportunity.

Continuing can also be the wrong response. The original task may be complete, organisers may be depleted, or a single arrangement may no longer serve different groups. Holding everyone together can consume resources and prevent useful work elsewhere. Conversely, ending too quickly can lose an explanation, a capable contributor or an opportunity that people still need.

How can participants choose and carry out a continuation or ending that preserves worthwhile possibilities without carrying unsupported promises into the future?

### CB.11:3 - Forces

| Force | Decision it changes |
| --- | --- |
| Continuity and changed purpose | A familiar arrangement can retain value while a different form better serves the next work. |
| Useful relationships and controlled infrastructure | People may wish to stay connected but still need access, contact routes and resources. |
| Succession and voluntary limits | A departing organiser can help a transition without owing indefinite support. |
| Common provision and local independence | Separation may serve different purposes while leaving a shared resource to coordinate. |
| Preservation and maintenance burden | Saving everything can retain unusable or sensitive material and create work nobody accepted. |
| Timely closure and unfinished obligations | A firm ending can release people while current users still need a receiving answer. |

### CB.11:4 - Solution

Recover what people still need and what has changed, compare feasible arrangements, obtain the decisions and provision, then perform the transfer or ending at the level of actual use.

**Working reminder:** locate the change; recover what remains worthwhile; choose and provide the next arrangement; test receiving use; settle the old promise.

#### CB.11:4.1 - Identify what is changing and who can decide it

Name the concrete trigger and its timing. A sponsor may withdraw funding at the end of a period; a host may stop volunteering next month; members may want different activities. Separate what is already decided from a proposal, concern or unexplained drop in participation.

Recover the affected arrangement. Which work will stop or change? Who relies on it? Which resource, access or authority will disappear? Ask both the people doing visible activity and those obtaining quieter benefits, including independent users of maintained material.

Distinguish control over components. A platform owner can decide what it provides; an organiser can end their own contribution; an authorised community body can decide matters within its mandate. Independent participants still decide what they are willing and able to do together. Use CB.13 when the collective decision arrangement itself is missing or disputed.

Respond to an immediate harmful or unsupported condition through the applicable owner, using CB.8 or CB.9 according to the problem. A necessary short-term suspension can protect participants while the larger choice remains open. State what is suspended and where people can obtain the remaining response.

#### CB.11:4.2 - Recover the useful work and the means of obtaining it

Use CB.10's current account or a bounded inquiry to identify what is worth continuing, what has failed and what is no longer needed. Recover the received benefit and the burden of providing it. A founder's preferred form and a sponsor's preferred metric are inputs to the choice, not a substitute for the affected uses.

Follow one consequential use through its actual dependencies. A monthly case discussion can depend on a qualified facilitator, permitted case material, access to the venue and a way to return the result. A public guide can depend on a maintainer and a correction route while requiring no continuing meeting.

Use [C.36.RP (FPF C.36.RP:4)](../../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/129-c-36-rp---sustain-and-renew-shared-ways-of-working.md#c36rp4---solution) when the missing contribution concerns obtaining and using a shared way of working. It locates the gap and compares access, capable help, learning, tools and method development. Retaining a finished answer can leave its interpretation or adaptation unavailable. Obtain the contribution that the later use actually needs.

Include relationships whose continuation requires willing participation. Ask who wants to remain in contact and through what arrangement. Preserve an available way to reconnect where people agree; do not treat a copied membership list as a renewed relationship.

Retain a worthwhile but not yet used possibility when there is a reason and proportionate means to keep it available. The choice need not be between funding a full programme and discarding every trace of its work. An accessible explanation with a clear maintenance limit may be sufficient.

#### CB.11:4.3 - Compare feasible continuations and endings

Construct alternatives that answer the actual change. The following forms have different practical consequences:

| Alternative | What must become true for it to work |
| --- | --- |
| Continue the present arrangement | Its useful purpose and the threatened provision remain or are restored. |
| Reduce or change activities | A smaller or different promise has willing contributors, adequate support and understandable effects on current users. |
| Pause for a stated period or condition | People know which services stop, who handles current obligations and what decision will restart or end the pause. |
| Transfer to another host | A willing capable receiver obtains the necessary access, authority, resources and means of work. |
| Separate into different arrangements | Each group can operate its chosen contribution, and any remaining shared resource or mandate has workable terms. |
| End the organised activity | Current obligations are settled and worthwhile reusable contributions have an appropriate destination or a clear ending. |

Compare benefit, burden, timing and transition loss with the people affected. Include retaining the arrangement when its difficulty can be repaired. Include ending when no worthwhile and feasible continuation is available. Growth, retention of every participant and a larger institution are not universal completion conditions.

Separate a promising alternative from a provided one. A person interested in taking over may still lack time, capability or resource permission. Use CB.7 to develop willing organising work and CB.9 to obtain support. If the needed contribution cannot be provided before the old one ends, reduce or suspend the dependent promise.

[ECO.9 (ECO.9:4)](../../eco/19-eco-9---decide-whether-and-how-to-change-economic-rules-thro.md#eco94---solution) develops a change to economic rules through the practices people can actually perform, including entry, exit, transition burdens and wider dependencies. Use it when those rules are the obstacle. [OCE.14 (OCE.14:4)](../oce/36-oce-14---decide-whether-and-how-to-revise-the-organization-f.md#oce144---solution) supplies a needed organisational revision.

When the effect is uncertain and reversal is practical, try a bounded continuation before committing the whole arrangement. Name the result and burden that will inform the decision, who can stop the trial and what remains available if it fails. A trial must fit the remaining support period.

#### CB.11:4.4 - Obtain the decision and prepare its practical consequences

Take each required decision through its actual authority. Obtain the resource owner's access decision, the successor's agreement and the community decision where one is required. Keep objections and accepted losses visible through CB.13. A departing volunteer may state the end of their own contribution while others decide how to replace it.

Translate the chosen alternative into what affected people need to do. Name the ending and starting dates, the remaining activities, how to reach the new receiver, which promises cease and where unfinished matters will go. Explain a change before users continue relying on the old offer.

Agree the handover work and its limit with both the departing and receiving contributors. Include the time needed to explain an unusual case, obtain access and practise a consequential operation. CB.7 and C.36.RP help obtain capability where it is missing; a title or a folder transfer cannot supply it.

Recover rights and permissions for the material that will move. Public instructions, contributed examples, private discussions and contact details can have different permitted uses. Obtain the relevant permission or leave the restricted material with an appropriate existing receiver. A useful summary can sometimes carry a lesson without carrying the personal account.

If a common resource remains after separation, agree its access, upkeep, decision and dispute arrangements through ECO.7 and the applicable mandate. If those terms cannot be made workable, choose a different resource or narrow what the groups share.

#### CB.11:4.5 - Transfer through receiving use

Provide the chosen receiver with the contributions their work requires. This can include explanations, tools, access, contact with a qualified practitioner and a way to obtain corrections. Preserve the conditions under which a method or resource can be used; the recipient needs to recognise when it is unsuitable.

Have the receiver perform the next consequential work under the new conditions. For an organiser, that could be preparing and running a meeting, responding to an access problem and returning its result. For an independent user, it could be finding the guide, applying the relevant instruction and reaching its maintained correction route.

Observe the gap that the attempt reveals. A missed permission calls for an access decision; an uninterpretable method calls for explanation or learning; a defective method calls for its actual development through Core's Method-development family or the domain practice. Repair the gap and try the affected receiving use again. Keep other usable contributions available.

For a platform move, obtain the applicable export, hosting and access conditions, then inspect the transferred content and a real receiving task. Navigation, links, search, contribution permissions and private material can behave differently. The practical test concerns the functions people rely on, not just the number of files copied.

Finish the departing contributor's responsibility at the agreed boundary. State which work has transferred, what remains unsupported and who has accepted any remaining handover. If the new arrangement cannot carry an essential promise, tell its users and reduce or stop that promise before declaring transfer complete.

#### CB.11:4.6 - Settle the arrangement that is ending

Review the actual remaining commitments with their receivers. Complete, transfer, cancel or renegotiate each one through the applicable arrangement. Paid bookings, promised feedback, borrowed equipment and unresolved participant matters can need different actions. A general closure announcement does not settle them.

Decide what remains accessible and who, if anyone, maintains it. Preserve useful methods, explanations and contact routes only with the permissions and provision needed for that use. Identify obsolete guidance as such where a reader could otherwise rely on it. A maintained public resource and an unmaintained historical archive serve different purposes.

Settle funds, resource access and credentials with the people who hold the corresponding authority. Follow the existing terms for unused contributions or shared property. Obtain a qualified decision where those terms are unresolved; the organiser should not invent a right to distribute or retain them.

Tell affected people what has ended, what remains usable and where the last unresolved work belongs. A final gathering or acknowledgement can help people recognise their shared contribution when they want it. It is not a condition that someone must satisfy before leaving.

Close the promised work at the scope actually achieved. An activity can end successfully after supplying its useful result. Relationships or cultural practice may continue elsewhere; say what has been arranged or observed and leave unobserved continuation open.

#### CB.11:4.7 - Observe the chosen continuation and revise its limits

After a transition, use a small number of relevant returns through CB.10. Can the next host obtain the necessary help? Can independent users still find and use the practice? Did separation remove the conflict or merely move an unresolved shared-resource burden?

Compare the result and cost with the choice. Revise the activity, provide a missing contribution or end the trial when its grounds fail. Preserve the distinction between a successful first use and continuation across later people or time; C.36 supplies the broader cultural question and its evidence requirements.

Revisit the arrangement when purpose, support or context changes again. The Guidebook's context-oriented development can reveal a new external constraint, partner or way to contribute. It can justify collaboration, challenge to an external condition or a different local agenda. It does not require the community to approximate a universal ideal of maturity.

### CB.11:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### CB.11:5.1 - A departing founder and a closing platform

In a constructed volunteer repair circle, the founder will stop organising in two months and end the privately funded discussion platform. Participants still value a monthly case exchange and the public guide to preparing a useful case request. Some only use the guide and do not want to join another discussion service.

The group compares retaining two monthly meetings through paid coordination, reducing to one meeting with two willing organisers, and ending meetings while keeping the guide. The paid option lacks confirmed funding. Two members can provide the reduced schedule, and a local workshop offers an authorised room booking. Members choose that continuation through their existing decision arrangement, accepting the loss of the second monthly meeting.

The new organisers receive the booking route, the preparation method and access to a qualified facilitator. The founder agrees two handover sessions and no continuing response duty afterward. A willing maintainer accepts the public guide under its existing reuse terms and obtains hosting that will remain available after the old platform closes. Private case discussions remain with their permitted recipients; members receive an invitation to choose the new contact route.

In the first preparation attempt, the new organiser cannot obtain the room key at the required time. The workshop owner provides an actual keyholder arrangement. The organiser then prepares and runs the first reduced meeting with the facilitator, returns the discussed actions and confirms the next booking. An independent reader retrieves the transferred guide and includes the observations and prior actions in a case request. The receiving practitioner can identify the next inquiry. The reader also reaches the maintainer about a missing reference, and the maintainer corrects it.

The founder settles the old platform's remaining payment and returns borrowed equipment through their owners. An unfinished participant complaint retains its agreed receiver and response date, with a contact that remains reachable after closure. Before the platform closes, its entry page and a notice to participants give the ending date, the guide's independent address and the optional new group's contact route.

The result is a smaller activity with working support and continued access to the guide. The new organisers review the next two meetings against their available time.

#### CB.11:5.2 - Separate purposes with a remaining shared resource

A music group contains people who want informal playing and others preparing public performances. Repeated attempts to combine both in one session leave each dissatisfied. The members choose two independently organised sessions rather than treating one preference as a failure of commitment.

Both groups obtain capable hosts and times. They still use a shared set of instruments, so the owners and authorised users agree regular slots, responsibility for returning the set and priority to the first confirmed booking for additional dates. The informal group uses its first session to help two newcomers join familiar pieces; the performance group rehearses its agreed concert programme without shortening it for that introduction.

Both later request the set for a Saturday. The informal group's booking was confirmed first, so the performance group takes an available Sunday slot. Each returns the set in time for the next user. The groups retain their different activities while using the shared resource under an arrangement both accepted.

#### CB.11:5.3 - End the forum and retain independently usable practice

A professional discussion forum has few new questions, while a qualified guide continues to be used. A bounded CB.10 inquiry finds that several users can perform their intended work from the guide and existing professional help; they do not need regular discussion.

The organiser confirms maintenance and correction support for the guide, settles open forum questions and announces the end of new posts. One later independent user retrieves the guide and obtains a correction through the retained contact. The organised discussion has ended, while that means of using the practice remains available. Independent users continue through the guide and the professional help they can already obtain.

#### CB.11:5.4 - A pause that does not promise an unavailable service

A seasonal field group cannot obtain the authorised site access needed for its next activity. It pauses field meetings until a named access decision or a stated review date. A willing receiver handles current enquiries and tells participants which alternatives are available.

At the review date, access is still unavailable and no capable host can provide the alternative site. The group ends the field programme for that season, returns the borrowed equipment and leaves permitted guidance accessible.

#### CB.11:5.5 - Ordinary continuation

A reading circle still serves its purpose, its host remains willing, and the room and materials remain available. It continues under the existing terms.

### CB.11:6 - Bias-Annotation

Founders can overvalue the form they created; successors can undervalue the work that made it usable. Active participants can overlook independent users, and a sponsor can equate the end of its platform with the end of the practice. Recover actual receiving uses and voluntary limits before choosing. People who prefer to leave need an effective exit, while those continuing need more than an assumption that somebody will maintain the shared work.

### CB.11:7 - Conformance Checklist

For the intended transition or ending, establish that:

- The changed condition, affected promises and decision authorities are clear.
- Worthwhile uses and the contributions needed to obtain them have been recovered.
- The chosen continuation, pause, transfer, separation or ending has feasible provision and stated accepted losses.
- Successors and other contributors have accepted their work and its limits.
- Material, access and shared resources move only through applicable permissions and arrangements.
- Receiving use has occurred where an achieved transfer is claimed, and unresolved gaps change the promise.
- Current obligations and the old arrangement's remaining resources have an effective disposition.
- People can discover what remains available, and later observation is bounded to a live question.

A successful first use establishes that transfer at its observed scope. Continued cultural availability or changed population practice requires further evidence.

### CB.11:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Failure | Better move |
| --- | --- |
| Keep the founder's preferred format because it once worked | Recover current uses and compare feasible arrangements with affected people. |
| Claim that relationships will survive loss of the platform | Obtain willing contact and activity arrangements and observe their use. |
| Name a successor without providing time, access or capability | Establish and try the contributions their receiving work needs. |
| Copy every discussion and contact into a new space | Recover permitted uses and transfer only what the receiving arrangement may use. |
| Treat separation as failure while keeping an unresolved common resource | Allow different purposes and provide terms for the dependency that remains shared. |
| Preserve every old document as current guidance | Retain useful explanations with their conditions and distinguish an archive from maintained support. |
| Announce closure while leaving promised responses ownerless | Settle or transfer the actual obligations and give their receivers a reachable answer. |
| Maintain a pause with no next decision | Name the resumption condition or review date and decide again when it arrives. |

### CB.11:9 - Consequences

Useful work can continue through a smaller arrangement, another host, independent access or different groups. Organisers can leave with their remaining work transferred or ended, and an ending can release resources while preserving worthwhile contributions.

A transition consumes time and can reduce participation or lose a valued form. Some permissions or capabilities cannot be transferred. Making these limits explicit allows the remaining activity to be credible and gives people a real choice among what is still possible.

### CB.11:10 - Rationale

A community arrangement combines contributions that can change independently. Following the useful work exposes which of those contributions must move, be replaced or end. Receiving use tests the new combination where its value is supposed to appear.

Cultural continuation and organisational continuation therefore require related but distinct judgements. A practice can remain obtainable without the old group, and a group can remain active while losing a needed way of working. Preserving the means of use and settling actual commitments makes either a continuation or an ending useful.
