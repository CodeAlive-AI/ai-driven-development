## CB.3 - Make Entry and Changes of Participation Workable

> **Type:** Community-building method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### CB.3:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a person wants to enter, return to, or change their participation in a community, but the next useful action or its conditions are unclear. A newcomer reads the discussions without knowing whether a question is welcome. An experienced practitioner is invited to host a meeting without knowing the work involved. Someone returning after an absence finds that familiar people, tools and rules have changed.

The organiser helps the person obtain a form of participation that fits their purpose and present conditions. That can mean reading a useful resource, receiving help, making a small contribution, taking responsibility for an activity, or leaving a previous responsibility with a workable handover. Support can begin before any visible contribution.

**First useful result:** the person can choose and attempt an understandable next action, knows where a response or help will come from, and can reduce or decline the proposed involvement. An identified unavailable condition is a useful return when it prevents that action.

The organiser needs a credible account of what the arrangement offers, such as CB.1 supplies, and access to the people who can provide the promised help. The person's relevant domain knowledge may already be sufficient; difficulty entering a space does not establish a need for professional training.

**Ordinary non-use.** If the person already has a suitable way to participate and is using it, leave it available. If the central question is whether the community contribution is worthwhile, use CB.1. If an established organisational contribution has failed and its cause is unclear, OCE.10 supplies the deeper inquiry into the work episode.

### CB.3:2 - Problem

An invitation may be easy to send and difficult to accept. The person may face unfamiliar language, a public-disclosure demand, inaccessible material, uncertain permission, an unsuitable schedule or a commitment larger than they can sustain. An organiser can misread any of these as lack of motivation.

How can the organiser make useful participation obtainable while allowing people to contribute differently, receive help first, change their involvement and return after absence?

### CB.3:3 - Forces

| Force | Decision it changes |
| --- | --- |
| Clear guidance and personal choice | A concrete first action reduces uncertainty; a compulsory public introduction can exclude the person it was meant to welcome. |
| Relevant entry conditions and avoidable barriers | Some actions need competence or permission; unrelated hurdles consume effort without protecting that action. |
| Available help and organising capacity | A welcome creates an expectation of response which someone must be able to meet. |
| Continuity and changing circumstances | Familiar conventions ease return, while changed tools, responsibilities or personal commitments may require a new arrangement. |
| Recognition and actual access | Being told one belongs can matter, but does not provide a missing permission, response or opportunity. |

### CB.3:4 - Solution

Recover the action the person wants to make possible, establish the conditions that affect it, and offer a small workable choice with an available response. Observe the attempted use and change the obstacle or promise that the episode exposes.

**Working reminder:** find the wanted action; make its conditions visible; offer an attainable start; provide the promised response; support a change or return.

#### CB.3:4.1 - Recover where entry becomes difficult

Ask about one recent or imminent occasion: what brought the person here, what they hoped to obtain or contribute, what they could understand, and where the attempt stopped. Walk through the relevant path with their permission. A useful path might run from an invitation to finding a suitable question, preparing permitted material, obtaining help and knowing what to do afterwards. Inspect only the parts that can change this participation.

Compare the person's account with the arrangement they actually encounter. A link can work for the organiser and fail for an outside participant. A newcomer can understand the instructions yet expect that a basic question will be mocked. A returning member can have a valid account but no longer have the time required by an old responsibility.

Keep action-changing distinctions visible. Formal membership may permit access to a space; it does not establish that the person identifies with the group, feels welcome, is recognised by others or has performed the expected work. Find which of these is the live question before changing an entry condition. Often the useful correction is concrete: provide readable material, explain what question is welcome, obtain a response or clarify permission.

Use OCE.10 when an actual work episode needs comparison of rival explanations—for example, lack of access, unavailable time, uncertain role expectations or a punishing response to questions. Its inquiry and qualified return can prevent the organiser from treating a design defect as an individual attitude.

#### CB.3:4.2 - Offer forms of participation that serve the person

Choose alternatives by what the person wants to do and what the activity requires. The following forms can coexist and change over time; they are not ranks.

| Wanted participation | A feasible entry | Conditions to establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find a known answer | Read or search an applicable resource | The material is findable, understandable and accessible under its actual use conditions. |
| Understand what happens here | Observe a permitted activity or inspect a worked example | The observer knows the purpose, sharing limits and how to ask later; the other participants accept the observation where required. |
| Obtain help | Bring a bounded question, privately or with preparation if necessary | Someone can help formulate it and a suitable response route is available. |
| Contribute experience or a result | Respond to one relevant request or offer a small piece of work | The contribution has a recipient, a useful scope and an attainable quality condition. |
| Organise or maintain an activity | Prepare and try the relevant organising work with support | The person understands the work, has the needed capability and resources, and accepts the actual responsibility. |

A person can enter with substantial capability and begin with a demanding contribution. Another can continue benefiting through quiet use. Do not make access to a useful answer depend on a public performance that the answer does not require. Where the arrangement has a legitimate membership or confidentiality condition, explain its purpose and offer the accessible alternative, if one exists.

For each real option, make the requested effort and available response concrete. “You may ask privately; the volunteer host checks requests on the agreed day” is assessable. “Someone will always help” needs capacity the organiser may not have. Let the person ask for a smaller contribution or say that none of the options fits.

#### CB.3:4.3 - Remove the barrier that does not serve the work

Trace an entry demand to the action it protects. A technical intervention may require demonstrated competence and permission. A private case discussion may require an agreed disclosure boundary. A requirement to publish a long biography before reading an ordinary reference may add no useful protection.

Remove or reduce unnecessary effort. Put the first action near the invitation, explain unfamiliar terms with an example, avoid asking for the same information repeatedly, and let people obtain the relevant material in a usable form. Preserve requirements whose removal would make the contribution unfit or impermissible. If the organiser does not control a needed access condition, obtain a decision from its owner or return the limitation.

Test the resulting path from the participant's conditions. Can they find the material, understand the first request and reach the promised help? Include the language, device, schedule, physical access or confidentiality condition that caused the difficulty. An administrator's successful demonstration is insufficient when the participant faces a different condition.

Keep a meaningful refusal available. Explain any consequence of declining a specific responsibility. In an employment or service arrangement, distinguish an existing obligation from optional community participation and obtain the relevant agreement; calling a required act voluntary does not change its terms.

#### CB.3:4.4 - Provide support before demanding visible contribution

Offer help for the actual next action. A newcomer might need to see an example of an answerable question, prepare a permitted description with a peer, or speak with someone who can explain an unfamiliar convention. Match the help to the gap rather than requiring a general orientation programme.

Ask what the person wants to try and what makes it difficult. Give a reason for a necessary condition, real alternatives where they exist, and feedback that helps the person improve the attempt. Make the response usable: who will answer, within which accepted window, and how to get another route if that help is unavailable. The support requires capable, willing people and real time.

Sometimes the obstacle is the person's interpretation of an ordinary transition difficulty. After checking the actual conditions, a relevant, truthful account from a more experienced participant can show how a similar difficulty was overcome and where help was obtained. Invite the newcomer to consider whether that account fits their situation; they may reflect privately or choose to contribute advice for later newcomers. There is no requirement to disclose personal experience.

Use such accounts only when the available opportunities support them. If mistakes are actually ridiculed, correct that practice and its consequences. If no one answers newcomers, obtain the missing response. A reassuring story cannot make rejection or absent support imaginary.

Check both the usefulness of the help and its experienced terms. A completed introduction may have felt coercive or consumed more effort than the person could afford. Silence can also mean that quiet use is sufficient. Ask where the answer can change the next action, and respect a decision not to participate further.

#### CB.3:4.5 - Prepare changes of participation and return

When a person takes a different responsibility, explain the work it adds, the decisions they may make, the help they can obtain and how they can stop or hand it over. Skill in the community's subject does not automatically supply facilitation, moderation or maintenance capability. Obtain the missing preparation through a capable colleague or the relevant HCD contribution.

For a bounded first attempt, agree what the person will do, what support will remain present and what result or difficulty they will return. Choose that attempt from the responsibility itself. Someone preparing to host a case exchange can rehearse opening the question, separating clarification from advice and closing with the recipient's next step. The support provider remains responsible for the help they accepted.

Use OCE.6 when a real organisational appointment, resource allocation, permission or enabling relation must be established. A community invitation can propose the contribution; the appropriate owner establishes the relevant authority and access. For informal voluntary activity, a clear accepted arrangement can suffice where no formal assignment is required.

On return after absence, recover what has changed that affects the next action: the current material, contact, convention, access or responsibility. Supply a short update and a suitable next use. Do not require repeating unaffected orientation or explaining a private reason for absence. If the old contribution is no longer wanted or feasible, agree a smaller one or its end.

When stepping back would leave a relied-on activity unsupported, arrange the handover or change the activity's promise. Permission to leave remains meaningful; continuity requires another feasible contribution, not an indefinite obligation on the departing person.

#### CB.3:4.6 - Close on usable participation and revise locally

Observe whether the person could carry out the selected action under the offered conditions. Distinguish receiving the invitation, entering a space, obtaining help and producing a useful contribution. Ask what remains confusing or costly when that answer can improve the arrangement.

Continue a working option without requiring increased visibility. Change the conditions that defeat the selected action: clarify the request, repair access, change timing, obtain a suitable helper or revise the responsibility. Combine changes when the conditions depend on one another. Return to CB.1 when the available offer itself fails to serve the person's purpose. Use CB.2 for an initial exchange, CB.4 for its interaction design and CB.5 for a specific useful contribution.

An available entry can be recognised in an understandable invitation, material and response path. A stronger claim that people actually participate or feel accepted needs observations appropriate to that claim and those people. Registration, repeated attendance and a welcome message establish different things.

### CB.3:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### CB.3:5.1 - Three repairers need different entries

In this constructed continuation of the independent-repairer example, the association initially asks every newcomer to create a public profile, describe a customer case and attend a live welcome session.

One repairer needs a known procedure and has no wish to discuss it. The relevant reference can lawfully be read without a personal account. The organiser makes it directly accessible and supplies its applicability and correction route. The person obtains the wanted result without becoming visibly active.

A second repairer wants help but cannot publish the customer's material. A willing host offers a short private preparation within an agreed window. Together they determine which observations can be shared; if none suffice, the case uses the service provider's protected route. If a permitted description is possible, the repairer can ask a bounded question. A public introduction would not have supplied this condition.

A third newcomer is experienced in diagnosis and offers to lead a discussion. The organiser establishes that the person has not facilitated a case clinic before. They prepare a small session with an experienced host, practise the clarification/advice boundary and agree which work the host will retain. Subject expertise makes a valuable contribution without proving readiness for every organising responsibility.

The repaired entry is a set of relevant choices. The organiser observes whether each promised route works. Counting three public profiles would conceal the different results.

#### CB.3:5.2 - A first error can lead to another attempt

A newcomer in a voluntary reading group misinterprets an unfamiliar annotation convention and concludes that they are unsuited to the group. The host first checks the event: the convention was unexplained, and a willing reader can help. With that reader's permission, the host offers a brief account of a similar early mistake and shows the corrected example. The newcomer chooses a small second attempt and receives a useful response.

In a changed case, an established member has mocked newcomers' mistakes. The host addresses that behaviour and the protection of questions before using a reassuring story. The two cases require different repairs even though both people express the same worry.

#### CB.3:5.3 - Return and handover preserve a choice

A volunteer who used to welcome newcomers returns after three months with less available time. The old role included both introductions and checking unanswered questions. The group now has another person for introductions, while question checking remains unsupported. They agree a smaller fortnightly contribution and revise the advertised response window. The returning volunteer receives only the changed material and current contact details.

If nobody accepts the remaining work, the organiser reduces the offer or obtains another provider. The previous volunteer's membership does not supply an unlimited continuing commitment.

### CB.3:6 - Bias-Annotation

A growth-oriented account can treat quiet participants as an unfinished version of active contributors. The method instead starts from the person's wanted use and the arrangement's legitimate requirements. Organisers who know the space well can also underestimate unfamiliarity; checking a real entry from the participant's conditions exposes that difference.

### CB.3:7 - Conformance Checklist

A workable entry or change of participation makes it possible to answer:

- What useful action does the person want, and which condition currently matters?
- Which offered form fits their purpose, capability and available effort?
- Which entry demands protect that action, and which can be removed?
- Who will provide the promised response or help?
- What responsibility, permission or support changes when participation changes?
- What did the attempted use establish, and what remains unavailable?

### CB.3:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Wrong turn | Practical repair |
| --- | --- |
| Public self-presentation is required before any useful help | Offer an entry suited to the person's useful action, and obtain only information needed for it. |
| Quiet use is treated as a motivation defect | Ask whether it meets the person's purpose before prescribing more activity. |
| A reassuring message covers real exclusion or missing support | Change the actual response, access or protection condition. |
| Subject expertise is treated as organising competence | Prepare and observe the new responsibility with appropriate support. |
| Returning members must repeat everything | Supply the changes that affect their next use. |
| A volunteer can leave only after finding their own replacement | Put continuity work with the organiser and revise the unsupported promise. |

### CB.3:9 - Consequences

People can receive value before a large commitment, choose useful forms of participation and change them as circumstances change. The organiser obtains specific return questions about access, help and responsibility instead of one unexplained engagement problem.

The work consumes preparation and response capacity. Offering many options without capable support can make entry less reliable; retain only options the arrangement can supply.

### CB.3:10 - Rationale

Entry is a small use of the actual arrangement. If its first action depends on unavailable material, an unexplained convention or an absent response, persuasion leaves the dependency intact. Following that action reveals which change can help.

Choice and support work together. A bare choice among infeasible options supplies little autonomy; help tied to an unwanted public commitment can undermine it. Making a wanted action understandable, feasible and answerable allows participation to develop without prescribing one trajectory.

### CB.3:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The practical comparison is between a generic induction or escalating participation demand and an entry built around the person's wanted action and its actual conditions.

| Source line or developed treatment | Contribution used | Limit that changes use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spinks, The Business of Belonging (2021), physical PDF 102–118 | Proportion the invitation, make small entries intelligible, support quiet use, and prepare each change of responsibility. | The commitment curve is a heuristic. Increased activity and leadership are optional choices; artificial difficulty is not a reason to restrict useful access. |
| Self-determination theory; Slemp, Lee & Mossman (2021) | Provide meaningful choices and reasons, attainable action with useful feedback, and an available interpersonal response. | The workplace intervention review supports bounded design proposals; it does not establish their causal effect in every voluntary community. Observe the recipient's experience and the real work conditions. |
| Walton et al. (2023) | Truthful accounts of ordinary transition difficulties can support a newcomer's interpretation before visible contribution. | The studied student transition and actual opportunities constrain the inference. Use the adapted move only with real help and opportunity; it cannot correct actual rejection by reinterpretation alone. |
| OCE.10 and OCE.6 | Distinguish action-changing explanations; obtain the specific enabling relation where an organisational responsibility is current. | A community invitation supplies neither an employer's allocation nor a permission held by another owner. |

The accepted cost is deliberate inquiry and support in place of cheap uniform induction. Reconsider the entry when the offered help is unavailable, people experience pressure or exclusion, conditions change, or a simpler route supplies the same wanted result.

### CB.3:12 - Relations

- **Uses CB.1 and CB.2:** a worthwhile offer and an obtainable first exchange. A failed central offer returns to CB.1.
- **Supplies CB.4 and CB.5:** understood participation conditions and supported contributors; an interaction or contribution can expose a new entry difficulty.
- **Uses [OCE.10 (OCE.10:4)](../oce/32-oce-10---choose-a-response-to-participation-or-working-cultu.md#oce104---solution):** inquiry and response for a consequential participation gap.
- **Uses [OCE.6 (OCE.6:4)](../oce/28-oce-6---establish-holder-assignments-and-enabling-relations.md#oce64---solution):** an actual assignment or enabling relation required by the responsibility.
- **Uses [HCD.3, HCD.5 and HCD.6](../../human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/_index.md):** qualified diagnosis and preparation when learning a contribution is needed.
