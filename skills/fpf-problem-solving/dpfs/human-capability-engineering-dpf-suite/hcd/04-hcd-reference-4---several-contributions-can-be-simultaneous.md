## HCD.Reference:4 - Several contributions can be simultaneous

Perception, reasoning, bodily action, coordination, decision, evidence return, tool use, and specialist interfaces can all matter in one representative Work occurrence. Establish how these contributions work together in the representative task.

Start from the decision the recipient must make. Identify whose result will be used. Within a performance, follow the actions, exchanges and timing that could change that result. Within the method, follow the operations and conditions it requires. People can perform those actions or supply a result from outside the method. Follow each kind on its own terms. Which actions are parts of this performance? Which steps does the method require, and which result may a supplier provide? Which observations in the report concern this same occasion? At each of the scales the question reaches, ask which possible situations your description puts together. Keep a component's action fixed and vary how its result is used. Keep the whole result fixed and vary which participant or combination could have produced it. When relating an assignment, a method and a record, check that each applies to the same work and conditions where their grounds must hold together. If the recipient would act differently or owes a different explanation, keep the distinction in the model or identify evidence that resolves it in time. Stop at a reliable total, bound or account of the exchange when it settles the decision.

A profile and programme comparison therefore preserve claim identity, conditions, support dependence, uncertainty, critical-error boundaries, trade-offs and finite burdens. Use an aggregate score only if it preserves the distinctions needed for the question or decision.

Describe the contribution required in the person's work role and the conditions under which it is needed. The human capability holder is the person who fills that role. The same person can review evidence, coordinate a specialist and make a bounded proposal in the same Work, or fill different roles in different Work. HCD.4 therefore compares that person's contribution claims and their interfaces. A role description can help derive demand through HCD.1; personal starting evidence is still needed for diagnosis or a personal programme comparison.

Several people's development can support an organization's work, but an organizational result also depends on assignments, coordination, resources and other system contributions. Keep evidence for a personal conclusion attributable to that person. Use OCE and the relevant evidence or authorized provider for claims about organizational capability or change. A bounded cultural population is a different subject again: HCD.17 asks how one HCD practice value is transmitted, received, selected or retained among specified participants. It can examine that relation while individual capability consequences remain an HCD.11–HCD.13 question. A population becomes a capability holder only if the independent-System condition of E.23.CDI is separately established; for a question about demands on the participants, HCD.1 can use an obligation covering the group, a distribution or evidence about particular members, as the question requires. A named performer can be credited from a joint result when an action was necessary for that result and the conditions establish that only this performer could have supplied it. If a partner or substitute could also have supplied it, look for evidence of who acted when that attribution matters.


**Constituent actions in ongoing work.** A dancer has adequate strength, holds an axis when stationary and remembers a figure. During the partnered figure the dancer loses balance in the turn. This locates the difficulty within the performed figure. To choose useful help, ask what the dancer could perceive and do while turning with this partner. The same outcome could arise with a timely invitation and difficulty sustaining the turn, or with the partner’s invitation arriving too late. Use a suitable earlier observation or examine the transition with its timing intact if the choice of help depends on the cause. Where the evidence establishes difficulty coordinating the turn, HCD.2 can select practice that preserves the mastered contributions, and HCD.6 and HCD.10 can prepare the combination and its relevant variations. Success in a stationary exercise still counts for that exercise.
