## Library.Reference:6 - Several relations, not one hierarchy

The Library uses several structures at once. Their relations answer different questions.

| Relation | Architectural consequence | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discipline parthood | A constituent contributes to the continuing practice-and-knowledge assembly described by a Suite. | Maintenance knowledge and intervention practice participate in Engineering's account of sustained functioning. |
| Method composition | Constituent Methods together obtain the result of a larger Method under their joint conditions. | A way of answering a decision question can combine Methods for obtaining observations, interpreting them and comparing alternatives. |
| Specialization | An inherited construction is developed under additional subject conditions. | Somatic Movement Modeling gives bodily sensing and coordination constructions within human capability development. |
| Supplying use | One Method's result is consumed by another operation under stated conditions. | An engineering choice receives a financial constraint without absorbing Corporate Finance. |
| Framework dependency | A framework relies on claims or Methods in an identified supplying edition. | A domain language uses Core's distinction between capability, assignment and performed Work. |
| Publication assembly | Selected complete descriptions are included with ways to find them and links back to their sources. | OCE has its full home in the Management Suite and MNT in the Engineering Suite; uses from elsewhere return to those same complete descriptions. |

Discipline parthood is established for the field, Method parthood for the way of working, and Work containment for performed activity. A project's participants and timetable do not establish any of these other relations. [FPF C.32.MWA](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/82-c-32-mwa---synthesize-an-architecture-account-of-methods-and.md) helps recover the Method, Work, capability and provision structures when they do not correspond one-for-one.

The directory tree supplies an unambiguous home for full descriptions. It does not turn all semantic relations into a tree. A claim of multiple disciplinary parthood needs grounds for each assembly; repeated usefulness is insufficient. Nor does a graph with several parents automatically form a mathematical lattice. A formal model requires its selected elements, relation and mathematical properties, as [FPF E.11.PFP:4.7](../../sections/11-part-e---the-fpf-constitution-and-authoring-guides/38-e-11-pfp---framework-publication-form-profile-for-markdown.md#e11pfp47---explain-the-methods-at-each-selected-scale) explains.

OCE's organizational construction participates in the Management whole, a specialization of engineering. A management undertaking can also use MNT's full policy or programme Method while maintenance remains a constituent of the broader Engineering edition. In that use the manager can perform the Method: the supplying discipline need not be represented by a different person. Corporate Finance can similarly supply a constraint to an engineering project without that use establishing its whole field as an Engineering constituent. The subject and the relation of the complete field to the whole decide composition; a useful connection alone does not.

The transformed object also differs from the performer and recipient. Replacing a teacher with an AI assistant can leave the human-learning target unchanged. Replacing the learner with a robot changes the target and can require different learning and control Methods. Methods with the same input and output kinds can require different operations for those targets. Replacing an instrument can change motor demands and attainable musical material; changing listeners or cultural selection can change what is retained for other reasons. The complete MDPE.20 account preserves these distinct mechanisms. The general constructions remain in Core; the bodies that depend on a particular target and mechanism remain with their substantive fields.

### Library.Reference:6.1 - Connect structures through the result they must obtain

A change can leave the organization chart intact while making its service unavailable. A controller can retain the same components while a faster command sequence defeats its limits. Use the connected account here when a proposed change crosses structures whose parts or relations do not correspond one-for-one. If one understood relation and its subject Method already settle the question, use that answer directly.

Begin with what must become possible, for whom and under which conditions. Work backward to the contributions that could obtain it, then forward through their connections to the receiving result. The backward inquiry identifies necessary contributions; the forward inquiry asks whether they can work together. A list of available parts is insufficient when their inputs disagree, their results arrive too late or their combined demand exceeds a shared resource. A failed connection can require another constituent, another interface or provider, or a revised result agreed with its owner.

Several accounts can contribute to this reasoning. A **system composition** identifies constituents of a particular whole and the interactions through which it functions. A **functional account** groups contributions by what they do and relates the results they supply. A **Method composition** connects reusable ways of working under the whole Method's applicability and construction conditions. An account of **Work** concerns particular performances: what they constitute, what precedes or overlaps what, and what occupies the same means. The descriptions of those objects have their own structure. Their paragraphs, boxes and rows can be aligned without the described parts being identical.

Connect the accounts through the relation that carries the consequence. A tracking calculation can be allocated to a processor; the processor can be a constituent of a controller; an execution consumes processor time; the calculated command is used to act on a plant. Replacing the processor can preserve the calculation while invalidating its timing premise. Changing the meaning or permitted rate of commands can instead require a different calculation on the same processor. The needed change follows the premise and its dependents.

Composition makes a stronger claim than use or dependence. A constituent Method contributes to a reusable whole under its construction rule. Another Method can consume its result without becoming that whole's constituent. Likewise, a supplier can make delivery possible without being part of the receiving organization. Developing a Method changes a way of working; performing that development does not make it a higher constituent level of every Method it changes. Provision, development and delivery can nevertheless compete for the same person's time. Preserve that shared demand even when their Method boundaries remain separate.

Control adds another connection: information must support a choice that can influence the relevant action. In organizational work, identify who may change the commitment and how that choice reaches capable performers and available means. A reporting line, a feedback arrow and a relation of organizational parthood can coexist, but each supports a different inference. [FPF C.43](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/127-c-43---connect-observation-choice-and-action-in-control.md) explains the observation–choice–action connection, including its organizational use.

An abstraction keeps the distinctions needed for a selected answer and omits others. A two-box controller drawing may explain which contribution supplies a command while being insufficient to decide whether that command can be tracked. A more detailed account can recover that omitted condition without introducing another system constituent. Model scope concerns what is included; detail concerns the distinctions retained within it. A temporal scale, update period or planning horizon can change the answer again: a slower update changes a timing condition, while a longer planning horizon changes the interval considered. Neither change by itself grants authority or establishes a containing system.

Bring the findings back to the whole. Test a replacement against all receiving uses it can affect; retain independent uses whose premises still hold. If the old whole cannot carry the required behavior or composition claim, reconsider its boundary and identity rather than preserving it for a neat hierarchy. [FPF B.1.5.RS](../../sections/08-part-b---trans-disciplinary-reasoning-cluster/08-b-1-5-rs---evaluate-a-constituent-method-replacement-in-its.md) supplies the constituent-replacement construction. [FPF C.30.ASV:4.5a](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/67-c-30-asv---architecture-structural-view-adequacy.md#c30asv45a---select-structures-for-a-method-and-its-uses) helps choose the Method structures that can change the decision; [FPF C.32.MWA](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/82-c-32-mwa---synthesize-an-architecture-account-of-methods-and.md) develops their connected architecture account, alternatives and moved burdens. Where the unresolved question is how the Methods will be performed and supported together, [FPF C.32.MWA.PP](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/83-c-32-mwa-pp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-p.md) constructs and compares those arrangements.

These are different uses of one constructive return, not a universal calculation. A control design needs its error dynamics and physical limits; a service arrangement needs usable completion, qualified capacity and commitments. The two cases below show how their subject Methods determine different changes from the same whole-to-contributions reasoning. A construction explains what would hold under its premises. Actual systemhood, Method composition, performed Work, authority or disciplinary membership needs the corresponding subject grounds. In particular, a proposed larger discipline returns to [FPF C.20](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/39-c-20---composition-of-u-discipline.md) for its knowledge–practice couplings and continuing whole.

### Library.Reference:6.2 - Interpret levels, layers and scales through their relations

A level is relative to an identified relation and subject. Nested system constituents can support system-composition levels; constituent Methods can support Method-composition levels. Classification, refinement, abstraction, authority and time answer other questions even when a source also calls their positions levels. The depth of a composition depends on the selected whole. A taxonomic child is a narrower kind, whereas a constituent supplies a contribution to a whole.

An account of functional layers groups contributions by what they do and explains how their results connect. Planning and tracking can be functional layers in a control design. Delivery, support and development can be functional groupings in an operating arrangement. Several modules or people can contribute to one layer, and one module or person can contribute to several layers. Use a layered account when this grouping helps explain the required result; it does not require a matching organization of physical components or teams.

This gives a qualified answer to “are management levels functional layers?” A source can be grouping functions, composing organizational units, allocating decision rights or comparing horizons. Delivery, support and development performed by one team can be described functionally. If that team is a constituent of a firm under the applicable system-composition conditions, its system relation remains available as well. A manager's authority and planning horizon require their own account. The word *management* chooses none of these relations in advance.

“Method level” similarly needs its local meaning. It can concern a constituent, a narrower family, a refinement or the order in which an explanation introduces topics. For refinement, first determine whether the variation remains within one Method's identity. When two Methods are identified, [FPF A.3.1:4.8](../../sections/05-part-a---kernel-architecture-cluster/20-a-3-1---u-method-reusable-way-of-doing-with-explicit-applica.md#a3148---method-relations-composition-and-work-enactment) asks which semantics are retained, what is narrowed or strengthened and what action or result changes. More detailed description alone does not settle this comparison. Teaching one topic before another can make an explanation usable without making the earlier topic a constituent or execution stage of every later Method.

Use a qualified expression when it changes the reader's inference. The following English and Russian phrases illustrate that practice; they are explanatory choices, not new kinds or a rule for replacing source vocabulary.

Keep a source's well-defined term. For example, the ISA-95 account in [OPC 10030 §4.2.3](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/specs/OPC-10030/4.2.3) uses *activity levels*. Recover that activity relation rather than automatically replacing *levels* with *layers*. [FPF C.30.STRAT:4.2](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/66-c-30-strat---clarify-stratification-and-architecture-source.md#c30strat42---recovered-meanings-and-patterns-to-use) gives the local functional-layer/modular-level distinction and the other meanings to recover. Russian «управление» and English *control*, *management* and *governance* require contextual interpretation; [FPF E.10:9.8](../../sections/11-part-e---the-fpf-constitution-and-authoring-guides/25-e-10---unified-lexical-rules-for-fpf.md#e1098---control-management-regulation-governance-steering) and C.43 retain their different uses. When translating into another natural language, carry the subject, relation, conditions and example first, then choose the locally useful expression. Keep a qualified phrase or explained source term if one target word would merge action-changing meanings. The phrase pairs here establish no fixed equivalence for other languages. Revisit a translation when it changes which contribution is selected, composed, authorized or revised.

### Library.Reference:6.3 - A faster command can defeat an unchanged controller

Consider a constructed sampled controller with plant `x[k+1] = x[k] + u[k]` and tracking law `u[k] = (r[j] - x[k])/2`. The planner's command `r[j]` is held for `N` fast steps. The state is fully observed; this model has no disturbance, computation delay or unmodeled saturation. Commands satisfy `|r[j]| ≤ 8.9`; consecutive command changes have magnitude at most `δ`. Required sampled limits are `|x[k]| ≤ 10` and `|u[k]| ≤ 0.6`.

Planning and tracking are two functional contributions that could run on one processor. A separate system composition could include a controller, plant and their constituents. The functional grouping alone does not establish two physical Systems or two system-composition levels. The current question is whether the command and tracking contributions are compatible under the sampled limits.

Let `e[j] = x[jN] - r[j]` immediately after a command is selected. During the held-command interval the error halves at each fast step. At the next command change,

```text
e[j+1] = 2^(-N) e[j] - (r[j+1] - r[j]).
```

If the initial error satisfies `|e[0]| ≤ E`, the interval `|e| ≤ E` at command changes is invariant for `E = δ/(1 - 2^(-N))`: the next error is at most `2^(-N) E + δ = E`. Within each interval the error decreases. Hence `|u| ≤ E/2`, and `|x| ≤ |r| + E` is a sufficient state bound. This is the consequential join: the planner's jump and update conditions determine the tracking demand.

With `N = 4` and `δ = 1`, `E = 16/15`. The input magnitude is at most `8/15 < 0.6`, and the sufficient state bound is `8.9 + 16/15 = 299/30 < 10`. Under the stated initial and model conditions, every command sequence within this interface respects the sampled limits. Holding the current command is allowed by those constraints; the calculation does not establish attainment of every further planner objective.

Now require commands every two fast steps while retaining permitted unit jumps. The plant, processor and two functional contributions are unchanged. The error bound becomes `E = 4/3`, so the earlier sufficient input argument fails. There is also an actual violating trajectory: take `r[0] = 0` and `x[0] = 16/15`, admitted by the earlier design. After two steps, `x[2] = 4/15`. The permitted command `r[1] = -1` then gives `u[2] = -19/30`, whose magnitude exceeds `0.6`. The failure is in the changed temporal and command interface, not in the number of component boxes.

One repair keeps `N = 2` and the command-magnitude bound `8.9`, but reduces the jump bound to `δ = 0.9`. Require initial conditions `|e[0]| ≤ 1.2` and `|x[0]| ≤ 10`. The error envelope is invariant because `1.2/4 + 0.9 = 1.2`, so `|u| ≤ 0.6`. Check the state separately: `x[k+1] = (x[k] + r[j])/2` is a convex combination. From `|x[k]| ≤ 10` and `|r[j]| ≤ 8.9`, it follows that `|x[k+1]| ≤ 9.45 ≤ 10`. Thus the state set is invariant as well.

The coarser bound `|r| + E` would have suggested reducing the command magnitude to `8.8`. The separate state invariant shows that this extra restriction is unnecessary in the stipulated model. The revised interface still sacrifices the original unit-jump demand, and a transition from an operating controller must establish both new initial conditions. Accept this candidate only if the intended task permits that smaller jump. Retaining `N = 4` instead sacrifices the faster response. A timed command segment, another tracking law or stronger actuation changes other premises and requires another derivation.

[CAD.1–CAD.4 in Control Architecture Design](../../dpfs/engineering-dpf-suite/cad/_index.md) develop the corresponding architecture, interface, feasibility and co-design questions. This sampled calculation leaves real-plant adequacy, continuous-time constraints, shared-processor scheduling and the planner's further objectives to their required constructions. Keeping only two boxes loses the recurrence and cannot decide this change; retaining the recurrence, command conditions and initial state is enough for the demonstrated sampled-limit comparison. Processor detail becomes relevant if computation or delay changes that premise.

### Library.Reference:6.4 - The same organization can require different performance and provision

Consider a prospective diagnostic service with two accepted reports due by hour six of an eight-hour day. Each report needs two hours of analyst preparation followed by one hour of an authorized expert's judgement and acceptance. The analyst has eight available hours. The expert has four allocable hours with no further fixed calendar restriction in this constructed case. The protocol in use is qualified, and the expert is authorized to accept both reports.

The expert must also perform one hour of support that keeps the service usable and one hour of development of the reusable diagnostic protocol. A feasible schedule gives the analyst preparation at hours `0–2` and `2–4`, the expert support at `0–1`, protocol development at `1–2`, and reviews at `2–3` and `4–5`. Both reports arrive before hour six; expert occupancy totals four hours.

Delivery, support and development are different functional contributions made by the same person. Their three rows establish no three organizational units or management ranks. The reusable report Method also differs from the two planned performances and the separate protocol-development work. Developing a protocol changes a Method used in delivery; that development need not be a constituent of each report-producing Method. Yet all three kinds of contribution draw on the same expert capacity. Their distinct boundaries must remain connected in the service account.

Change the case class: additional evidence now makes each qualified review take `1.5` expert-hours. Keep the reporting promise, preparation and authority unchanged. Expert demand becomes `3 + 1 + 1 = 5` hours against four available. A total of analyst and expert labour would hide the shortage: spare analyst time cannot supply the qualified expert judgement. An unchanged organization chart therefore carries an unavailable promise.

One bounded alternative defers the hour of protocol development. This works only if the current protocol remains qualified and the development commitment permits a one-day delay. Keep analyst preparation at `0–2` and `2–4`; schedule support at `0–1` and reviews at `2–3.5` and `4–5.5`. Both reports meet the deadline and expert occupancy is four hours. Today's service is obtained by moving development into another period. This is not a sustainable recurring arrangement by arithmetic alone. If the protocol must change before today's reports can be accepted, deferral fails at that dependency despite the hours fitting.

A different arrangement obtains one complete review from a qualified external provider. The provider must be able to receive the prepared material, return an acceptable result before hour six and supply the acceptance contribution allowed by the service's rules. Count transfer, preparation, coordination and checking in the arrangement. The internal expert's retained review, support and development require `1.5 + 1 + 1 = 3.5` hours, leaving at most `0.5` expert-hour for additional coordination and checking; those activities must fit their actual windows as well. That capacity remainder is a necessary condition, not a completed external-service schedule. The provider relation can support delivery without making the provider organization part of the internal team. If qualification, timing or authority cannot be obtained, return the promise to the person entitled to reduce or defer it.

Replacing judgement with an automated field-completeness check changes the supplied result. Complete fields do not establish the diagnostic conclusion the recipient needs. That proposal returns to the subject Method and acceptance criterion, whereas qualified external review returns primarily to provision, capacity and authority. The same slot in a route can conceal different contributions.

[OPS.11.1 in Operations Management](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/ops/_index.md) explains receiving completion, route, occupancy, provision and authority. [STR.14 in Strategy](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/str/_index.md) reconciles the simultaneous service, support and development commitments. To develop the arrangement, use [FPF C.32.MWA.PP:4.5–4.8](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/83-c-32-mwa-pp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-p.md#c32mwapp45---generate-whole-alternatives-by-changing-an-operative-relation): change the relation that makes the missing contribution obtainable, follow the moved burden, place qualified work in its receiving windows, and compare the complete alternatives. Its :4.9 gives the selected realization its remaining contribution and conditions. A proposed schedule or provider choice still needs effective commitments before performance can rely on it.

Here the changed premise is work content and qualified resource demand; in the controller it is the dynamic command interface. Both changes are found by returning from the required result to the contribution and relation that could defeat it. The resulting calculation then changes the feasible whole. System composition can remain intact in either case while the Method connection, permitted interface, provision or promise changes. C.32.MWA connects these accounts; the subject Methods supply the operations that make each conclusion warranted.

### Library.Reference:6.5 - Compact cycles in working control arrangements

Use this section when familiar cycle names leave you unsure which way of working fits, what its diagram assumes, or why another repetition has stopped helping. Begin with the result to obtain or maintain. Identify which observations can change the next action, who can carry it out and which conditions must already hold. If an adequate way and its provision are available, use them directly.

The comparison below connects selected improvement, decision and learning approaches through their actual relationships. It explains when a short cycle is sufficient and which changed dependency requires another method or arrangement. [C.43](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/127-c-43---connect-observation-choice-and-action-in-control.md) supplies the general connection between observation, choice and action. The subject methods supply their own operations and conditions.

#### Library.Reference:6.5.1 - Establish what the cycle describes

A compact cycle can prescribe a way of acting, explain a process or remind a prepared practitioner of a known way. Recover that contribution from the source. A prescription needs operations, conditions and a rule for choosing the next move. An explanatory model represents relationships and needs grounds for the claims made about them. A reminder relies on a fuller way that the practitioner can already recover. A source may combine these contributions; distinguish its particular claims.

For a prescribed way, a description of the Method lets a performer recover its operations and conditions. A person, team or software system performs particular work with actual inputs, means and authority. Organizing that work makes information and coordinated action available when needed. The participants' interactions realize the control arrangement. An architecture description represents selected relationships in that arrangement and the controlled system for a particular question.

An organization can precede the choice of a method, and its participants can perform work that changes the organization itself. A proposed architecture can guide construction before the arrangement exists. It remains a proposal until the relevant relationships are realized.

For an explanatory heuristic, use its distinctions to investigate what happens, develop an explanation or design an intervention. Select the intervention's operations and conditions on their own grounds. The expansive-learning model below, for example, helps investigate transformation of collective activity; its ideal cycle does not prescribe a universal sequence for every participant.

One Method can be performed by one person or several departments. One organization can use several Methods. One participant can observe, choose, act and reconsider the way of working; several participants can also share these contributions. Four labels on a diagram establish neither four components nor four occurrences of Work. Where a formal identity claim about a Method, its description or actual Work is needed, use the defining rules for that claim.

[C.32.MWA.PP](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/83-c-32-mwa-pp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-p.md) supplies the connection of performance and provision. C.30.LCA guides the description of a control architecture.

#### Library.Reference:6.5.2 - Follow the relationships that make the next action possible

**Observation must become a useful distinction for choice.** A queue alone does not tell the participants whether delay comes from insufficient capacity, unready inputs or a missing decision right. Interpretation connects observations with the situation and consequences of possible actions. An adequate familiar judgement can suffice; an explicit prediction, causal investigation or additional observation is needed when the subject method and question require it.

**Choice depends on the result and acceptable losses.** Increasing output, keeping a promise, preserving quality and developing a person's ability can call for different actions. A criterion can change, but changing it is a decision with affected recipients and authority of its own. Consent to participate, authority over shared capacity and permission to change a commitment retain their organizational meaning when described through control.

**The selected action must be obtainable.** A recommendation, permission, commitment and completed action contribute differently. Correct information can arrive too late; a manager can agree with a change while lacking the required resource. A compact method often relies on existing performance, coordination and provision. A fuller source method may itself explain how to build them.

**Build a joint proposal where the undertaking is still open.** Participants can begin with a shared concern, a provisional direction or an existing goal. Develop the proposal from the actual work: a contribution, its grounds and the support it needs. The return can change the goal or its local interpretation, the proposed way, the provision or the participants. Bring each change to those who can decide it and to people whose contributions or reliance change. The undertaking becomes supportable when the required agreement, authority and means are in place.

**Distinguish what is returning.** Before execution, participants examine a proposal's grounds, compatibility and acceptability. Their responses can regulate the work of constructing that proposal; they are not observations of the proposed operation's results. Once the operation runs, its actual consequences can inform a change to it or its conditions. A coach can also use the participant's own reasoning and attempts to develop their ability. These returns concern different subjects and require different grounds, even when one conversation serves all three.

**Consequences return to the premise they can change.** They may support a new account of the situation, a different current action, a revised choice rule, changed means or allocation, development of a performer, or reconsideration of a goal. A good answer does not establish all these results. Retaining an adequate action can also be the useful outcome of feedback.

When recurrence threatens the required result and its cause is unresolved, investigate what reproduces the departure. Its frequency alone does not identify a cause or select a remedy. Connect the observation to a discriminating explanation, an available change and a later observation that can test its effect. The change may concern the current action, a choice rule or the conditions that let participants act together. Adequate current response can continue while that inquiry proceeds. When a setting rule itself no longer fits the result, environment or means, reconsider the rule and the grounds for choosing its replacement.

**Timing and joint action connect the relationships.** Observation frequency, decision time, action duration and the goal's horizon differ. Several locally permissible choices can together exceed a shared resource. The useful repair changes the information, choice, authority, allocation or work on which the joint result depends. C.43:4.1–4.6 develops these general dependencies.

A level has meaning relative to a relationship and a selected whole. System composition, organizational authority, control of a controller, timescale, Method generality and description detail answer different questions, as Library.Reference:6.2 explains. Functional explanation groups operations by their contributions and connects their results. One person or module can supply several contributions; one contribution can require several participants.

To establish control of a controller, show the additional working relationship: observations about its performance, a criterion for selecting changes to its goal, rule or provision, an action that makes the selected change effective, and a return from the resulting performance. One control activity then becomes the subject of another. An organizational rank needs separate grounds in the actual allocation of authority or composition.

Consider two constructed arrangements. In the first, a dispatcher receives a reliable queue state, selects the next job by an adequate urgency rule, assigns a capable performer and receives completion information. The goal, rule, capacity and authority remain adequate over the relevant interval. This observation–choice–action–return relationship is sufficient for selecting the next job.

In the second, the same dispatcher also has responsibility and authority to revise the urgency rule while preserving commitments. From accumulated outcomes and acceptable losses, the dispatcher compares rules, introduces a selected rule and observes whether later decisions improve. The revised rule guides job selection, and the results of job selection inform its next revision. One person supplies both relationships. By contrast, updating a counter or threshold according to an already fixed rule can remain part of one adaptive controller. The additional relationship follows from what is selected, changed and assessed.

Distribution concerns participation in these relationships. Teams can coordinate shared capacity through effective agreements without a common superior; several managers can pass along one decision without independently revising its grounds. Describe the rights, interactions and means that an organizational change actually changes.

Adaptation and learning likewise need a subject. An updated state estimate, retained AI memory, trained model weights, a distinction acquired by a person and revised organizational norms are different results. A single episode can contribute to several when their relationships and evidence are preserved. C.43:4.8 supplies development of the method and its provision; C.40.JD connects development of the method and performer. Use these contributions when their problems arise.

**Connect learning to subsequent use.** For continuing joint work, ask what must remain obtainable after the present encounter. The result may depend on a person's ability to make a distinction, an explanation another practitioner can recover, or an interaction through which specialists combine different contributions. Provide the needed connection and observe its use. Joint work can rely on complementary abilities; developing the same ability in every participant is an additional goal.

When participants seek to improve how they learn together, identify the interaction they can change and who can make the change effective. A facilitator can improve questioning within an agreed encounter. Changing access to information, protected practice time or rights over shared decisions requires the corresponding participants and authority. Later work must show whether the changed condition lets the needed contribution reach its use.

#### Library.Reference:6.5.3 - Preserve the different family connections

The field is organized here by the relationship that a prescription helps build or change, or an explanatory model helps understand. Maintaining work, testing a process change, regulating flow, acting in changing interaction, developing ability and transforming collective practice overlap. A method can address several of these difficulties. The following connections explain significant variants; they are not a closed classification of cycle names.

##### Library.Reference:6.5.3.1 - Process performance, an improvement test and sustained use

In the [Model for Improvement](https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement), an improvement aim, useful measures and an idea for change guide PDSA. Performers predict the result of a bounded test, conduct it, compare observations with the prediction and select the next move. The test supplies grounds for a decision. Sustained use of the change requires everyday work to be organized. [IHI distinguishes testing from implementation](https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement/implementing-changes), including training, documentation and supporting conditions.

**Choose what the next test must establish.** An early PDSA can answer a small question about feasibility or the participants' response. A claim that the change caused an improvement needs observations and a comparison that can distinguish the relevant alternatives; the number of completed cycles does not supply that basis. Implementation establishes continuing use through the actual workflow, responsibilities and support. It can itself require tests. When [spreading a change](https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement/spreading-changes), the receiving participants must establish how it fits and works in their conditions. Knowledge from a trial, sustained practice and the organization's ability to conduct further improvement are different results.

**Connect the change with its implementation.** IHI's [Core Components Guide](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/health-services/articles/10.3389/frhs.2026.1751580/full) distinguishes a *content theory*, explaining why selected changes should improve the system, from an *execution theory*, explaining how the initiative will make their use obtainable. Develop these together with people who perform and receive the work: connect the problem, aim and proposed changes with time, data, learning, cooperation and responsibility. Observe both the system's results and the conduct of the initiative. Revisit their connections as understanding changes. The components support an iterative design; their numbered presentation does not prescribe a linear sequence.

**Return evidence to the contribution it can change.** [A.3.1.MI:4.5](../../sections/05-part-a---kernel-architecture-cluster/28-a-3-1-mi---introduce-a-method-into-practice-and-decide-what.md#a31mi45---explain-a-changed-result-before-selecting-an-adaptation) supplies the fuller comparison when the next action depends on distinguishing delivery, mechanism and context. If the intended action did not occur, inspect access, allocation, capability and support before rejecting its proposed mechanism. If it occurred but the expected consequence did not, examine that mechanism, the relevant conditions and competing explanations. A useful result obtained through additional help can justify retaining that help while leaving the original explanation unresolved. Distinguish improvement in the service, the intended performer's acquired ability and the organization's ability to obtain the work. Meetings, coaching hours and completed tests can describe activity; later performance under the required conditions is needed for the corresponding ability claim.

Use [IS.3](../../dpfs/is/15-is-3---construct-and-adapt-a-support-strategy-with-its-recip.md) when the missing contribution is how the practice becomes usable. Work through a consequential operation with its performer, recipient and provider; compare preserving adequate support, repairing its conditions, obtaining expert performance or developing a needed ability. A different introduction can preserve the target way. A change to its decisive operation or criterion returns to that way's owner. [OPS.22](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/ops/56-ops-22---interpret-operating-performance-and-decide-the-resp.md) supplies interpretation of actual operating results; [OCE.13](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/oce/35-oce-13---observe-and-compare-organization-change-consequence.md) compares consequences when the organization arrangement changes, including work transferred to other participants. Use their sufficient existing results directly.

**Connect continuing improvement to organizational decisions.** [Quality as an Organizational Strategy](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/health-services/articles/10.3389/frhs.2026.1726688/full) connects the need served and the organization's purpose with its interdependent processes, measures, information and improvement work. Information reaches distinct uses: an immediate response to a problem, feedback to an existing improvement effort, or analysis for future planning. Planning connects strategic objectives to the processes that need change and produces both operating plans and a resourced improvement portfolio. Leaders sponsor the work, help remove obstacles and use its results to learn about the organization. Existing improvement projects can provide the starting point while the other connections develop. A process map makes dependencies discussable; its arrangement on the page does not establish decision rights.

[STR.12](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/str/30-str-12---observe-signals-to-continue-or-reconsider-strategic.md) connects a qualified signal to the premise and authorized response it can change. Use [STR.16](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/str/34-str-16---construct-a-feasible-portfolio-with-shared-or-branc.md) when several worthwhile undertakings and their support still need a feasible combination. Separate one undertaking's need for another's result from competition for the same person or resource. Obtain the required contributions and commitments, then observe whether they let the receiving work proceed. Experience can warrant revising an initiative, its support, the portfolio or the strategic direction. Each revision needs the people authorized to decide it and those who can perform the change. These relationships can be distributed among participants or supplied by the same people.

A local test with adequate provision can finish its bounded question without constructing a new organization-wide programme. Retain a sufficient current method and arrangement. Expand the inquiry or arrangement when the needed evidence, continuing work, shared capacity or decision right is missing or changes. Close the present question at its supported scope while preserving the particular later observation or dependency that can reopen it.

Recover the selected formulation of PDCA or PDSA. The [Deming Institute's PDSA account](https://deming.org/explore/pdsa/) emphasizes study of a prediction and permits reconsidering theory and goals. [ASQ's PDCA](https://asq.org/quality-resources/pdca-cycle) also includes analysis and learning in Check. Treating every PDCA as compliance checking would lose that variant. An experiment can inform a goal change; the actual organization must still provide the decision right.

Imai's SDCA/PDCA distinction makes the relationship between maintaining and changing a standard explicit. Performers sustain work under the current standard and discover departures; improvement supplies grounds to revise that standard. Further work uses the revision, and its results inform the next reconsideration. One process owner can participate in both relationships. The basis is *Gemba Kaizen*, second edition, chapter 1, and the [Kaizen Institute's account of standardization and improvement](https://kaizen.com/insights/standardize-improve-standards/). Maintaining an adequate way need not carry the burden of another improvement investigation.

[DMAIC](https://asq.org/quality-resources/dmaic) adds useful measurement and investigation of causes before changing the process. Its Control phase organizes sustained results and response to later departures. ASQ distinguishes DMADV where designing a new process is required. [A3](https://info.lean.org/hubfs/ebook%20files/How-to-use-A3-PDF-final.pdf), particularly pages 3–9 of the LEI account, connects problem investigation with participants' examination of the reasoning, agreement and responsibility for action. The sheet supports those interactions. These variants explain how a PDSA test can contribute inside a more developed undertaking.

**Distinguish the difficulty before choosing the response.** Begin with the result, population, period and requirement that matter to the recipient. For a defective item, apply the relevant acceptance or protective rule. A statistical signal concerns departure from an established process behavior. An unreliable measurement leaves the attributed property uncertain. A missed operation raises a question about actual execution and its conditions. These difficulties can coexist. [OPS.18](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/ops/52-ops-18---control-operating-quality-and-reliability.md) connects each evidence question to its available action and authority. Act on a known requirement violation when its response is already warranted, while obtaining any diagnosis still needed for prevention. A stable process can repeatedly produce unacceptable output; statistical stability alone does not settle acceptance or process capability.

**Establish what the observations represent.** Recover the unit or event, inclusion rule, time order, measuring procedure and relevant operating conditions. [OPS.15](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/ops/48-ops-15---build-a-decision-specific-operating-account.md) supplies the population and event account; [C.16:5.1–5.4](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/27-c-16---measurement-metrics-characterization.md#c1651---name-the-measurand-and-measurement-subject) connects an indication to the intended property, calibration and uncertainty. Repeated readings of one unchanged item can expose measurement variation; they do not sample the variation among produced items. When a change of gauge, operator or recording rule could explain the apparent process change, compare that explanation before adjusting the process. [NIST's Gauge R&R guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section4/mpc41.htm) identifies repeatability, reproducibility, stability and bias as distinct measurement questions. More readings under the same shared bias leave that bias unresolved.

For monitoring, establish the baseline and a suitable observation and response rule, then compare later behavior with that basis. [NIST distinguishes baseline establishment from subsequent monitoring](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section1/pmc12.htm). Dependence, changing case mix or grouping can change the interpretation and the monitor needed. A new baseline requires grounds for the changed reference; an unfavorable observation alone is no reason to erase the departure. Use the existing qualified monitor or acceptance plan when it already answers the question.

**Make the investigation serve a possible change.** DMAIC connects a defined problem to usable measurements, an account of causes, a proposed remedy and continuing control. Return between these contributions when a finding changes the question: a recording error can invalidate the apparent defect pattern; a failed remedy can challenge the proposed mechanism. [C.28.CM](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/55-c-28-cm---construct-and-challenge-a-causal-model.md) supplies the comparison of material causal explanations, including measurement and selection. Choose another observation for the difference it could make to the response. If the retained explanations already support the same sufficient protection, use it with its stated limits. Investigate further when a different mechanism would change the remedy, its permissibility or its burden.

**Compare interventions through their consequences.** Finding that an input affects an output does not yet establish how much changing that input would improve ordinary production. For an output Y that depends linearly on input X, write `Y = a + bX + e`: a is an offset, b the sensitivity and e the remaining influence. With independent X and e and finite variances, `Var(Y) = b² Var(X) + Var(e)`. A suitable experiment can estimate the effect b, while the ordinary distribution of X needs its own basis; experimenter-selected levels need not reproduce it. This is a useful distinction in [Panahi, Steiner and de Mast's verification study](https://doi.org/10.1080/08982112.2023.2253303), §§2–3.1. Compare feasible changes to the input distribution, sensitivity, centering or process design against the required output and their full consequences. A statistically significant effect or a large explained share alone does not select the remedy.

Use [C.28.MR](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/56-c-28-mr---derive-an-intervention-consequence-by-mechanism-re.md) when the consequence needs derivation: state the mechanism replaced, retain the other relevant mechanisms and input conditions, and calculate the requested result. A common cause of X and Y can leave output variation after X is fixed; their squared correlation alone does not quantify the reduction from that intervention. Carry model uncertainty and uncertainty in estimated quantities into the comparison when they can change the decision. [MMP.13](../../dpfs/foundational-thinking-dpf-suite/mmp/18-mmp-13---infer-unknowns-statistically-under-a-stated-observa.md) supplies inference under the actual observation law. Process requirements can concern tails, centering or asymmetric consequences; a smaller variance is useful only through the result it improves.

**Select observations for the distinction needed.** A study aimed at separating causes can deliberately choose contrasting cases. The [2025 review of leveraged sample selection](https://doi.org/10.1080/08982112.2024.2410012) explains how parts near opposite ends of a known baseline can help investigate variation. That choice needs a justified baseline, an appropriate study and analysis that account for selection. Very extreme cases may belong to another failure mechanism. Such an investigation answers a different question from estimating the ordinary frequency of defects. [MMP.16](../../dpfs/foundational-thinking-dpf-suite/mmp/22-mmp-16---design-observations-to-separate-mathematical-model.md) connects the alternatives, obtainable records and receiving decision. Use existing information when it is sufficient; obtain a new study when its attainable contribution warrants its cost and delay.

**A conditional calculation can reject an insufficient remedy.** Suppose a centered, stable process is modeled by `Y = 10 + 0.5X + e`, with independent normal X and e, means zero, `Var(X) = 4` and `Var(e) = 1`. The measurement basis and execution are assumed adequate. Individual output must lie between 8 and 12, and the process objective is at most 2% outside that interval. Under these assumptions:

| Compared condition | Output variance | Modeled fraction outside 8–12 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Current process | 2 | 15.73% |
| Reduce Var(X) from 4 to 1; retain sensitivity 0.5 | 1.25 | 7.36% |
| Reduce sensitivity from 0.5 to 0.25; retain Var(X) = 4 | 1.25 | 7.36% |
| Hold X at zero; retain the residual mechanism | 1 | 4.55% |

The alternatives leave the stated centering and residual distribution unchanged. Even ideal control of X misses the 2% objective. To meet the 2% objective, obtain another effective change, such as reducing residual variation. If the requirement itself is reconsidered, the authorized decision changes the target of the comparison. The calculation can end the search for a sufficient X-only remedy while containment continues. It does not choose among the remaining changes or establish that the assumed model describes an actual process. A measured improvement would still need its own observations and comparison.

**Return the chosen change to sustainable work.** DMAIC Control and SDCA connect the selected way to actual performance, observation and a response to departure. Make the operating method and required inputs obtainable, assign capable performers, and provide the time, means and rights needed to act. State who can contain output, restore operation, authorize restart or revise the standard. Observe whether the changed practice continues and whether the required result follows. [OPS.16](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/ops/50-ops-16---decide-whether-and-how-to-change-an-operating-metho.md) compares a reusable method change with alternatives; OPS.18 supplies containment and restart. Use the implementation connection above when the missing contribution is how the practice becomes usable.

When a departure from a standard leaves the next response unresolved, distinguish the missing contribution. Restore missing tools, access or time when these prevented performance. If the difficulty concerns an operation the person has not yet mastered, arrange an attempt, usable instruction, correction and follow-up. The [TWI Institute's account of Job Instruction](https://www.twi-institute.com/twi-job-instruction-removes-barriers/) connects preparation and demonstration with trying the job and subsequent help; [HCD.9](../../dpfs/human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/26-hcd-9---perform-deliberate-practice-with-feedback.md) supplies the feedback and retry under stated support conditions. When adequate performance under the intended conditions still fails the requirement, reconsider the way and its causal grounds. Statistical process capability and a person's ability to perform the work are different claims. Retain a useful reference, colleague or other support when it belongs to the intended work; successful assisted performance need not imply independent ability.

Maintaining a sufficient standard and its provision is an ordinary completion of this control question. A local correction can finish without launching a new DMAIC project. Reopen the affected relation when measurement, process behavior, requirements, execution or provision changes enough to alter the warranted action. A missing decision right or shared resource returns to the organizational connections above; another repetition of the local cycle cannot supply it.

##### Library.Reference:6.5.3.2 - TOC: current flow, its settings and its transformation

In the Buffer Management account of [Cox III and Lynn H. Boyd, §3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7476509/), buffer state guides a current response to a threat to flow. Recurrent states can also lead to resizing the buffer, changing conditions of later response. [Five Focusing Steps](https://www.tocinstitute.org/five-focusing-steps.html) relates the result of the whole system to its constraint: identify it, exploit its available capability, subordinate other decisions, elevate capability when needed and return to the focus when the constraint changes.

In the same Cox–Boyd account, Change Question Sequence connects undesirable effects and their causes with designing a change, involving participants, implementing it and sustaining the result. These contributions can connect as follows: current regulation reveals a problem; inquiry identifies the constraint or rule to change; implementation establishes new conditions for current work.

That is one possible arrangement for a stated problem. Membership in TOC does not impose four levels of reaction, tuning, focusing and transformation. Buffer resizing can be explained within one adaptive controller, or its management can be distinguished when its criteria, consequences and rights need separate attention. Cox–Boyd supplies one POOGI line, not the canonical structure of every TOC practice.

**Connect current response to changed conditions.** In [Cox III's PASS execution account for patient flow, §5, steps 8–10](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/20476965.2019.1646105), staff respond to buffer warnings and record the causes of threatened flow. Recurring causes inform a selected change whose effect is then monitored. Significant environmental change can require reconsidering the system through the thinking processes and focusing steps. The connection is observation → explanation → change → subsequent performance. A buffer warning supports a current response; a recorded recurrence supports investigation; neither alone establishes which condition to change. If the present response restores adequate flow and no consequential recurring cause remains unresolved, another transformation is unnecessary for that departure. If an input, allocation or rule repeatedly recreates the departure, select a change to that condition and make its implementation and subsequent assessment possible.

**Reconnect flow across organizations.** A locally improved operation can still receive unsuitable inputs or pass work into an unavailable downstream service. The controlled whole then depends on the links between participants as well as their local performance. [Cox III and Mabin's healthcare supply-chain proposal, §§4–5.3](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/20476965.2025.2460632) addresses local units and their reconnection: practices develop their own flow, then work with partners on urgency, scheduling, roles and the conditions of transfer. Owners and managers participate in choosing and adopting these arrangements. The proposed decomposition into local problems and subsequent linking of organizations (stages I and III) were not fully implemented, and the described referral route excludes emergency-department entry. The paper supplies a design and implementation approach within those limits, not evidence of a completed transformation of every link.

An effective agreement connects the receiving work to participants who can supply the needed information, capacity and action on the agreed terms. A beneficial proposal or a shared aim does not confer authority over another organization's resources. Examine the effects on each participant and the conditions under which they can agree. If their commitments remain incompatible, return to the commitments or the arrangement that could reconcile them. Local gains leave this dependency unresolved until the participants can coordinate the required work. Decomposing a system into practices, allocating authority and coordinating across practices describe different relationships; none by itself establishes a hierarchy of controllers.

**Connect operating rules, tactical settings and strategic direction.** The Demand Driven Institute's [Demand Driven Material Requirements Planning (DDMRP)](https://www.demanddriveninstitute.com/ddmrp) includes tactical adaptation; its [Demand Driven Operating Model (DDOM)](https://www.demanddriveninstitute.com/demand-driven-operating-model) connects order generation, scheduling and execution. In the [Demand Driven Adaptive Enterprise (DDAE)](https://www.demanddriveninstitute.com/demand-driven-adaptive-enterprise-m), [Demand Driven S&OP](https://www.demanddriveninstitute.com/demand-driven-s-op) uses past and projected operations together with strategic direction to revise configuration. It returns capability projections and recommendations to strategic leadership. [Adaptive S&OP](https://www.demanddriveninstitute.com/adaptive-s-op) can revise direction and business assumptions; the resulting requirements guide tactical configuration and operation. Beginning this bidirectional adaptation requires at least a partly implemented operating model, capable participants and a defined direction. These are the Institute's current methodological formulations, not comparative evidence that DDAE is preferable in every setting.

Executing a formula that updates a buffer parameter can realize an already prescribed operating rule. Assessing that rule's suitability is a further relationship: use outcomes and a criterion to choose whether to retain or revise the rule, make an authorized change effective when needed, and assess later performance. The same participant can supply both relationships. Explain the additional relationship when it matters to the decision; the names operational, tactical and strategic do not establish three organizational ranks. DDAE is a related synthesis with its own conditions, not a definition of every POOGI practice.

**Let causal and dynamic accounts revise each other.** [Mabin and Cavana's combined TOC/qualitative system dynamics framework](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/sdr.1768) connects stakeholder concerns and behaviour over time with causal-loop modelling, TOC investigation of effects, assumptions and conflicts, and revision of the proposed intervention. Insights return to the dynamic account; goal and causal accounts can also be revised as understanding develops. Select this inquiry when feedback, delayed effects or interactions between participants could change the proposed intervention. A familiar adequately explained operating choice need not carry the whole investigation.

Use necessary-condition reasoning to identify prerequisites and sufficient-cause reasoning to examine how conditions together would produce an effect. Dynamic inquiry examines feedback and delays over time. The qualitative TOC trees and signed feedback links do not by themselves supply estimated effect sizes, delays or an established future trajectory. The paper's revised behaviour-over-time curves are hypothetical, and implementation and organizational learning were not demonstrated in its teaching exercise. Use the combined account to generate and examine a proposed change; obtain observations of implementation and effects before claiming that the change worked.

##### Library.Reference:6.5.3.3 - Boyd: orientation and coordinated initiative

In Boyd's original OODA, orientation shapes what a participant notices and how the participant acts. Implicit guidance permits action without a separate explicit decision at every step. New information, consequences of action, analysis and synthesis can change orientation. An explicit decision can be a hypothesis and action its test. Recover these relationships from the full diagram and explanation rather than a four-arrow mnemonic.

The organizational account connects common understanding and intent with participants' initiative. They choose actions in local conditions; information about what happens can refine direction and interaction. In *Organic Design*, Boyd develops his own distinction between command/control and appreciation/leadership. Understanding and assessment connect to directing joint activity, with observation conducted in a way that does not obstruct operations.

FPF's general control account can explain these functional relationships while preserving Boyd's particular meanings of control and leadership. Their correspondence does not establish a common organizational hierarchy for every OODA use. The basis is the [original briefings in Air University's *A Discourse on Winning and Losing*](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo94257/pdf/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo94257.pdf), *Organic Design* at printed pages 218–253, especially 248–253, and the complete *Essence of Winning and Losing*, including the implicit connections.

**Distinguish freedom in execution from responsibility for an operation.** A participant may choose how to carry out a task while the purpose, shared allocations and decisions about other participants remain elsewhere. Entrusting an operation adds decisions about how its contributions will be planned, made available, performed and assessed. Recover which of those decisions the recipient may actually make and which commitments still require another owner. This applies the goal, mandate and exchange distinctions in [C.32.LCA:4.2–4.8](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/75-c-32-lca---construct-and-compare-a-control-architecture.md).

The US Air Force's [AFDP 3-0.1, *Command and Control*, 22 January 2025, pp. 4–12](https://www.doctrine.af.mil/Portals/61/documents/AFDP_3-0_1/AFDP3-0.1CommandandControl.pdf) develops a current doctrinal distinction alongside its decision cycle: decentralized execution permits initiative within intent, while distributed control delegates authority to conduct the planning, preparation, execution and assessment of assigned operations. The receiving organization needs the relevant capability and capacity. Distribution depends on the operation and communication conditions; centralized control remains a conditional alternative. Its military authority structure is particular to that source.

For a project, construct the actual allocation through C.32.LCA. A service team allowed to reorder its jobs may still need the service owner's decision to change a promised result or obtain another team's capacity. A wider mandate is useful only if the team can obtain the information, means and cooperation required to use it. Establish any needed reservation or agreement before relying on it. When communication may fail, make the permitted continuation and the observation that ends it usable in advance; on return, reconcile changed commitments and pending actions. If one existing decision point already has the needed information, rights and timely capacity, retain that arrangement. A missing input, unavailable receiver or conflicting commitment selects the relation to repair; another OODA repetition alone cannot supply it.

##### Library.Reference:6.5.3.4 - Kata: direction, learner development and help for the coach

Rother's Improvement Kata connects a desired direction with a current process, a near target condition and successive observations or experiments. Use [E.23:4.4.1](../../sections/11-part-e---the-fpf-constitution-and-authoring-guides/63-e-23---quality-improvement-loop-method.md#e23441---improve-a-work-process-through-target-conditions-and-experiments) for the process-improvement way: it explains how to derive the next target from actual work, distinguish an obstacle from a preferred solution, choose among observation, exploratory change and hypothesis testing, and use the result to determine the next move. Its contract-entry case follows that reasoning through a changed condition. A target condition specifies a desired operating state and outcome within a period; it is not a list of the solutions presumed necessary to reach it.

The learner's ability to perform that reasoning is a second subject. [HCD.9:4.10](../../dpfs/human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/26-hcd-9---perform-deliberate-practice-with-feedback.md#hcd9410---coach-scientific-reasoning-during-process-improvement) supplies coached practice of the particular actions: expose the learner's reasoning, locate a missing or unsupported connection, provide usable help and inspect the learner's response. The second coach applies the same attempt-and-feedback discipline to the coach's actual conversation. An improved process, a corrected learner inference and a better coaching action therefore have distinct grounds, although the same episode can supply evidence for each.

A prepared practitioner can use the process-improvement way without adding a novice coaching programme. When development of the reasoning is intended, select a manageable process and enough coaching for the learner's current difficulty. A conversation can finish when a useful next step and expected learning are clear, including a return to observation.

Starter routines give learners a way to practise scientific thinking and novice coaches a way to learn how to observe and guide it. Use the prescribed routine while acquiring the thinking or coaching pattern. Once the participant has internalized that pattern, they can retain a useful routine or adapt its form when doing so better supports the work. Preserve the learner's prediction, observation of what happened and use of the difference in the next step.

Choose the next practice episode from the learner's actual attempt, the help received and the conditions in which the ability will be needed. [HCD.9 and HCD.10](../../dpfs/human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/_index.md) supply this connection: HCD.9 makes the attempt and use of feedback observable; HCD.10 helps choose whether to keep the present practice or change the task or support, and which observation is needed to retain, revise or reverse that choice. Changing task conditions or help can preserve the same starter routine. Keep or restore support when the task needs it; later work may legitimately include references, tools or a coach. A harder subject problem may require additional knowledge, means or authority. When the person can already perform the required work with its permitted support, use that arrangement and close the bounded practice question.

Organizational deployment adds another relationship: a target condition at one organizational level becomes direction for the next. A manager can be a learner in relation to one coach and a coach to others. Rother usually combines management and coaching roles, while allowing exceptions; the second coach can be a peer or staff specialist. These relationships therefore cannot be recovered from a single ladder of job titles. The distinction between large and small PDCA concerns progress toward a target condition and individual experiments, a different relationship again.

**Coordinate experiments that depend on other participants.** In Rother and Aulinger's *Toyota Kata Culture* (2017), chapter 4, short group coaching cycles expose obstacles at interfaces while individual coaching develops each learner's way of thinking. The group examines target and actual conditions, the obstacle being addressed, the next step and its expected result, and when that result can be observed. Reflection on the preceding experiment uses its expectation and actual result. Participants can affect one another's work without reporting to one another.

A learner can request help, the coach can notice the need, or another team can report a problem. Determine what contribution is missing. An organizational obstacle may require another person's resource, cooperation or decision; an unresolved technical cause may require subject help or further observation. Examine the next experiment's consequences for other work, then involve the people who can supply the contribution or whose work it changes. Use direct contact where it is available; a coach can mediate a connection that the learner cannot make.

In the book's constructed case, Roger needs to stop machining for changeover experiments toward a 54-minute target condition. He needs four hours of buffer stock before stopping. Steve involves Thomas because Roger cannot directly obtain that contribution in the depicted arrangement. Asked what prevents producing the stock, Thomas explains that the necessary raw material is about to arrive and expects the buffer to be built by the end of the week. The dialogue identifies a prerequisite and the provider's own dependency. In applying this arrangement, establish that the stock and timing actually fit before relying on the stoppage. [LDR.1](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/ldr/_index.md) supplies the working conversation; LDR.2 supplies agreement when contributions or commitments need reconciling.

A different case starts when Steve notices Robert's difficulty. Robert reports a delay every third machine cycle and unsatisfactory results from root-cause analysis. Steve proposes visiting the process together after the group cycle to choose the next step from what they observe.

The illustrated summary board distinguishes a missing coaching-cycle card from a red card indicating delay or a need for help. Ask what prevented the work and whether the pair can handle it locally. The arrangement permits more frequent coaching at the affected point and further help when that is insufficient. Its schedule and display can vary with the work. Follow the selected contribution into the experiment and return its consequences to the target and other participants' work.

**Carry the experiment's facts into the wider dialogue.** Chapter 3 shows Nancy's difficulty in relying on Steve's recollection of his learners' storyboards. Visiting the processes and boards can provide the basis. Where that is impractical, the book illustrates summary cards prepared immediately after individual coaching cycles from the learners' experimenting records. They carry target and actual conditions, learning, the obstacle addressed, and the next step with its expectation. Another means of carrying those facts can serve the same purpose.

Nancy first examines the relevant learners' experiments, then Steve's own obstacle and experiment. Steve has been trying to make production time available for Roger's experiments and learns that the scheduler supplies the best information about suitable times.

**Develop the ability to sustain the arrangement.** A second coach observes a coaching cycle and gives focused feedback for the next attempt; [HCD.9:4.10](../../dpfs/human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/26-hcd-9---perform-deliberate-practice-with-feedback.md#hcd9410---coach-scientific-reasoning-during-process-improvement) explains this practice. The book's Conclusion applies Improvement Kata to deployment itself: examine the actual practice, identify the next obstacle to an expansion that can be supported, try a change and use its result. Choose the scope of expansion from the coaching capability that can actually be provided.

**Begin when experienced internal coaches are absent.** The *Practice Guide* (2018), chapter 3, gives two ways to start. An experienced external coach can first coach managers as Improvement Kata learners; they subsequently learn to coach others. Alternatively, three participants can practise through temporary rotation of learner, coach and observing second-coach roles. With separate focus processes, each person works as learner on their own process and as coach and observer for the others; the group performs all three rotations each day. With one shared process, the participants rotate roles periodically; the book illustrates a change after ten coaching cycles. These arrangements require different process access and amounts of shared practice time. Choose the arrangement whose actual practice can be sustained.

At the start the coaching roles may exist in name only. Use the observed interactions and feedback in HCD.9:4.10 to develop and assess what each participant can perform; obtain qualified help where the group cannot supply the judgement it needs. The book presents rotation as a temporary way to acquire experience, typically over six to eight weeks of practice. That illustrative period can guide preparation; actual capability determines which coaching work participants can undertake.

[Aulinger's *Coaching-Lerngruppen*, version 3.0, 2026](https://www.verbesserungskata.de/app/download/5790421637/Aufbau%2Beiner%2BKata-Organisation%2B3.0.pdf), printed pp. 7–17, proposes one way to prepare coaches: four participants rotate through learner, coach and observing second-coach roles, then give feedback on the coaching. Preserve each person's coaching attempt and count the time of participants and helpers, including preparation and correction. Use [HCD.7](../../dpfs/human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/24-hcd-7---arrange-providers-access-tools-and-ai-support-for-hu.md) to establish who can provide the required judgement and support. If a conversation stalls, use [HCD.26](../../dpfs/human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/43-hcd-26---regulate-shared-learning.md) to identify the missing contribution, choose a feasible intervention and inspect its use. An unavailable instrument or decision right returns to its provider or owner. When a prepared coach and supported individual practice supply the required development, retain them. Attendance, role rotation and conversation counts answer different questions from demonstrated coaching ability.

For the direction, deployment and coaching relationships, the basis is the [*Improvement Kata Handbook*, v32.0](https://community.obeya-association.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2024/08/Toyota-Kata_Full_IK_Handbook_v32.0_compressed.pdf), PDF pages 129–148, the diagram at 135, and pages 479–484.

##### Library.Reference:6.5.3.5 - Learning through work and changing collective activity

Learning approaches differ in what they develop and how that development reaches action. Begin with the contribution needed in subsequent work. A person may need a new distinction; practitioners may need an intelligible shared way; a group may need to investigate a problem it can act on; or the conditions of participation may prevent useful inquiry. Connect the selected development to that work, including the person who can use its result. One undertaking can require several of these contributions.

**Develop a person's use of experience.** [Kolb and Kolb's 2018 clarification](https://learningfromexperience.com/downloads/research-library/eight-important-things-to-know-about-the-experiential-learning-cycle.pdf) connects experience, reflection, conceptualization and experimentation while allowing different entry points and sequences. Facilitation, subject explanation, standards and coaching serve different learning needs. Select their contributions from the learner, subject and intended performance. A written plan for future experimentation can support reflection; its subsequent execution needs a later observation.

[HCD.15:4.2.1](../../dpfs/human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/_index.md) supplies a useful start from successful work: recover the action and help, compare the person's explanation with a serious alternative, and retain a bounded lesson or select the next practice. HCD.9 connects feedback to another attempt; HCD.12 distinguishes use under a meaningful change from learning with a newly supplied source. Keep a sufficient explanation and action when no worthwhile learning question remains.

**Develop a question participants can investigate together.** [Senge, Hamilton and Kania](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/the_dawn_of_system_leadership) connect seeing wider relationships, examining assumptions through conversation and forming a shared direction. Their methodological account uses mapping, encounters with other practice settings and inquiry to develop these capacities. Select a tool for the relationship it helps participants examine and for the action that understanding can change.

[CB.4](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/cb/_index.md) supplies preparation, access, facilitation and the return from a shared encounter. Where participants need to learn the reasoning, HCD.23 has them explain a consequential connection, compare its grounds and use it themselves. Where the result needed is a joint proposal, §6.5.3.8 returns its unresolved contributions and commitments to LDR and GOALS. An adequate existing answer can instead finish the inquiry.

**Make practical knowledge usable beyond its first holder.** The [Nonaka–Toyama–Konno account of SECI and ba](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781847876621_A23446119/preview-9781847876621_A23446119.pdf) relates shared experience, articulation, combination of explicit accounts and learning through use. In *ba*, the shared context, participants interpret contributions and act together. Its individual, group and organizational scales describe expansion of knowledge creation. Control levels still require the relationships in §6.5.2. [Nonaka and Takeuchi's later treatment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9753931/) keeps knowledge creation connected to practical action.

Choose what is missing in the receiving practice. Demonstration and joint work can expose a distinction that an instruction leaves implicit. An explanation can make it available to an absent practitioner; subsequent use can expose a missing condition or a defective way. CB.14 develops this connection from contrasting cases through a qualified method contribution, trial, receiving use and maintenance. It first returns a sufficient existing answer. An account's availability and a person's ability to use it require different observations. KCS in §6.5.3.7 develops the corresponding connections within service work.

**Join inquiry to a real action and a learning return.** [WIAL's action-learning account](https://wial.org/action-learning/) starts with a significant problem for which a group is responsible and able to act; a coach helps it learn through inquiry and action. The [INTRAC set guide](https://www.intrac.org/app/uploads/2016/09/Action-Learning-Sets-An-INTRAC-guide-1.pdf) also supports successive individual cases: questions help the owner choose an action within their influence, and later meetings return what happened. Choose the variant from the actual problem owner and capacity to act.

Recover the problem with the people who have relevant experience and examine the assumptions that could change the choice. The responsible owner or group selects an action, obtains its necessary permission and means, and returns consequences to the unresolved question. CB.5 supplies a case owner's reasoned continuation and the response to contributed help. A useful conversation can finish at a clarified question when action is not yet obtainable; its learning claim must retain that limit. Use the selected source protocol where its particular question, advice and participation rules matter.

The participants can also improve the encounter itself. [Cauwelier's 2023 WIAL guidance](https://wial.org/wp-content/uploads/STOP-REMINDING-ABOUT-GROUND-RULE-1-1.pdf) moves from repeated reminders about questioning to the group's examination of why and how it communicates. This can change facilitation or a shared practice rule. The next encounter supplies an opportunity to observe that change. Reconsidering the premises that govern work is a further possible subject:

In [Argyris's *Double Loop Learning in Organizations*](https://6-30partners.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/Chris-Argyris-Double-Loop-Learning-in-Organisations.pdf), participants can correct action while retaining its governing premises, or investigate premises that reproduce the problem. The latter move requires discovering norms actually used, making disputed assumptions testable in joint inquiry and changing subsequent action. A declared value can differ from the value governing decisions. Defensive interaction can prevent the inquiry from changing what happens. The result must return to the relationship between the situation and action. Single- and double-loop learning distinguish what is reconsidered and made effective; they do not establish two jobs.

In expansive learning, the object of collective activity and the way of working together can change. Participants encounter contradictions, examine the existing arrangement, model and test a new one, change practice and examine consequences. Tools, rules, division of labour and interaction with neighbouring activity can change together. The aim of the transformation can itself emerge in the work.

[Engeström and Sannino, 2010, pages 6–8](https://mickhammond.blog/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/expansive-learning-.pdf) identify the ideal cycle as a conceptual heuristic. It helps investigate formation of the object and transformation of practice. A claim about a particular controller still requires showing who selects and implements which changes in that realization. The source's action/activity distinction supplies levels of analysis; it does not establish a universal seven-stage control prescription.

**Let joint inquiry change the problem being investigated.** In [Restrepo and colleagues' collaborative-learning account](https://km4djournal.org/index.php/km4dj/article/download/247/316/691), the participants, their criteria and the working question develop together. The intended users' different practices can expose a missing condition in the researcher's initial problem. Dialogue informs discovery through observation, trials or scenarios; application then tests the proposed way in the participants' circumstances and can reveal missing skills or poor fit. These relationships can recur as the collaboration develops.

[CB.14:4.2 and :5.5](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/cb/24-cb-14---develop-and-maintain-a-shared-repertoire-of-practice.md#cb1442---use-differences-to-find-a-useful-exchange) explain this contribution within joint practice development. The farming case changes a comparison of feeding practices after recovering differences in available labour and fodder. It follows that change through the professional study contribution, receiving guidance and a grant condition that can prevent the revised question from being pursued. CB.13 supplies the shared decision arrangement; OCE.14 supplies revision of an established organizational relation where that is the missing contribution.

The authors' [Kenyan dairy-project explanation](https://www.ditsl.org/images/articles/research/ditsl_m_fostering_change_through_collaborative_learning.pdf), p.2, describes farmers with defined roles in decisions, comparison of how they regulate milk production and quality, video proposals for action funds, and peer exchange and experiments. That realization shows how a question participants can shape reaches a trial they can perform.


[Romme and van Witteloostuijn's Endenburg case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236028159_Circular_organizing_and_triple_loop_learning) describes policy decisions linked across circles by a leader and elected representative, with information access and ordinary execution retained. Selecting this arrangement elsewhere requires comparison with the actual decision work and participation conditions. [Johannessen and colleagues' urban-water cases](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/eet.1843) connect learning with trusted exchange, capable intermediaries and institutional support, while reporting incomplete or temporary wider change. A local learning result therefore needs its own connection to any claimed continuing organizational result.

The term *triple-loop learning* requires a source-specific reading. [Tosey, Visser and Saunders](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258171998_The_origins_and_conceptualizations_of_%27triple-loop%27_learning_A_critical_review) distinguish accounts concerned with higher governing purposes, reflection on how learning occurs, and Bateson's more radical change of learning premises. These have different objects and proposed relations. Identify the proposed change and its means; the label supplies neither an implementation nor a universal third control level.

For example, two teams may already solve their respective cases but lack an account that lets a receiving team recognize a consequential difference. CB.14 can obtain that account and try its use. If a practitioner can recover the account but cannot yet apply the distinction, select supported practice. If the teams understand the distinction but cannot obtain permission for the required action, take the permission question to the person entitled to decide it. The same eventual work result can depend on different repairs. An available explanation or direct authorized response may suffice; a recurring learning group is useful only when its continuing contribution warrants the work.

##### Library.Reference:6.5.3.6 - AI: action, retained experience and model training

In [ReAct](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.03629v3), an observation returned by external action informs further reasoning and choice in the task. In [Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11366v4), evaluation of an attempt and reflection produce textual memory for later attempts. Retention matters to future behaviour, but recording context does not alone establish another control level. For a question about organizing adaptation, explain how retained conclusions are selected, used and checked. For a question about answering a request, one controller with memory may be an adequate description. Actor, evaluator and reflector name functional contributions, not necessarily distinct physical participants or levels.

In [SCoRe, 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/871ac99fdc5282d0301934d23945ebaa-Paper-Conference.pdf), training changes model parameters to improve subsequent self-correction. This connects training work to later performance. A correct answer available during training is not thereby available during correction at test time. Preserve that difference when deciding what checking means the operating arrangement can use.

These papers support particular mechanisms and bounded experimental conclusions. E.23:11 explains further agent and harness mechanisms and their evidence limits. Relative advantage, transfer to another task and coverage of current AI practice require the corresponding comparison.

**Choose what must change in the human–AI work.** Start from the result its recipient needs and follow the actual attempt: what information reached the performer, what was chosen, what was executed, what returned and how that return changed the continuation. A compiler diagnostic, a current-state observation, an expert judgement and a preference supply different grounds. Relate each to the question it can settle. A proposed call needs an available interface and authority; its reported success needs the evidence required by the receiving use. Use [SYSE.42](../../dpfs/engineering-dpf-suite/syse/63-syse-42---use-a-selected-tool-and-apply-its-result.md) to bind and perform the operation and apply its qualified return.

Use the trace to select what needs to change: the current answer, an input, retained experience, the procedure or assistance rule, model parameters, a person's ability, or the provision of work. These are possible changed objects, not phases which every attempt must traverse. Explain how the chosen change will reach a later action and which observation would justify retaining it. Keep three decisions separate: ending the current search, accepting the current result and retaining a way for future use. A precise report of a missing fact can justify stopping without completing the requested intervention; a correct result on this occasion can suffice without developing a reusable procedure.

**Retained experience needs both content and a way to use it.** [ACE v3, 2026, §§3 and 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618v3) develops context through generator attempts, reflection and a curator's incremental updates to identifiable entries. Later attempts consume the retained guidance; entries can be revised or removed. Its limits include dependence on feedback and reflection quality, and cases where a simple reusable rule is enough. [Agentic Experience Learning, EMNLP 2026, §3](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1316/) instead distinguishes records of episodes from policies for retrieving experience: a fast selection process updates its evidence about available policies, while a slower synthesis can propose new retrieval policies. Its reported financial comparison freezes learning before final evaluation. That experiment therefore does not establish the behavior of indefinitely changing deployed memory.

For the receiving work, determine whether the needed record was preserved, retrieved and used. [SYSE.43](../../dpfs/engineering-dpf-suite/syse/64-syse-43---maintain-external-memory-for-continuing-work.md) develops that connection. If a relevant return was dropped from the next input, restore it; if the procedure ignores an available result, change the consuming operation. Adding records or another reflector would address a different object. A fixed rule can update memory or select an action without another controller. When a participant selects and changes the rule itself, recover that participant's observations, criterion, authority and subsequent check before describing the additional control relation.

**A changed instruction must change performed work.** [GEPA v2, 2026, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457v2) executes candidate prompts within a retained program, uses traces and task feedback to propose revisions, and compares candidates on their actual execution. This changes prompts while the underlying model weights stay fixed. Its candidate pool preserves alternatives useful on different cases; the data used to choose among them serve selection, while separate test data support a further-use claim. Apply [C.40:4.7](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/105-c-40---develop-branching-search-from-reusable-material.md) when choosing what to retain from such search. A directly understood procedure repair can be sufficient; a prompt search is useful when its exploration and comparison supply a needed contribution.

**Model training changes another part of the arrangement.** [TSRL, EMNLP 2026, §4](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.19/) combines reinforcement learning with expert guidance selected for difficult states and changes the weight of that supervision during training. The returned product is an updated policy, whose later performance still needs checking. To construct such a change, [SYSE.45](../../dpfs/engineering-dpf-suite/syse/66-syse-45---train-an-llm-policy-from-qualified-interaction-exp.md) connects qualified interaction experience to the selected parameter update and tests new behavior alongside earlier useful behavior. Changing facts, execution access and permissions remain runtime contributions wherever the task needs them. Without appropriate training access or a qualified target, select another available change or return that missing condition.

**Choose the human contribution by its result.** A person can supply a fact unavailable to the model, clarify an intention, challenge an inference, make an authorized commitment, perform an action, or learn to judge and act. Recover the contribution needed now and the conditions for supplying it. Subject competence and authority to change a promise are different grounds. An approval request is useful only when its recipient can make the required decision; explanation or a corrected factual premise can be the missing contribution instead. [Library.Reference:9.3](10-library-reference-9---open-ended-development-belongs-across.md#libraryreference93---acquiring-sustaining-and-changing-a-shared-humanai-professional-culture) connects supported performance, development and joint work.

When the intended result includes a person's ability, preserve that person's needed judgement or action and observe it under the intended help. HCD.7 and HCD.9–HCD.13 supply the support, practice and evidence choices. [Shen and Tamkin's 2026 study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245v2) makes the distinction consequential: in its short experiment with programmers learning an unfamiliar Python library, the group offered AI assistance scored lower on the subsequent unaided quiz. The main time comparison was inconclusive, and the study did not measure long-term retention. Its exploratory interaction patterns do not establish one generally superior teaching method. A good joint artifact and the learner's acquired contribution therefore remain separate questions.

**Reconsider the surrounding arrangement when its conditions change.** A new model, tool or workload can make an old support unnecessary or reveal a missing one. [Anthropic's March 2026 engineering account](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps) describes removing some resets and subdivisions as models changed while retaining useful evaluation. Treat it as an experienced design account, not a controlled comparison proving one agent organization best. Use [SYSE.46](../../dpfs/engineering-dpf-suite/syse/67-syse-46---test-an-agent-and-its-support-in-representative-wo.md) to compare the actual arrangements at the intended reliance, including their needed checks and full burden.

[Engineering.Reference:19.7](../../dpfs/engineering-dpf-suite/suite-reference/21-engineering-reference-19---make-an-available-contribution-wo.md#engineeringreference197---develop-the-arrangement-across-attempts) develops the connected choice from a failed attempt to a supported repair, construction and later use. It distinguishes a missing premise from a defective help decision, a lost input and an ignored return, and compares fixed-model changes with training. Use that construction when the trace leaves the intervention unresolved. If the current method and its available support already obtain the required result, complete the work with them. Select further development for a specified improvement whose expected value warrants its burden.

##### Library.Reference:6.5.3.8 - Hoshin/catchball: develop direction through feasible contributions

Use this connection when direction, local proposals and shared support do not yet form an undertaking the participants can carry out. A common aim can leave departments planning incompatible contributions. A locally sensible target can also misrepresent the result wanted by the recipient. Hoshin connects direction with investigation of the work, proposals in return, joint execution and reconsideration. The [Liker–Shook account](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/hoshin-kanri-as-a-foundational-piece-of-a-lean-management-system/) relates this construction to horizontal as well as vertical dependence and to the development of problem-solving capability.

**Develop the proposal in both directions.** Start from the question that is actually open. An existing direction can be adequate; a shared concern may instead need a worthwhile direction to be constructed. A participant investigates the present work and proposes a contribution, explaining why the way should help, what they can undertake and what they need from others. The return can reveal an unsuitable local measure, an unsupported causal assumption or unavailable provision. Those imply different changes: refine the local interpretation, investigate or replace the means, obtain support, or reconsider the aim through its owner. [Graban's account of catchball](https://www.leanblog.org/2026/03/strategy-deployment-catch-ball-chuck-rock/) makes adjustment in both directions explicit. The proposal can inform a decision before it warrants a promise.

Bring the proposal to the people whose contributions it needs or whose work it displaces. Keep the dependencies between departments explicit: whose specialist time, permission or result must be available to whom and when. Compare the whole demand and timing, obtain the relevant decisions, and return changes to the affected participants. Some can contribute knowledge while another holds the authority to change a goal, commit resources or accept revised terms. [Reich's account of getting started](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/getting-started-with-hoshin-kanri/) makes leadership participation and differing stakeholder powers consequential conditions. Select an attainable organizational scope with the required participation; introducing a form cannot confer the missing mandate.

**Choose the form of exchange for the result it must supply.** A group can examine a prepared plan, expose dependencies and improve it. When the participant must also become able to construct such a plan, provide for their own substantive attempt: they investigate the work, formulate the proposal and use a capable coach's response to improve their reasoning. [Howell's comparison](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/expanding-the-purpose-of-catchball%EF%BF%BC/) explains this distinction between group revision and the problem owner's development. The latter needs practice time and suitable help. A better group plan does not by itself establish each participant's ability, and a management title does not supply coaching competence.

When relevant knowledge is widely dispersed, individual proposals followed by collective examination can make contributions available that a small planning group cannot supply. In the historical [Tennant–Roberts Rover study](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0024-6301(01)00039-5), interviews, a Delphi survey and workshops contributed to a revised milestone proposal before executive approval. Survey estimates informed discussion; they did not determine binding dates. The authors report dropping the Delphi stage in later iterations once shared understanding was available. Select the more involved exchange when obtaining the missing contributions warrants its cost. Use an adequate ordinary conversation or existing agreement directly when it already supplies the needed result.

**Use execution results to reconsider the undertaking.** Once work begins, compare what happened with the proposal's grounds and with the commitments still in force. A stalled contribution may need a different method, support, sequence or agreement; a changed recipient need may require revising the goal. Obtain the decision that makes the selected change effective. In [Reich's interview account](https://www.leanblog.org/2025/06/hoshin-kanri-managing-on-purpose-mark-reich/), review examines actual work and missing support, while the organization can also revise how it develops direction, gathers proposals and reviews them. Choose the review interval so that needed operating responses can occur in time. The subject of revision determines the relationship; annual and monthly meetings do not by themselves establish two control levels.

Hoshin can supply direction and provision for Improvement Kata; experiments and acquired capability can inform later proposals. [Rother's clarification](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mike-rother-472900297_this-question-comes-up-from-time-to-time-activity-7374093405125087232-8c7X) calls for revisiting the challenge at the end of each target-condition cycle, using what has been learned. It does not justify changing direction merely because a step is difficult. Keep clear which target condition and experiment the returned results concern, and obtain the authority needed to change any affected commitment. Either practice can be sufficient without the other when the other difficulty is absent.

For the common operations, [GOALS.1, GOALS.3 and GOALS.4](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/goals/_index.md) supply goal meaning, joint feasibility and warranted revision. For a learning question, locate the result that later work needs. A bounded lesson from an existing episode may suffice. If a person must acquire the operation, arrange their attempt, help and later use. If different practitioners need a reusable way, connect their experience to its development and receiving practice. If participation or authority prevents that work, change the relevant condition through its owner. Preserve current service and useful learning while obtaining the missing contribution.

Reuse observations when their subjects, periods and conditions fit each use. A transfer that another organization cannot perform calls for a feasible agreement or a revised commitment. Reconsider a setting rule or direction when its consequences no longer fit the required result. #### Library.Reference:6.5.5 - A flow problem becomes a commitment problem

This constructed case shows how a changed dependency changes the useful move. A print shop must complete four accepted orders in a shift. Each needs one hour of finishing, and the finishing station has four available hours. Assume no other limiting operation or setup time; materials can be prepared beforehand. Traced episodes show the station losing time because kits arrive unready.

The required result is ready work reaching the operation that limits output. Five Focusing Steps supports focusing there. [OPS.9](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/ops/_index.md) uses sufficient observations already available and compares a serious alternative only when it remains live and could change the treatment. Once the grounds support a preparation or release change, the team can make it within its existing authority.

The team predicts that complete kits prepared before scheduled release will remove the observed starvation while preserving quality and available time. It tests a bounded change on suitable orders, observing release, waiting, completion and unexpected effects. [OPS.16](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/ops/_index.md) permits direct use of an adequate existing PDSA record.

Suppose two prepared orders finish in two hours without the former wait. The team retains the rule for that order type and tested conditions. Applying it more widely needs the corresponding provision; this small test does not establish the effect for a different order mix or unavailable preparation.

The selected methods guide focusing and studying the change. Workers prepare kits, the dispatcher decides release and the operator performs finishing. Effective agreements give the dispatcher readiness information and authority over release. An architecture account can represent these relationships and their timing conditions. Observations of performance establish what actually happened.

Now sales accepts two more orders for the same deadline. The dispatcher cannot change promises or purchase outside finishing. Six finishing hours are required within four available hours. Under the stated assumptions, capacity establishes the shortfall.

Return to joint commitments and authority. [OPS.19](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/ops/_index.md) compares permissible continuations: renegotiating a deadline, obtaining qualified capacity or reducing promised scope. Suppose an already authorized provider offers two hours of suitable finishing and can return the orders before the deadline at known cost and conditions. The entitled owner can select four internal hours and two external hours. Until that decision is effective, the dispatcher can continue permissible work and return the unsupported additional promise.

Use C.32.LCA if future work requires changed information about new commitments, authority to agree them or a provider relationship. A direct agreement among existing participants may suffice. The kit-preparation rule remains useful: the changed premise is the promised total output and its provision.

The general distinction is between changing a local release policy under available conditions and reconciling commitments with resources when load changes. The arithmetic makes one condition inspectable; the required relationship explains the return.

#### Library.Reference:6.5.6 - A useful AI answer can leave a learning goal unmet

Consider a team that wants both useful process-change decisions and development of an employee's ability to formulate a testable expectation, choose a small informative experiment and learn from a discrepancy. Assume a qualified practice already supplies the criteria for permissible experiments.

Improvement Kata connects direction, current and target conditions and experiments. Coaching Kata guides help with the learner's performance. They are useful candidates where this difficulty and qualified help are present.

The employee proposes an expectation and experiment. A helper examines the distinction the employee used, supplies relevant feedback and leaves a substantive next attempt to the employee. Operational work can use the result now. [HCD.9 in Human Capability Development](../../dpfs/human-capability-engineering-dpf-suite/hcd/_index.md) returns what the person actually performed and with what help; HCD.12 examines transfer when later work differs materially. C.40.JD connects development of the method, performer and provision. A useful experiment alone does not establish all these results.

Now an available AI can formulate the expectation, design a permissible experiment and prepare a good report. Delegation can improve immediate performance. If the person's ability remains a required result, delegating every relevant operation removes the action through which it was to be developed and observed. A higher report score does not settle that question.

Change the allocation. In a suitable safe case, the person first formulates an expectation, then uses a qualified comparison and corrects the relevant step. AI can supply that comparison where its capability and conditions are adequate; a human checker is not automatically required. The person and helper need enough time for the selected work. If provision is unavailable, preserve the obtainable service result and keep the learning promise conditional or return it to its owner.

If independent human ability is no longer required and its goal has been legitimately removed, full delegation to AI can be appropriate. If the person must later act without AI, evidence must concern that future condition. The goal and recipient of the result change the arrangement.

[GOALS.4 and GOALS.Reference:2.1](../../dpfs/management-dpf-suite/goals/_index.md) supply reconsideration of goals and the service/learning distinction; C.40.JD supplies their connection to development. Unlike the print-shop case, obtaining an external contribution may leave one of two required results unobtained. Both cases use the same general question: which relationship must hold for the required result, and what changed in it?
