## Library.Reference:5 - Why some DPFs stand outside the Suites

A standalone DPF is an independently usable domain language whose own subject and useful results warrant its boundary, without a demonstrated constituent relation to one of the described assemblies in this Library edition.

The ten standalone languages have the following distinct contributions:

| DPF | Contribution that retains its own boundary |
| --- | --- |
| [Decision Design (PSDA)](../../dpfs/psda/_index.md) | Constructing a decision situation, ends and possible means, alternatives, consequence and uncertainty accounts and warranted recommendations, together with the engagement, participation and facilitation that make them usable. |
| [Research Method Practice (RMP)](../../dpfs/rmp/_index.md) | Inquiry and criticized knowledge contributions, including source recovery, observation, research design and evidence limits. |
| [Explanation Design (EXD)](../../dpfs/exd/_index.md) | Designing explanations of warranted connections and assessing an existing explanation. Examine what grounds the connection and what the intended recipient could recover and use under stated conditions. Evidence of the recipient’s work can establish what they actually recovered. |
| [Narrativization and Narrative Studies (NSTD)](../../dpfs/nstd/_index.md) | Constructing and interpreting narratives: forming working accounts and fictional or hypothetical events, choosing purpose, viewpoint and presentation, and examining their use with recoverable source relations. |
| [Implementation Facilitation (IS)](../../dpfs/is/_index.md) | Agreeing, constructing, adapting and continuing facilitation through which participants make an interpretable practice workable under actual conditions, including brokerage and their further facilitation capability. |
| [Financial Domain Modeling (FDM)](../../dpfs/fdm/_index.md) | Financial parties, positions, rights, obligations, events and flows with their domain meanings. |
| [Economic Reasoning and Coordination (ECO)](../../dpfs/eco/_index.md) | Scarcity, incentives, exchange, commitments, institutions and coordination under economic conditions. |
| [Defense and Transmission Modeling (DTM)](../../dpfs/dtm/_index.md) | How practices persist and spread, how they help or impair work, and how protective action changes those outcomes. |
| [Music and Dance Practice Engineering (MDPE)](../../dpfs/mdpe/_index.md) | Musical and dance creation, performance, learning, transmission and cultural continuation, including engineered performing configurations and changes to their supporting environment. |
| [Embodied Rhythmics (RHY)](../../dpfs/rhy/_index.md) | Performed rhythmic relations, their representation, transformation, coordination, practice and observation. |

Use Economic Reasoning and Financial Domain Modeling together when a decision depends on both the use of a scarce resource and a financial right, obligation or position. Work back from the conclusion or action its recipient needs. The people who use a resource, the parties to a contract and the members of a reporting group can form different sets. To choose the boundaries of the model, hold the aggregate fixed and vary which party can decide, which claims can be exercised together or how the result is distributed. Keep the differences that change the required answer or its grounds. A direct rule or an available account can already settle the question.

For example, a group saving can coexist with a member who cannot make a payment by the due date. [ECO.1](../../dpfs/eco/11-eco-1---recover-the-basis-for-an-economic-calculation.md#eco142---connect-the-comparison-to-feasible-exchange-and-use) or [ECO.7](../../dpfs/eco/17-eco-7---coordinate-use-and-renewal-of-a-shared-resource.md#eco743---test-ordinary-use-and-changed-conditions) can establish which use would be displaced and what supply can reach the proposed use. [FDM.2](../../dpfs/fdm/10-fdm-2---choose-party-and-group-boundaries-for-financial-mode.md#fdm244---build-the-warranted-aggregate) or [FDM.4](../../dpfs/fdm/12-fdm-4---establish-what-a-financial-action-changed.md#fdm444---derive-the-effect-and-remaining-position) can establish who can command funds in time or which obligation changed. A sufficient lower bound can settle the payment question. A decision about the effect on all participants may also need to account for who bears the displaced cost. Use the contributions on which the original decision depends.

Within Decision Design, construction of alternatives and professional engagement have different completion conditions. A sound comparison can exist while representation, consent or the conditions for useful advice remain unresolved. Conversely, a well-conducted engagement does not supply a missing mathematical or empirical argument. One language keeps these Methods independently selectable while explaining their necessary connections. Its warranted recommendation is not the authorized choice, implementation of that choice, or engineering of an operational decision service.

Narrativization helps an author decide whose experience and knowledge the reader can follow and in what order the reader can discover a connection. A scene may concern one participant while its sources concern several organizations and its conclusion concerns a practice shared across them. Choose what to show from what the reader must be able to understand or do. Establish the relation between those subjects before using the scene to explain the wider practice. This use of narrative in research, teaching or management leaves narrative work a discipline in its own right.

Several of these Methods can serve one commission. For an explanation of a past decision, distinguish what participants could know from what they actually used. A dated record may settle the question if it connects the relevant information to the decision. A request to design how decisions will be made across several services may also require a way of relating their decisions when each service can act on its own. Ask which differences between possible arrangements would change the recipient’s action or its grounds. [Research Method Practice](../../dpfs/rmp/31-rmp-1-decide-whether-a-question-needs-research-and-when-to-s.md#rmp14---solution) can establish a missing premise. [Decision Design](../../dpfs/psda/20-psda-3---construct-complementary-situation-and-option-models.md#psda34---solution) can use that premise to compare the arrangements. To design a procedure, establish who is entitled to make each decision. Determine what they can learn before each choice and how those choices can combine. Return a rule they can use to meet the joint requirement, the conditions under which it works or grounds that rule out the required procedure. If feasibility still turns on an unknown condition, identify it. An existing rule can suffice. For a commission to advise on a choice, a warranted recommendation can complete the work. Where the recipient must put the practice to work, [Implementation Facilitation](../../dpfs/is/13-is-1---agree-a-useful-implementation-support-undertaking.md#is14---solution) helps identify what the recipient will do, what can be supplied by others and which contributions must work together. If a warranted answer already settles the commission, the recipient can use it directly.

Standalone placement is open to substantive revision. A newly established larger discipline could include a field under [FPF C.20](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/39-c-20---composition-of-u-discipline.md); a changed problem family could justify repartition.
