## Library.Reference:1 - One connected language, several kinds of whole

The Library becomes useful when a question crosses boundaries that an isolated answer leaves unresolved. A sound model may be unusable with the available observations. A feasible design may lack an authorized commitment. A helpful AI answer may leave the person unable to make the next judgement independently. In each case, the missing contribution has to be identified and connected to the work that consumes it.

The common language makes these differences expressible. Core distinguishes a System, a Method, its description, performed Work, capability, evidence and authority. The domain languages explain what those distinctions require in particular practices. A mathematical consequence, a corporate decision and an acquired bodily skill therefore retain different construction and qualification conditions while participating in one undertaking.

FPF Library is an organized collection of related framework publications with this connected public account. Its unity of description comes from shared meanings, general Methods, explicit dependencies and developed returns between contributions. That does not establish one discipline above every field in the collection. A Library-wide explanation can be necessary even when the explained publications describe several independently constituted disciplines.

Within the Library, a Suite makes a stronger subject claim: it describes a composite discipline through constituent DPFs and their connections. Engineering, Management, Foundational Thinking and Human Capability Engineering each have an account of that whole. A DPF outside those Suites keeps its own subject; its Methods can supply contributions to the same work. Neither arrangement determines how many publications a particular task needs.

This distinction follows [FPF E.4's ecosystem architecture](../../sections/11-part-e---the-fpf-constitution-and-authoring-guides/05-e-4---fpf-ecosystem-architecture-framework-families-products.md) and [C.20's construction of a discipline (FPF C.20:4)](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/39-c-20---composition-of-u-discipline.md#c204---solution---recover-the-whole-from-direct-construction). The requirement for a shared explanation, including alternatives, whole conditions and worked connections, comes from [FPF E.11.PFP:4.7](../../sections/11-part-e---the-fpf-constitution-and-authoring-guides/39-e-11-pfp---framework-publication-form-profile-for-markdown.md#e11pfp47---explain-the-methods-at-each-selected-scale).

### Library.Reference:1.1 - Long mantras and connected reasoning

A language of patterns needs more than useful separate Methods. Its reader also needs to understand why particular questions belong together, how their answers constrain one another, which alternatives remain possible and what a changed premise reopens. A **long mantra** gives a memorable traversal of that connected reasoning. Its short wording helps practitioners recall the connected questions. The full explanation supplies their reasons, subject knowledge and limits. A calculation can use a mathematical formula at one of these steps.

For teaching, unfold the reasoning in an order that makes its dependencies clear. Let the learner supply the domain's terms, the pertinent facts and the operations the work requires. A spoken account, a text with blanks or a table of general types and local answers can express the same reasoning. When a term changes the type of the thing being discussed, correct it at the inference or action it would change. On a fresh case let the learner choose the distinctions and the starting point. A checklist can reveal a missing consideration. Test the proposed answers together. Could they all hold under the stated conditions and still leave the required result out of reach? An unsupported answer that matters directs the next inquiry.

In practice, begin where evidence or an available way of working gives you a foothold. Follow the dependencies that matter to the result. The same questions can be used again on a related subject, or revisited later on the same subject as conditions change. Keep an actual prerequisite with the operation that needs it. When the reasoning is familiar, give the recipient the conclusion or proposed action in the language of their work. Keep the general terms visible when you need to teach the distinctions or resolve a disagreement about them. [Reason about the types used to model a domain](07-library-reference-6---several-relations-not-one-hierarchy.md#libraryreference621---reason-about-the-types-used-to-model-a-domain)

The system-thinking long mantra is one instance. FPF A.1.STM first follows intended receiving use at the operating time. From the system boundary, it examines the larger whole and the joint functioning of its parts to derive the system's contribution. That account precedes inward reasoning about construction and backward reasoning about obtaining Methods, capable creators and their cooperation. The system-of-interest (SoI) is the product System that an extended enterprise is organized to create, change, sustain in use or retire. Agents acting for the participating organizations agree their objectives and commitments in light of that product's use. Organizational changes retain that reference while treating the creator as an engineering subject with its own requirements and local acceptance. An independent supplier can have its own product SoI: a lathe is the machine builder's product and a means for its customer to make parts. The receiving uses and agreed obligations establish how those purposes connect. These questions connect large areas of engineering and organizational knowledge. The mantra shows where material laws, production techniques and cooperation arrangements are needed and why the supplied answers must fit together. [Engineering.Reference:0.5](../../dpfs/engineering-dpf-suite/suite-reference/02-engineering-reference-0---engineering-specialization-and-mai.md#engineeringreference05---the-system-thinking-long-mantra-across-engineering-contributions) explains the connection across that Suite.

[B.5.PI:4.2.1](../../sections/08-part-b---trans-disciplinary-reasoning-cluster/43-b-5-pi---initiate-inquiry-from-work.md) explains how to use a suitable mantra to look for consequential problems that are not yet felt. Choose it by the work and its needed result, apply the supplying Method's actual connections to a current or possible case, and turn an unsupported connection into an inquiry. A noticed difficulty can reconstruct those same grounds through examination of what the work should enable. STM develops that transition for a systemic project question; other inquiries retain their own subjects. Neither a prompt nor a blank answer alone establishes an actual problem.

A development recommender uses these contributions to form advice rather than merely rank learning materials. Apply PSDA.14 in the Decision Design Principles Framework to obtain a grounded recommendation, and PSDA.15 to connect it to feasible observation and reconsideration. The actual choice, work and capability in receiving use determine what changed. Technical control, economic comparison and developmental recommendation can therefore share a connected reasoning Method while retaining their particular means and conclusions.

These accounts can meet in one undertaking without collapsing into one Method. A strategic decision can change the proposed system's use; an engineering limit can reopen a strategy; changing an offer can alter the knowledge and cooperation needed to fulfil it. Follow the shared subject or premise and bring its changed conditions to the reasoning that uses them. Shared words alone do not establish a dependency.

The teaching order makes a connection understandable one part at a time. Actual reasoning can begin at a later question, develop answers together, discover an incompatibility or return to an earlier assumption. The analogy with simultaneous equations captures this mutual constraint; the answers can be incompatible or non-unique. FPF A.22.CGUS develops the alternatives and conditions when that structure needs to be made explicit; its requirements for a formal claim are additional to using an ordinary reminder.

The general explanation of a mantra stays with the pattern or framework that supplies it. A Framework Architectural Reference connects the language's Methods; a Suite Reference explains the additional connections across its constituent fields; this Library Reference explains connections and boundaries across publications. README pages make the important entry visible. These levels should lead to the full reasoning rather than repeat several competing definitions or hide a missing Method behind a navigation link. FPF E.11.PFP:4.7.2 explains this publication arrangement.

#### Library.Reference:1.1.1 - Rhythmic coordination across different practices

Rhythmic performance, technical control and organizational work can all require one action to keep its timing while another varies. Identify the required timing relation and its reference. A specification states what should happen. A proposed schedule and the limits on the available means can establish whether that timing is feasible. For an account of an actual event, use evidence of its timing relative to the reference. When the task calls for a change, [C.43:4.5.1](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/128-c-43---connect-observation-choice-and-action-in-control.md#c43451---coordinate-recurring-work-and-separately-timed-actions) connects preparation, execution and recovery to the timing that must be preserved.

In Embodied Rhythmics, [RHY.11:4.4.1](../../dpfs/rhy/24-rhy-11---coordinate-simultaneous-rhythmic-layers.md#rhy11441---keep-a-common-time-while-allowing-different-actions) explains how to vary the position of an accent while keeping a supporting pulse. [RHY.11:5.4](../../dpfs/rhy/24-rhy-11---coordinate-simultaneous-rhythmic-layers.md#rhy1154---move-an-accent-without-moving-the-pulse) shows this in a passage. An accent after the beat can be exactly where the score places it. Compare the intended placement with the observed timing of both the accent and the next pulse. A recording can establish which relation held while leaving its cause open. If a correction is needed, distinguish an accent that missed its intended place with the pulse intact from an action that also delayed the next pulse. RHY.10 helps distinguish phase, tempo, grouping and cue delivery. If a person needs to learn the coordination, RHY.12 connects practice to the intended performance. Establish what the person can do from their own performance with the support available to them.

In Control Architecture Design, CAD.2 specifies event order, behavior between updates and reset conditions; CAD.5 relates sampling phase, information age and observation gaps to the model used for control. CAD.4 checks their shared implementation resources. A changed event time can change which information a command uses, so it returns to these constructions and the dependent feasibility or error calculation. The correspondence with rhythmics concerns the required temporal relations and their construction, not an imported model of human timing.

In Operations Management, OPS.13:4.5 selects periodic review, an event signal or their combination from the time and work needed for a useful response. OPS.10.2 supplies joint resource occupations, preparation and recovery, and the revision of the remaining schedule. An exception may need action before the next regular review; providing that response must also account for the work it interrupts and what must continue afterwards. The common choice is which temporal relations to preserve and which to change.

RHY can specify a rhythmic variation, assess what performers did or help them realize a passage. CAD explains how timing affects the information a controller uses and the response it can produce. OPS connects response times to workable commitments and the capacity to meet them. A timing relation found in one field can suggest what to examine in another. Each field supplies the means and evidence for its own conclusion.

### Library.Reference:1.2 - Holonic thinking across different kinds of whole

Holonic thinking connects a whole with the parts and relations that constitute it, and with its possible contribution to a larger whole. It helps answer a practical question: **what must be connected, retained or changed for the whole to do what the work requires?** System thinking is one important application. The same question also arises when constructing a method, understanding performed work, assembling knowledge or developing a discipline.

Begin with the whole's needed contribution and conditions of use, or with an existing whole whose construction needs explanation. Recover or propose its parts and the relations through which their contributions combine. Then bring the result back to the parts. A requirement on the whole can change the choice of a part or its connection; a limit found in a part can require another whole construction or a narrower use. The useful result is a reason for retaining or changing that construction.

The construction rule depends on the kind of whole. [FPF A.1](../../sections/05-part-a---kernel-architecture-cluster/02-a-1---recognize-a-whole-with-parts.md) supplies the common holon criterion; the subject patterns explain what constitutes each kind.

| Whole being considered | What the holonic reasoning connects | Full contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System | Constituents and their organization with functioning and use; the whole's required contribution with choices about its construction. | A.1 and A.1.STM, with the relevant system-composition Methods. |
| Method | Reusable ways of doing through their prerequisites, results, joins and alternatives; the combined way with the result it must obtain or preserve. | A.3.1 and B.1.5; B.1.5.RS for replacement and B.1.5.EW for constituent Methods in ongoing work. |
| Performed Work | Actual performance and its admitted sub-occurrences, including their time, performers and relations; the whole history with what the receiving work needs to establish. | A.15.1. |
| Episteme | Claims and their interpretation with the premises and inferential relations required by a conclusion. | C.2.1 and B.1.3. |
| Discipline | Knowledge that explains and constrains reusable practice with practice results that can test and revise that knowledge. | C.20. |

B.1:4.4 gives the corresponding modeling move. For a conclusion about a whole, apply the warranted composition rule to its constituents and their organization. For a design constraint on the whole, recover the permitted constituent configurations and which choices are still open. For a proposed construction, qualify the answer by its design assumptions. An actual enclosing system or performed assembly needs its own grounds.

The table illustrates applications of holonic thinking to different kinds of whole. A system's components, a Method's constituents and a Work occurrence's parts have different identity and composition conditions. Reasoning across them preserves those differences.

The distinction is especially useful between **methodological thinking** and reasoning about **performed work**. Methodological thinking asks how a way of doing is selected, constructed, combined or changed. Its holonic application concerns a Method whole. An account of performed work asks what actually happened and how its parts belong to that occurrence. A reusable laboratory Method can permit a retry; a particular test can contain an interruption and two attempts. The permitted branch and the performed attempts answer different questions. A five-minute log window alone identifies neither a submethod nor an admitted Work part. Plans and descriptions of future work retain their prospective meaning.

A live performance can show a feature of a reusable method without a written description. To rely on the demonstration, identify what the observer can see and which feature that observation warrants. A.3.1.MR recovers candidate methods when the way of working is uncertain. A.2.2 tests a different claim about what the observer can now do independently.

The connections between kinds make the shared account useful. Knowledge supplies grounds and limits for constructing or selecting a Method. Capable Systems perform Work that enacts Methods. Results of that Work can support claims used to revise a Method or a system design. The relevant discipline can organize this continuing relation between knowledge and practice. Each connection needs its own explanation: enactment, production, evidential use and parthood are different relations.

For example, a laboratory Method compares two readings and repeats a reading when their difference exceeds a tolerance. If the receiving scientific claim requires independent measurement paths, two readings from the same dependent path cannot supply that independence. The required conclusion changes the Method and the provision of apparatus. A better account of the Work history helps establish what was performed, but does not make the method or apparatus adequate by itself. The whole's requirement thus changes the parts through a specific reason.

To develop an explanation at this scale, use [C.39.WA](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/99-c-39-wa---develop-an-account-of-the-whole-subject.md). Recover the question and its subject, construct the relevant grounds of unity, and show how they govern the choice and change of parts. Connect worked cases to that explicit construction and vary them where its premises or branches differ. When a local result must answer an encompassing request, [C.39.QR](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/98-c-39-qr---return-results-to-the-original-question.md) supplies the return: identify the relation, justify the inference, compare what is now available with the original use and obtain the missing contribution. These Methods organize the reasoning; the subject patterns above supply the relevant conditions of composition and identity.

#### Library.Reference:1.2.1 - Related forms of thinking retain their questions

The holonic aspect of system thinking concerns Systems, the holonic aspect of methodological thinking concerns Methods, and the holonic aspect of reasoning about performed work concerns Work. These distinctions help explain the common contribution. The familiar names also have wider uses. A.1.STM connects the proposed system with its intended use, creators, Methods and cooperation; OPS.23 connects work with capacity, commitments, service, money and people's conditions.

Management and strategic reasoning connect several of these subjects. C.43 relates observations, choices and actions under the conditions of the same continuation. STR.18 relates worthwhile contributions, their obtaining ways, feasible means and commitments. Their holonic questions can concern systems, methods, work and knowledge together. “Strategic” and “operational” name different questions here; the words alone place neither the thinker nor the object at a particular system level.

Mathematical thinking also has several contributions. A proof account or theory can be considered as a connected whole of claims. A reusable proof Method can compose submethods. Mathematical thinking additionally constructs objects, operations, representations, witnesses and countermodels, and develops conjectures. A mathematical object that a claim concerns is different from the claim-bearing Episteme. [MATH.Reference:1–3](../../dpfs/foundational-thinking-dpf-suite/math/01-math-reference-1---problem-frame---construct-mathematics-for.md) develops that broader repertoire.

This distinction changes the next action. An adequate proof of existence may settle one mathematical question while a later use requires a way to obtain a witness. MATH.12 examines what construction the proof supplies; another mathematical contribution may be needed. A constructive account of mathematical work preserves the distinction between existence, a witness-producing operation and its execution; it need not exclude classical reasoning.

Synthesis connects what one contribution supplies with what another needs. The work is interdisciplinary when the shared result depends on contributions from distinct disciplines. For a transdisciplinary principle, state the relation or operation and the conditions under which it can be interpreted in different disciplines. To show its use within one field, identify the distinction, inference or action it guided there. To establish a new discipline, [FPF C.20:4.1](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/39-c-20---composition-of-u-discipline.md#c2041---run-the-complete-recognition-test) asks how its knowledge and reusable practices form a whole that can persist through change.

#### Library.Reference:1.2.2 - Use and translate the terms through their subject

In introductory writing, “holonic thinking about systems, methods, performed work, knowledge and disciplines” names the subjects directly. A shorter adjective works when the surrounding explanation makes the subject and operation clear.

Existing usages can differ. English “systematic” can mean methodical organization; it is not a substitute for FPF's “system thinking.” Richmond's [Operational Thinking](https://thesystemsthinker.com/operational-thinking/) concerns how actual processes work and their causal organization. The French historical expression *pensée opératoire* concerns logical operations in [Piaget's account](https://www.unige.ch/piaget/piaget1947PIN_03). Neither expression should be translated into the Work-holon application merely from its word form.

[E.10's lexical guidance](../../sections/11-part-e---the-fpf-constitution-and-authoring-guides/26-e-10---unified-lexical-rules-for-fpf.md#e1099---thinking-terms-and-the-whole-being-considered) applies the distinction in authoring. Preserve the source meaning, make the intended subject and relation clear, and use the full subject Method when a decision depends on its construction. The shared explanation gives an entry into that work; recognition of a holonic question and assurance that a particular whole satisfies its conditions remain separate results.

### Library.Reference:1.3 - A canon for shared human–AI culture

The Library articulates a culture of thinking and acting oriented toward the open-ended development of people, AI agents and their joint practice. It can serve as that culture’s **canon**: a common, revisable body of explanations, methods, worked cases and grounds for judgement. Participants can use this repertoire to choose and perform work, examine its results and develop better ways of continuing. The canon makes the shared practice recoverable and revisable across different situations.

People and AI agents can both sustain and renew this culture through their work. An AI contribution may be assistance with one operation or participation as a teammate responsible for an agreed contribution. The working arrangement determines who can perform, decide, check and change what. Shared use therefore combines different capabilities and responsibilities; each participant needs access to the contributions relevant to its work. No individual has to internalize the whole Library. [C.32.MWA.PP](../../sections/09-part-c---kernel-extension-specifications/83-c-32-mwa-pp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-p.md) explains how to construct and compare such arrangements.

Acquiring and sustaining the practice requires different means for different participants. People may need explanation, repeated practice, feedback and support for participation. AI agents need suitable capabilities, access to the relevant material, execution support and continuity of attention and task context. Giving an agent a text can make the method available. To establish use of that source, trace an instruction or rule from it to an operation, interpretation or choice in the agent's work. An execution record can show that the agent loaded that edition and carried out its sole prescribed step. The resulting product can meet its commission whether the agent learned the rule there, already knew it or obtained the answer from a teammate. [C.36.RP and C.36.MC](10-library-reference-9---open-ended-development-belongs-across.md#libraryreference917---how-does-a-useful-episode-become-a-renewable-cultural-contribution) connect the shared repertoire to these different ways of obtaining, reconstructing and renewing practice.

For example, a person and an AI teammate can use the same engineering methods while dividing model construction, criticism and decisions according to capability and authority. A changed requirement calls for recovering the affected assumptions and revising their connected work. The person may need to learn a missing judgement; the agent may need better access to evidence or a changed execution procedure. A useful result can then expose a further attainable problem or a better method. [The connected development account](10-library-reference-9---open-ended-development-belongs-across.md#libraryreference91---open-ended-development-through-connected-contributions) explains that continuation and the distinctions between assistance, learning, cultural renewal and development. A publication supplies the canon; actual use, retention and renewal establish the culture in practice.

For the connected practical account, section [9.2](10-library-reference-9---open-ended-development-belongs-across.md#libraryreference92---help-enablement-and-the-organization-of-professional-support) explains how to choose and organize help, and section [9.3](10-library-reference-9---open-ended-development-belongs-across.md#libraryreference93---acquiring-sustaining-and-changing-a-shared-humanai-professional-culture) explains how people and AI acquire, sustain and renew their shared practice. Its [forecast case](10-library-reference-9---open-ended-development-belongs-across.md#libraryreference9321---derive-the-next-contribution-from-the-result-and-its-conditions) shows how available conditions change the useful continuation.
