## How to read and update the registry

Types: **N** — normative standard; **G** — official guidance; **R** — the publisher's own research/benchmark; **E** — methodology or expert authored material; **C** — catalog for orientation. The type is not a quality rating and not a guarantee of applicability.

Distinguish the publication date, the revision date, the component release date and our verification date. "Not given" means the date could not be established from the page as read. A year in a Laws of UX URL is not evidence of the exact publication date. Do not substitute a search engine's indexing date or a footer copyright for these fields.

Update selectively: when a standard or platform changes, a link becomes unavailable, sources contradict each other, or freshness is requested. Read the document itself; a snippet is not enough for a numeric requirement. Keep the ID when the canonical URL changes, record the new verification date and revisit the affected rules. Do not create an automatic schedule unless asked.

## Corpus boundaries

For Material, the readable official Android guides DS10–DS11 were used. The Material 3 Expressive pages and the main Apple HIG section returned a JS shell in this verification; their content is not included as evidence that was read. DS12 is a separate, accessible official Apple page. The main PAIR URL /guidebook/ also returned no text; the registry lists the chapters that were read.

Growth.Design is included as an analog in format and a source of ideas. The skill's practical norms rest on applicable standards, official guidelines, research and explicitly marked skill synthesis. Historical articles are kept where they explain a lasting principle; their interface examples are not presented as the state of products in 2026.

Not used: the paid Baymard corpus, unavailable full texts, marketing promises of conversion growth, and legal conclusions drawn from secondary reviews. An article's presence in the registry does not mean every piece of its advice is accepted.
