# Ecommerce: catalog, search and checkout

The basis is Baymard's public own research and benchmarks, verified **2026-10-21**. Apply it to matching commercial tasks. Do not claim access to all paid guidelines, and do not carry findings automatically from retail over to enterprise SaaS.

## EC-01 — Checkout

Source: [Checkout UX 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-of-checkout-ux), Baymard Institute; published 2024-11-13, updated 2025-11-25.

Make guest checkout discoverable when the service does not require an account. Show the expected delivery date or range so people do not have to work out the timing themselves. Remove unnecessary input, explain why any non-standard required fields are needed, and keep the answers when an error occurs. Check the purchase from cart to confirmation on the devices available to you.

Do not carry this article's password-length advice over into security requirements. Do not promise a conversion lift from someone else's sample; measure the effect in your own product.

## EC-02 — Search

Source: [Ecommerce Search UX 2026](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ecommerce-search-query-types), Baymard Institute; published 2024-09-12, updated 2026-04-29.

Test queries by exact name, model, product type, synonym and typo. For a multilingual catalog, test localized terms. Match results to intent; offer a category or a refinement when it helps people continue. Redirect to a category only on a reliable, unambiguous match. Otherwise keep the query visible and editable. Build a set of real queries from logs or research tasks; do not present invented examples as observations.

## EC-03 — Categories and filters

Source: [Homepage and Category Navigation UX 2026](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice), Baymard Institute; published 2024-08-06, updated 2026-10-13.

In a mobile catalog, make the main categories discoverable. Distinguish the product type from the attribute people want to narrow the set by: combinable attributes are often easier to offer as filters. Do not split the assortment into incompatible branches when the task requires combining attributes. Test selection by several conditions and the way out of a zero-result page. Do not use this finding as a universal ban on hierarchical navigation.

## Transaction transparency: skill synthesis with H04 and DS04

Before the final action, show a clear order composition, payment frequency, the full known cost and the material terms. Provide a way to review and change the data. When the price is unknown, explain what affects the calculation. Check refusal, return and cancellation where they apply to the service. Do not make marketing consent a condition of an unrelated operation without an established need. Determine legal requirements from sources relevant to the jurisdiction, not from this UX section.

After submission, distinguish order confirmation, payment acceptance and actual fulfillment. On failure, do not prompt people to repeat the payment without checking its state. The last rules are skill synthesis on error handling, not numeric Baymard findings.
